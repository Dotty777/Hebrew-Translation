## Text

**22:1** A psalm of David. The LORD is my shepherd; I will not lack anything.

**22:2** He makes me lie down in meadows of fresh grass; He guides me beside restful waters.

**22:3** He brings back my soul; He leads me in tracks of righteousness for the sake of His name.

**22:4** Even though I walk in a valley of death's shadow, I will not fear evil, for You are with me; Your rod and Your staff, they comfort me.

**22:5** You arrange a table before me in front of my foes; You have anointed my head with oil; my cup is brimming over.

**22:6** Surely goodness and loyal love will pursue me all the days of my life, and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for length of days.

## Translator Notes

- **22:1** — This is Hebrew/MT Psalm 23 (Saints and Greek/Latin numbering: Psalm 22). The superscription mizmor ledawid is part of verse 1 in the Hebrew.
- **22:2** — Menuchoth, literally "rests," describes waters where one can rest.
- **22:3** — Nafshi yeshovev is literally "He brings back my soul (life)," meaning He restores or revives it. Ma'gele-tsedeq are "tracks" or "paths of righteousness," which can also mean the right paths.
- **22:4** — The Masoretic pointing tsalmaweth reads as a compound of tsel ("shadow") and maweth ("death"), rendered "death's shadow"; many scholars instead read tsalmuth, "deep darkness." Ra means evil or harm.
- **22:5** — Dishanta is literally "You have made fat"; with oil it means to anoint lavishly. Rewayah means "saturation, overflowing."
- **22:6** — Radaf means "pursue" or "chase." Chesed is rendered "loyal love," love that keeps covenant. The pointing weshavti looks like "and I will return" (from shuv), but with "in the house" and "for length of days" it is usually taken as a form of yashav, "dwell"; that sense is followed here. Le'orekh yamim is literally "for length of days," meaning for a long life or forever.
