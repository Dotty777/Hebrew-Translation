## Text

**2:1** In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuchadnezzar had dreams; his spirit was troubled, and his sleep left him.

**2:2** Then the king gave orders to summon the magicians, the conjurers, the sorcerers, and the Chaldeans to tell the king his dreams. So they came and stood before the king.

**2:3** The king said to them, "I have had a dream, and my spirit is troubled to know the dream."

**2:4** Then the Chaldeans spoke to the king in Aramaic: "O king, live forever! Tell the dream to your servants, and we will declare the interpretation."

**2:5** The king answered and said to the Chaldeans, "The word from me is firm: if you do not make known to me the dream and its interpretation, you will be torn limb from limb, and your houses will be made a garbage heap.

**2:6** But if you declare the dream and its interpretation, you will receive from me gifts and a reward and great honor. Therefore declare to me the dream and its interpretation."

**2:7** They answered a second time and said, "Let the king tell the dream to his servants, and we will declare the interpretation."

**2:8** The king answered and said, "I know for certain that you are buying time, because you have seen that the word from me is firm,

**2:9** that if you do not make the dream known to me, there is one sentence for you. You have agreed together to speak lying and corrupt words before me until the time changes. Therefore tell me the dream, and I will know that you can declare to me its interpretation."

**2:10** The Chaldeans answered before the king and said, "There is not a man on the dry land who is able to declare the king's matter, inasmuch as no king, however great and powerful, has asked a thing like this of any magician or conjurer or Chaldean.

**2:11** The thing that the king is asking is difficult, and there is no one else who can declare it before the king except gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh."

**2:12** Because of this the king became angry and very furious, and he gave orders to destroy all the wise men of Babylon.

**2:13** So the decree went out, and the wise men were to be killed; and they looked for Daniel and his companions, to kill them.

**2:14** Then Daniel responded with counsel and discretion to Arioch, the chief of the king's guard, who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.

**2:15** He answered and said to Arioch, the king's officer, "Why is the decree from the king so harsh?" Then Arioch made the matter known to Daniel.

**2:16** So Daniel went in and asked the king to give him time, so that he might declare the interpretation to the king.

**2:17** Then Daniel went to his house and made the matter known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions,

**2:18** so that they might ask for compassion from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his companions would not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

**2:19** Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

**2:20** Daniel answered and said, "Let the name of God be blessed from everlasting to everlasting, for wisdom and power belong to Him.

**2:21** He changes the times and the seasons; He removes kings and sets up kings; He gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who know understanding.

**2:22** He reveals the deep and hidden things; He knows what is in the darkness, and the light dwells with Him.

**2:23** To You, O God of my fathers, I give thanks and praise, for You have given me wisdom and power; and now You have made known to me what we asked of You, for You have made known to us the king's matter."

**2:24** Therefore Daniel went in to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon. He went and said this to him: "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon. Bring me in before the king, and I will declare the interpretation to the king."

**2:25** Then Arioch hurriedly brought Daniel in before the king and said this to him: "I have found a man among the exiles from Judah who will make known the interpretation to the king."

**2:26** The king answered and said to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, "Are you able to make known to me the dream that I have seen and its interpretation?"

**2:27** Daniel answered before the king and said, "The mystery that the king is asking about, no wise men, conjurers, magicians, or diviners are able to declare to the king.

**2:28** But there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and He has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will happen in the latter days. Your dream and the visions of your head on your bed were these:

**2:29** As for you, O king, your thoughts came up on your bed about what will happen after this; and He who reveals mysteries has made known to you what will happen.

**2:30** As for me, this mystery has been revealed to me not because of any wisdom that is in me more than in all the living, but in order that the interpretation may be made known to the king, and that you may know the thoughts of your heart.

**2:31** You, O king, were looking, and there was a single great statue. That statue, which was large and whose brightness was extraordinary, was standing in front of you, and its appearance was terrifying.

**2:32** As for that statue, its head was of fine gold, its chest and its arms of silver, its belly and its thighs of bronze,

**2:33** its legs of iron, its feet partly of iron and partly of clay.

**2:34** You kept looking until a stone was cut out without hands, and it struck the statue on its feet of iron and clay and crushed them.

**2:35** Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver, and the gold were crushed all at once and became like chaff from the summer threshing floors; and the wind carried them away, so that not a trace of them was found. But the stone that struck the statue became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.

**2:36** This was the dream, and we will tell its interpretation before the king.

**2:37** You, O king, are the king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom, the power, the strength, and the glory;

**2:38** and wherever the sons of men dwell, He has given the beasts of the field and the birds of the heavens into your hand and has made you ruler over them all. You are the head of gold.

**2:39** After you another kingdom will arise, lower than you, and another, a third kingdom, of bronze, which will rule over all the earth.

**2:40** Then there will be a fourth kingdom, strong as iron, inasmuch as iron crushes and shatters everything; and like iron that smashes, it will crush and smash all these.

**2:41** And as you saw the feet and the toes, partly of potter's clay and partly of iron, it will be a divided kingdom; but some of the firmness of iron will be in it, inasmuch as you saw the iron mixed with muddy clay.

**2:42** And as the toes of the feet were partly of iron and partly of clay, so part of the kingdom will be strong and part of it will be brittle.

**2:43** And as you saw the iron mixed with muddy clay, they will mix themselves with the seed of men, but they will not hold together, one with another, just as iron does not mix with clay.

**2:44** In the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that will never be destroyed, and the kingdom will not be left to another people. It will crush and bring to an end all these kingdoms, and it will stand forever,

**2:45** inasmuch as you saw that a stone was cut out of the mountain without hands and that it crushed the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver, and the gold. A great God has made known to the king what will happen after this. The dream is certain, and its interpretation is trustworthy."

**2:46** Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face and bowed down to Daniel, and he gave orders to present to him an offering and soothing aromas.

**2:47** The king answered Daniel and said, "Truly your God is the God of gods and the Lord of kings and a revealer of mysteries, since you have been able to reveal this mystery."

**2:48** Then the king made Daniel great and gave him many great gifts, and he made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon and chief prefect over all the wise men of Babylon.

**2:49** And Daniel made a request of the king, and he appointed Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego over the administration of the province of Babylon; but Daniel was at the king's court.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — Shenatho nihyethah alaw is literally "his sleep was upon him" or "came to an end on him"; the sense is that he could not sleep.
- **2:2** — The four classes are chartummim (magicians), ashshafim (conjurers or enchanters), mekhashshefim (sorcerers) and kasdim (Chaldeans, here a class of learned diviners).
- **2:4** — The Hebrew narrative switches to Aramaic after "in Aramaic"; the Aramaic continues through 7:28. Ketiv l'bdyk / qere le'avdakh both mean "to your servants"; the qere is translated.
- **2:5** — Ketiv/qere differ only in the spelling of "to the Chaldeans." Azda is a Persian loanword probably meaning "certain, publicly announced" (older translations took it as "gone"). Haddamin tit'avdun is literally "you will be made into pieces." Newali means "refuse heap."
- **2:8** — Idanna antun zavnin is literally "you are buying the time," that is, stalling.
- **2:9** — Chadah-hi dathkhon is literally "one is your law," meaning there is only one sentence for you. The qere hizdemintun ("you have agreed together") is translated in place of the ketiv spelling.
- **2:10** — The ketiv/qere difference is only in the spelling of "Chaldeans." Kol-qovel di is a stock Aramaic connective, rendered "inasmuch as" in this chapter.
- **2:11** — "Flesh" (bisra) means mortal humanity.
- **2:14** — Rav-tabbachayya is literally "chief of the slaughterers"; the title came to mean the commander of the royal bodyguard.
- **2:18** — Elah shemayya, rendered "the God of heaven," is a regular title in Persian-period Aramaic; shemayya has the same plural form as Hebrew shamayim.
- **2:22** — The qere nehora ("light") is translated; the ketiv differs only in spelling.
- **2:25** — Bene galutha di Yehud is literally "the sons of the exile of Judah."
- **2:26** — The ketiv/qere difference (ha'ithakh, "are you") is one of spelling only.
- **2:27** — Gazrin, literally "cutters/deciders," are probably astrologers or diviners who determine fates.
- **2:28** — Be'acharith yomayya is literally "in the end of the days." The "visions of your head" are what passed through his mind.
- **2:29** — The qere ant ("you") is translated, and likewise in 2:31, 2:37 and 2:38. "He who reveals mysteries" (gale razayya) refers to God, so the pronoun is capitalized.
- **2:31** — Wa'alu is an interjection like "look!"; it is rendered "and there was."
- **2:33** — Chasaf is clay or earthenware; the qere forms minnehen (feminine "some of them") are translated as "partly ... partly."
- **2:38** — Bene anasha is rendered literally as "sons of men" (human beings). The qere dayerin ("dwell") is translated.
- **2:39** — The qere ara' ("lower, inferior") and telitha'ah ("third") are translated; the ketiv differ in spelling.
- **2:40** — The qere revi'a'ah ("fourth") is translated.
- **2:41** — Nitsbetha means firmness or hardness. Chasaf di-fechar is "potter's clay," and chasaf tina is "clay of mud."
- **2:43** — The qere wedi ("and as") is translated. "Mix themselves with the seed of men" probably refers to intermarriage between royal houses, but the Aramaic is kept literal.
- **2:46** — Segid means to bow down or pay homage; "bowed down" keeps the act without deciding on its theology. Nichochin are pleasing or soothing aromas (incense).
- **2:47** — Mare malkhin is rendered "Lord of kings" because it refers to God.
- **2:48** — Rabbi is "made great," that is, promoted. Rav-signin is "chief of the prefects."
- **2:49** — Bithra malka is literally "in the gate of the king," meaning the royal court where officials served.
