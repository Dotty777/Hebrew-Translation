## Text

**4:1** How gold has darkened, how the choice fine gold has changed! Holy stones are poured out at the head of all the streets.

**4:2** Zion's precious sons, valued against refined gold, how they are counted as clay jars, the work of the hands of a potter!

**4:3** Even jackals bare the breast; they nurse their cubs. Yet the daughter of my people has turned cruel, like ostriches in the wilderness.

**4:4** The tongue of a nursing infant clings to his palate from thirst; young children beg for bread, yet there is no one breaking it for them.

**4:5** Those who fed on delicacies lie desolate in the streets; those nurtured on crimson embrace refuse heaps.

**4:6** And the iniquity of the daughter of my people became greater than the sin of Sodom, which was overthrown in an instant, and no hands fell upon her.

**4:7** Her nobles were brighter than snow, whiter than milk; they were ruddier in frame than corals—their form was lapis lazuli.

**4:8** Their appearance has turned darker than soot; they go unrecognized in the streets. Their skin has drawn tight over their bones; it has dried up, it has become like wood.

**4:9** Better off were those killed by the sword than those killed by famine, for these drain away, pierced through for lack of the yield of the field.

**4:10** The hands of compassionate women cooked their children; they became food for them in the shattering of the daughter of my people.

**4:11** The LORD exhausted His wrath; He poured out His burning anger, and He kindled a fire in Zion, and it devoured her foundations.

**4:12** The kings of the earth, all who dwell in the world, did not believe that adversary and enemy would enter the gates of Jerusalem.

**4:13** On account of the sins of her prophets, the iniquities of her priests, who shed the blood of the righteous within her.

**4:14** Blind, they wandered in the streets; they were polluted with blood, so that no one could touch their clothing.

**4:15** "Turn aside! Unclean!" people called out to them, "Turn aside, turn aside, do not touch!" When they fled and also wandered, people said among the nations, "They will no longer sojourn."

**4:16** The face of the LORD has scattered them; He will no longer look upon them. They showed no honor to priests, and they showed no favor to elders.

**4:17** Still our eyes fail for our futile help; from our watchtower we have watched for a nation that cannot save.

**4:18** They stalked our steps so that we could not walk in our public squares; our end approached, our days were complete, for our end had come.

**4:19** Swifter were our pursuers than the eagles of the heavens; on the mountains they hotly pursued us, in the wilderness they lay in ambush for us.

**4:20** The breath of our nostrils, the anointed of the LORD, was caught in their pits—the one about whom we said, "In his shadow we will live among the nations."

**4:21** Exult and be glad, daughter of Edom, who dwells in the land of Uz; even to you the cup will pass—you will grow drunk and strip yourself naked.

**4:22** The penalty for your iniquity has ended, daughter of Zion; He will not send you into exile again. He will call your iniquity to account, daughter of Edom; He will lay bare your sins.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — Ketiv/Qere variants: the Qere reads tannim ("jackals," standard Hebrew plural agreeing with the plural verbs) against the Ketiv tannin (an Aramaizing plural or singular "sea monster"), and reads the single-word Qere kay'enim ("like ostriches") where the Ketiv splits the consonants into two words.
- **4:6** — The nouns for "iniquity" and "sin" here encompass both guilt and its resulting punishment. The clause "and no hands fell upon her" renders the Qal perfect of hul ("whirl/fall upon"), contrasting Sodom's sudden overthrow with Jerusalem's prolonged siege.
- **4:12** — Ketiv/Qere variant: the Qere omits the conjunction before "all" (kol), translated asyndetically, whereas the Ketiv reads "and all" (wkol).
- **4:17** — Ketiv/Qere variant: the Qere reads odenu (first-person plural, "still we"), whereas the Ketiv reads an archaic/adverbial form odynh ("still/yet").
- **4:22** — Hebrew awon ("iniquity") denotes both guilt and the penalty it brings. The verse also plays on two stems of the root glh: Hiphil lehaglotek ("send you into exile") for Zion and Piel gillah ("lay bare") for Edom.
