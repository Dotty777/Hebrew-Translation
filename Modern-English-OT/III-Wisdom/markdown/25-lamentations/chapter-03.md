## Text

**3:1** I am the man who has seen affliction by the rod of His fury.

**3:2** He has driven me and led me into darkness, and not light.

**3:3** Indeed, against me He repeatedly turns His hand all day long.

**3:4** He has worn out my flesh and my skin; He has broken my bones in pieces.

**3:5** He has built against me and encircled me with poison and hardship.

**3:6** He has made me sit in dark places, like those long since dead.

**3:7** He has walled me in, and I cannot leave; He has made my bronze shackles heavy.

**3:8** Even though I cry out and call for help, He blocks my prayer.

**3:9** He has walled up my ways with cut stone; He has twisted my paths.

**3:10** He is to me a bear waiting in ambush, a lion in hiding places.

**3:11** He has diverted my ways and ripped me apart; He has left me desolate.

**3:12** He has bent His bow and stationed me as a target for the arrow.

**3:13** He has caused the sons of His quiver to enter my kidneys.

**3:14** I have become an object of laughter to all my people, their song all day long.

**3:15** He has sated me with bitter herbs; He has made me drink my fill of wormwood.

**3:16** And He has crushed my teeth with gravel; He has pressed me down into the ashes.

**3:17** And You have rejected my soul from peace; I have forgotten what is good.

**3:18** So I said, "My endurance and my hope from the LORD have perished."

**3:19** Remember my affliction and my homelessness, wormwood and poison.

**3:20** My soul surely remembers and sinks down within me.

**3:21** This I bring back to my heart; therefore I will hope.

**3:25** The LORD is good to those waiting for Him, to a soul that seeks Him.

**3:26** It is good to wait in silence for the deliverance of the LORD.

**3:27** It is good for a man to carry a yoke in his youth.

**3:28** Let him sit alone and keep silent, because He has imposed it upon him.

**3:30** Let him give his cheek to the one striking him; let him have his fill of reproach.

**3:31** For the Lord will not reject for all time.

**3:32** For though He brings grief, He will show compassion according to the abundance of His acts of loyal love.

**3:33** For it is not from His heart that He afflicts or brings grief to the sons of man.

**3:34** To crush under one's feet all the prisoners of the earth,

**3:35** to pervert a man's justice before the face of the Most High,

**3:36** to wrong a person in his case—the Lord does not approve.

**3:37** Who is this who has spoken and it came to be, if the Lord has not commanded it?

**3:38** Do not calamities and good come from the mouth of the Most High?

**3:39** Why should a living person complain, a man about his sins?

**3:40** Let us search our ways and probe them, and let us turn back to the LORD.

**3:41** Let us lift up our heart together with our hands to God in the heavens.

**3:42** We ourselves have transgressed and rebelled; You have not forgiven.

**3:43** You have shrouded Yourself in anger and pursued us. You have killed; You have not spared.

**3:44** You have shrouded Yourself in a cloud so that prayer cannot pass through.

**3:45** You make us sweepings and refuse in the midst of the peoples.

**3:46** All our enemies have opened their mouths wide at us.

**3:47** Terror and pitfall have fallen to us, desolation and ruin.

**3:48** My eye flows down with streams of water over the ruin of the daughter of my people.

**3:49** My eye pours out and will not cease, with no pause,

**3:50** until the LORD gazes down from the heavens and sees.

**3:51** My eye torments my soul because of all the daughters of my city.

**3:52** My enemies relentlessly hunted me like a bird, without cause.

**3:53** They silenced my life in the pit and hurled a stone at me.

**3:54** Waters flooded over my head; I said, "I have been cut off."

**3:55** I called out Your name, LORD, from the deepest pit.

**3:56** You heard my voice; do not hide Your ear from my gasping, from my cry for help.

**3:57** You drew near on the day I called to You; You said, "Do not be afraid."

**3:58** Lord, You pleaded the cases of my soul; You redeemed my life.

**3:59** LORD, You have seen the injustice done to me; judge my cause.

**3:60** You have seen all their vengefulness, all their plans against me.

**3:61** LORD, You have heard their reproach, all their plans against me.

**3:62** The lips of those who rise against me and their muttering are against me all day long.

**3:63** Look at their sitting down and their rising up; I am their taunt song.

**3:64** You will pay recompense to them, LORD, in accordance with the work of their hands.

**3:65** You will give them obstinacy of heart, Your curse upon them.

**3:66** You will pursue them in anger and wipe them out from beneath the heavens of the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **3:10** — The ketiv and qere are alternate Hebrew forms for "lion" (aryeh vs. ari) with no difference in meaning.
- **3:13** — "The sons of His quiver" is a literal Hebrew poetic idiom for arrows, and "kidneys" denotes the innermost vitals.
- **3:17** — The Qal verb can be parsed as either second-person masculine singular ("You have rejected my soul from peace") or third-person feminine singular ("my soul has been estranged from peace"). Because Qal zanach is regularly transitive with God as subject (cf. 3:31), it is translated here as a direct address to God.
- **3:20** — The qere reads a Qal third-person feminine singular verb ("sinks down") with "my soul" as subject of both verbs; the ketiv consonants can be read either as Qal from a verb meaning "broods/complains" or as Hiphil ("brings down").
- **3:26** — The Hebrew clause is terse, literally "good, both waiting and in silence," pairing a verbal adjective for waiting with an adverb meaning silently.
- **3:36** — Literally "the Lord has not seen," using the verb raah in the sense of looking favorably upon or approving; some alternatively take it as a rhetorical question ("does the Lord not see?").
- **3:41** — The preposition el ("to") is used here in the sense of al ("together with" or "upon"), picturing lifting the heart together with upturned hands in prayer.
- **3:51** — The Poel verb olalah ("deals harshly with" or "torments") is used elsewhere in Lamentations for severe affliction (1:12, 22; 2:20), and the preposition min is causal ("because of").
- **3:56** — The rare noun ravchati ("my gasping" or "my relief," cf. Exod 8:11) derives from a root meaning "breathe freely" or "find relief"; paired with "my cry for help" after "Your ear," it denotes either a desperate gasp for breath or an appeal for relief.
- **3:65** — The rare construct phrase meginnat-lev literally means "a covering of heart" (from a root meaning "cover" or "shield"), denoting obstinacy or dullness of heart; some alternatively connect it to a root meaning "anguish."
- **3:22 *(additional)*** — Follows the Masoretic Hebrew first-person plural verb rather than the third-person emendation adopted by some ancient versions.
