## Text

**5:1** Remember, LORD, what has become of us; look closely and see our disgrace.

**5:2** Our inheritance has been turned over to outsiders, our houses to foreigners.

**5:3** We have become orphans and are without a father; our mothers are like widows.

**5:4** We drink our water for silver; our wood comes at a price.

**5:5** At our necks we are pursued; we have grown weary, and no rest is given to us.

**5:6** To Egypt we gave our hand, to Assyria, to be satisfied with bread.

**5:7** Our fathers sinned, and they are gone; yet we have borne their iniquities.

**5:8** Slaves have ruled over us; there is no rescuer from their hand.

**5:9** At the risk of our lives we bring in our bread, because of the sword of the wilderness.

**5:10** Our skin is scorched like an oven because of the raging heat of famine.

**5:11** Women in Zion they violated, virgins in the cities of Judah.

**5:12** Princes were hanged by their hands; the faces of elders were not honored.

**5:13** Young men carried the handmill, and boys stumbled under the wood.

**5:14** Elders have ceased from the gate, young men from their music.

**5:15** The gladness of our heart has ceased; our dancing has turned into mourning.

**5:16** The crown of our head has fallen; woe now to us, for we have sinned!

**5:17** Over this our heart has become faint; over these things our eyes have darkened.

**5:18** Over Mount Zion, which lies desolate—foxes walk about on it.

**5:19** You, LORD, sit enthroned forever; Your throne is for generation after generation.

**5:20** Why do You permanently forget us, why do You abandon us for so long a time?

**5:21** Bring us back to Yourself, LORD, that we may return; renew our days as in former times.

**5:22** Unless You have completely rejected us and are furious with us to the utmost.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — The MT reads al tsawwarenu nirdaphnu (preposition al with Niphal perfect of rdp, literally "upon our neck we are pursued"), an idiom for relentless pursuit hard at one's heels. The Qere also adds the conjunction "and" before "no" (wlo; Ketiv lo).
- **5:7** — Ketiv/Qere variants: the Qere supplies the conjunction ("and/yet") before both enam ("they are gone") and anahnu ("we"), sharpening the contrast between the fathers' sin and the survivors' burden.
- **5:12** — Hebrew beyadam (literally "by their hand") can mean either that princes were hanged by the enemies' hand or that they were hung up by their own hands.
- **5:21** — Ketiv/Qere variant: following the Hiphil imperative hashibenu ("bring us back"), the Qere reads the Qal cohortative wnashubah ("that we may return"), whereas the Ketiv reads the simple Qal imperfect wnashub ("and we will return").
- **5:22** — The opening conjunction ki im is a noted crux in Biblical Hebrew and can be read as "unless" (qualifying the petition of v. 21), "for surely," or "even though."
