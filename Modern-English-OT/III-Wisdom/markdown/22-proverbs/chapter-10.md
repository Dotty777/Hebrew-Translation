## Text

**10:1** The proverbs of Solomon: A wise son gladdens a father, but a foolish son is the grief of his mother.

**10:2** Treasures of wickedness yield no profit, but righteousness rescues from death.

**10:3** The LORD does not leave the soul of a righteous person hungry, but He thrusts aside the craving of the wicked.

**10:4** Impoverished is one who works with a slack hand, but the hand of the diligent brings wealth.

**10:5** One who gathers in summer is a discerning son; one who sleeps soundly in harvest is a son who brings disgrace.

**10:6** Blessings are upon the head of a righteous person, but the mouth of the wicked hides violence.

**10:7** The remembrance of a righteous person is for blessing, but the name of the wicked rots away.

**10:8** One wise of heart accepts commands, but one foolish of lips will fall to ruin.

**10:9** One who walks in integrity walks in safety, but one who twists his ways will be exposed.

**10:10** One who winks the eye causes pain, and one foolish of lips will fall to ruin.

**10:11** The mouth of a righteous person is a wellspring of life, but the mouth of the wicked hides violence.

**10:12** Hatred rouses quarrels, but love covers over all transgressions.

**10:13** On the lips of an understanding person wisdom is found, and for the back of one lacking sense, a rod.

**10:14** Wise people treasure up knowledge, but the mouth of a fool is ruin close at hand.

**10:15** The wealth of a rich person is his strong city; the ruin of the needy is their poverty.

**10:16** The earnings of a righteous person lead to life; the income of a wicked person leads to sin.

**10:17** One who keeps discipline is a path to life, but one who abandons reproof causes others to stray.

**10:18** One who hides hatred has lips of falsehood, and one who brings forth slander is a fool.

**10:19** In an abundance of words, transgression does not cease, but one who holds back his lips is discerning.

**10:20** Select silver is the tongue of a righteous person; the heart of the wicked is worth very little.

**10:21** The lips of a righteous person shepherd many, but through lack of sense fools die.

**10:22** It is the blessing of the LORD that brings wealth, and He does not add sorrow with it.

**10:23** Carrying out a wicked scheme is like amusement to a fool, and wisdom is the same for a man of understanding.

**10:24** The very thing a wicked person dreads will come upon him, but the desire of the righteous will be given to them.

**10:25** When a whirlwind sweeps past, a wicked person is no more, but a righteous person is a foundation forever.

**10:26** As vinegar is to the teeth and as smoke is to the eyes, so is the lazy person to those who send him.

**10:27** The fear of the LORD adds days, but the years of the wicked will grow short.

**10:28** The expectation of the righteous is joy, but the hope of the wicked will perish.

**10:29** The way of the LORD is a fortress for the person of integrity, but ruin for doers of iniquity.

**10:30** A righteous person will never be moved, but the wicked will not inhabit the land.

**10:31** The mouth of a righteous person yields wisdom, but a tongue of perversity will be cut off.

**10:32** The lips of a righteous person know what pleases, but the mouth of the wicked knows perversity.

## Translator Notes

- **10:4** — Hebrew rash ("impoverished") and oseh ("working") are both masculine singular participles, indicating a person working with a slack hand rather than taking the feminine noun kaph ("hand") as subject.
- **10:6** — Because both "the mouth of the wicked" and "violence" are masculine singular without an object marker, the Hebrew clause (repeated verbatim in 10:11) can be read either as "the mouth of the wicked hides violence" or "violence covers the mouth of the wicked."
- **10:8** — The rare Niphal verb yillabet (found only here, 10:10, and Hosea 4:14) denotes being thrust down or falling to ruin; in 10:10 the Masoretic Hebrew repeats this entire second line verbatim.
- **10:17** — Hebrew literally equates the one who keeps discipline with "a path to life" (though some read an implied "on the path to life"); matteh is a Hiphil participle meaning "causes to stray," though sometimes taken intransitively as "goes astray."
- **10:22** — The verb "brings wealth" is feminine singular, agreeing with "blessing," while "does not add" shifts to masculine singular with "the LORD" as subject and the feminine suffix "with it" referring back to the blessing; alternatively, the second clause can be read "and toil adds nothing to it."
