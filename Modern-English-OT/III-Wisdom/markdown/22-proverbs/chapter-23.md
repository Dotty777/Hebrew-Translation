## Text

**23:1** When you sit to eat with a ruler, take careful note of what is before you,

**23:2** and set a knife to your throat if you have a strong appetite.

**23:3** Do not crave his delicacies, for it is bread of deception.

**23:4** Do not toil to become rich; cease from your own understanding.

**23:5** Will you cast your eyes upon it, and it is gone? For it surely makes wings for itself; like an eagle it flies into the sky.

**23:6** Do not eat the bread of one with a grudging eye, and do not crave his delicacies.

**23:7** For just as he reckons within himself, so is he. "Eat and drink," he tells you, yet his heart is not with you.

**23:8** The morsel you ate you will vomit up, and you will ruin your pleasant words.

**23:9** Do not speak in the ears of a fool, for he will despise the insight of your words.

**23:10** Do not shift an ancient boundary, and do not enter the fields of orphans,

**23:11** for their Redeemer is mighty; He Himself will argue their case against you.

**23:12** Bring your heart to discipline, and your ear to sayings of knowledge.

**23:13** Do not withhold discipline from a youth; if you strike him with the rod, he will not die.

**23:14** Strike him with the rod yourself, and you will rescue his soul from Sheol.

**23:15** My son, if your heart grows wise, my own heart will rejoice as well,

**23:16** and my inner being will exult when your lips speak uprightness.

**23:17** Do not let your heart envy sinners; rather, be zealous in the fear of the LORD all day long.

**23:18** For indeed there is a future, and your expectation will not be cut off.

**23:19** You, my son, listen and grow wise, and guide your heart in the way.

**23:20** Do not be among heavy wine drinkers, among those who gorge themselves on meat,

**23:21** for a heavy drinker and a glutton will become impoverished, and drowsiness will dress them in rags.

**23:22** Listen to your father who fathered you, and when your mother has grown old, do not despise her.

**23:24** The father of a righteous one will surely exult, and the one who fathers a wise one will be glad in him.

**23:25** May your father and your mother be glad, and may she who gave birth to you exult.

**23:26** Give your heart to me, my son, and let your eyes watch my ways.

**23:27** For a prostitute is a deep pit, and a foreign woman is a narrow well.

**23:28** Moreover, she lies in ambush like a bandit, and multiplies the treacherous among mankind.

**23:29** Who has woe? Who has misery? Who has quarrels? Who has lamenting? Who has wounds for no reason? Who has bloodshot eyes?

**23:30** Those who stay late over wine, those who go to search out mixed wine.

**23:31** Do not gaze at wine when it turns red, when it gives its gleam in the cup, when it glides smoothly.

**23:32** At the end, like a serpent it bites, and like a viper it stings.

**23:33** Your eyes will see strange sights, and your heart will speak twisted things.

**23:34** And you will be like one lying down in the heart of the sea, and like one lying atop a mast.

**23:35** "They struck me, yet I felt no pain; they pounded me, yet I did not know it. When will I wake up? I will seek it once again."

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**23:23** *(after `23:22`)* Acquire truth and do not sell it—wisdom, discipline, and understanding.

## Translator Notes

- **23:5** — Two ketiv/qere variants occur here: the read qere has Hiphil 'will you cause your eyes to fly' (ketiv Qal) and Qal 'it flies' (ketiv a defective form), creating wordplay on the Hebrew root for flying.
- **23:7** — The rare verb rendered 'reckons' occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and denotes inward calculation or tallying, fitting the stingy host of verses 6-8.
- **23:24** — Three ketiv/qere variants occur here: the read qere uses the infinitive absolute of the root for exulting (ketiv an alternate hollow spelling) and places the conjunction 'and' before 'the one who fathers' rather than before 'will be glad in him.'
- **23:26** — The read qere has 'watch' (from the root for guarding or observing), whereas the written ketiv has 'delight in' (from a different root meaning to take pleasure in).
- **23:29** — The read qere and written ketiv are variant plural forms of the same noun meaning 'quarrels' or 'contentions.'
- **23:31** — The read qere has 'in the cup,' whereas the written ketiv reads 'in the purse' or 'bag'; 'gives its gleam' is literally 'gives its eye,' a Hebrew idiom for surface sparkle.
