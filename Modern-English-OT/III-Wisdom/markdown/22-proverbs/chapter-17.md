## Text

**17:1** Better is a dry piece of bread and tranquility with it than a house filled with feasts of strife.

**17:2** A servant who acts prudently will rule over a son who brings shame, and among brothers he will share an inheritance.

**17:3** A crucible is for silver and a smelting furnace is for gold, and the LORD is the examiner of hearts.

**17:4** An evildoer pays attention to lips of iniquity; a liar lends an ear to a tongue of destruction.

**17:5** One who mocks the poor reproaches his Maker; one who rejoices over disaster will not be held guiltless.

**17:6** Children's children are the crown of the elderly, and the splendor of children is their fathers.

**17:7** Refined speech is not suitable for a senseless fool; how much less is lying speech for a noble.

**17:8** A bribe is a stone of favor in the eyes of its owner; wherever he turns, he succeeds.

**17:9** One who covers over a transgression seeks love, but one who repeats a matter divides close companions.

**17:10** A rebuke sinks deeper into a discerning person than striking a fool a hundred times.

**17:11** An evil person seeks nothing but rebellion, and a cruel messenger will be sent out against him.

**17:12** Let a person encounter a bear deprived of her cubs, and not a fool in his folly.

**17:13** One who repays evil in return for good—evil will not depart from his house.

**17:14** The start of contention is releasing water, so before it erupts, abandon the dispute.

**17:15** One who acquits a wicked person and one who condemns a righteous person—both of them are an abomination to the LORD.

**17:16** Why is there a purchase price in a fool's hand to acquire wisdom, when he has no sense?

**17:17** At all times a friend shows love, and a brother is born for distress.

**17:18** A person lacking sense strikes hands, pledging a guarantee before his neighbor.

**17:19** One who loves transgression loves quarreling; one who raises his doorway high seeks ruin.

**17:20** One who is crooked in heart will not find good, and one who is twisted in his tongue will fall into harm.

**17:21** One who fathers a fool does so to his own grief, and the father of a senseless fool does not rejoice.

**17:22** A glad heart promotes healing, but a stricken spirit dries out the bones.

**17:23** A wicked person accepts a bribe from the bosom to turn aside the paths of justice.

**17:24** Before the face of a discerning person is wisdom, but a fool's eyes are at the ends of the earth.

**17:25** A vexation to his father is a foolish son, and bitterness to her who gave him birth.

**17:26** Indeed, fining the righteous is not good, nor is striking nobles for uprightness.

**17:27** One who holds back his words possesses knowledge; one of a restrained spirit is a person of understanding.

**17:28** Even a fool who remains silent is regarded as wise; when he shuts his lips, he is regarded as discerning.

## Translator Notes

- **17:11** — Hebrew fronts the object 'only rebellion' before the verb 'seeks' and subject 'an evil person' (some alternatively read 'a rebellious person seeks only evil').
- **17:13** — Ketiv/qere variant: the qere reads the Qal imperfect 'tamush' ('will depart'), whereas the ketiv writes the Hiphil 'tamish', used intransitively with essentially the same sense.
- **17:22** — Hebrew 'yeitiv gehah' pairs the Hiphil imperfect of 'yatav' ('make good/improve') with the hapax legomenon 'gehah' ('healing/cure', cognate with the verb 'yigheh' in Hos 5:13).
- **17:27** — Ketiv/qere variant: this translates the qere 'yeqar-ruach' ('restrained/sparing of spirit', from 'yaqar' meaning rare/prized, cf. Prov 25:17), while the ketiv writes 'veqar-ruach' ('and cool of spirit').
