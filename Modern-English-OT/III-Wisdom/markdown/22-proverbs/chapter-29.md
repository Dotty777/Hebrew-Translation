# Proverbs 29 — Saints Modern English (Hebrew / Masoretic Text)

## Text

**29:1** A man repeatedly reproved who stiffens his neck will be shattered in an instant, and there is no healing.

**29:2** When the righteous multiply, the people are glad, but when a wicked person rules, people groan.

**29:3** A man who loves wisdom gladdens his father, but one who associates with prostitutes destroys wealth.

**29:4** A king establishes a land by justice, but a man of exactions tears it down.

**29:5** A man who flatters his neighbor spreads out a net over his footsteps.

**29:6** In the transgression of an evil man is a snare, but a righteous person shouts for joy and is glad.

**29:7** A righteous person knows the case of the poor; a wicked person does not understand knowledge.

**29:8** Men of scoffing inflame a city, but the wise turn back anger.

**29:9** When a wise man contends in court with a foolish man, he rages and laughs, and there is no rest.

**29:10** Men of bloodshed hate a blameless person, but the upright care for his life.

**29:11** A fool lets out all his spirit, but a wise person stills it in reserve.

**29:12** If a ruler listens closely to a word of falsehood, all his attendants are wicked.

**29:13** A poor man and an oppressor meet together; the LORD brings light to the eyes of both of them.

**29:14** A king who judges the poor in truth—his throne will be established forever.

**29:15** A rod and reproof impart wisdom, but a boy left unrestrained puts his mother to shame.

**29:16** When the wicked multiply, transgression multiplies, but the righteous will see their downfall.

**29:17** Discipline your son, and he will grant you rest, and he will give delights to your soul.

**29:18** Without vision, a people goes unrestrained, but the one who observes the law is blessed.

**29:19** By words a servant will not be disciplined, for he understands, yet there is no response.

**29:20** Have you seen a man who rushes in his words? There is greater hope for a fool than for him.

**29:21** One who pampers his servant from youth will see him become an heir in the end.

**29:22** A man of anger provokes strife, and a person of wrath is full of transgression.

**29:23** A person's pride brings him low, but one who is lowly of spirit lays hold of honor.

**29:24** One who shares with a thief hates his own life; he hears the curse, yet does not report it.

**29:25** Dread of people sets a snare, but the one who trusts in the LORD will be lifted to safety.

**29:26** Many seek a ruler's face, yet from the LORD comes a person's justice.

**29:27** An abomination to the righteous is a man of injustice, and an abomination to a wicked person is one of upright way.

## Translator Notes

- **29:4** — Hebrew ish terumot literally means 'a man of contributions,' using terumah (ordinarily a sacred offering) here for royal exactions or bribes.
- **29:10** — Hebrew literally reads 'but the upright seek his life,' using the Piel of baqash ('seek') here in a protective sense of caring for the blameless person's life rather than its usual hostile idiom elsewhere.
- **29:11** — Hebrew be'achor yeshabbechennah literally means 'stills it in the rear,' using the Piel of shabach ('calm, still') for holding one's spirit quietly in reserve.
- **29:21** — Hebrew manon is a hapax legomenon of uncertain meaning, often derived from the root nun ('offspring') to mean 'heir' or 'son,' though others understand it as 'grief' or 'ingrate.'
