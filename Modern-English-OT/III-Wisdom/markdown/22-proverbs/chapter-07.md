## Text

**7:1** My son, guard my sayings, and treasure up my commands with you.

**7:2** Guard my commands and live, and guard my teaching as the pupil of your eyes.

**7:3** Tie them on your fingers; inscribe them on the tablet of your heart.

**7:4** Say to wisdom, "You are my sister," and call understanding a close relative,

**7:5** to guard you from a strange woman, from a foreign woman who makes her sayings smooth.

**7:6** For at the window of my house, through my lattice, I looked down,

**7:7** and I saw among the naive, I noticed among the young men, a youth lacking sense,

**7:8** passing down the street beside her corner, and striding along the way to her house,

**7:9** at twilight, in the evening of the day, in the dead of night and gloom.

**7:10** And look, a woman came to meet him, in the attire of a prostitute and secretive of heart.

**7:11** She is boisterous and defiant; in her house her feet do not settle.

**7:12** Now outside, now in the open squares, and beside every corner she lies in ambush.

**7:13** And she took hold of him and kissed him; she made her face brazen and said to him,

**7:14** "Sacrifices of peace offerings were due from me; today I have fulfilled my vows.

**7:15** Therefore I came out to meet you, to seek your face eagerly; I have found you.

**7:16** I have spread my couch with tapestries, colored cloths of linen from Egypt.

**7:17** I have scented my bed with myrrh, aloes, and cinnamon.

**7:18** Come, let us drink deeply of lovemaking until the morning; let us revel in love.

**7:19** For the man is not in his house; he has gone on a journey far away.

**7:20** He took the bag of silver in his hand; on the day of the full moon he will come to his house."

**7:21** She turned him aside with her abundance of persuasion; with the smoothness of her lips she drove him on.

**7:22** At once he goes after her, like an ox that comes to slaughter, and as in shackles to a fool's discipline,

**7:23** until an arrow splits his liver, like a bird hurrying to a snare, and he does not know that it is at the cost of his life.

**7:24** And now, sons, listen to me, and pay attention to the sayings of my mouth.

**7:25** Do not let your heart swerve to her ways; do not wander along her paths.

**7:26** For many are the mortally wounded she has brought down, and numerous are all those she has killed.

**7:27** Her house is the ways to Sheol, descending to the inner rooms of death.

## Translator Notes

- **7:5** — The phrases "strange woman" (ishah zarah) and "foreign woman" (nokriyyah) are recurring Proverbs idioms for an illicit or outside woman, that is, an adulteress. The verb hecheliqah is Hiphil of chalaq ("make smooth"), denoting flattering or seductive speech.
- **7:9** — The noun ishon, which means "pupil" of the eye in 7:2, is used metaphorically here for the darkest center or "dead" of night, verbally linking the young man's folly back to the father's warning.
- **7:10** — The construct phrase netsurat lev (Qal passive participle of natsar, "guard/keep hidden," plus lev, "heart") literally means "guarded/hidden of heart," describing a woman who conceals her scheming intent.
- **7:16** — Both chatuvot ("colored/striped cloths") and the Egyptian loanword etun ("fine linen/yarn," a hapax legomenon) are rare textile terms; the verb ravadti ("I have spread") is cognate with marvaddim ("tapestries").
- **7:22** — The Masoretic clause ukhe'ekes el-musar evil literally reads "and like a fetter to the discipline of a fool," a compressed expression rendered here without adopting ancient versional emendations.
