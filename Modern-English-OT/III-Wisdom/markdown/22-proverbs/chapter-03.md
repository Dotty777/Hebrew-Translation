## Text

**3:1** My son, do not forget my instruction, and let your heart guard my commandments.

**3:2** For they will add to you length of days, years of life, and peace.

**3:3** Do not let loyal love and faithfulness forsake you; tie them around your neck, write them on the tablet of your heart.

**3:4** Then you will find favor and good insight in the eyes of God and mankind.

**3:5** Trust in the LORD with your whole heart, and do not lean on your understanding.

**3:6** In all your ways know Him, and He will straighten your paths.

**3:7** Do not be wise in your eyes; fear the LORD and turn aside from evil.

**3:8** It will be healing for your navel and refreshment for your bones.

**3:9** Honor the LORD from your wealth and from the firstfruits of all your yield.

**3:10** Then your storehouses will be filled with abundance, and your vats will burst with new wine.

**3:11** My son, do not spurn the LORD's discipline, and do not loathe His reproof.

**3:12** For the LORD reproves the one He loves, just as a father does a son in whom he delights.

**3:13** Blessed is the person who finds wisdom, and the person who obtains discernment.

**3:14** For her gain is better than the gain of silver, and her yield is better than fine gold.

**3:15** She is more precious than corals, and none of the things you desire can equal her.

**3:16** Length of days is in her right hand; in her left hand are wealth and honor.

**3:17** Her ways are ways of delight, and all her pathways are peace.

**3:18** She is a tree of life for those who grasp her, and each one who holds her fast is blessed.

**3:19** The LORD by wisdom founded the earth; He established the heavens by discernment.

**3:20** By His knowledge the deeps were split open, and the clouds drip dew.

**3:21** My son, do not let them depart from your eyes; guard sound wisdom and discretion.

**3:22** And they will be life for your soul and grace for your neck.

**3:23** Then you will walk in security along your way, and your foot will not trip.

**3:24** If you lie down, you will not be frightened; you will lie down, and sweet will be your sleep.

**3:25** Do not fear sudden terror, nor the devastation of the wicked when it comes.

**3:26** For the LORD will be your confidence, and He will keep your foot from capture.

**3:27** Do not withhold good from those entitled to it, when it is in the power of your hand to act.

**3:28** Do not say to your neighbor, "Go and return, and tomorrow I will give," when it is with you.

**3:29** Do not plot evil against your neighbor while he dwells in security beside you.

**3:30** Do not quarrel with a person without cause, if he has dealt you no harm.

**3:31** Do not be envious of a man of violence, and choose none of his ways.

**3:32** For the crooked person is an abomination to the LORD, but His intimate counsel is with the upright.

**3:33** The LORD's curse is in the house of a wicked person, but He blesses the dwelling place of the righteous.

**3:34** Though He Himself scoffs at the scoffers, to the humble He gives grace.

**3:35** Wise people will inherit honor, but fools lift up disgrace.

## Translator Notes

- **3:8** — Hebrew lesharekha follows the Masoretic vocalization of shor ("navel"), denoting the core of the body; the Septuagint reflects a different reading ("your flesh/body").
- **3:12** — Hebrew ukhe'av ("just as a father") follows the Masoretic vocalization, with the Hiphil verb yokhiach ("reproves") carried over from the first clause; the Septuagint reads the consonants as a verb ("causes pain / scourges").
- **3:27** — Follows the Qere yadekha (singular, "your hand") in the idiom "in the power of your hand," whereas the Ketiv reads plural yadeikha ("your hands"). Hebrew mibbe'alav literally means "from its owners."
- **3:34** — Follows the Qere vela'anavim ("and to the humble"), whereas the Ketiv reads vela'aniyyim ("and to the afflicted/poor"). In the first clause, the Hiphil verb yalits ("scoffs") echoes the cognate participle letsim ("scoffers").
- **3:35** — Hebrew merim is a singular Hiphil participle of rum ("lifts up / carries off") used distributively with plural kesilim ("fools"), similar to 3:18; alternatively, qalon ("disgrace") may be taken as subject ("disgrace exalts fools").
