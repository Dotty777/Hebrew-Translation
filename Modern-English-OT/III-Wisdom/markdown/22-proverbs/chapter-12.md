# Proverbs 12 — Saints Modern English (Hebrew / Masoretic Text)

## Text

**12:1** One who loves discipline loves knowledge, but one who hates rebuke is brutish.

**12:2** A good person gains favor from the LORD, but He condemns a man of wicked schemes.

**12:3** A person will not stand firm through wickedness, but the root of the righteous will never be shaken.

**12:4** A worthy wife is her husband's crown, but one who causes shame is like decay in his bones.

**12:5** The plans of the righteous are justice; the counsels of the wicked are deceit.

**12:6** The words of the wicked are an ambush for blood, yet the mouth of the upright rescues them.

**12:7** The wicked are overturned and are gone, yet the household of the righteous stands.

**12:8** According to his insight a man is praised, but one of warped heart will be held in contempt.

**12:9** Better is one held in low esteem who has a servant than one who exalts himself and lacks bread.

**12:10** A righteous person is mindful of the life of his animal, yet the compassion of the wicked is cruel.

**12:11** One who cultivates his land will be filled with bread, but one who pursues empty things is devoid of sense.

**12:12** A wicked person craves the catch of evildoers, while the root of the righteous produces fruit.

**12:13** In the transgression of lips is a snare for an evil person, but a righteous person comes out of distress.

**12:14** From the fruit of a man's mouth he will be filled with good, and the recompense of a person's hands will be repaid to him.

**12:15** A fool's way is right in his eyes, yet one who listens to counsel is wise.

**12:16** A fool's vexation becomes known that very day, yet one who conceals an insult is shrewd.

**12:17** One who speaks out truthfully declares what is right, while a witness of lies declares deceit.

**12:18** There is one who speaks recklessly like stabs of a sword, while the tongue of the wise gives healing.

**12:19** A truthful lip stands firm forever, whereas a tongue of falsehood is only for an instant.

**12:20** Deceit is in the heart of those who contrive evil, while for those who counsel peace there is joy.

**12:21** No calamity will come upon the righteous, yet the wicked are full of trouble.

**12:22** False lips are an abomination to the LORD, yet those who practice faithfulness are His pleasure.

**12:23** A shrewd person conceals knowledge, yet the heart of fools announces folly.

**12:24** The hand of the diligent will govern, yet slackness will be subjected to forced labor.

**12:25** Anxiety in the heart of a man bows it down, while a good word gladdens it.

**12:26** A righteous person guides his companion, but the way of the wicked causes them to stray.

**12:27** A slack person does not roast his game, but diligence is a person's precious wealth.

**12:28** Along the path of righteousness there is life, and on the course of that pathway there is no death.

## Translator Notes

- **12:12** — Hebrew metsod can mean 'hunting net' or 'catch/spoil,' and the Qal imperfect yitten literally means 'gives,' used absolutely here for producing fruit.
- **12:14** — The Qere (read text) has the Hiphil imperfect yashiv ('will be repaid,' literally 'one will render back'), whereas the Ketiv (written text) has the Qal yashuv ('will return').
- **12:26** — Hebrew yater mereehu is ambiguous: it is translated here as the Hiphil of tur ('guides') with the noun merea ('companion'), though it can also be parsed as 'excels/is superior to his neighbor' or 'examines his friend.'
- **12:27** — The rare verb yacharok ('roast') occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and is clarified by Aramaic and later Hebrew cognates; the compressed second clause literally reads 'and a person's wealth, precious, diligence' (or 'precious is a diligent person's wealth').
- **12:28** — The Masoretic text reads al-mawet ('no death'), a rare use of the negative particle al directly before a noun rather than the preposition el ('to'), following the phrase derek netivah ('way of a pathway').
