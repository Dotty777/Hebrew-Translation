## Text

**25:1** These too are proverbs of Solomon that the men of Hezekiah, king of Judah, transcribed.

**25:2** The glory of God is concealing a matter, and the glory of kings is searching out a matter.

**25:3** The heavens for height and the earth for depth, and the heart of kings—there is no searching it out.

**25:4** Remove dross from silver, and a vessel comes out for the refiner.

**25:5** Remove a wicked person from before a king, and his throne will stand firm in righteousness.

**25:6** Do not exalt yourself before a king, and in the place of the great do not stand.

**25:7** For it is better to be told, "Come up here," than to be brought low before a noble whom your eyes have seen.

**25:8** Do not go out hastily to dispute; otherwise, what will you do at its end when your neighbor shames you?

**25:9** Plead your dispute with your neighbor, and do not disclose the secret of another,

**25:10** or one who hears will disgrace you, and your bad reputation will not turn back.

**25:11** Golden apples in silver settings—a word spoken at its proper moment.

**25:12** A gold earring and a jewel of pure gold—a wise reprover to an ear that listens.

**25:13** Like the coolness of snow on a day of harvest is a trustworthy messenger to those who send him, and he restores the soul of his masters.

**25:14** Clouds and wind, and no rain—a man who boasts of a false gift.

**25:15** By patience a ruler is persuaded, and a gentle tongue breaks a bone.

**25:16** If you find honey, eat what is sufficient for you, or you will get your fill of it and vomit it up.

**25:17** Keep your foot scarce in your neighbor's house, or he will get his fill of you and hate you.

**25:18** A war club and a sword and a sharpened arrow—a man who testifies against his neighbor as a false witness.

**25:19** A broken tooth and a faltering foot—reliance on a treacherous person on a day of distress.

**25:20** One who removes a garment on a day of cold, vinegar on alkali—so is one who sings songs to a sorrowful heart.

**25:21** If the one who hates you is hungry, feed him bread, and if he is thirsty, give him a drink of water,

**25:22** for you will pile glowing coals onto his head, and the LORD will repay you.

**25:23** A north wind produces rain, and a secret tongue, an indignant face.

**25:24** It is better to dwell on a corner of a roof than with a quarrelsome wife in a shared house.

**25:25** Cold water upon an exhausted soul—so is a good report from a faraway land.

**25:26** A fouled spring and a ruined fountain—a righteous person wavering before the wicked.

**25:27** Eating honey in excess is not good, and searching out one's own glory is no glory.

**25:28** A breached city without a wall—a man who has no restraint over his spirit.

## Translator Notes

- **25:7** — The Masoretic verse division keeps the final clause, "whom your eyes have seen," at the end of 25:7 modifying "a noble," whereas some ancient versions attach it to the opening of 25:8.
- **25:11** — The noun translated "silver settings" denotes figured or carved ornamental work, and the final word translated "at its proper moment" is a Hebrew hapax legomenon whose exact sense is debated, often associated with fitting turns or circumstances.
- **25:24** — The Qere reads midyanim and the Ketiv reads medonim, a minor morphological variant with the same sense ("quarrels" or "contentions"); the same Ketiv/Qere pair recurs in 26:21.
- **25:27** — The second clause is notoriously terse in the Masoretic Text, literally "and searching out their glory [is] glory"; the negative from "not good" in the first half is widely taken to carry over into the second clause, with the third-person plural suffix used indefinitely ("one's own").
