## Text

**8:1** Does not wisdom call out, and insight give forth her voice?

**8:2** At the top of the heights beside the way, at the junction of the paths she stations herself;

**8:3** beside the gates, at the opening of the town, at the entry of the doorways she cries aloud:

**8:4** "To you, men, I call out, and my voice is to the sons of mankind.

**8:5** Grasp prudence, you naive ones, and you fools, grasp sense.

**8:6** Listen, for I speak noble things, and the opening of my lips is uprightness.

**8:7** For my palate utters truth, and wickedness is a detestable thing to my lips.

**8:8** All the sayings of my mouth are in righteousness; nothing in them is twisted or crooked.

**8:9** All of them are plain to the one who understands, and upright to those who find knowledge.

**8:10** Take my discipline and not silver, and knowledge rather than select gold.

**8:11** For wisdom is better than corals, and all desirable things cannot match her.

**8:12** I, wisdom, inhabit prudence, and I find knowledge of discretion.

**8:13** The fear of the LORD is hating evil; pride and arrogance, an evil way, and a mouth of perversity I hate.

**8:14** Counsel and sound wisdom belong to me; I am understanding; might belongs to me.

**8:15** Through me kings reign, and rulers enact justice;

**8:16** through me princes govern, and nobles, all righteous judges.

**8:17** I myself love those who love me, and those who seek me eagerly will find me.

**8:18** Wealth and honor are with me, lasting riches and righteousness.

**8:19** My fruit is better than gold, even refined gold, and my produce than select silver.

**8:20** On the road of righteousness I walk, in the middle of the paths of justice,

**8:21** to cause those who love me to inherit wealth, and I will fill their storehouses.

**8:22** The LORD acquired me at the beginning of His way, before His deeds from long ago.

**8:23** From eternity I was installed, from the start, from the earliest origins of the earth.

**8:24** When there were no deeps, I was brought to birth, when there were no springs heavy with water.

**8:25** Before the mountains were sunk in place, before the hills, I was brought to birth,

**8:26** while He had not yet made the earth and open fields, or the first of the world's soil.

**8:27** When He set up the heavens, there I was; when He traced a circle across the face of the deep,

**8:28** when He strengthened the skies above, when the fountains of the deep grew strong,

**8:29** when He set the sea its limit, so that the waters would not cross His command, when He traced out the foundations of the earth,

**8:30** then I was beside Him, a master artisan, and I was a delight day by day, rejoicing before Him at every moment,

**8:31** rejoicing in the world of His earth, and my delight was with the sons of mankind.

**8:32** And now, sons, listen to me, and blessed are those who observe my ways.

**8:34** Blessed is the person who listens to me, keeping watch at my doors day by day, guarding the doorposts of my entrances.

**8:35** For the one who finds me has found life and gains favor from the LORD,

**8:36** but the one who misses me does violence to his own life; all who hate me love death."

## Translator Notes

- **8:17** — Ketiv reads "those who love her" (third-person feminine singular suffix), whereas the qere followed here reads "those who love me" (first-person singular suffix), matching Wisdom's first-person address.
- **8:22** — The Qal verb qanah ("acquired") primarily means "acquire" or "possess" throughout Proverbs, though in poetic creation contexts (compare Gen 14:19; Deut 32:6) it can also carry the sense "bring forth" or "create."
- **8:23** — The Niphal perfect nissakti comes from nasak ("install/appoint," as in Ps 2:6, or "pour out/cast"), though some lexicons connect the form to sakak ("weave/form," compare Ps 139:13).
- **8:30** — The noun amon is a famous lexical crux, understood either as "master artisan/craftsman" (compare Akkadian ummanu) or, connecting it with aman ("nurse/raise"), as "ward" or "nursling."
- **8:35** — Ketiv writes the verb with a final yod (either dittography from the preceding "the one who finds me" or a plural participle "finders of"), while the qere followed here is the Qal perfect third-person masculine singular matsa ("has found"), matching the singular verb "gains" in the second clause.
