## Text

**13:1** A wise son listens to a father's discipline, but a scoffer does not heed rebuke.

**13:2** From the fruit of a man's mouth he eats what is good, while the appetite of the treacherous is violence.

**13:3** One who guards his mouth keeps his life, but for the one who opens his lips wide there is ruin.

**13:4** A sluggard's soul craves, yet there is nothing, while the soul of the diligent will be richly nourished.

**13:5** A righteous man hates a word of falsehood, but a wicked man causes a stench and brings disgrace.

**13:6** Righteousness guards the one of blameless way, while wickedness overthrows the sinner.

**13:7** There is one who acts rich yet has nothing at all, and another who acts poor yet has abundant wealth.

**13:8** The ransom for a man's life is his riches, but a poor man does not hear rebuke.

**13:9** The light of the righteous is joyful, while the lamp of the wicked dies out.

**13:10** Through presumption comes only quarreling, but wisdom is with those who take counsel.

**13:11** Wealth from emptiness will dwindle, but one who gathers handful by handful will make it increase.

**13:12** Prolonged expectation sickens the heart, but a desire that arrives is a tree of life.

**13:13** One who despises a word will be bound in debt to it, but the one who fears a commandment will himself be rewarded.

**13:14** The instruction of a wise man is a spring of life, for turning away from the snares of death.

**13:15** Good insight yields favor, but the way of the treacherous is rugged.

**13:16** Every prudent person acts with knowledge, while a fool spreads out folly.

**13:17** A wicked messenger falls into harm, but a faithful emissary brings healing.

**13:18** Poverty and disgrace are for the one who disregards discipline, while the one who heeds reproof will be honored.

**13:19** A longing realized is pleasant to the soul, but an abomination to fools is turning away from evil.

**13:20** One who walks with the wise will grow wise, but one who associates with fools will come to harm.

**13:21** Misfortune pursues sinners, but the righteous will be repaid with good.

**13:22** A good man bequeaths an inheritance to children's children, while the wealth of a sinner is kept in store for the righteous.

**13:23** Much food is in the tilled field of the poor, yet some is swept away for lack of justice.

**13:24** One who holds back his rod hates his son, but the one who loves him diligently seeks discipline for him.

**13:25** A righteous man eats to the satisfaction of his soul, but the stomach of the wicked goes wanting.

## Translator Notes

- **13:6** — Both objects use abstract nouns for concrete persons: the first literally means 'blamelessness of way' (standing for the blameless person), and the second literally means 'sin' (standing for the sinner).
- **13:8** — The Hebrew noun rendered 'rebuke' (the same word used in v. 1) can also carry the contextual sense 'threat,' contrasting the rich person who must ransom his life with the poor person who faces no extortion threat.
- **13:11** — 'From emptiness' translates Hebrew mehebel (lit. 'from vapor/emptiness,' i.e., fleeting or futile schemes), and 'handful by handful' renders the idiom al-yad (lit. 'upon hand,' meaning gradually or by steady labor).
- **13:15** — The Masoretic text reads etan ('perennial, unyielding, rough'), depicting the way of the treacherous as hard and rugged terrain.
- **13:20** — Ketiv/Qere: the Qere reads a participle and imperfect ('One who walks... will grow wise'), whereas the Ketiv reads an infinitive absolute and imperative ('Walk with the wise and become wise').
