## Text

**1:1** Proverbs of Solomon, son of David, king of Israel:

**1:2** for knowing wisdom and discipline, for discerning sayings of insight;

**1:3** for receiving discipline in prudent conduct—righteousness, justice, and equity;

**1:4** for giving shrewdness to the naive, to a young man knowledge and discretion.

**1:5** Let a wise person listen and increase learning, and let a discerning person acquire guidance,

**1:6** to discern a proverb and a figurative saying, words of the wise and their riddles.

**1:7** Fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge; fools scorn wisdom and discipline.

**1:8** Listen, my son, to your father's discipline, and do not abandon your mother's teaching,

**1:9** for they are a wreath of grace for your head and necklaces for your neck.

**1:10** My son, if sinners lure you, do not consent.

**1:11** If they say, "Come with us; let us lie in ambush for blood, let us lurk for an innocent person without cause;

**1:12** let us swallow them alive, like Sheol, and intact, like those descending to the pit.

**1:13** We will find all valuable wealth; we will fill our houses with spoils.

**1:14** Cast your lot in our midst; there will be one purse for all of us"—

**1:15** my son, do not walk along the way with them; restrain your foot from their path,

**1:16** for their feet run toward evil, and they rush to shed blood.

**1:17** For in vain the net is spread before the eyes of every winged creature,

**1:18** yet they lie in ambush for their own blood; they lurk for their own lives.

**1:19** Such are the paths of everyone who grasps dishonest gain; it takes the life of its owner.

**1:20** Wisdom shouts aloud in the street; in the open squares she lifts up her voice.

**1:21** At the head of the bustling streets she calls out; at the entrances of the gates in the city she speaks her sayings:

**1:22** "How long, you naive ones, will you love naivety, and scoffers take pleasure in scoffing for themselves, and fools hate knowledge?

**1:23** Turn back at my reproof! Look, I will pour out my spirit to you; I will make known my words to you.

**1:24** Because I called out and you refused, I extended my hand and no one paid heed,

**1:25** and you disregarded all my counsel and did not consent to my reproof,

**1:26** I too will laugh at your disaster; I will mock when your terror comes,

**1:27** when your terror comes like a tempest and your disaster arrives like a whirlwind, when trouble and anguish come upon you.

**1:28** Then they will call to me, yet I will not answer; they will seek me earnestly, yet they will not find me,

**1:29** because they hated knowledge, and the fear of the LORD they did not choose;

**1:30** they did not consent to my counsel; they treated all my reproof with contempt.

**1:31** So they will eat from the fruit of their way, and they will be filled with their own counsels.

**1:32** For the turning away of the naive kills them, and the carefree ease of fools destroys them.

**1:33** But the one who listens to me will dwell in security, and will be untroubled, free from the terror of harm."

## Translator Notes

- **1:17** — Literally "every owner of a wing" (kol-ba'al kanaf), an idiom for any winged creature; Hebrew chinnam ("in vain") echoes the same word in 1:11 ("without cause"), underscoring the irony that the wicked walk into their own trap.
- **1:20** — Hebrew chokhmot is a feminine plural form used as an abstract intensive for personified Wisdom, governed by singular verbs in 1:20b-21 (titten, tiqra, tomer; taronnah is formally feminine plural or an uncommon 3fs form).
- **1:27** — Qere reads kesho'ah ("like a tempest"); ketiv ksh'wh is an orthographic variant of the same word.
- **1:31** — Hebrew mo'atsotehem ("their own counsels") shares the same root (y-'-ts) as Wisdom's atsati ("my counsel") in 1:25 and 1:30.
