# Proverbs 15 — Saints Modern English (Hebrew / Masoretic Text)

## Text

**15:1** A gentle reply turns back wrath, but a hurtful word brings up anger.

**15:2** The tongue of the wise makes knowledge pleasing, but the mouth of fools pours out folly.

**15:3** In every place are the eyes of the LORD, watching over the evil and the good.

**15:4** A healing tongue is a tree of life, but perversity in it is a breaking of the spirit.

**15:5** A fool spurns his father's discipline, but one who keeps reproof shows prudence.

**15:6** In the house of the righteous is abundant wealth, yet in the revenue of the wicked is trouble.

**15:7** The lips of the wise scatter knowledge, but the heart of fools is not so.

**15:8** The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination to the LORD, yet the prayer of the upright is His pleasure.

**15:9** An abomination to the LORD is the way of the wicked, but the one who pursues righteousness He loves.

**15:10** Harsh discipline is for one who abandons the path; the one who hates reproof will die.

**15:11** Sheol and Abaddon are before the LORD; how much more the hearts of the sons of mankind!

**15:12** A mocker does not love being reproved; to the wise he will not go.

**15:13** A joyful heart brightens the face, but by grief of heart the spirit is stricken.

**15:14** The heart of a discerning person seeks knowledge, yet the mouth of fools grazes on folly.

**15:15** All the days of an afflicted person are bad, yet one with a glad heart has a continual banquet.

**15:16** Better is a little in the fear of the LORD than abundant treasure with turmoil in it.

**15:17** Better is a meal of vegetables where love is than a fattened ox with hatred in it.

**15:18** A wrathful man provokes strife, but one who is slow to anger stills a quarrel.

**15:19** The way of a sluggard is like a hedge of briers, but the path of the upright is a built-up highway.

**15:20** A wise son gladdens a father, yet a foolish person despises his mother.

**15:21** Folly is joy to one lacking sense, but a man of understanding makes his walk straight.

**15:22** Plans are thwarted where there is no counsel, but with an abundance of counselors they stand.

**15:23** There is joy for a man in the reply of his mouth, and a word in its season—how good it is!

**15:24** The path of life goes upward for the insightful, so as to turn aside from Sheol below.

**15:25** The LORD pulls down the house of the proud, yet He sets firm the boundary of a widow.

**15:26** An abomination to the LORD are plans of evil, but pure are pleasant sayings.

**15:27** One who grasps for unjust gain troubles his own house, but one who hates gifts will live. Through loyal love and faithfulness iniquity is atoned for, and through the fear of the LORD a person turns aside from evil.

**15:28** The heart of a righteous person meditates on how to reply, yet the mouth of the wicked pours forth evil things. When the LORD delights in a man's ways, He causes even his enemies to make peace with him.

**15:29** Far is the LORD from the wicked, yet the prayer of the righteous He hears. Better is a little in righteousness than abundant revenues without justice. A man's heart devises his way, yet the LORD establishes his step.

**15:30** The light of the eyes gladdens the heart; a good report nourishes the bones.

**15:32** One who casts off discipline rejects his own soul, but one who listens to reproof acquires sense.

**15:33** The fear of the LORD is the discipline of wisdom, and before honor is humility.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**15:31** *(after `15:30`)* An ear that listens to life-giving correction will lodge among the wise.

**16:1** *(after `15:33`)* The preparations of the heart belong to a person, but the response of the tongue comes from the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **15:11** — Abaddon (Hebrew avaddon, 'destruction') is a poetic synonym for Sheol, the realm of the dead.
- **15:14** — Translated from the Qere ufi ('and the mouth of'); the Ketiv reads ufenei ('and the face of').
- **15:22** — The Hiphil infinitive absolute hafer ('frustrating') functions impersonally ('plans are thwarted'), and the feminine singular verb taqum ('it stands') refers collectively to 'plans' (mahashavot).
- **15:30** — The Piel verb tedashen literally means 'makes fat' (fills with marrow and vitality), an idiom for nourishing or restoring health.
