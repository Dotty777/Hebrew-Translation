## Text

**5:1** My son, listen attentively to my wisdom; turn your ear to my understanding,

**5:2** so that you may guard discretion, and your lips may keep knowledge.

**5:3** For the lips of a strange woman drip flowing honey, and her palate is smoother than oil;

**5:4** yet her end is as bitter as wormwood, as sharp as a double-edged sword.

**5:5** Her feet descend to death; her steps hold fast to Sheol.

**5:6** She does not weigh the path of life; her tracks wander, and she does not know it.

**5:7** And now, sons, listen to me, and do not turn aside from the words of my mouth.

**5:8** Set your way far from her, and do not draw near to the entrance of her house,

**5:9** or else you will give your splendor to others and your years to a cruel one,

**5:10** or else strangers will have their fill of your strength, and your toils will be in the house of a foreigner,

**5:11** and you will groan at your end, when your flesh and your body waste away,

**5:12** and you will say, "How I hated discipline, and my heart scorned correction!

**5:13** I did not heed the voice of my teachers, and to my instructors I did not turn my ear.

**5:14** I was nearly in complete disaster in the middle of the assembly and community."

**5:15** Drink water from your own cistern, and flowing waters from within your own well.

**5:16** Should your springs disperse outside, streams of water in the open squares?

**5:17** May they belong to you alone, and none to strangers with you.

**5:18** May your fountain be blessed, and rejoice over the wife of your youth.

**5:19** A loving doe and a graceful wild goat—may her breasts satisfy you to the full at all times, and in her love may you be intoxicated continually.

**5:20** Why then, my son, should you be intoxicated with a strange woman and embrace the bosom of a foreign woman?

**5:21** For the ways of a man lie directly before the eyes of the LORD, and He weighs all his tracks.

**5:22** His own iniquities will trap the wicked man, and by the cords of his sin he will be held fast.

**5:23** He will die from a lack of discipline, and through the abundance of his folly he will go astray.

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — Hebrew zarah ("a strange woman") denotes a woman outside one's own marriage and household, that is, an adulteress, paired with nokriyyah ("a foreign woman") in v. 20. Literally "her palate" (hikkah) is used by metonymy for seductive speech.
- **5:6** — Both verbs (Piel imperfect tefalles, "weigh/level," and Qal imperfect teda, "know") can be parsed as either 3rd feminine singular ("she") or 2nd masculine singular ("you"). Reading them as 3rd feminine singular treats pen as an emphatic negation continuing the description of the strange woman from vv. 3-5; read as 2nd masculine singular, the clause warns, "so that you do not weigh the path of life, her tracks wander without your knowing."
- **5:16** — The Masoretic Text has no interrogative particle here, but the contrast with v. 17 ("to you alone, and none to strangers") supports reading the verse as an unmarked rhetorical question; alternatively it may be read as a jussive wish, "May your springs disperse outside."
- **5:19** — The Qal verb shagah ("reel, stagger, go astray") creates a deliberate wordplay across vv. 19, 20, and 23, moving from joyful intoxication with one's own wife (v. 19) to illicit infatuation with a strange woman (v. 20) and fatal wandering through folly (v. 23).
