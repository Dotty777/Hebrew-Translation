## Text

**30:1** The words of Agur son of Jakeh, the oracle. The utterance of the man to Ithiel, to Ithiel and Ucal:

**30:2** Surely I am more brutish than any man, and I have no human understanding.

**30:3** And I have not learned wisdom, nor do I know the knowledge of the Holy One.

**30:4** Who has ascended to the heavens and descended? Who has gathered the wind in his cupped hands? Who has bound up the waters in a cloak? Who has set in place all the ends of the earth? What is his name, and what is his son's name, if you know?

**30:5** Every utterance of God is refined; He is a shield for those who seek refuge in Him.

**30:6** Do not add to His words, or else He will rebuke you and you will be proven false.

**30:7** Two things I have asked from You; do not withhold them from me before I die:

**30:8** Put deceit and a lying word far from me; do not give me poverty or wealth; feed me the bread of my allotted portion,

**30:9** or else I might be satisfied and disown You and say, "Who is the LORD?" or else I might become impoverished and steal and violate the name of my God.

**30:10** Do not malign a servant to his master, or else he will curse you and you will incur guilt.

**30:11** There is a generation that curses its father and does not bless its mother.

**30:12** There is a generation that is pure in its own eyes, yet has not been washed from its filth.

**30:13** There is a generation—how lofty are its eyes, and its eyelids are lifted up!

**30:14** There is a generation whose teeth are swords and whose jaw-teeth are knives, to devour the afflicted from the earth and the needy from among humanity.

**30:15** The leech has two daughters: "Give! Give!" There are three things that are never satisfied, four that do not say, "Enough":

**30:16** Sheol, and a closed womb, earth that is not satisfied with water, and fire that does not say, "Enough."

**30:17** An eye that mocks a father and despises obedience to a mother—ravens of the valley will gouge it out, and young eagles will eat it.

**30:18** There are three things too marvelous for me, and four that I do not know:

**30:19** the way of the eagle in the heavens, the way of a serpent over a rock, the way of a ship in the heart of the sea, and the way of a man with a young woman.

**30:20** Such is the way of an adulterous woman: she eats and wipes her mouth, and says, "I have done no iniquity."

**30:21** Beneath three things the earth trembles, and beneath four it cannot endure:

**30:22** beneath a servant when he becomes king, and a fool when he is satisfied with bread;

**30:23** beneath a hated woman when she is married, and a maidservant when she dispossesses her mistress.

**30:24** Four are the small things of the earth, yet they are supremely wise:

**30:25** the ants are a people without strength, yet they prepare their food in summer;

**30:26** rock hyraxes are a people without might, yet they set their house in the cliff;

**30:27** locusts have no king, yet they all march out in divisions;

**30:28** a lizard you can catch with the hands, yet it is in a king's palaces.

**30:29** There are three things splendid in stride, and four that are splendid in walking:

**30:30** a lion, mighty among the beasts, and it does not turn back before anything;

**30:31** a strutting rooster, or a male goat, and a king against whom there is no rising up.

**30:32** If you have acted foolishly in lifting yourself up, or if you have schemed, put your hand to your mouth.

**30:33** For squeezing milk brings out curds, and squeezing the nose brings out blood, and squeezing anger brings out strife.

## Translator Notes

- **30:1** — The Masoretic Text points hammassa as 'the oracle' (though some read it as a place or tribal name, 'of Massa') and vocalizes le'iti'el and ve'ukal as personal names ('to Ithiel, to Ithiel and Ucal'), which many scholars repoint as a cry of weariness ('I am weary, God... and spent').
- **30:3** — Hebrew qedoshim is a plural of majesty for 'the Holy One' (as in 9:10), and the negative particle lo ('not') in the first clause carries over into the second clause.
- **30:18** — Ketiv reads the feminine numeral ve'arba ('and four'); Qere reads the masculine ve'arba'ah to agree with masculine sheloshah ('three') and the masculine suffix on yeda'tim ('I know them').
- **30:28** — The Piel imperfect tetappes is ambiguous in person and gender: it may be parsed as second-person masculine singular ('you can catch with the hands') or as third-person feminine singular with 'lizard' as subject ('it grasps with its hands').
- **30:31** — Hebrew zarzir motnayim literally means 'girded of loins,' an uncertain creature traditionally identified as a strutting rooster or a greyhound; alqum immo literally reads 'no rising up against him' (al plus qum), though some compare Arabic al-qawm ('with his army with him').
