## Text

**21:1** A king's heart is like streams of water in the hand of the LORD; He turns it toward whatever He desires.

**21:2** Every way of a man is straight in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs hearts.

**21:3** Doing righteousness and justice is preferred by the LORD over sacrifice.

**21:4** Lofty eyes and an arrogant heart—the lamp of the wicked—are sin.

**21:6** Gaining treasures by a lying tongue is a driven vapor for those who seek death.

**21:7** The violence of the wicked will drag them off, because they refuse to do justice.

**21:8** Twisted is the way of a guilty man, but as for the pure, his action is straight.

**21:9** It is better to live on a corner of a roof than with a contentious wife in a shared house.

**21:10** The soul of a wicked man craves evil; his neighbor is shown no favor in his eyes.

**21:11** When a scoffer is punished, the simple grows wise, and when insight is given to a wise man, he receives knowledge.

**21:12** The Righteous One examines the house of a wicked man, overturning the wicked to ruin.

**21:13** One who stops up his ear against the outcry of the poor—he too will call out and will not be answered.

**21:14** A gift in secret quells anger, and a bribe in the bosom, fierce wrath.

**21:15** To do justice is a joy to the righteous, yet terror to workers of iniquity.

**21:16** A person who strays from the way of insight will rest in the gathering of the dead.

**21:17** One who loves pleasure will be a man of want; one who loves wine and oil will not grow rich.

**21:18** A ransom for the righteous is a wicked man, and in place of the upright is a treacherous one.

**21:19** It is better to live in a wilderness land than with a contentious and irritable wife.

**21:20** Desirable treasure and oil are in the dwelling of a wise person, yet a foolish person swallows it up.

**21:21** One who pursues righteousness and steadfast love finds life, righteousness, and honor.

**21:22** A wise man scales a city of warriors and pulls down the stronghold of its confidence.

**21:23** One who guards his mouth and his tongue guards his soul from distresses.

**21:24** An insolent, arrogant man—"Scoffer" is his name—acts with the fury of insolence.

**21:25** The craving of a sluggard kills him, because his hands refuse to work.

**21:26** All day long he greedily craves, yet a righteous man gives and does not withhold.

**21:27** The sacrifice of the wicked is an abomination, all the more when he brings it with a wicked purpose.

**21:28** A lying witness will perish, but a man who listens will speak forever.

**21:29** A wicked man hardens his face, but as for the upright, he considers his way.

**21:30** There is no wisdom, there is no understanding, and there is no counsel against the LORD.

**21:31** A horse is prepared for the day of battle, yet to the LORD belongs the victory.

## Translator Notes

- **21:4** — Hebrew nir (defective spelling of nir) can mean either lamp (a variant of ner, as in 1 Kgs 11:36 and 2 Sam 22:29, matching Prov 13:9 and 24:20) or freshly plowed ground (as in Prov 13:23). Both senses fit the Masoretic vocalization, though lamp accords closely with the surrounding imagery in Proverbs.
- **21:6** — The Masoretic Text reads mevaqshei-mavet (Piel participle of baqash, 'those who seek death'), standing in terse apposition to the preceding phrase. Some ancient versions instead reflect moqshei-mavet ('snares of death').
- **21:8** — Hebrew vazar is a hapax legomenon meaning guilty or laden with guilt (cognate with Arabic wizr), forming a direct antonym to zakh ('pure') in the second clause. Some earlier interpreters divided it as ve-zar ('and strange').
- **21:12** — Hebrew tsaddiq ('Righteous One') lacks an article, as poetic epithets for God often do, and is widely understood as referring to God because the same subject overturns the wicked to ruin (compare 22:12). Others understand it as a righteous human observer.
- **21:29** — The translation follows the Masoretic Qere yavin darko ('considers his way', from bin with a singular noun). The Ketiv reads yakhin derakhav ('establishes his ways', Hiphil of kun with a plural noun).
