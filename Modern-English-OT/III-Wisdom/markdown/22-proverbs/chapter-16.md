## Text

**16:2** All a man's ways are pure in his own eyes, yet the LORD weighs spirits.

**16:5** An abomination to the LORD is everyone lofty in heart; rest assured, he will not be held guiltless.

**16:9** The LORD has fashioned everything for its purpose, and even the wicked for the day of calamity.

**16:10** An oracle is on a king's lips; in judgment his mouth does not act unfaithfully.

**16:11** A balance and scales of justice belong to the LORD; all the weights of the bag are His handiwork.

**16:12** An abomination to kings is committing wickedness, for by righteousness a throne is established.

**16:13** Righteous lips are the delight of kings, and they love one who speaks what is upright.

**16:14** A king's wrath is messengers of death, yet a wise man pacifies it.

**16:15** Life is in the light of a king's face, and his favor is like a cloud of spring rain.

**16:16** To acquire wisdom—how much better it is than gold! And to acquire understanding is to be chosen over silver.

**16:17** The highway of the upright is to turn aside from evil; one who watches over his way guards his soul.

**16:18** Before ruin comes pride, and before stumbling comes a lofty spirit.

**16:19** Better to be lowly in spirit with the humble than to divide plunder with the proud.

**16:20** One who considers a matter wisely will find good, and blessed is the one who trusts in the LORD.

**16:21** One who is wise in heart will be called discerning, and sweetness of lips adds persuasiveness.

**16:22** A fountain of life is insight to its possessor, but the discipline of fools is folly.

**16:23** The heart of a wise person gives insight to his mouth, and upon his lips it adds persuasiveness.

**16:24** Pleasant sayings are a honeycomb, sweet to the soul and healing for the bones.

**16:25** There is a way that is right before a man, yet its outcome is the ways of death.

**16:26** A laborer's appetite labors for him, for his mouth presses upon him.

**16:27** A worthless man digs up evil, and what is on his lip is like a scorching fire.

**16:28** A perverse man sends out strife, and a whisperer separates close companions.

**16:29** A violent man lures his neighbor and leads him along a way that is not good.

**16:30** One who closes his eyes to devise perverse things, pursing his lips, brings evil to completion.

**16:31** Gray hair is a crown of splendor; in the way of righteousness it is found.

**16:32** Better is one slow to anger than a mighty warrior, and one who rules over his spirit than one who captures a city.

**16:33** Into the lap the lot is cast, yet from the LORD is its whole decision.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**16:3** *(after `16:2`)* Entrust your works to the LORD, and your plans will stand firm.

## Translator Notes

- **16:5** — Hebrew yad leyad ('hand to hand') is an oath or handshake idiom expressing firm assurance, and the Niphal yinnaqeh means 'be cleared of guilt' or 'go unpunished.'
- **16:9** — The third-person suffix on lamma'anehu can refer either to each created thing ("for its purpose") or to the LORD ("for His own purpose"). Native Masoretic 16:4 maps to Saints.Bible parallel display slot 16:9 (within the reordered sequence MT 15:27–16:9).
- **16:19** — Translated from the Qere anavim ('the humble'); the Ketiv reads aniyyim ('the poor' or 'the afflicted').
- **16:26** — Hebrew nefesh here denotes physical appetite or hunger, and the hapax verb akhaf ('presses upon') conveys the relentless urgency of needing food.
- **16:27** — Translated from the Qere sefato (singular, 'his lip'); the Ketiv reads sefatav (dual, 'his lips').
- **16:3 *(additional)*** — Literally 'Roll your works onto the LORD.'
