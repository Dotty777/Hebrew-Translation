## Text

**24:1** Do not envy men of evil, and do not crave to be with them,

**24:2** for their heart plots violence, and their lips speak trouble.

**24:3** Through wisdom a house is built, and through discernment it is firmly established;

**24:4** and through knowledge rooms are filled with all precious and pleasant wealth.

**24:5** A wise man has strength, and a man of knowledge intensifies power.

**24:6** For by skillful guidance you will wage your war, and victory is in a multitude of counselors.

**24:7** Wisdom is too lofty for a fool; at the gate he does not open his mouth.

**24:8** Whoever plots to do evil, people will call him a master of schemes.

**24:9** The scheming of folly is sin, and a mocker is an abomination to mankind.

**24:10** If you falter in a day of distress, your strength is meager.

**24:11** Rescue those being taken to death, and surely hold back those tottering to slaughter.

**24:12** If you say, "Look, we did not know this," does not the One who weighs hearts discern it? Does not the One who guards your soul know it? And will He not repay a person according to his deed?

**24:13** Eat honey, my son, for it is good, and honeycomb is sweet to your palate.

**24:14** Know that wisdom is just like that for your soul; if you find it, then there is a future, and your expectation will not be cut off.

**24:15** Do not lie in ambush, wicked man, against the dwelling of a righteous person; do not ravage his resting place,

**24:16** for seven times a righteous person falls and rises, but the wicked stumble in disaster.

**24:17** When your enemy falls, do not rejoice, and when he stumbles, do not let your heart exult,

**24:18** otherwise the LORD will see it and it will be evil in His eyes, and He will turn His anger away from him.

**24:19** Do not fret over evildoers; do not envy the wicked,

**24:20** for there will be no future for the evil person; the lamp of the wicked will go out.

**24:21** Fear the LORD, my son, and the king; do not mingle with those prone to change,

**24:22** for suddenly their disaster will arise, and who knows the ruin from the two of them?

**24:23** These also belong to the wise: Showing partiality in judgment is not good.

**24:24** One who says to a wicked person, "You are righteous"—peoples will curse him, nations will denounce him;

**24:25** but it will go well for those who rebuke, and upon them a blessing of good will come.

**24:26** One who gives a straightforward reply kisses the lips.

**24:27** Prepare your work outdoors, and get it ready for yourself in the field; afterward, build your house.

**24:28** Do not be a witness without cause against your neighbor. And would you deceive with your lips?

**24:29** Do not say, "Just as he did to me, so I will do to him; I will repay the man according to his deed."

**24:30** I went past the field of a lazy man, and past the vineyard of a person lacking understanding,

**24:31** and look, all of it had grown up with thistles, its surface was covered with nettles, and its stone wall was torn down.

**24:32** Then I myself gazed at it and set my heart to it; I saw it, and I took instruction:

**24:33** A little sleep, a little dozing, a little folding of the hands to lie down—

**24:34** and your poverty will come prowling, and your needs like an armed warrior.

## Translator Notes

- **24:10** — The Hebrew plays on two words from the same root: 'distress' (tsarah) and 'meager' (tsar, literally 'narrow').
- **24:11** — The Hebrew particle 'im' with the imperfect ('surely hold back') functions here as an emphatic entreaty, though some read it conditionally as an ellipsis ('if you hold back').
- **24:17** — The read qere is singular 'your enemy,' matching the singular suffix in 'when he stumbles,' whereas the written ketiv is plural 'your enemies.'
- **24:21** — The Qal participle rendered 'those prone to change' literally means 'those who change' or 'differ,' referring here to fickle people or political dissenters.
- **24:28** — Following the Masoretic vocalization, the second verb combines the conjunction 'and,' the interrogative prefix, and a Piel perfect ('and would you deceive?') as a rhetorical question.
- **24:34** — Unlike the parallel in 6:11, the Masoretic Text here uses the Hithpael participle 'prowling' without a comparative preposition, along with plural 'your needs'; 'an armed warrior' is literally 'a man of a shield.'
