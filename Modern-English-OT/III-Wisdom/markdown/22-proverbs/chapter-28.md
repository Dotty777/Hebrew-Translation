# Proverbs 28 — Saints Modern English (Hebrew / Masoretic Text)

## Text

**28:1** The wicked flee with no one pursuing, but the righteous are confident like a young lion.

**28:2** By the transgression of a land its rulers are many, yet by a discerning and knowledgeable person, right order lasts long.

**28:3** A poor man who oppresses the needy is a sweeping rain that leaves no bread.

**28:4** Those who abandon the law praise the wicked, but those who keep the law contend against them.

**28:5** Men of evil do not understand justice, yet those who seek the LORD understand everything.

**28:6** A poor person who walks in his integrity is better than one who is crooked in his ways, though he is rich.

**28:7** One who guards the law is a discerning son, but one who associates with gluttons brings shame on his father.

**28:8** One who multiplies his wealth through interest and usury amasses it for the one who shows favor to the needy.

**28:9** One who turns his ear away from hearing the law—even his prayer is a detestable thing.

**28:10** One who misleads the upright onto an evil path—he himself will fall into his own pit, while the blameless will inherit what is good.

**28:11** Wise in his own eyes is a rich man, but a needy person who has discernment searches him out.

**28:12** When the righteous exult, there is great splendor, yet when the wicked rise, people must be searched for.

**28:13** One who covers his transgressions will not succeed, but one who confesses and abandons them will be shown mercy.

**28:14** Blessed is the person who fears continually, but one who hardens his heart will fall into trouble.

**28:15** A roaring lion and a prowling bear—that is a wicked ruler over a needy people.

**28:16** A leader who lacks understanding abounds in acts of oppression, while one who hates dishonest gain will lengthen his days.

**28:17** A person weighed down by the blood of a life will flee to the pit; let no one lend him support.

**28:18** One who walks blamelessly will be saved, yet one who is crooked in his ways will fall at once.

**28:19** One who tills his ground will get his fill of bread, but one who chases empty things will get his fill of poverty.

**28:20** A trustworthy man abounds in blessings, but one who rushes to get rich will not be held guiltless.

**28:21** Showing partiality is not good, yet for a morsel of bread a man will transgress.

**28:22** A stingy man is frantic for wealth, and he does not know that lack will come upon him.

**28:23** One who reproves a person will find greater favor afterward than one who flatters with the tongue.

**28:24** One who robs his father and his mother and says, "There is no transgression," is a partner to a destructive man.

**28:25** One with a greedy appetite stirs up contention, but one who trusts in the LORD will thrive.

**28:26** One who trusts in his heart is a fool, while one who walks in wisdom will be rescued.

**28:27** One who gives to the poor has no lack, yet one who hides his eyes abounds in curses.

**28:28** When the wicked rise, people conceal themselves, yet when they perish, the righteous multiply.

## Translator Notes

- **28:2** — Following the Masoretic disjunctive accent on yodea, ken is taken as right order or stability with the intransitive Hiphil ya'arikh meaning lasts long; alternatively, ken may be adverbial meaning so or thus it endures.
- **28:12** — The Pual imperfect yechuppas from chapas literally means is searched for, implying that people go into hiding when the wicked rise, paralleling yissater in 28:28.
- **28:16** — The ketiv reads the plural construct form sone'e meaning haters of, while the qere reads the singular participle sone meaning one who hates, agreeing with the singular Hiphil verb ya'arikh.
- **28:18** — Hebrew derakhayim is a dual form meaning two ways, conveying duplicity as in 28:6, creating a wordplay with the feminine singular be'achat, which can mean either in one of them or idiomatically at once.
- **28:23** — Hebrew acharay is pointed like after me, but is widely understood as an unusual adverbial form of achar meaning afterward or in the end.
