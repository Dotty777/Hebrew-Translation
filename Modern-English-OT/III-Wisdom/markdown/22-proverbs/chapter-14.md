# Proverbs 14 — Saints Modern English (Hebrew / Masoretic Text)

## Text

**14:1** The wise among women builds her house, but folly demolishes it with her own hands.

**14:2** Whoever walks in his uprightness fears the LORD, but the one crooked in his ways despises Him.

**14:3** In a fool's mouth is a rod of pride, but the lips of the wise will keep them.

**14:4** Without oxen the feeding trough is clean, but abundant harvests come through the strength of an ox.

**14:5** A faithful witness will not lie, but a witness of falsehood breathes out lies.

**14:6** A scoffer searches for wisdom, yet there is none, while knowledge is easy for the discerning.

**14:7** Go away from the presence of a foolish man, for you will not perceive lips of knowledge.

**14:8** A prudent person's wisdom is to understand his way, while the folly of fools is deceit.

**14:9** Guilt mocks fools, but among the upright there is favor.

**14:10** The heart knows the bitterness of its own soul, and an outsider does not share in its joy.

**14:11** The house of the wicked will be wiped out, but the tent of the upright will blossom.

**14:12** There is a way that is right before a man, yet its outcome is the ways of death.

**14:13** Even amid laughter a heart can ache, and the outcome of joy is grief.

**14:14** One who turns back in heart will be filled from his ways, and a good man will be filled from himself.

**14:15** A naive person believes every word, but a prudent person pays close attention to his step.

**14:16** A wise man fears and turns away from evil, but a fool is hotheaded and overconfident.

**14:17** A short-tempered person commits folly, and a man of schemes will be hated.

**14:18** The naive inherit folly, while the prudent crown themselves with knowledge.

**14:19** Evil people bow low before good people, and the wicked at the gates of a righteous man.

**14:20** Even by his neighbor a poor man is hated, but those who love a rich man are many.

**14:21** One who despises his neighbor sins, but blessed is the one who shows favor to the humble.

**14:22** Do not those who plan evil wander astray? Yet loyal love and truth belong to those who plan good.

**14:23** In all toil there will be gain, but talk of the lips leads only to poverty.

**14:24** The crown of wise people is their wealth; the folly of fools is folly.

**14:25** A truthful witness rescues lives, while one who breathes out lies is deceit.

**14:26** In the fear of the LORD there is firm confidence, and for his children there will be a refuge.

**14:27** The fear of the LORD is a spring of life, for turning away from the snares of death.

**14:28** In an abundance of people is a king's splendor, but in the absence of a populace is a ruler's ruin.

**14:29** One who is slow to anger is abundant in understanding, but one who is short of spirit lifts up folly.

**14:30** A calm heart is life to the body, but envy is decay to the bones.

**14:31** One who oppresses the poor reproaches his Maker, but one who shows favor to the needy honors Him.

**14:32** A wicked man is overthrown by his evil, while in his death a righteous man has a refuge.

**14:33** In the heart of a discerning person wisdom rests, but in the midst of fools it is made known.

**14:34** Righteousness lifts a nation high, but sin is a disgrace to peoples.

**14:35** A king's favor is for an insightful servant, while his wrath will be against one who brings shame.

## Translator Notes

- **14:9** — Because the Hiphil verb 'mocks' is singular while 'fools' is plural, singular 'guilt' (which can also mean 'guilt offering') is taken here as the subject; alternatively, taking 'fools' distributively, 'Fools mock at guilt (or the guilt offering).'
- **14:14** — Hebrew literally reads 'and from upon him' (i.e., from himself or from his own ways), with the verb 'will be filled' implied from the first clause.
- **14:21** — Ketiv/Qere: the Qere reads 'the humble' (or 'the afflicted'), whereas the Ketiv reads 'the poor' (or 'the needy').
- **14:32** — The Masoretic Text reads choseh bemoto (participle "taking/finding refuge" with temporal "in his death," i.e., having a refuge even at death); the Septuagint reads 'in his own piety.' 'By his evil' (bera'ato) in the first clause can likewise mean 'in his calamity.'
- **14:34** — Hebrew chesed here is a rare homonym meaning 'disgrace' or 'reproach' (cf. Lev 20:17), distinct from chesed ('loyal love') in v. 22.
