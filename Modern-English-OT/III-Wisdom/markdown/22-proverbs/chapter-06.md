## Text

**6:1** My son, if you have become a guarantor for your neighbor, if you have struck your palms for a stranger,

**6:2** you have been ensnared by the words of your mouth; you have been trapped by the words of your mouth.

**6:3** Do this then, my son, and free yourself, for you have come into your neighbor's palm: go, humble yourself, and press your neighbor hard.

**6:4** Do not grant sleep to your eyes, nor slumber to your eyelids.

**6:5** Free yourself like a gazelle from the hand, and like a bird from the hand of a trapper.

**6:6** Go to the ant, sluggard; look at her ways and become wise.

**6:7** Though she has no commander, overseer, or ruler,

**6:8** she makes ready her bread in the summer and collects her food at the harvest.

**6:9** How long, sluggard, will you lie down? When will you get up out of your sleep?

**6:10** A bit of sleep, a bit of slumber, a bit of clasping the hands to lie down,

**6:11** and your poverty will come like a wanderer, and your scarcity like a shielded warrior.

**6:12** A worthless person, a man of iniquity, walks about with a crooked mouth;

**6:13** he winks with his eyes, shuffles with his feet, and points with his fingers.

**6:14** Perversities are in his heart; he devises evil at all times; he unleashes quarrels.

**6:15** Therefore his disaster will come suddenly; in an instant he will be shattered, with no healing.

**6:16** These are six things the LORD hates, and seven are an abomination to His soul:

**6:17** lofty eyes, a tongue of falsehood, and hands that spill innocent blood,

**6:18** a heart devising plans of iniquity, feet hastening to run to evil,

**6:19** a witness of falsehood who breathes out lies, and one who unleashes quarrels among brothers.

**6:20** Keep, my son, your father's command, and do not abandon your mother's teaching.

**6:21** Tie them to your heart continually; bind them around your neck.

**6:22** When you walk about, it will guide you; when you lie down, it will keep watch over you; and when you wake up, it will converse with you.

**6:23** For a command is a lamp and teaching is a light, and the corrections of discipline are the way of life,

**6:24** to guard you from an evil woman, from the smoothness of a foreign woman's tongue.

**6:25** Do not covet her beauty in your heart, and do not let her seize you with her eyelids.

**6:26** For the cost of a prostitute amounts to a loaf of bread, while a man's wife hunts a precious life.

**6:27** Can a man gather fire into his bosom and his garments not be burned?

**6:28** Or can a man walk over glowing coals and his feet not be seared?

**6:29** Just so is the one who goes in to the wife of his neighbor; no one who touches her will escape punishment.

**6:30** People do not look down on a thief if he steals to fill his appetite when he is hungry;

**6:31** yet if he is caught, he must repay sevenfold; all the wealth of his house he must give.

**6:32** One who commits adultery with a woman is lacking in sense; one who destroys his own life does it.

**6:33** Blows and dishonor he will find, and his shame will not be erased.

**6:34** For jealousy is a man's fury, and he will show no compassion on the day of vengeance.

**6:35** He will have no regard for any ransom, and he will not consent even if you multiply bribes.

## Translator Notes

- **6:5** — The Masoretic Text literally reads miyyad ("from the hand") without specifying "of the hunter," leaving the parallel term yaqush ("a trapper") in the second clause to supply the implied agent.
- **6:14** — Ketiv reads medanim (as in v. 19) while Qere reads midyanim; both are plural nouns meaning "quarrels/strife," and the translation follows the Qere.
- **6:16** — Ketiv reads the plural to'evot ("abominations"), whereas Qere reads the singular construct to'avat ("an abomination"); the translation follows the singular Qere.
- **6:22** — The verbs tanheh (Hiphil imperfect), tishmor (Qal imperfect), and teshiheka (Qal imperfect), together with the explicit pronoun hi, shift from the plural "them" in v. 21 to feminine singular ("it"), referring to the mother's torah ("teaching") or to parental instruction as a single whole.
- **6:26** — The opening clause is elliptical (literally "for a prostitute woman, up to a loaf of bread"), contrasting the small fee of a prostitute with the fatal cost of adultery with a married woman. An older grammatical reading supplies a passive verb: "on account of a prostitute, one is reduced to a loaf of bread."
