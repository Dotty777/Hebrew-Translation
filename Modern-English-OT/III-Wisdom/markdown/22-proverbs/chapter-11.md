# Proverbs 11 — Saints Modern English (Hebrew / Masoretic Text)

## Text

**11:1** Deceitful scales are an abomination to the LORD, but a full weight is His pleasure.

**11:2** When arrogance comes, disgrace also comes, but wisdom is with the modest.

**11:3** The integrity of the upright leads them, but the perversity of the treacherous ruins them.

**11:5** The righteousness of a blameless person makes his way straight, but by his wickedness a wicked person falls.

**11:6** The righteousness of the upright rescues them, yet the treacherous are trapped by their own craving.

**11:7** At the death of a wicked person, hope perishes, and the expectation of strength perishes as well.

**11:8** A righteous person is freed from distress, and a wicked person comes in his place.

**11:9** By his mouth a godless person ruins his neighbor, but by knowledge the righteous are freed.

**11:10** At the well-being of the righteous a city exults, and when the wicked perish there is a jubilant shout.

**11:11** Through the blessing of the upright a city rises high, but by the mouth of the wicked it is demolished.

**11:12** One who scorns his neighbor is devoid of sense, while a man of discernment stays silent.

**11:13** One who goes about with gossip uncovers a secret, but one faithful in spirit keeps a matter concealed.

**11:14** Without guidance a people falls, but deliverance is in a multitude of counselors.

**11:15** One who acts as guarantor for a stranger will surely come to harm, but one who hates hand-pledges is safe.

**11:16** A gracious woman keeps hold of honor, and ruthless men keep hold of wealth.

**11:17** A man of kindness benefits his own soul, but a cruel man brings trouble on his own flesh.

**11:18** A wicked person earns a false wage, while one who sows righteousness has a genuine reward.

**11:19** Steadfast righteousness leads to life, but one who pursues evil goes to his death.

**11:20** The twisted in heart are an abomination to the LORD, but those blameless in their way are His pleasure.

**11:21** Rest assured, an evil person will not be held guiltless, but the descendants of the righteous will escape.

**11:22** Like a ring of gold in a pig's snout, so is a beautiful woman who turns aside from good sense.

**11:23** The longing of the righteous is solely good; the hope of the wicked is wrath.

**11:24** There is one who scatters and still has more added, and one who holds back more than is right, only to come to want.

**11:25** A soul of blessing will be richly supplied, and one who waters others will also be watered himself.

**11:26** Whoever keeps back grain, the people curse him, but blessing rests on the head of one who sells it.

**11:27** One who eagerly seeks good looks for favor, but as for one who searches after evil, it will come to him.

**11:28** One who trusts in his wealth will himself fall, but like a leaf the righteous will flourish.

**11:29** One who brings trouble on his household will inherit wind, and a fool is a servant to the wise in heart.

**11:30** The fruit of a righteous person is a tree of life, and one who wins souls is wise.

**11:31** If indeed a righteous person is recompensed on the earth, how much more a wicked person and a sinner!

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**11:4** *(after `11:3`)* Wealth brings no benefit on the day of wrath, but righteousness rescues from death.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — The Qere (read text) has the Qal imperfect yeshoddem ('ruins them,' from shadad), matching the imperfect verb in the first clause; the Ketiv (written text) has an initial waw instead of yod, likely an orthographic variant.
- **11:7** — Hebrew onim is the plural of on ('strength' or 'wealth'); some interpreters instead relate it to awen ('iniquity').
- **11:15** — Hebrew ra-yeroa pairs a cognate noun with a Niphal verb from the root meaning 'to shatter/come to harm' for emphasis; 'hand-pledges' renders toqim, literally 'those who strike [hands],' referring to sealing a surety agreement.
- **11:21** — 'Rest assured' translates the Hebrew idiom yad leyad, literally 'hand to hand,' expressing a solemn pledge or certainty.
- **11:25** — The rare verb form yore is taken as a passive (Hophal) or by-form meaning 'will be watered/refreshed,' answering the Hiphil participle marweh ('one who waters') in the same clause.
