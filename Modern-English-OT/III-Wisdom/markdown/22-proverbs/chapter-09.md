## Text

**9:1** Wisdom has built her house; she has carved out her seven pillars.

**9:2** She has slaughtered her animals, she has blended her wine; indeed, she has arranged her table.

**9:3** She has sent out her servant girls; she calls out from the summits of the heights of the city:

**9:4** "Anyone who is naive, let him turn aside here!" As for the one lacking sense, she says to him,

**9:5** "Come, dine on my bread, and drink from the wine I have blended.

**9:6** Abandon naivety and live, and walk straight in the way of understanding.

**9:7** One who corrects a mocker receives dishonor for himself, and one who reproves a wicked person gets himself a blemish.

**9:8** Do not reprove a mocker, or he will hate you; reprove a wise person, and he will love you.

**9:9** Give teaching to a wise person, and he will become wiser still; instruct a righteous person, and he will add to his learning.

**9:10** The start of wisdom is the fear of the LORD, and knowing the Holy One is understanding.

**9:11** For through me your days will multiply, and to you years of life will be added.

**9:12** If you are wise, it is for yourself that you are wise; and if you mock, you will bear it by yourself."

**9:13** The woman of folly is noisy; she is naive and does not know a thing.

**9:14** She sits at the entrance of her house, on a seat at the heights of the city,

**9:15** to call out to those passing along the way, who keep their paths straight:

**9:16** "Anyone who is naive, let him turn aside here!" And as for the one lacking sense, she says to him,

**9:17** "Stolen water tastes sweet, and secret bread is delightful."

**9:18** Yet he does not know that the departed are there, that in the depths of Sheol are her invited guests.

## Translator Notes

- **9:1** — Hebrew hokmot is plural in form construed with a singular verb, an abstract plural of fullness personifying Wisdom.
- **9:6** — Hebrew petayim is the plural of peti ("naive"), functioning here either as an abstract plural ("naivety") or concretely ("leave the company of the naive").
- **9:10** — Hebrew qedoshim ("holy ones") is plural in form, functioning here as a plural of majesty for "the Holy One" in parallel with "the LORD."
- **9:18** — Hebrew rephaim refers to the departed spirits or shades of the dead dwelling in Sheol; "her invited guests" literally renders the passive participle of qara ("those she called"), echoing her call in 9:15.
