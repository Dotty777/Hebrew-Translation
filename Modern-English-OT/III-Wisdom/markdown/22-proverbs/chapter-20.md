## Text

**20:1** Wine is a scoffer, strong drink is boisterous, and anyone who goes astray by it will not become wise.

**20:2** Like the growling of a young lion is the terror of a king; one who provokes him to fury forfeits his life.

**20:3** Refraining from strife is an honor for a person, but every fool breaks out in quarreling.

**20:4** In autumn a sluggard does not plow, so he begs at harvest, and there is nothing.

**20:5** Counsel in a person's heart is deep water, yet a person of understanding will draw it up.

**20:6** Many people proclaim, each one, his loyal love, yet who can find a faithful person?

**20:7** A righteous person walks in his integrity; blessed are his children after him.

**20:8** A king seated on a throne of judgment winnows every evil with his eyes.

**20:9** Who can say, "I have purified my heart; I am clean of my sin"? The lamp of one who curses his father and his mother will go out in deep darkness. When an inheritance is gained hastily at first, its outcome will not be blessed. Do not say, "I will pay back evil"; wait for the LORD, and He will rescue you.

**20:10** Unequal weights and unequal measures, both of them are an abomination to the LORD.

**20:11** Even by his deeds a child makes himself known, whether his work is pure and whether it is upright.

**20:12** An ear that hears and an eye that sees, the LORD made both of them.

**20:13** Do not love sleep, or you will be impoverished; open your eyes, and you will have your fill of bread.

**20:23** An abomination to the LORD are unequal weights, and deceitful scales are not good.

**20:24** From the LORD are a man's steps; so how can a person understand his way?

**20:25** It is a snare for a person to blurt out, "It is sacred," and to reflect after vows.

**20:26** A wise king winnows the wicked and brings a wheel back over them.

**20:27** The lamp of the LORD is a person's spirit, searching all the chambers of the inner being.

**20:28** Loyal love and faithfulness guard a king, and by loyal love he upholds his throne.

**20:29** Young men's glory is their strength, and old men's splendor is gray hair.

**20:30** Wounding bruises are a scouring away of evil, and blows scour the chambers of the inner being.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**20:14** *(after `20:13`)* "No good, no good," the buyer says, but once he walks off, then he boasts.

**20:15** *(after `20:13`)* There is gold and an abundance of rubies, yet lips of knowledge are a precious ornament.

**20:16** *(after `20:13`)* Take his garment, since he has put up security for a stranger, and hold him in pledge on behalf of a foreign woman.

**20:17** *(after `20:13`)* Sweet to a man is bread of deceit, but afterward his mouth will fill with gravel.

**20:18** *(after `20:13`)* Plans stand firm through counsel; wage war with skillful guidance.

**20:19** *(after `20:13`)* One who goes about as a gossip exposes a secret; do not associate with anyone who opens his lips wide.

## Translator Notes

- **20:4** — Ketiv reads the asyndetic Qal imperfect "yish'al" ("he begs/seeks"); the translated qere reads the waw-consecutive Qal perfect "wesha'al" ("so he begs/seeks").
- **20:9** — The sentences after the first are Masoretic 20:20-22, carried in this verse following the Saints (Greek) verse order. Translated qere readings: be'eshun ("in deep darkness"; ketiv be'ishon, "in the pupil/middle of darkness") and the Pual participle mevohelet ("gained hastily"; ketiv mevuchelet, "gained greedily" or "loathed").
- **20:25** — The rare verb "yala'" denotes speaking rashly or blurting out a dedicatory declaration ("It is sacred"); an older interpretation connected it to a root meaning "to swallow/devour" something holy.
- **20:30** — Ketiv reads "tamriq" (a Hiphil verbal form "scours away" or a variant noun); the translated qere reads the noun "tamruq" ("a scouring / cleansing").
- **20:16 *(additional)*** — Following the traditional reading 'a foreign woman'; the written consonantal text reads 'foreigners.'
