## Text

**22:1** A reputation is to be chosen over abundant wealth; better than silver and gold is favor.

**22:2** Rich and poor meet each other; the Maker of them all is the LORD.

**22:3** A shrewd person sees calamity and hides himself, while the simple continue on and are punished.

**22:4** The reward of humility—the fear of the LORD—is wealth and honor and life.

**22:5** Thorns and traps are on the way of a perverse man; one who guards his soul will stay far away from them.

**22:7** A rich man rules over the poor, and a borrower is a slave to the man who lends.

**22:8** One who sows injustice will reap trouble, and the rod of his fury will come to an end.

**22:9** One with a generous eye will himself be blessed, because he gives from his bread to the poor.

**22:10** Drive out a scoffer, and contention will leave, and dispute and disgrace will cease.

**22:11** One who loves purity of heart, whose lips are gracious—a king is his friend.

**22:12** The eyes of the LORD guard knowledge, but He overturns the words of a treacherous man.

**22:13** A sluggard says, "There is a lion outside! In the middle of the public squares I will be murdered!"

**22:14** A deep pit is the mouth of forbidden women; one denounced by the LORD will fall into it.

**22:15** Foolishness is fastened in the heart of a youth; a rod of discipline will put it far away from him.

**22:16** One who oppresses the poor to gain more for himself, and one who gives to a rich man, will come only to want.

**22:17** Turn your ear and listen to the words of the wise, and set your heart on my knowledge.

**22:18** For it is pleasant when you guard them within you; they will be ready together on your lips.

**22:19** So that your confidence may be in the LORD, I have informed you today—even you.

**22:20** Have I not written for you noble things with counsels and knowledge,

**22:21** to make known to you the reliability of sayings of truth, so that you may bring back true sayings to those who sent you?

**22:22** Do not rob a poor man because he is poor, and do not crush an afflicted man in the gate.

**22:23** For the LORD will argue their case, and He will despoil of life those who despoil them.

**22:24** Do not befriend a man prone to anger, and do not go with a furious man,

**22:25** or else you will learn his paths and take a snare for your soul.

**22:26** Do not be among those who strike hands, among those who guarantee debts.

**22:27** If you have nothing to pay with, why should he take your bed from beneath you?

**22:28** Do not displace an ancient boundary that your fathers established.

**22:29** Have you seen a man proficient in his work? Before kings he will take his stand; he will not take his stand before obscure men.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**22:6** *(after `22:5`)* Train a child according to his way; even when he grows old, he will not turn aside from it.

## Translator Notes

- **22:11** — The translation follows the Masoretic Qere tohar-lev ('purity of heart', construct noun). The Ketiv reads tahor ('pure', adjective).
- **22:16** — The Hebrew is terse and asyndetic, juxtaposing two participles ('one who oppresses... one who gives...') before the single predicate 'only to want.' Both exploiting the poor for personal gain and currying favor with the rich end in poverty.
- **22:20** — The translation follows the vocalized Masoretic Qere shalishim ('noble things', plural of shalish, officer or noble, comparable to Prov 8:6). The Ketiv reads shilshom ('formerly' or 'previously'), while many scholars revocalize the Qere as sheloshim ('thirty' sayings).
- **22:21** — Hebrew qosht ('reliability' or 'truth') is a rare noun cognate with Aramaic qeshot ('truth'). The final participle sholekhekha ('those who sent you') is masculine plural in the Masoretic Text.
- **22:23** — The rare verb qava ('despoil' or 'rob', elsewhere only in Mal 3:8-9) is repeated in a double-accusative construction ('despoil those who despoil them of life') to highlight poetic retribution.
- **22:6 *(additional)*** — Literally 'according to the mouth of his way,' meaning either the path suited to him or the way he ought to go.
