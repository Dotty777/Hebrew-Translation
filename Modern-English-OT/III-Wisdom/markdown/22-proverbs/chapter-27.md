## Text

**27:1** Do not boast in the day of tomorrow, because you do not know what a day will give birth to.

**27:2** Let a stranger praise you, and not your mouth; a foreigner, and not your lips.

**27:3** Heavy is a stone and weighty is sand, yet the vexation of a fool is heavier than both of them.

**27:4** Fury is cruel, and anger is a torrent, yet who can stand in the face of jealousy?

**27:5** Open reproof is better than concealed love.

**27:6** Trustworthy are the wounds of one who loves, but profuse are the kisses of one who hates.

**27:7** A satisfied soul tramples on honeycomb, yet to a hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet.

**27:8** Like a bird wandering from its nest, so is a man wandering from his place.

**27:9** Oil and incense gladden a heart, and the sweetness of one's friend comes from heartfelt counsel.

**27:10** Do not abandon your friend and your father's friend, and do not enter your brother's house on the day of your disaster; a nearby neighbor is better than a distant brother.

**27:11** Be wise, my son, and gladden my heart, so that I may return an answer to the one who taunts me.

**27:12** A shrewd person sees trouble and hides himself, while the naive pass on and suffer punishment.

**27:13** Take his garment, because he has stood as security for a stranger; and on behalf of a foreign woman, take a pledge from him.

**27:14** One who blesses his friend in a loud voice early in the morning—it will be counted as a curse to him.

**27:15** A persistent dripping on a day of pouring rain and a quarrelsome wife are alike.

**27:16** Whoever holds her back holds back the wind, and his right hand encounters oil.

**27:17** Iron is sharpened by iron, and a man sharpens the face of his friend.

**27:18** One who tends a fig tree will eat its fruit, and one who watches over his master will be honored.

**27:19** As in water face corresponds to face, so does a person's heart to a person.

**27:20** Sheol and Abaddon are not satisfied, and the eyes of a person are not satisfied.

**27:21** A crucible is for silver and a furnace is for gold, and a man is measured by his praise.

**27:22** Even if you pound the fool in a mortar among crushed grain with a pestle, his folly will not leave him.

**27:23** Know thoroughly the condition of your flock; set your heart on the herds.

**27:24** For wealth is not forever, and does a crown last from generation to generation?

**27:25** The grass vanishes, fresh shoots appear, and the vegetation of the mountains is gathered.

**27:26** Lambs are for your clothing, and male goats are the price of a field.

**27:27** And there is enough goat milk for your food, for your household's food, and sustenance for your female servants.

## Translator Notes

- **27:6** — The Niphal participle natarot from atar means profuse or excessive, contrasting with the trustworthy wounds of one who loves, though some understand it by contrast as deceitful.
- **27:9** — The Hebrew me'atsat-nefesh is literally from counsel of the soul, denoting heartfelt or sincere counsel; some alternatively take the preposition comparatively as sweeter than one's own counsel.
- **27:15** — The verb nishtawah is a rare Nithpael hybrid passive-reflexive form of shawah meaning be alike or matched. The ketiv reads medonim and the qere reads midyanim, a variant noun pattern of the same word for quarrels or contentions.
- **27:16** — The final clause is difficult; yiqra is taken here from qara II meaning meet or encounter, picturing a hand trying to grasp slippery oil, though some take it from qara I meaning oil announces itself on his right hand.
- **27:24** — The ketiv reads dor without the conjunction, while the qere reads wador, yielding the standard idiom from generation to generation.
