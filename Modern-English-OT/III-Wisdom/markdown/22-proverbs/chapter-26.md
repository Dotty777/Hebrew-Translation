## Text

**26:1** Like snow during summer and like rain during harvest, so honor is not suitable for a fool.

**26:2** Like a bird in wandering, like a swallow in flying, so a curse without cause comes to him.

**26:3** A whip for a horse, a bit for a donkey, and a rod for the back of fools.

**26:4** Do not reply to a fool in keeping with his folly, otherwise you too will become like him.

**26:5** Reply to a fool in keeping with his folly, otherwise he will become wise in his own eyes.

**26:6** Cutting off his own feet, drinking violence—so is one who sends messages by a fool's hand.

**26:7** Legs hang limp from a lame person—and so too a proverb in the mouth of fools.

**26:8** Like binding a stone into a sling, so is the one who gives a fool honor.

**26:9** A thorn goes up into a drunkard's hand—and so too a proverb in the mouth of fools.

**26:10** An archer piercing everyone—so is one who hires a fool, and one who hires passersby.

**26:11** As a dog turns back to its vomit, a fool repeats himself in his folly.

**26:12** Have you seen a man wise in his own eyes? A fool has more hope than he does.

**26:13** A sluggard says, "A lion is on the road, a lion among the public squares!"

**26:14** The door turns on its hinge, and a sluggard on his bed.

**26:15** A sluggard buries his hand in the bowl; he grows too weary to return it to his mouth.

**26:16** In his own eyes a sluggard is wiser than seven who answer with discernment.

**26:17** One who seizes the ears of a dog—so is a passerby who gets worked up over a dispute not his own.

**26:18** Like a crazed person who shoots firebrands, arrows, and death,

**26:19** so is a man who has deceived his neighbor and says, "Am I not joking?"

**26:20** Where wood is lacking, a fire dies out, and where there is no gossiper, strife subsides.

**26:21** Charcoal for glowing coals and wood for fire—so is a quarrelsome man for kindling a dispute.

**26:22** The words of a gossiper are like savory morsels, and they descend into the inner chambers of the belly.

**26:23** Silver dross plated over earthenware—ardent lips and an evil heart.

**26:24** With his lips the one who hates disguises himself, while within himself he stores up deceit.

**26:25** When he makes his voice gracious, do not trust in him, for seven abominations are in his heart.

**26:26** Though hatred conceals itself in deception, his evil will be uncovered in the assembly.

**26:27** One who digs a pit falls into it, and one who rolls a stone—it turns back upon him.

**26:28** A false tongue hates those crushed by it, and a smooth mouth produces downfall.

## Translator Notes

- **26:2** — Translates the Qere lo ("to him"), traditionally understood to mean that an undeserved curse returns upon the one who utters it; the Ketiv reads lo' ("not"), meaning a causeless curse does not arrive or alight.
- **26:7** — The opening verb dalyu is a rare and anomalous form generally derived from a root meaning to hang limp or dangle uselessly.
- **26:8** — The noun margemah ("sling") occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible; while most lexicons understand the phrase as binding a stone into a sling, some interpret it as placing a pebble on a stone heap.
- **26:10** — One of the most lexically difficult verses in Proverbs; rendering the opening words as "an archer piercing everyone" preserves the Masoretic consonants and matches the surrounding proverbs about fools, though the same words can also be construed as "a master who produces everything."
- **26:22** — The Hithpael participle translated "savory morsels" occurs only here and in Proverbs 18:8; it is generally explained from a cognate root meaning to swallow greedily, though older interpretations sometimes rendered it as "wounds."
