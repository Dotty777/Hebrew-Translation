## Text

**1:1** The Song of Songs, which belongs to Solomon.

**1:2** May he kiss me with kisses of his mouth, for your caresses are better than wine.

**1:3** In fragrance your oils are pleasing; your name is oil poured out; therefore young women love you.

**1:4** Draw me; let us run after you! The king has brought me into his chambers. Let us exult and be glad in you; let us praise your caresses more than wine. Rightly they love you.

**1:5** I am dark and lovely, daughters of Jerusalem, like the tents of Kedar, like the curtains of Solomon.

**1:6** Do not stare at me because I am darkened, because the sun has scorched me. The sons of my mother grew angry with me; they set me to guard the vineyards, yet my own vineyard I have not guarded.

**1:7** Tell me, you whom my soul loves, where you shepherd your flock, where you let it lie down at midday; for why should I be like one who veils herself beside the flocks of your companions?

**1:8** If you do not know for yourself, most beautiful of women, go out in the tracks of the flock and tend your young goats beside the dwellings of the shepherds.

**1:9** I have likened you, my companion, to a mare among the chariots of Pharaoh.

**1:10** Your cheeks are lovely with circlets, your neck with strings of beads.

**1:11** We will make for you circlets of gold, with studs of silver.

**1:12** While the king was at his table, my nard gave its fragrance.

**1:13** A sachet of myrrh is my beloved to me; between my breasts he spends the night.

**1:14** A cluster of henna is my beloved to me, in the vineyards of En-gedi.

**1:15** Look, you are beautiful, my companion; look, you are beautiful! Your eyes are doves.

**1:16** Look, you are beautiful, my beloved, indeed pleasant! Indeed, our couch is lush.

**1:17** The beams of our houses are cedar; our paneling is cypress.

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — Hebrew wordplay links shemen ("oil") and shemekha ("your name"). The verb turaq ("poured out") is a 3fs Hophal form from riq, treating the normally masculine noun shemen as feminine here.
- **1:4** — The plural noun mesharim literally means "uprightness" or "straightness"; it is taken adverbially here ("rightly"), though some read it as an abstract subject ("the upright").
- **1:7** — The Masoretic Text reads ke'otyah, the feminine active participle of atah ("wrap/veil oneself"), rendered "like one who veils herself"; several ancient versions reflect a transposed root meaning "wandering."
- **1:17** — The Ketiv reads rchytnw (with chet), while the translated Qere reads rahitenu ("our paneling" or "our rafters," a hapax legomenon). Berotim is an Aramaicized spelling of Hebrew beroshim ("cypresses").
