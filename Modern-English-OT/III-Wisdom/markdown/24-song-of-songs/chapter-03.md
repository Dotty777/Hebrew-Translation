## Text

**3:1** On my bed during the nights, I sought the one whom my soul loves; I sought him, yet I did not find him.

**3:2** "Let me rise now and go around in the city, in the streets and in the squares; let me seek the one whom my soul loves." I sought him, yet I did not find him.

**3:3** The watchmen who go around in the city found me. "Have you seen the one whom my soul loves?"

**3:4** Barely had I passed beyond them when I found the one whom my soul loves. I took hold of him and would not release him until I brought him to the house of my mother, and into the chamber of the one who conceived me.

**3:5** I put you under oath, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles or by the does of the field: do not rouse or stir up love until it desires.

**3:6** Who is this ascending from the wilderness like columns of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with all the powders of a merchant?

**3:7** Look, it is Solomon's litter! Sixty warriors are around it, from the warriors of Israel.

**3:8** All of them hold swords, trained in warfare, each man with his sword at his thigh against terror during the nights.

**3:9** King Solomon made a palanquin for himself from the trees of Lebanon.

**3:10** He made its pillars of silver, its backrest of gold, its seat of purple; its interior is inlaid with love by the daughters of Jerusalem.

**3:11** Go out and look, daughters of Zion, at King Solomon, at the crown with which his mother crowned him on his wedding day, and on the day of his heart's joy.

## Translator Notes

- **3:6** — The question mi zot ("Who is this") uses the feminine demonstrative zot, anticipating either the bride or the feminine noun mittah ("litter," v. 7). Avqat ("powder") is a hapax legomenon related to avaq ("dust").
- **3:8** — Achuzei cherev is literally a Qal passive participle construct ("grasped of sword"), a Hebrew idiom for men armed with or holding swords.
- **3:9** — Appiryon ("palanquin") is a hapax legomenon denoting an ornate portable royal carriage or covered litter, likely a foreign loanword.
- **3:10** — Refidato ("its backrest") is a hapax legomenon from rafad ("spread out/support"). The striking phrase ratsuf ahavah ("inlaid with love") is translated directly from the Masoretic Text.
