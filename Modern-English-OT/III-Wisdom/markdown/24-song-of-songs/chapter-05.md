## Text

**5:1** I have come into my garden, my sister, my bride; I have gathered my myrrh with my spice; I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey; I have drunk my wine with my milk. Eat, companions; drink, and be drunk, lovers!

**5:2** I was sleeping, yet my heart was awake. The sound of my beloved knocking: "Open to me, my sister, my companion, my dove, my flawless one! For my head is filled with dew, my locks with the drops of night."

**5:3** I have taken off my tunic; how can I put it on? I have washed my feet; how can I soil them?

**5:4** My beloved stretched out his hand through the opening, and my inward parts stirred for him.

**5:5** I myself rose to open for my beloved, and my hands dripped myrrh, and my fingers flowing myrrh, onto the handles of the lock.

**5:6** I myself opened for my beloved, yet my beloved had slipped away and passed on. My soul went out when he spoke. I sought him, yet I did not find him; I called him, yet he did not answer me.

**5:7** The guards who go around in the city found me; they struck me, they wounded me; they took my wrap off me, the guards of the walls.

**5:8** I put you under oath, daughters of Jerusalem: if you find my beloved, what will you tell him? That I am sick with love.

**5:9** What is your beloved above any other beloved, most beautiful among women? What is your beloved above any other beloved, that you have made us swear like this?

**5:10** My beloved is gleaming and ruddy, standing out above ten thousand.

**5:11** His head is choice, fine gold; his locks are in waves, black as the raven.

**5:12** His eyes are like doves by streams of water, bathing in milk, perched by a brimming pool.

**5:13** His cheeks are like a bed of spice, towers of fragrant herbs; his lips are lilies, dripping with flowing myrrh.

**5:14** His hands are cylinders of gold, inlaid with beryl; his belly is smooth ivory, covered with sapphires.

**5:15** His legs are pillars of marble, founded on sockets of fine gold. His appearance is like Lebanon, select as the cedars.

**5:16** His mouth is all sweetness, and all of him is delight. This is my beloved, and this is my companion, daughters of Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — The plural dodim, rendered lovers, may be read either as a vocative parallel to re'im, companions, or adverbially as be drunk with love or caresses.
- **5:4** — Hebrew me'ay, my inward parts, idiomatically denotes the seat of deep emotion; alav, for him, follows the Masoretic Text rather than the variant within me reflected in the Septuagint.
- **5:6** — Hebrew nafshi yats'ah, my soul went out, is an idiom for fainting or despair; bedabbero, when he spoke, is the Piel infinitive construct of dabar, speak, with a third masculine singular suffix.
- **5:12** — The noun mille't, rendered brimming pool, is a hapax legomenon from male', be full, understood either as a full water basin parallel to streams of water or as a gemstone setting as in Exodus 28:17.
- **5:13** — The Masoretic vocalization reads migdelot, towers or raised mounds, construct plural of migdal, whereas the Septuagint reflects a Piel participle megaddelot, yielding or growing.
