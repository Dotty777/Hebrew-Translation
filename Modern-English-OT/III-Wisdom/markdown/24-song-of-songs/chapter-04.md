## Text

**4:1** Look, you are beautiful, my companion; look, you are beautiful! Your eyes are doves behind your veil. Your hair is like a flock of goats gliding down from Mount Gilead.

**4:2** Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes coming up from the washing, all of them bearing twins, with not a bereaved one among them.

**4:3** Like a thread of scarlet are your lips, and your speech is lovely. Like a slice of pomegranate is your cheek behind your veil.

**4:4** Like the tower of David is your neck, built in tiers; a thousand shields hang on it, all the bucklers of the warriors.

**4:5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, a gazelle's twins, grazing among the lilies.

**4:6** Until the day breathes and the shadows take flight, I will make my way to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense.

**4:7** You are wholly beautiful, my companion, and no blemish is in you.

**4:8** With me from Lebanon, my bride, come with me from Lebanon! Descend from the summit of Amana, from the summit of Senir and Hermon, from dens of lions, from mountains of leopards.

**4:9** You have taken my heart, my sister, my bride; you have taken my heart with one of your eyes, with one pendant of your necklace.

**4:10** How beautiful are your caresses, my sister, my bride! How much better are your caresses than wine, and the scent of your oils than all spices!

**4:11** Your lips drip honey from the comb, my bride; honey and milk are beneath your tongue, and the scent of your garments is like the scent of Lebanon.

**4:12** A locked garden is my sister, my bride, a locked spring, a sealed fountain.

**4:13** Your shoots are a pomegranate orchard with precious fruits, henna with spikenard,

**4:14** spikenard and saffron, calamus and cinnamon, with all frankincense trees, myrrh and aloes, with all the foremost spices.

**4:15** A fountain of gardens, a well of living waters, and running streams from Lebanon.

**4:16** Awake, north wind, and come, south wind! Blow across my garden; let its spices flow forth. May my beloved come into his garden and eat its precious fruits.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The verb galash, rendered gliding down, occurs only here and in 6:5 and describes the undulating movement of a flock descending a slope; tsammah, rendered veil, is also sometimes understood as locks or braids.
- **4:3** — The noun midbar, rendered speech, is a hapax legomenon from dabar, to speak, and can denote either speech or the mouth as the organ of speech; raqqah, rendered cheek, properly denotes the temple or side of the face.
- **4:4** — The word talpiyyot, rendered tiers, is a hapax legomenon of uncertain meaning, variously understood as courses of stone, weapons or an armory, or battlements.
- **4:9** — The ketiv reads masculine b'hd while the qere reads feminine be'achat, one, to agree with feminine ayin, eye, which is followed here; with one of your eyes idiomatically suggests a single glance. The verb libbavtini, you have taken my heart, is a denominative Piel from levav, heart.
- **4:12** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads gal, spring or fountain, parallel to ma'yan, rather than repeating gan, garden, as in the Septuagint and Vulgate.
