## Text

**8:1** If only you were like a brother to me, one who nursed at the breasts of my mother! If I found you outside, I would kiss you, and yet no one would scorn me.

**8:2** I would lead you, I would bring you into the house of my mother, she who used to teach me. I would give you spiced wine to drink, from the fresh juice of my pomegranate.

**8:3** His left arm is beneath my head, and his right arm embraces me.

**8:4** I put you under oath, daughters of Jerusalem: do not rouse and do not awaken love until it desires.

**8:5** Who is this coming up out of the wilderness, leaning against her beloved? Beneath the apple tree I awakened you; there your mother was in labor with you, there she who gave birth to you was in labor.

**8:6** Place me like a seal on your heart, like a seal on your arm; for love is as mighty as death, jealousy is as relentless as Sheol. Its bolts are bolts of fire, a flame of the LORD.

**8:7** Many waters cannot extinguish love, and rivers will not wash it away. If a man should give all the wealth of his house for love, he would be utterly scorned.

**8:8** We have a young sister, and she has no breasts. What will we do for our sister on the day she is spoken for?

**8:9** If she is a wall, we will build a turret of silver upon her; and if she is a door, we will barricade her with a board of cedar.

**8:10** I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers; then in his eyes I became like one finding peace.

**8:11** A vineyard belonged to Solomon at Baal-hamon; he gave the vineyard over to keepers; each man was to bring a thousand silver pieces for its fruit.

**8:12** My vineyard, my own, is before me; the thousand is yours, Solomon, and two hundred are for those who keep its fruit.

**8:13** You who dwell in the gardens, companions are listening attentively to your voice; let me hear it!

**8:14** Flee, my beloved, and be like a gazelle or a young stag upon mountains of spices.

## Translator Notes

- **8:2** — The Piel imperfect form "telammedeni" is morphologically ambiguous and may be read as third-person feminine singular ("she who used to teach me," referring to the mother) or second-person masculine singular ("you would teach me," referring to the beloved).
- **8:4** — Unlike the parallel adjurations in 2:7 and 3:5, which use the oath particle "im," the Masoretic Text here uses the interrogative "mah" ("why") rhetorically as an emphatic prohibition ("do not"), pairing the Hiphil and Polel stems of the verb "ur" ("rouse" and "awaken").
- **8:6** — The compound word "shalhevetyah" is written as a single word in the Masoretic Text and literally means "a flame of the LORD" (using the shortened divine name Yah), which may also function as a Hebrew superlative idiom meaning "a blazing flame." "Bolts" translates "reshafim," referring to fiery flashes, bolts, or sparks.
- **8:7** — The final Hebrew clause literally reads "scorning, they would scorn him" (an emphatic Qal infinitive absolute plus finite verb with an impersonal plural subject); the masculine pronoun "lo" ("him") may also refer to "it," namely the wealth offered.
