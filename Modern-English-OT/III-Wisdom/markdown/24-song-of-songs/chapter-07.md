## Text

**7:1** Turn back, turn back, Shulammite; turn back, turn back, so that we may gaze on you! Why do you gaze on the Shulammite as on the dance of the two camps?

**7:2** How lovely are your feet in sandals, daughter of a noble! The curves of your thighs are like ornaments, the work of the hands of an artisan.

**7:3** Your navel is a rounded basin; may it not lack blended wine. Your belly is a mound of wheat, hedged in with lilies.

**7:4** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a female gazelle.

**7:5** Your neck is like a tower of ivory; your eyes are pools in Heshbon beside the gate of Bath-rabbim. Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon, looking out toward Damascus.

**7:6** Your head upon you is like Carmel, and the flowing hair of your head is like purple; a king is bound in the tresses.

**7:7** How lovely and how pleasant you are, love, in delights!

**7:8** This stature of yours resembles a palm tree, and your breasts are like clusters.

**7:9** I said, "I will climb a palm tree; I will grasp its fruit stalks." Now may your breasts be like clusters of the vine, and the aroma of your breath like apples.

**7:10** And your palate is like the finest wine, flowing smoothly for my beloved, gliding over the lips of those who sleep.

**7:11** I belong to my beloved, and his longing is toward me.

**7:12** Come, my beloved, let us go out to the field; let us stay the night in the villages.

**7:13** Let us rise early to go to the vineyards; let us see if the vine has budded, if the blossom has opened, if the pomegranates have bloomed. There I will give my love to you.

**7:14** The mandrakes give off fragrance, and at our doorways are all kinds of choice fruits, new as well as old; my beloved, I have stored them up for you.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The Hebrew phrase "mah-techezu" can mean either "Why do you gaze..." or "What do you see...". "The two camps" translates the dual noun with the definite article, which may also refer to the place name Mahanaim.
- **7:6** — "Tresses" translates a Hebrew plural noun ("rehatim") that means "watering troughs" or "channels" elsewhere (Gen 30:38; Exod 2:16), used here poetically for flowing curls of hair.
- **7:10** — The Hebrew abruptly shifts from second-person "your palate" to first-person "for my beloved," suggesting a quick change of speaker. The rare participle "dovev" ("gliding over") can also mean "gently moving," and the Masoretic Text reads "those who sleep" ("yeshenim") rather than "teeth" as in some ancient versions.
- **7:12** — The plural Hebrew noun "kefarim" may mean either "villages" (from "kafar") or "henna blossoms" (from "kofer," as in 4:13).
