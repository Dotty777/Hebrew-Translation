## Text

**19:1** For the music director. A psalm of David.

**19:2** May the LORD answer you on the day of distress; may the name of the God of Jacob keep you safe on high.

**19:3** May He send your help from the sanctuary, and from Zion may He sustain you.

**19:4** May He remember all your grain offerings, and regard your burnt offering as rich. Selah.

**19:5** May He give to you according to your heart, and all your counsel may He fulfill.

**19:6** Let us shout with joy over your deliverance, and in the name of our God let us raise banners. May the LORD fulfill all your requests.

**19:7** Now I know that the LORD has saved His anointed; He will answer him from His holy heavens with the mighty saving acts of His right hand.

**19:8** Some make mention of chariots, and some of horses, but we make mention of the name of the LORD our God.

**19:9** They have collapsed and fallen, but we have risen and stood firm.

**19:10** LORD, save! May the King answer us on the day of our calling.

## Translator Notes

- **19:4** — The Piel imperfect yedashsheneh (from dashen, "be fat/rich," with the emphatic ending -eh) literally means "regard as fat/rich," an idiom for accepting a burnt offering with favor, or possibly "reduce to ashes."
- **19:6** — The Qal imperfect nidgol (from dagal, denominative of degel, "banner/standard") means "raise banners" as a sign of allegiance and victory in God's name.
- **19:8** — The Hiphil imperfect nazkir (from zakar, "remember") with the preposition be- means "make mention of," "invoke," or "boast in," and is understood by ellipsis across the first two clauses.
- **19:10** — Following the Masoretic accentuation, which places the main pause after hoshi'ah ("save!"), "the King" serves as the subject of "answer us," referring to the LORD. Some ancient versions instead take "the king" as the direct object of "save."
