## Text

**41:1** For the music director: a Maskil of the sons of Korah.

**41:2** Just as a deer yearns for channels of water, so my soul yearns for You, God.

**41:3** My soul is thirsty for God, for the living God. When will I come and appear before the face of God?

**41:4** My tears have been bread for me day and night, while it is said to me all day long, "Where is your God?"

**41:5** I remember these things, and I pour out my soul within me: how I would pass along in the throng, walking in procession with them to the house of God, with the sound of jubilant shouting and thanksgiving, a crowd celebrating a festival.

**41:6** Why are you bowed down, my soul, and groaning within me? Wait in hope for God, for I will yet give thanks to Him for the deliverance of His presence.

**41:7** My God, my soul is bowed down within me; therefore I remember You from the land of Jordan and the Hermon summits, from Mount Mizar.

**41:8** Deep cries out to deep at the sound of Your cascades; all Your surges and Your waves have passed over me.

**41:9** In the daytime the LORD will command His loyal love, and in the night His song is with me, a prayer to the God of my life.

**41:10** I will say to God, my rock, "Why have You forgotten me? Why must I go in gloom because of the oppression of an enemy?"

**41:11** With a crushing in my bones, my foes reproach me, as they say to me all day long, "Where is your God?"

**41:12** Why are you bowed down, my soul, and why are you groaning within me? Wait in hope for God, for I will yet give thanks to Him, the deliverance of my face and my God.

## Translator Notes

- **41:2** — Hebrew ayyal (deer) is grammatically masculine singular while the verb ta'arog (yearns) is feminine singular, likely treating the noun as epicene or referring to a doe; afiqei-mayim literally denotes channels or watercourses of water.
- **41:3** — The Masoretic vocalization points we'era'eh as a Niphal (appear), taking penei elohim adverbially as before the face of God, whereas the unvocalized consonants could also be read as Qal (see the face of God).
- **41:5** — Hebrew bassakh (in the throng) is a biblical hapax legomenon; eddaddem is an unusual Hithpael imperfect of dadah (walk slowly/in procession) with an attached third-person plural suffix, meaning either walking in procession with them or leading them in procession.
- **41:6** — The Masoretic text literally reads yeshu'ot panav (the salvations/deliverances of His face, i.e., His presence) and places elohai (My God) at the beginning of 41:7, contrasting with the refrains at 41:12 and 42:5 which read yeshu'ot panai welohai (the deliverance of my face and my God).
- **41:8** — Hebrew tsinnorekha is the plural of the rare noun tsinnor (water-channel, cataract, or cascade), occurring elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible only in 2 Samuel 5:8.
