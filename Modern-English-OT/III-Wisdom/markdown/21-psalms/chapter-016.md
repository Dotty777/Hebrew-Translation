## Text

**16:1** A prayer of David. Hear a righteous plea, LORD; pay attention to my outcry; lend an ear to my prayer, not from lips of deceit.

**16:2** From Your presence let my verdict go forth; let Your eyes gaze upon what is upright.

**16:3** You have examined my heart; You have inspected me at night; You have refined me and find nothing; I have resolved that my mouth will not transgress.

**16:4** Concerning the deeds of mankind, by the word of Your lips I have guarded against the paths of the violent.

**16:5** My steps have kept firm hold of Your tracks; my feet have not slipped.

**16:6** I have called to You, for You will answer me, God; turn Your ear to me; hear my utterance.

**16:7** Show marvelously Your acts of loyal love, You who by Your right hand save those who take refuge from adversaries.

**16:8** Guard me like the pupil of the eye; in the shadow of Your wings conceal me,

**16:9** from the wicked who ravage me; my mortal enemies close in around me.

**16:10** They have closed up their fat; with their mouth they speak in arrogance.

**16:11** At our steps they have now surrounded us; they fix their eyes to bring us down to the ground.

**16:12** He resembles a lion longing to tear apart, and a young lion crouching in secret places.

**16:13** Rise up, LORD; confront him face to face, bring him to his knees; deliver my life from the wicked by Your sword,

**16:14** from men by Your hand, LORD, from men of this world, whose portion is in life, and with what You have stored up You fill their belly; they are satisfied with children, and they leave their surplus to their little ones.

**16:15** As for me, in righteousness I will gaze upon Your face; upon awaking, I will be satisfied with Your likeness.

## Translator Notes

- **16:4** — Hebrew shamarti ("I have kept/guarded") is used here in the sense of "guard against" or "keep away from."
- **16:10** — Hebrew "their fat" (chelbamo) is a poetic idiom for a callous, unfeeling heart closed against compassion.
- **16:11** — Qere reads "they have surrounded us" (sebabunu), matching "our steps"; ketiv reads "they have surrounded me" (sebabuni).
- **16:13** — Hebrew literally reads "Your sword" (and "Your hand" in 16:14) without a preposition, understood here instrumentally ("by Your sword," "by Your hand"), though it may also be taken in apposition to the wicked.
- **16:14** — Qere reads a Qal passive participle (tzefunekha, "what You have stored up"); ketiv has an alternate spelling of the noun (tzefinekha).
