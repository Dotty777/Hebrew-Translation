## Text

**62:1** A psalm of David, while he was in the wilderness of Judah.

**62:2** God, You are my God; I diligently seek You. For You my soul thirsts, for You my flesh pines, in a parched and weary land without water.

**62:3** So in the sanctuary I have gazed upon You, to see Your strength and Your glory.

**62:4** Because Your loyal love is better than life, my lips will extol You.

**62:5** So I will bless You throughout my life; in Your name I will raise my hands.

**62:6** As with fat and richness my soul will be satisfied, and with lips of ringing joy my mouth will give praise.

**62:7** Whenever I remember You on my bed, during the night watches I meditate on You.

**62:8** For You have been a help to me, and in the shadow of Your wings I will shout for joy.

**62:9** My soul clings close behind You; Your right hand holds me fast.

**62:10** But those who seek my life for ruin will go into the lowest regions of the earth.

**62:11** They will be surrendered to the power of the sword; they will become a share for jackals.

**62:12** Yet the king will be glad in God; everyone who swears by Him will exult, for the mouth of those who speak falsehood will be shut.

## Translator Notes

- **62:2** — The verb translated "pines" (kamah) is a hapax legomenon in Biblical Hebrew, expressing fainting or intense longing. The masculine singular adjective ayeph ("weary") stands in grammatical tension with the feminine construct phrase erets-tsiyyah ("parched land"), either treating "land" as masculine or describing the speaker's own exhaustion.
- **62:10** — The prepositional phrase leshoah ("for ruin") can describe either the enemies' destructive aim in seeking the psalmist's life or, ironically, the ruin that will fall upon themselves.
- **62:11** — The verb yaggiruhu ("they will be surrendered") is literally an impersonal third-person plural Hiphil of nagar ("pour out" or "hand over") with a collective singular object suffix ("him"), paired with the idiom al-yedey-cherev (literally "upon the hands of the sword").
