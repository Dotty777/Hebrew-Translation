## Text

**59:1** For the music director, according to Lily of Testimony. A miktam of David, for teaching,

**59:2** when he fought with Aram-naharaim and with Aram-zobah, and Joab returned and struck down Edom in the Valley of Salt, twelve thousand.

**59:3** God, You have cast us off; You have broken through us. You have been angry; restore us!

**59:4** You have caused the land to quake, You have cracked it open; heal its fractures, for it staggers.

**59:5** You have shown Your people hardship; You have made us drink wine of reeling.

**59:6** You have given those who fear You a banner to be lifted high on account of truth. Selah.

**59:7** So that Your beloved ones may be rescued, save with Your right hand and answer me!

**59:8** God spoke in His holiness: "I will exult; I will apportion Shechem and measure the Valley of Succoth.

**59:9** Mine is Gilead, and Mine is Manasseh; and Ephraim is the stronghold of My head; Judah is My ruler's staff.

**59:10** Moab is My washing basin; onto Edom I will cast My sandal; over Me, Philistia, shout in triumph!"

**59:11** Who will bring me to a fortified city? Who has guided me as far as Edom?

**59:12** Have You not cast us off, God? And You do not go out, God, with our armies.

**59:13** Give us help against the foe, for worthless is the deliverance of man.

**59:14** Through God we will act with valor, and He Himself will trample our foes.

## Translator Notes

- **59:4** — The verb translated 'cracked it open' (Hebrew patsam, Qal) occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and denotes splitting or cleaving apart. 'Heal' renders refah, a Qal imperative of rafa spelled with a final he instead of aleph.
- **59:6** — The Masoretic text pairs nes ('banner') with the Hithpolel infinitive lehithnoses ('to be lifted high / raised as a banner') and reads the rare noun qoshet ('truth'). Some ancient versions presuppose a different Hebrew word meaning 'bow' and derive the verb from 'flee.'
- **59:7** — The translation follows the read text (qere, 'and answer me'). The written text (ketiv) reads 'and answer us.'
- **59:10** — The Masoretic text addresses Philistia directly with a second-person feminine singular Hithpolel imperative ('over Me, Philistia, shout in triumph!'), likely ironic in tone, whereas a parallel psalm elsewhere in the Psalter has a first-person singular verb.
