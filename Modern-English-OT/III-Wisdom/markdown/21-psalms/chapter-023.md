## Text

**23:1** A psalm of David. To the LORD belong the earth and its fullness, the world and those who inhabit it.

**23:2** For He Himself founded it upon the seas and set it firm upon the rivers.

**23:3** Who can go up onto the LORD's mountain, and who can stand in the place of His holiness?

**23:4** The one with clean hands and a pure heart, who has not lifted up My soul to falsehood and has not sworn with deceit.

**23:5** He will obtain blessing from the LORD, and righteousness from the God of his deliverance.

**23:6** This is the generation of those seeking Him, those searching for Your face—Jacob. Selah.

**23:7** Raise your heads, gates, and be raised up, ancient doorways, so that the King of glory may enter.

**23:8** Who is this, the King of glory? The LORD, powerful and mighty, the LORD, mighty in war.

**23:9** Raise your heads, gates, and raise them up, ancient doorways, so that the King of glory may enter.

**23:10** Who is He, this King of glory? The LORD of armies—He is the King of glory. Selah.

## Translator Notes

- **23:4** — The Hebrew text reads nafshi ("My soul"), shifting momentarily to God's own voice in the oath idiom of lifting up God's life or name to falsehood (cf. Exod 20:7). The traditional Masoretic Qere reads nafsho ("his soul").
- **23:6** — The form dorashav combines the consonantal Ketiv (singular) with the Qere vowels (plural, "those seeking Him"). The Masoretic text reads simply "Jacob" in apposition to the seeking generation, without "God of," which the Septuagint and Syriac supply.
- **23:9** — Unlike verse 7, which uses the Niphal imperative of nasa ("be raised up"), verse 9 uses the Qal imperative (se'u, "raise [them] up").
