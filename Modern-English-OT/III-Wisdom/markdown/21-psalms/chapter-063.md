## Text

**63:1** For the music director. A psalm of David.

**63:2** God, hear my voice in my complaint; guard my life from terror of the enemy.

**63:3** Hide me from the secret council of evildoers, from the uproar of those who practice iniquity,

**63:4** who have sharpened their tongue like a sword; they have aimed their arrow, a bitter word,

**63:5** to shoot from hiding places at a blameless person; suddenly they shoot him, and they do not fear.

**63:6** They strengthen themselves in an evil plan; they speak of hiding snares; they say, "Who will see them?"

**63:7** They search out injustices: "We have completed a carefully searched-out plot." And the inward part of a man and the heart are deep.

**63:8** But God will shoot them with an arrow; suddenly their wounds will come.

**63:9** And they will be made to stumble, with their own tongue against them; all who look upon them will shake their heads.

**63:10** Then all humanity will fear, and they will declare the deed of God and ponder His work.

**63:11** The righteous one will be glad in the LORD and find refuge in Him, and all the upright of heart will exult.

## Translator Notes

- **63:4** — The verb darekhu ("aimed," from darakh, literally "tread" or "bend") is normally used for bending a bow with the foot and is extended here to aiming an arrow, which stands in direct apposition to "a bitter word."
- **63:5** — Hebrew links the verbs yarah ("shoot") and yare ("fear") in a deliberate soundplay running from "they shoot him, and they do not fear" here to God shooting them in v. 8 and all humanity fearing in v. 10.
- **63:7** — The Masoretic Text reads tamnu, first-person plural Qal perfect of tamam ("we have completed"), introducing the plotters' boast without an explicit speech verb, alongside a threefold play on the root chaphas ("search out," "plot," and Pual participle "searched-out").
- **63:9** — The form vayyakhshiluhu is a third-person plural Hiphil of kashal with a collective singular suffix (literally "they will cause him to stumble," functioning passively), followed by the nominal clause "against them is their own tongue." The Hithpolel yitnodedu ("will shake their heads") can alternatively mean "will flee away."
