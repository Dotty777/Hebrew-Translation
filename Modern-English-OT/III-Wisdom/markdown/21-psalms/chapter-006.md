## Text

**6:1** For the music director, on stringed instruments, according to the Sheminith. A psalm of David.

**6:2** LORD, do not reprove me in Your anger, and do not discipline me in Your fury.

**6:3** Show me favor, LORD, for I am feeble; heal me, LORD, for my bones are terrified.

**6:4** And my soul is deeply terrified; yet You, LORD, how long?

**6:5** Turn back, LORD, rescue my soul; save me for the sake of Your loyal love.

**6:6** For there is no remembrance of You in death; in Sheol, who gives thanks to You?

**6:7** I am exhausted from my groaning; each night I cause my bed to swim; with my tears I melt my couch.

**6:8** From grief my eye has wasted away; it has grown old on account of all my adversaries.

**6:9** Turn away from me, all who practice iniquity, because the LORD heard the sound of my weeping.

**6:10** The LORD heard my plea for favor; the LORD will accept my prayer.

**6:11** All my enemies will be put to shame and deeply terrified; they will turn back; in an instant they will be put to shame.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — Ha-sheminit (the Sheminith, literally the eighth) is a musical term understood either as an eight-stringed instrument or as a lower octave or mode.
- **6:4** — Ketiv/Qere: ketiv writes the shorter defective spelling wt of the second-person masculine singular pronoun, while qere supplies the standard spelling we-attah (and You).
- **6:7** — Both ascheh (Hiphil of sachah, cause to swim) and amseh (Hiphil of masah, melt or dissolve) are vivid poetic hyperboles depicting profuse weeping.
- **6:11** — Yibbahalu (Niphal of bahal, be terrified) echoes the psalmist's own affliction in 6:3-4 (nivhalu, nivhalah), marking a dramatic reversal upon his enemies.
