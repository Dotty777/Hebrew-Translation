## Text

**31:1** Of David. A Maskil. Blessed is the one whose transgression has been lifted away, whose sin has been covered.

**31:2** Blessed is the person to whom the LORD reckons no iniquity, and in whose spirit is no deception.

**31:3** When I remained silent, my bones wore away from my groaning throughout the day.

**31:4** For day and night Your hand weighed heavily on me; my moisture was turned into the parching heat of summer. Selah.

**31:5** My sin I made known to You, and my iniquity I did not cover up; I said, "I will make confession of my transgressions to the LORD," and You lifted away the iniquity of my sin. Selah.

**31:6** Because of this, let every faithful one pray to You at a time when You can be found; surely, in a flood of many waters, they will not reach him.

**31:7** You are a shelter for me; from distress You guard me; with joyful cries of deliverance You surround me. Selah.

**31:8** I will give you insight and teach you in the way that you should walk; I will give counsel with My eye on you.

**31:9** Do not be like a horse, like a mule with no understanding, whose trappings are bit and bridle to restrain it, or else it will not come near you.

**31:10** Many pains belong to the wicked, but loyal love surrounds the one who trusts in the LORD.

**31:11** Be glad in the LORD and exult, you righteous ones, and shout joyfully, all you upright of heart.

## Translator Notes

- **31:1** — Maskil is a Hebrew musical or literary term derived from the root for giving insight, echoed in the verb 'give you insight' in verse 8. Hebrew nesuy-pesha literally means 'one lifted away of transgression,' from the verb nasa ('lift/carry away,' idiomatically 'forgive').
- **31:4** — Hebrew leshaddi refers to moisture or vital sap, and becharvonei qayits combines the preposition 'in/by' with a plural noun from the root for dryness or drought to express the parching heat of summer.
- **31:6** — Hebrew le'et metso literally reads 'at a time of finding,' using the Qal infinitive construct of matsa ('find'), commonly understood as a time when God can be found.
- **31:8** — The speaker shifts here to the first person singular; most interpreters understand the LORD as replying to the psalmist, though some read the psalmist as addressing listeners in the voice of a wisdom teacher.
- **31:9** — The Hebrew syntax of the second half is terse and difficult: edyo ('its trappings' or 'its ornament,' taken by some as 'its temper') is paired with the rare Qal infinitive livlom ('to curb/restrain') and bal qerov elekha (literally 'not coming near to you').
