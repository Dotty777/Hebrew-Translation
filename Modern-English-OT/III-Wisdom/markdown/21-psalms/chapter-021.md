## Text

**21:1** For the music director, according to The Doe of the Dawn. A psalm of David.

**21:2** My God, my God, why have You abandoned me? Why are You far from my salvation, from the words of my roaring?

**21:3** My God, I call out by day, yet You do not answer, and by night, yet there is no quiet for me.

**21:4** Yet You are holy, seated upon the praises of Israel.

**21:5** Our fathers trusted in You; they trusted, and You brought them to safety.

**21:6** They cried out to You and escaped; they trusted in You and were not ashamed.

**21:7** Yet I am a worm and not a man, a reproach to mankind and one despised by the people.

**21:8** All who see me jeer at me; they open their lips wide, they shake their heads.

**21:9** "Entrust yourself to the LORD! Let Him bring him to safety; let Him rescue him, for He takes pleasure in him."

**21:10** Yet You are the One who drew me out of the womb, making me trust at my mother's breasts.

**21:11** I was cast upon You from the womb; from my mother's womb You are my God.

**21:12** Do not stay far from me, for distress is near, for there is no helper.

**21:13** Numerous bulls have surrounded me; the mighty ones of Bashan have encircled me.

**21:14** They have opened their mouths wide against me, like a tearing and roaring lion.

**21:15** Like water I have been poured out, and all my bones have come apart; my heart has become like wax, melted within my inward parts.

**21:16** My strength has dried up like a pottery shard; my tongue is stuck to my jaws, and You place me in the dust of death.

**21:17** For dogs have surrounded me; a band of evildoers has closed in around me, like a lion at my hands and my feet.

**21:18** I count all my bones; they look on, they gloat over me.

**21:19** They divide up my garments for themselves, and over my clothing they cast a lot.

**21:20** But You, LORD, do not stay far off; my strength, hurry to my aid.

**21:21** Rescue my soul from the sword, my only life from the paw of a dog.

**21:22** Save me from a lion's mouth; and from the horns of wild oxen You have answered me.

**21:23** I will recount Your name to my brothers; within the assembly I will praise You.

**21:24** Praise Him, you who fear the LORD; all the seed of Jacob, honor Him, and stand in awe before Him, all the seed of Israel.

**21:25** For He has not despised and has not detested the affliction of the afflicted one, nor has He hidden His face from him; but when he cried out for help to Him, He listened.

**21:26** My praise in a great assembly comes from You; I will fulfill my vows in the presence of those who fear Him.

**21:27** The humble will eat and have their fill; those who seek Him will praise the LORD. May your heart live on forever.

**21:28** All the ends of the earth will remember and return to the LORD, and all the clans of the nations will bow down before You.

**21:29** For the kingdom belongs to the LORD, and He is ruler over the nations.

**21:30** All the thriving of the earth eat and bow down; all who descend to the dust will kneel before Him, even one who could not keep his own soul alive.

**21:31** A seed will serve Him; it will be recounted about the Lord to the future generation.

**21:32** They will come and announce His righteousness to a people yet to be born, that He has acted.

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — The masculine singular adjective "far" (rachoq) continues the opening question addressed to God, with "why" carried over elliptically. "My roaring" (sha'agati) literally denotes a lion's roar, anticipating the roaring lion of verse 14.
- **21:9** — Hebrew gol (Qal imperative or infinitive absolute of galal, "to roll") is an idiom for rolling one's burden onto the LORD, i.e., entrusting oneself to Him. If read as an infinitive absolute substituting for a finite verb, the opening clause means "He entrusted himself to the LORD."
- **21:17** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads ka'ari ("like a lion"), leaving the verb elliptical in the third clause. Ancient versions including the Septuagint and a Judean Desert scroll fragment reflect a verbal reading ("they pierced" or "they dug").
- **21:22** — The Masoretic Hebrew ends the verse with the Qal perfect verb anitani ("You have answered me"), marking the abrupt shift from lament to praise in verses 23-32.
- **21:30** — Translates the Masoretic Hebrew aklu ("eat," echoing verse 27) and dishne-erets (literally "fat ones of the earth," meaning the thriving or prosperous). The closing clause reads literally "and his soul he has not kept alive" (Piel perfect of chayah).
