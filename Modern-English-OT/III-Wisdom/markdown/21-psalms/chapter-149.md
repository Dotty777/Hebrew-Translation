## Text

**149:1** Praise the LORD! Sing a new song to the LORD, His praise in the assembly of the devoted ones.

**149:2** May Israel be joyful in its Maker; may the sons of Zion exult in their King.

**149:3** May they praise His name with dancing; with hand drum and lyre may they make music to Him.

**149:4** For the LORD is pleased with His people; He adorns the humble with deliverance.

**149:5** May the devoted ones rejoice in glory; may they shout with joy on their beds.

**149:6** High praises of God are in their throats, and double-edged swords are in their hands,

**149:7** to carry out vengeance on the nations, punishments on the peoples,

**149:8** to bind their kings in chains and their nobles in iron fetters,

**149:9** to carry out on them the written judgment. It is an honor for all His devoted ones. Praise the LORD!

## Translator Notes

- **149:1** — Hebrew halelu-yah ("Hallelujah"), a Piel imperative of halal ("praise") with Yah, the shortened poetic form of the divine name, recurs in 149:9, 150:1, and 150:6. "Devoted ones" translates chasidim, denoting those bound to God in covenant loyalty (chesed).
- **149:2** — Hebrew be'osav uses a masculine plural Qal active participle of 'asah ("make") with a singular suffix, literally "in its Makers," an idiom known as the plural of majesty referring to God (cf. Job 35:10; Isa 54:5).
- **149:6** — Hebrew cherev pifiyyot is literally "a sword of edges" (from a reduplicated plural of peh, "mouth" or "edge"). Both "throat" and "hand" are singular in Hebrew with plural suffixes, used distributively for each worshiper.
- **149:9** — Hebrew mishpat katuv ("written judgment," using the Qal passive participle of katav) refers to a recorded divine decree; hadar ("honor") carries the nuance of royal splendor or distinction.
