## Text

**142:1** A psalm of David. LORD, hear my prayer; listen closely to my pleas for favor. By Your faithfulness answer me, by Your righteousness.

**142:2** And do not come into judgment with Your servant, for no living being will be righteous before You.

**142:3** For an enemy has pursued my soul; he has crushed my life to the earth; he has caused me to dwell in dark places, like the dead of long ago.

**142:4** So my spirit has grown faint within me; inside me my heart is desolate.

**142:5** I remember days from ancient times; I meditate on all Your deeds; I reflect on the work of Your hands.

**142:6** I spread out my hands toward You; my soul is like a parched land for You. Selah.

**142:7** Hurry, answer me, LORD; my spirit is spent. Do not hide Your face from me, or else I will be like those who descend into the pit.

**142:8** Let me hear Your loyal love in the morning, for in You I have trusted. Make known to me the way I should walk, for to You I have lifted up my soul.

**142:9** Rescue me from my enemies, LORD; with You I have hidden myself.

**142:10** Instruct me to do Your will, for You are my God; may Your good Spirit guide me in a level land.

**142:11** For the sake of Your name, LORD, keep me alive; in Your righteousness, bring my soul out of distress.

**142:12** And in Your loyal love, wipe out my enemies, and destroy all the foes of my soul, for I am Your servant.

## Translator Notes

- **142:6** — The second clause is verbless in Hebrew ("my soul is like a parched land for You"), leaving the implied thirsting or longing unstated.
- **142:9** — Eleykha khissiti uses the Piel perfect of kasah ("cover, conceal") without an explicit object—literally "to You I have covered [myself]," meaning hidden myself or taken cover with You, whereas the Septuagint implies a different verb ("I have fled").
- **142:10** — Tovah ("good") lacks the definite article and bears the Masoretic atnach, and the Hiphil imperfect tancheni ("guide me") from nachah can be parsed as either third-person feminine singular ("may Your good Spirit guide me") or second-person masculine singular ("Your Spirit is good; guide me").
