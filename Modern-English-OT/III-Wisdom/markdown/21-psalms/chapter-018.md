## Text

**18:1** For the music director. A psalm of David.

**18:2** The heavens recount the glory of God, and the expanse announces the work of His hands.

**18:3** Day to day pours out utterance, and night to night discloses knowledge.

**18:4** There is no utterance, and there are no words; their voice is unheard.

**18:5** Through all the earth their measuring line has gone out, and to the end of the world their words. For the sun He has set a tent in them.

**18:6** And it is like a bridegroom going out from his canopy; it exults like a mighty man to run a course.

**18:7** From one end of the heavens is its going out, and its circuit reaches to their ends; nothing remains hidden from its heat.

**18:8** The instruction of the LORD is flawless, restoring the soul; the testimony of the LORD is reliable, making the naive wise.

**18:9** The precepts of the LORD are upright, gladdening the heart; the commandment of the LORD is radiant, illuminating the eyes.

**18:10** The fear of the LORD is pure, standing forever. The judgments of the LORD are truth; they are altogether righteous.

**18:11** More desirable are they than gold and than abundant fine gold, and sweeter than honey and drippings from the honeycombs.

**18:12** Also, Your servant takes warning from them; in observing them there is great reward.

**18:13** Who can perceive errors? Absolve me from hidden faults.

**18:14** Also restrain Your servant from presumptuous deeds; do not let them rule over me. Then I will be without blame, and I will be absolved of great transgression.

**18:15** May the utterances of my mouth and the musing of my heart find acceptance before You, LORD, my rock and my redeemer.

## Translator Notes

- **18:5** — The Masoretic Text reads qavvam ("their measuring line," from qav), denoting the reach of the heavens' witness across the earth. Ancient versions such as the Septuagint reflect qolam ("their voice"), but the translation follows the Hebrew text.
- **18:13** — The rare noun shegi'ot (from shagah, "go astray") denotes inadvertent or unwitting errors, paired with the Niphal participle nistarot ("hidden things/faults"). The Piel imperative naqqeni ("absolve me," from naqah) is a forensic term meaning to declare innocent or free from guilt.
- **18:14** — The plural substantive zedim literally means "insolent/presumptuous ones" and elsewhere denotes arrogant people; here it is rendered "presumptuous deeds" in contrast with the inadvertent errors of v. 13, though it may alternatively refer to insolent people. The form 'etam is a Qal imperfect 1cs from tamam ("be complete/blameless"), echoing temimah ("flawless") in v. 8.
- **18:15** — The idiom hayah leratson ("be for acceptance / find acceptance") draws on sacrificial language for an offering accepted before the LORD (cf. Exod 28:38; Lev 1:3).
