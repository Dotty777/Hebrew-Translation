## Text

**5:1** For the music leader, for the Nehiloth. A psalm of David.

**5:2** Listen closely to my words, LORD; discern my groaning.

**5:3** Pay attention to the sound of my cry for help, my King and my God, for it is to You I pray.

**5:4** LORD, in the morning You will hear my voice; in the morning I will set my plea in order for You and keep watch.

**5:5** For You are not a God who finds delight in wickedness; evil does not sojourn with You.

**5:6** The boastful will not take their stand before Your eyes; You hate all who practice iniquity.

**5:7** You destroy speakers of falsehood; the LORD detests a man of bloodshed and treachery.

**5:8** Yet as for me, by the abundance of Your faithful love I will come into Your house; I will bow low toward Your holy sanctuary in fear of You.

**5:9** LORD, guide me in Your righteousness on account of my foes; make Your way level before me.

**5:10** For there is nothing trustworthy in his mouth; their inward being is destruction. Their throat is an open tomb; they make their tongue smooth.

**5:11** Pronounce them guilty, God; let them fall through their schemes. Because of the multitude of their transgressions drive them out, since they have rebelled against You.

**5:12** Yet may all who seek refuge in You rejoice; may they shout for joy forever, and may You extend shelter over them; and may those who love Your name be jubilant in You.

**5:13** For You, LORD, bless the righteous one; You encircle him with favor like a large shield.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Ha-nechilot (the Nehiloth) is a unique musical heading of uncertain etymology, traditionally associated with flutes or wind instruments.
- **5:4** — Eerokh (Qal imperfect of arakh, arrange or set in order) has no explicit object in Hebrew and may denote either ordering one's verbal plea or laying out a morning sacrifice.
- **5:9** — Ketiv/Qere: ketiv reads hwshr (treating the root yashar as an initial-waw verb in the Hiphil imperative), while qere reads hayshar (preserving the initial yod); both mean make level or straight.
- **5:10** — MT has a singular suffix in befihu (in his mouth), a distributive singular referring to each foe before shifting to plural suffixes; yachaliqun (Hiphil of chalaq, make smooth) is an idiom for flattering speech.
- **5:13** — Taaterennu is Qal imperfect of atar (surround or encircle, whereas the Piel stem means crown); tsinnah denotes a large full-body shield rather than a smaller buckler.
