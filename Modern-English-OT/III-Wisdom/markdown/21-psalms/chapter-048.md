## Text

**48:1** For the music director; a psalm of the sons of Korah.

**48:2** Listen to this, all peoples; give ear, all who dwell in the world,

**48:3** both lowly and high, rich and needy together.

**48:4** My mouth will speak wisdom, and the musing of my heart is understanding.

**48:5** I will turn my ear to a proverb; I will unfold my riddle with the lyre.

**48:6** Why should I fear in days of evil, when the iniquity of my deceivers surrounds me,

**48:7** those who rely on their wealth and boast in the greatness of their riches?

**48:8** A man cannot in any way redeem a brother; he cannot give to God his ransom—

**48:9** for the redemption of their soul is costly, and it ceases forever—

**48:10** so that he might live on perpetually and not see the pit.

**48:11** For he sees that the wise die; the fool and the brutish person perish together and leave their wealth to others.

**48:12** Their inward thought is that their houses are forever, their dwellings from generation to generation; they have called lands by their own names.

**48:13** Yet man in splendor will not abide; he resembles the beasts that perish.

**48:14** This is their way—folly is theirs; yet those after them delight in their sayings. Selah.

**48:15** Like a flock they are set for Sheol; death will shepherd them, and the upright will have dominion over them in the morning; their form is for Sheol to wear away, far from its dwelling.

**48:16** Surely God will redeem my soul from the hand of Sheol, for He will take me. Selah.

**48:17** Do not fear when a man grows wealthy, when the glory of his house multiplies;

**48:18** for in his death he will take nothing; his glory will not go down after him.

**48:19** Though in his lifetime he blesses his soul, and people praise you when you do good for yourself,

**48:20** his soul will go to the generation of his fathers; they will never see light.

**48:21** Man in splendor who does not understand resembles the beasts that perish.

## Translator Notes

- **48:3** — בְּנֵי אָדָם ('sons of mankind') and בְּנֵי־אִישׁ ('sons of a man [of rank]') form a Hebrew idiom contrasting ordinary people with those of high standing.
- **48:6** — עֲקֵבַי is ambiguous and can mean either 'my deceivers/supplanters' (from the root עקב, linking to the plural participle in v. 7) or 'my heels' (i.e., 'iniquity at my heels').
- **48:8** — Translates the Masoretic text אָח ('a brother'); some ancient versions and commentators emend to אַךְ ('surely').
- **48:12** — Translates the Masoretic text קִרְבָּם ('their inward thought'); the Septuagint and Syriac reflect transposed consonants קִבְרָם ('their graves').
- **48:15** — Ketiv וצירם ('their image') vs. Qere וְצוּרָם ('their form/shape'); the translation follows the Qere. The closing phrase מִזְּבֻל לוֹ literally means 'away from a dwelling for it,' i.e., far from its dwelling.
