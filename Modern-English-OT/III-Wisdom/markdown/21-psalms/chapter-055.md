## Text

**55:1** For the music director, according to The Dove of Distant Silence. A Miktam of David, when the Philistines captured him in Gath.

**55:2** Show me grace, God, for mortal man has trampled me; fighting all day long, he presses hard against me.

**55:3** My lurking foes have trampled on me all day long, for many are fighting haughtily against me.

**55:4** On the day I am afraid, in You I will trust.

**55:5** In God I will praise His word. In God I have trusted; I will not be afraid. What could flesh do to me?

**55:6** They twist my words all day long; all their thoughts against me are for evil.

**55:7** They band together, they lie hidden; they themselves keep watch on my footprints, just as they have lain in wait for my life.

**55:8** Because of iniquity, is there escape for them? In anger, bring down the peoples, God.

**55:9** My wandering You Yourself have counted; place my tears in Your wineskin. Are they not in Your register?

**55:10** Then my enemies will turn backward on the day I call. I know this: God is for me.

**55:11** In God I will praise the word; in the LORD I will praise the word.

**55:12** In God I have trusted; I will not be afraid. What could man do to me?

**55:13** Upon me, God, are the vows to You; I will pay offerings of thanksgiving to You.

**55:14** For You have rescued my life from death—surely my feet from stumbling—in order to walk in the presence of God, in the light of the living.

## Translator Notes

- **55:1** — The obscure musical heading yonat 'elem rechoqim literally reads 'dove of silence of distant places' in MT; an alternative vocalization 'elim would mean 'terebinths/oaks.'
- **55:3** — The noun marom ('height, loftiness') is taken here adverbially ('haughtily/proudly'), though some construe it as a vocative divine title ('Most High' or 'Exalted One').
- **55:7** — The Qere reads Qal yitsponu ('they lie hidden/lurk'), whereas the Ketiv reads Hiphil yatspinu ('they conceal' or 'set an ambush').
- **55:8** — MT literally reads 'because of iniquity, escape for them' (using the noun/infinitive pallet, 'escape,' as in Ps 32:7 MT), understood here as an unmarked rhetorical question.
- **55:9** — The Hebrew employs a double wordplay linking nodi ('my wandering') with no'dekha ('Your wineskin') and safartah ('You have counted') with sifratekha ('Your register/record').
