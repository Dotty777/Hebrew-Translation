## Text

**7:1** A Shiggaion of David, which he sang to the LORD regarding the words of Cush, a Benjaminite.

**7:2** LORD, my God, I have taken refuge in You; save me from all my pursuers and rescue me,

**7:3** or else he will tear my soul like a lion, ripping it apart while there is no one to rescue.

**7:4** LORD, my God, if I have done this thing, if wrongdoing is in my hands,

**7:5** if I have repaid the one at peace with me with evil—though I delivered my adversary without cause—

**7:6** then let an enemy pursue my soul and overtake it; let him tread my life to the ground and make my glory dwell in the dust. Selah.

**7:7** Arise, LORD, in Your anger; be lifted up against the wrath of my adversaries, and awake on my behalf; You have commanded judgment.

**7:8** And let the congregation of peoples surround You, and above it return to the height.

**7:9** The LORD passes judgment on the peoples; judge me, LORD, according to my righteousness and according to my integrity within me.

**7:10** May the evil of the wicked come to an end, and may You establish the righteous, and the righteous God tests hearts and kidneys.

**7:11** My shield rests upon God, who saves the upright of heart.

**7:12** God is a righteous judge, and a God who is indignant every day.

**7:13** If he does not turn back, He will sharpen His sword; He has bent His bow and readied it.

**7:14** And for Himself He has prepared weapons of death; He makes His arrows fiery.

**7:15** Look, he is in labor with iniquity; he conceives trouble and gives birth to falsehood.

**7:16** He dug a pit and excavated it, and fell into the pit he was making.

**7:17** His trouble will return on his own head, and his violence will come down on the crown of his head.

**7:18** I will thank the LORD according to His righteousness, and I will sing praises to the name of the LORD Most High.

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — Shiggaion (Hebrew shiggayon) is a musical or poetic term of uncertain meaning, occurring elsewhere in the plural in Habakkuk 3:1.
- **7:5** — The verb form (Piel of chalats) regularly means "deliver" or "rescue" elsewhere in the Psalms, yielding a contrastive aside here; some lexicons posit a distinct sense "strip" or "plunder" to run parallel with the preceding clause.
- **7:6** — The form yiraddof displays an unusual Masoretic pointing combining features of the Qal (yirdof) and Piel (yeraddef) stems of radaf ("pursue").
- **7:10** — Hebrew kelayot ("kidneys") is the idiomatic term for the seat of one's deepest emotions, desires, and inward motives.
