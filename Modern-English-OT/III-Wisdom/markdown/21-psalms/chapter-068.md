## Text

**68:1** For the music director, on Lilies. Of David.

**68:2** Save me, God, for the waters have come up to my throat.

**68:3** I have sunk in the mire of the deep, and there is no place to stand; I have come into the depths of the waters, and a torrent has swept over me.

**68:4** I have grown weary in my crying out; my throat is parched; my eyes have failed while waiting for my God.

**68:5** Those who hate me for no reason outnumber the hairs of my head; mighty are those who would destroy me, who are falsely my enemies; what I did not steal, I must then return.

**68:6** God, You Yourself know my folly, and my guilty deeds are not concealed from You.

**68:7** Do not let those who wait for You be put to shame because of me, Lord GOD of hosts; do not let those who seek You be disgraced because of me, God of Israel.

**68:8** For on Your account I have borne reproach; disgrace has covered my face.

**68:9** I have become estranged from my brothers, and a foreigner to the sons of my mother.

**68:10** For zeal for Your house has devoured me, and the taunts of those who taunt You have fallen on me.

**68:11** I wept with my soul in fasting, and it became reproaches against me.

**68:12** I made sackcloth my garment, and I became a byword for them.

**68:13** Those who sit at the gate talk about me, and I am the song of those who drink strong drink.

**68:14** But as for me, my prayer is to You, LORD, at a time of favor; God, in the abundance of Your loyal love, answer me in the faithfulness of Your salvation.

**68:15** Rescue me from the mud, and let me not sink; let me be rescued from those who hate me and from the depths of the waters.

**68:16** Do not let the torrent of waters sweep over me, and do not let the deep swallow me; do not let the pit shut its mouth over me.

**68:17** Answer me, LORD, for Your loyal love is good; according to the abundance of Your mercies, turn toward me.

**68:18** And do not hide Your face from Your servant, for I am in distress; hurry, answer me.

**68:19** Come near to my soul, redeem it; because of my enemies, ransom me.

**68:20** You Yourself know my reproach, and my shame, and my disgrace; before You are all my adversaries.

**68:21** Reproach has broken my heart, so that I am desperately ill; I hoped for sympathy, but there was none, and for comforters, but I did not find any.

**68:22** They put poison into my food, and for my thirst they made me drink vinegar.

**68:23** Let their table before them become a snare, and for those at peace, a trap.

**68:24** Let their eyes grow dark so they cannot see, and make their loins totter continually.

**68:25** Pour out Your fury on them, and let Your fierce anger overtake them.

**68:26** Let their encampment become desolate; in their tents let there be no inhabitant.

**68:27** For they have pursued the one whom You Yourself struck, and they talk of the pain of those You have wounded.

**68:28** Place iniquity upon their iniquity, and do not let them come into Your righteousness.

**68:29** Let them be wiped out from the book of the living, and do not let them be written with the righteous.

**68:30** But as for me, I am poor and in pain; may Your salvation, God, lift me securely on high.

**68:31** I will praise God's name in song, and with thanksgiving I will magnify Him.

**68:32** And it will be more pleasing to the LORD than an ox, a bull bearing horns and hooves.

**68:33** The humble have seen it and will rejoice; you who seek God, may your heart live.

**68:34** For the LORD listens to the needy, and He does not despise His prisoners.

**68:35** May the heavens and the earth praise Him, the seas and all that moves in them.

**68:36** For God will save Zion and will build the cities of Judah, and they will live there and take possession of it.

**68:37** And the seed of His servants will inherit it, and those who love His name will settle in it.

## Translator Notes

- **68:2** — Hebrew nefesh literally means throat or neck in drowning imagery, as well as life or soul.
- **68:11** — The Masoretic text reads literally, I wept in fasting my soul, taking my soul as an adverbial specification rather than supplying the verb humbled found in the Septuagint.
- **68:23** — The Masoretic text vocalizes lishlomim, meaning for those at peace or in their security, whereas the Septuagint reflects a different vocalization meaning for retribution.
- **68:27** — The Masoretic text reads yesapperu, Piel of sfr meaning they talk of or recount, whereas the Septuagint reflects a different verb meaning they add to.
- **68:32** — Hebrew maqrin mafris consists of two Hiphil denominative participles, literally putting forth horns and having hooves, describing a mature, ritually clean sacrificial bull.
