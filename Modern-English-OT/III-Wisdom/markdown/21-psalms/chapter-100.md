## Text

**100:1** Of David. A psalm. Of loyal love and justice I will sing; to You, LORD, I will make melody.

**100:2** I will give attention to the blameless way. When will You come to me? I will walk in the integrity of my heart within my house.

**100:3** I will not place before my eyes any vile thing. I hate the practice of unfaithful deeds; it will not cling to me.

**100:4** A twisted heart will depart from me; evil I will not know.

**100:5** The one who slanders his neighbor in secret, him I will wipe out; the one with haughty eyes and an arrogant heart, him I cannot endure.

**100:6** My eyes are on the trustworthy of the land, so that they may dwell with me; the one who walks in the blameless way, he will serve me.

**100:7** Within my house, one who practices deceit will not dwell; one who speaks lies will not be established before my eyes.

**100:8** Morning by morning I will wipe out all the wicked of the land, to cut off from the city of the LORD all workers of iniquity.

## Translator Notes

- **100:2** — Hebrew askilah (Hiphil cohortative of sakhal) with the preposition be- can mean either 'I will give attention to' (ponder with insight) or 'I will act wisely in' the blameless way.
- **100:3** — Hebrew asoh-setim pairs the Qal infinitive construct of asah ('do, practice') with the rare plural noun setim (from sut, 'swerve, turn aside'), meaning either 'the practice of unfaithful deeds' or 'the work of those who turn aside.'
- **100:5** — Ketiv and qere differ only in spelling: the ketiv writes meloshni plene with a vav, while the qere writes it defectively with qamets hatuf; both are participles of lashan ('slander') with the archaic poetic ending -i (hireq compaginis). Hebrew rehav levav ('an arrogant heart') is literally 'broad of heart,' an idiom for swollen pride.
- **100:8** — Hebrew labbeqarim ('morning by morning') is a distributive plural meaning 'every morning,' reflecting the ancient practice of administering royal justice each morning.
