## Text

**69:1** For the music director. Of David. To bring to remembrance.

**69:2** God, hurry to rescue me; LORD, hurry to my help!

**69:3** May those who seek my life be put to shame and humiliated; may those who delight in my harm be turned backward and disgraced.

**69:4** Let them turn back on account of their shame, those who say, "Aha, aha!"

**69:5** May all who seek You exult and rejoice in You, and may those who love Your salvation continually say, "May God be magnified!"

**69:6** But as for me, I am poor and needy; God, hurry to me! You are my help and the One who delivers me; LORD, do not delay.

## Translator Notes

- **69:1** — Hebrew lehazkir is a Hiphil infinitive construct of zkr, literally to cause to remember or to bring to remembrance, probably a liturgical heading.
- **69:2** — The final imperative chushah, hurry, governs both halves of the verse through poetic ellipsis.
- **69:4** — Hebrew al-eqev, literally on the heel of, is an idiom meaning on account of, and the Masoretic verb yashuvu, let them turn back, differs from the parallel psalm, which reads be appalled.
