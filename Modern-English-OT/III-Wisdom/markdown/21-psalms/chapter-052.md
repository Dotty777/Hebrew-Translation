## Text

**52:1** For the music director, according to Mahalath. A Maskil of David.

**52:2** A fool says in his heart, "There is no God." They have acted corruptly and committed detestable injustice; no one does what is good.

**52:3** God looked down from heaven on the children of mankind to see whether there is anyone with insight, anyone seeking God.

**52:4** Every one of them has turned back; together they have become tainted. No one does what is good, not even a single one.

**52:5** Do they not know, the workers of wickedness, who eat My people just as they eat bread? They do not call on God.

**52:6** There they trembled in dread where there was no dread, for God has scattered the bones of the one encamping against you; you have shamed them, because God has rejected them.

**52:7** If only Israel's salvation would come from Zion! When God restores His people's fortunes, may Jacob exult, may Israel be joyful.

## Translator Notes

- **52:1** — Mahalath is an uncertain musical or liturgical heading, appearing also in the superscription of Psalm 88 MT, likely naming a tune or instrument.
- **52:5** — The first-person suffix in "My people" introduces God's own voice even though the next clause refers to God in the third person, a shift common in prophetic and poetic speech.
- **52:6** — This verse diverges notably from its parallel in Psalm 14:5-6, featuring a triple repetition of the root pahad, "dread," and announcing the scattering of the besieger's bones.
- **52:7** — "If only ... would come" renders the Hebrew optative idiom mi yitten, literally "who will give," and "salvation" translates an intensive plural yeshuot, literally "deliverances."
