## Text

**146:1** Praise the LORD! For it is good to make music to our God, for it is pleasant; praise is fitting.

**146:2** The LORD builds Jerusalem; He gathers the banished of Israel.

**146:3** He is the One who heals those broken in heart and bandages their wounds.

**146:4** He reckons the number of the stars; He calls them all by name.

**146:5** Our Lord is great and vast in strength; His understanding is without number.

**146:6** The LORD upholds the humble; He brings the wicked low to the ground.

**146:7** Respond to the LORD with thanksgiving; make music to our God with the lyre,

**146:8** who covers the skies with clouds, who prepares rain for the earth, who causes grass to sprout on the mountains.

**146:9** He gives to the animals their food, to the raven's young that call out.

**146:10** He does not delight in the might of the horse; He is not pleased with the legs of a man.

**146:11** The LORD is pleased with those who fear Him, with those who wait for His loyal love.

## Translator Notes

- **146:1** — The form rendered "to make music" is a Piel infinitive construct with a feminine ending taking "our God" as its direct object and serving as the subject of "good." The masculine adjective "pleasant" either refers impersonally to making music ("it is pleasant") or describes God ("He is pleasant").
- **146:5** — Literally "to His understanding there is no number," intentionally echoing "number" in 146:4 to contrast the countable stars with God's uncountable understanding.
- **146:7** — The Qal imperative "Respond" comes from the Hebrew root meaning "answer" or "respond," used in liturgical contexts for responsive singing and praise.
