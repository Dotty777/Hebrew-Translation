## Text

**11:1** For the music leader, on the Sheminith. A psalm of David.

**11:2** Save, LORD, for the loyal one has come to an end; for the trustworthy have vanished from among the sons of mankind.

**11:3** They speak deceit, each one with his neighbor; with a lip of flattery and a double heart they speak.

**11:4** May the LORD cut off all lips of flattery, the tongue speaking boastful things,

**11:5** those who have said, "By our tongue we will triumph; our lips are with us; who is master over us?"

**11:6** "On account of the plundering of the afflicted, on account of the groaning of the needy, now I will arise," says the LORD; "I will set him in the deliverance for which he longs."

**11:7** The utterances of the LORD are pure utterances, silver smelted in a crucible on the ground, purified sevenfold.

**11:8** You, LORD, will guard them; You will protect him from this generation forever.

**11:9** All around, the wicked walk about when depravity rises high among the sons of mankind.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — Hebrew hasheminit literally means "the eighth" (cf. 1 Chronicles 15:21), probably denoting either an eight-stringed instrument or a lower-octave musical mode.
- **11:6** — The terse clause ashit beyesha yafiach lo uses the Hiphil imperfect of puach ("breathe, pant, puff") in an unmarked relative clause, understood here as "in the deliverance for which he longs." Alternatively, taking puach as "scoff" or "snort," it may be rendered "I will set in safety the one at whom they scoff."
- **11:7** — Hebrew ba'alil la'aretz contains the unique noun alil, traditionally understood as a crucible or smelting furnace, with la'aretz denoting either a furnace set on the ground or an earthen vessel.
- **11:8** — The verb titzrennu carries the standard 3rd-person masculine singular energic suffix -ennu ("him"), shifting from plural "them" to each individual afflicted person as in 11:6, though some construe the suffix as 1st-person plural ("us").
- **11:9** — Hebrew zullut ("depravity" or "vileness") is a hapax legomenon derived from the root zalal ("be worthless, vile"), paired here with the Qal infinitive construct of rum ("rise high").
