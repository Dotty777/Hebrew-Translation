## Text

**121:1** A song of the ascents, of David. I was joyful when they said to me, "To the house of the LORD we will go."

**121:2** Our feet have been standing inside your gates, Jerusalem.

**121:3** Jerusalem, built like a city that is joined together as one,

**121:4** where the tribes ascend, the tribes of the LORD, a decree for Israel, to offer thanks to the name of the LORD.

**121:5** For there thrones stand for judgment, thrones belonging to the house of David.

**121:6** Ask for the peace of Jerusalem; may those who love you be at ease.

**121:7** May peace be within your rampart, tranquility within your citadels.

**121:8** On account of my brothers and my companions, let me now speak peace within you.

**121:9** On account of the house of the LORD our God, I will seek good for you.

## Translator Notes

- **121:3** — The Pual verb chubberah-lah yachdav means literally "has been joined to itself together," depicting Jerusalem as a compact, tightly unified city.
- **121:4** — Hebrew uses the shortened poetic divine name Yah ("the LORD") in "tribes of the LORD"; edut ("decree," or "testimony") refers to the covenant stipulation requiring Israel's pilgrimage festivals.
- **121:6** — The Hebrew creates a sound play linking Yerushalayim ("Jerusalem"), shalom ("peace"), the Qal verb yishlayu ("may they be at ease"), and its cognate noun shalvah ("tranquility") in verse 7; the imperative sha'alu literally means "ask for" (that is, pray for).
- **121:8** — The Piel cohortative adabberah-na shalom bakh ("let me now speak peace within you") uses the Hebrew idiom "speak peace" (parallel to "seek good for you" in verse 9), though it may also be read as direct speech, "let me now say, 'Peace be within you.'"
