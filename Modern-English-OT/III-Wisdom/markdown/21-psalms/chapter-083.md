## Text

**83:1** For the music director, upon the Gittith. A psalm of the sons of Korah.

**83:2** How beloved are Your dwelling places, LORD of hosts!

**83:3** My soul yearns and even grows faint for the courts of the LORD; my heart and my flesh cry out joyfully to the living God.

**83:4** Even a bird has found a house, and a swallow a nest for herself where she sets her fledglings, beside Your altars, LORD of hosts, my King and my God.

**83:5** Blessed are those who dwell in Your house; continually they praise You. Selah.

**83:6** Blessed is the person whose strength is in You; highways are in their heart.

**83:7** Passing through the valley of Baca, they turn it into a spring; the early rain also clothes it with blessings.

**83:8** They walk from strength to strength; each appears before God in Zion.

**83:9** LORD God of hosts, hear my prayer; listen, God of Jacob. Selah.

**83:10** Look upon our shield, God, and regard the face of Your anointed.

**83:11** For better is a day in Your courts than a thousand; I have chosen to stand at the threshold in the house of my God rather than to lodge in tents of wickedness.

**83:12** For the LORD God is sun and shield; the LORD gives favor and glory; He does not withhold good from those who walk in integrity.

**83:13** LORD of hosts, blessed is the person who trusts in You.

## Translator Notes

- **83:4** — The preposition 'et before "Your altars" is understood here as "beside" or "at," though some take it as an exclamatory object marker.
- **83:6** — Hebrew mesillot bilvavam ("highways are in their heart") refers to the pilgrimage roads leading to Zion and shifts from collective singular 'adam ("person") to a plural pronominal suffix.
- **83:7** — Baca may denote a balsam shrub or evoke "weeping"; the Masoretic pointing reads berakhot ("blessings") rather than berekhot ("pools"), and moreh here denotes the autumn early rain.
- **83:10** — Hebrew maginnenu ("our shield") may be read either as the direct object of re'eh ("look upon"), in parallel with "Your anointed" (the king), or as a vocative title for God (cf. v. 12).
- **83:11** — Hebrew histofef is a Hithpoel infinitive construct formed from saf ("threshold"), occurring only here, and dur ("lodge, dwell") is attested in this sense only here in Biblical Hebrew.
