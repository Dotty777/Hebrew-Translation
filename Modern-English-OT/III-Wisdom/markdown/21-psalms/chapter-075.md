## Text

**75:1** For the music director, with stringed instruments. A psalm of Asaph, a song.

**75:2** God is known in Judah; in Israel His name is great.

**75:3** And His shelter is in Salem, and His dwelling is in Zion.

**75:4** There He shattered the fiery bolts of the bow, shield and sword and battle. Selah.

**75:5** Radiant are You, more majestic than the mountains of prey.

**75:6** The stouthearted were despoiled; they slept their sleep, and none of the men of valor found their hands.

**75:7** At Your rebuke, God of Jacob, both chariot and horse fell into a deep sleep.

**75:8** You, You are to be feared! And who can stand before You from the moment of Your anger?

**75:9** From heaven You made judgment heard; the earth feared and grew still,

**75:10** when God rose up for judgment, to deliver all the humble of the earth. Selah.

**75:11** Surely the fury of mankind will praise You; the remnant of furies You will gird on.

**75:12** Make vows and pay them to the LORD your God; let all those around Him bring tribute to the One to be feared.

**75:13** He cuts off the spirit of rulers; to the kings of the earth, He is to be feared.

## Translator Notes

- **75:4** — "Fiery bolts of the bow" is literally "flames of the bow," a poetic metaphor for swift, flashing arrows.
- **75:5** — "More majestic than the mountains of prey" translates the Masoretic preposition comparatively; it may alternatively be taken spatially as "majestic from the mountains of prey," continuing the lion-den imagery of verse 3.
- **75:6** — "Were despoiled" translates a rare Hithpolel perfect of the verb meaning to plunder, displaying an Aramaizing initial prefix. "Found their hands" is a literal Hebrew idiom for being able to act or fight.
- **75:11** — "The remnant of furies You will gird on" renders the Masoretic Qal imperfect of the verb meaning to tie or gird around the waist, picturing God wearing remaining human rage like a belt or trophy, or binding it in restraint.
- **75:13** — The Qal imperfect verb rendered "cuts off" is normally used of harvesting or snipping off grape clusters, applied here metaphorically to cutting away the spirit of rulers.
