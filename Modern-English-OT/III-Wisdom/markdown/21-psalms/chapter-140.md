## Text

**140:1** A psalm of David. LORD, I have called to You; hurry to me. Listen closely to my voice when I call to You.

**140:2** May my prayer be established as incense before You, the raising of my hands as an evening offering.

**140:3** Place a guard at my mouth, LORD; watch over the door of my lips.

**140:4** Do not turn my heart to an evil matter, to engage in deeds in wickedness with men who are workers of iniquity, and may I not dine on their delicacies.

**140:5** May a righteous man strike me in kindness and rebuke me; it is oil for the head—may my head not refuse it. For even still, my prayer is against their evil deeds.

**140:6** Their judges will be cast down along the sides of the crag, and they will hear my words, for they are sweet.

**140:7** As one plows and splits open the ground, our bones have been dispersed at the mouth of Sheol.

**140:8** For toward You are my eyes, GOD, the Lord; in You I have taken refuge; do not pour out my soul.

**140:9** Guard me from the grasp of the trap they have set for me, and from the snares of workers of iniquity.

**140:10** May the wicked fall into their own nets, while at the same time I myself pass through.

## Translator Notes

- **140:3** — The verb nitsrah is an unusual vocalization of the Qal imperative masculine singular (with paragogic heh and dagesh forte in the second radical) of natsar ("keep watch"), and dal is a rare poetic masculine form corresponding to delet ("door").
- **140:5** — In this famously terse Masoretic verse, shemen rosh can mean either "oil for the head" or "finest oil" (playing on roshi, "my head"), and yani is a defective Hiphil jussive of nu ("refuse, reject"). The final clause literally reads "for still, even my prayer is against [or amid] their evil deeds."
- **140:6** — The phrase bidey-sela literally means "by the hands of a crag," a Hebrew idiom for the sides of a cliff. The Niphal perfect nishmetu ("cast down") with the following weqatal may be rendered with future/prophetic or past/gnomic force.
- **140:8** — The Masoretic text reverses the usual divine title, placing the divine name before Adonai (the divine name pointed as Elohim: "GOD, the Lord"). The apocopated Piel jussive tear from arah literally means "pour out" or "empty out," conveying leaving one's life defenseless to death.
- **140:10** — The noun phrase literally reads "into his nets," a distributive singular referring to each of the wicked. Following the Masoretic atnach after resha'im ("the wicked"), yachad ("together, at the same time") opens the second clause alongside the emphatic pronoun anokhi ("I myself").
