## Text

**14:1** A psalm of David. LORD, who may sojourn in Your tent? Who may dwell on Your holy mountain?

**14:2** One who walks blamelessly, and practices righteousness, and speaks truth within his heart.

**14:3** He does not spread slander with his tongue; he does no harm to his companion, and he does not carry a reproach against his neighbor.

**14:4** A contemptible person is rejected in his eyes, yet he honors those who fear the LORD; he takes an oath to his own detriment and does not alter it.

**14:5** He does not lend his money at interest, and he takes no bribe against an innocent person. Anyone who does these things will never be shaken.

## Translator Notes

- **14:1** — The verb "sojourn" translates the Qal imperfect of gur (to stay temporarily as a guest under a host's protection), paired with the Qal imperfect of shakan ("dwell") in the parallel clause.
- **14:3** — The verb "spread slander" translates the Qal perfect of ragal (denominative from regel, "foot," literally going about talebearing), which occurs in the Qal stem only here in Biblical Hebrew.
- **14:4** — Following the Masoretic punctuation, the Niphal participle nivzeh ("a contemptible person") is read as the subject and the Niphal participle nimas ("is rejected") as the predicate; other translations reverse them ("a rejected person is despised in his eyes"). Also, le-hara (Hiphil infinitive construct of ra'a) means "to his own detriment/hurt," whereas the Septuagint read "to his neighbor."
