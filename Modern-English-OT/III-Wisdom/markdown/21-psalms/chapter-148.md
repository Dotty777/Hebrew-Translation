## Text

**148:1** Praise the LORD! Praise the LORD from the heavens; praise Him in the heights.

**148:2** Praise Him, all His angels; praise Him, all His armies.

**148:3** Praise Him, sun and moon; praise Him, all bright stars.

**148:4** Praise Him, heavens of the heavens, and the waters above the heavens.

**148:5** May they praise the name of the LORD, for He Himself commanded, and they were created.

**148:6** And He stationed them forever and ever; He set a decree, and it will not pass away.

**148:7** Praise the LORD from the earth, sea monsters and all depths,

**148:8** fire and hail, snow and fog, storm wind carrying out His word,

**148:9** mountains and all hills, fruit-bearing trees and all cedars,

**148:10** wild animals and all livestock, crawling creatures and winged birds,

**148:11** kings of the earth and all nations, rulers and all judges of the earth,

**148:12** young men and young women as well, the elderly together with children.

**148:13** May they praise the name of the LORD, for His name alone is lifted high; His splendor is above earth and heaven.

**148:14** And He has raised a horn for His people, praise for all His loyal ones, for the children of Israel, a people close to Him. Praise the LORD!

## Translator Notes

- **148:2** — As in 147:8, the consonants are written without a yod (ketiv singular, "His host"), while the vocalization reads the qere plural ("His hosts" or "His armies"), denoting God's heavenly forces.
- **148:6** — The masculine singular imperfect verb "will not pass away" takes "decree" as its subject; alternatively, it may be understood impersonally or distributively as "which none may transgress."
- **148:8** — The noun rendered "fog" literally denotes thick rising smoke or vapor and here refers to dense mist or fog alongside snow.
- **148:14** — "Horn" is a common Hebrew idiom for strength, dignity, or victory. The final phrase is literally "a people of His near one," a poetic expression for a people close to God.
