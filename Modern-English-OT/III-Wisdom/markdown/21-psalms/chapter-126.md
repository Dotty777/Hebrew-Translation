## Text

**126:1** A song of the ascents, of Solomon. If the LORD does not build a house, its builders toil on it in vain; if the LORD does not guard a city, a guard stays awake in vain.

**126:2** It is in vain for you to get up early, to stay late before resting, eating the bread of painful toil; surely He gives sleep to His beloved.

**126:3** Look, sons are an inheritance from the LORD; the fruit of the womb is a reward.

**126:4** Like arrows in a warrior's hand, so are the sons of one's youth.

**126:5** How happy is the man who has filled his quiver with them; they will not be put to shame when they speak with enemies in the gate.

## Translator Notes

- **126:1** — The Hebrew preposition le- in lishlomoh ("of Solomon") can indicate authorship ("by Solomon") or dedication ("for/concerning Solomon"). Both "a house" (bayit) and "a city" (ir) are indefinite in Hebrew, and the root shamar ("guard") is repeated in the participle shomer ("a guard").
- **126:2** — The final clause (ken yitten lidido shena) is a well-known Hebrew crux: shena ("sleep," spelled with an Aramaizing final aleph) is taken here as the direct object ("He gives sleep to His beloved"), though some read it adverbially ("so He provides for His beloved while sleeping"). The particle ken ("so/surely") is retained from the Masoretic Text rather than emended to ki ("for").
- **126:5** — The verbs "will not be put to shame" (yevoshu) and "speak" (yedabberu, Piel of dabar) shift to the plural in Hebrew, encompassing the father together with his sons (or such households generally) in legal or civic disputes at the city gate. Some scholars compare a homonymous root dabar ("drive back/subdue"), though "speak with" is the standard sense of Piel dabar with the preposition et.
