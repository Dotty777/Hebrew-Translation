## Text

**122:1** A song of the ascents. To You I have lifted my eyes, You who are seated in the heavens.

**122:2** Look, as the eyes of servants are turned toward the hand of their master, as the eyes of a female servant toward the hand of her mistress, so our eyes are turned toward the LORD our God until He shows us grace.

**122:3** Show us grace, LORD, show us grace, for we are more than sated with contempt.

**122:4** Our soul is more than sated with the mocking of the complacent, with the contempt of proud oppressors.

## Translator Notes

- **122:1** — The form hayyoshvi ("who are seated") is the Qal active participle of yashav ("sit, dwell") with an archaic poetic connecting vowel -i (hireq compaginis), not a first-person suffix.
- **122:2** — Adonehem is morphologically plural ("their masters" or honorific plural "their master"), paired here with singular yad ("hand") and parallel to singular "her mistress."
- **122:4** — The ketiv reads as a single word ("of the proud"), whereas the translated qere divides it into two words: construct plural adjective "proud" governing the Qal active participle of yanah ("oppressors"), meaning "of proud oppressors." The verb "is sated" also takes an ethical dative ("for itself") intensifying "our soul."
