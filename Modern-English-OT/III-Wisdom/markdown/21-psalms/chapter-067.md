## Text

**67:1** For the music director. Of David. A psalm. A song.

**67:2** May God rise up; may His enemies scatter, and may those who hate Him flee from His presence.

**67:3** As smoke is driven away, may You drive them away; as wax melts before fire, may the wicked perish before God.

**67:4** But let the righteous be glad and exult before God; let them be jubilant with gladness.

**67:5** Sing to God; sing praise to His name! Cast up a highway for the One who rides through the desert plains—His name is the LORD—and exult before Him.

**67:6** Father of orphans and judge of widows is God in His holy dwelling.

**67:7** God settles the lonely into a home; He brings out prisoners into prosperity; only the rebellious inhabit a scorched land.

**67:8** God, when You went forth before Your people, when You strode through the wasteland, Selah,

**67:9** the earth shook; indeed, the heavens dripped at the presence of God, the One of Sinai, at the presence of God, the God of Israel.

**67:10** You showered down a generous rain, God; Your inheritance, when it grew weary, You Yourself made firm.

**67:11** Your company settled in it; in Your goodness You made provision for the poor, God.

**67:12** The Lord gives the word; the women proclaiming the good news are a vast army.

**67:13** The kings of armies flee, they flee, and the woman at home divides the plunder.

**67:14** Though you lie down among the sheepfolds—wings of a dove overlaid with silver, and its pinions with pale yellow gold.

**67:15** When the Almighty dispersed kings in it, it snowed on Zalmon.

**67:16** A mountain of God is Mount Bashan; a many-peaked mountain is Mount Bashan.

**67:17** Why do you gaze with envy, you many-peaked mountains, at the mountain God desired for His dwelling? Indeed, the LORD will dwell there forever.

**67:18** The chariots of God are twenty thousand, thousands upon thousands; the Lord is among them—Sinai is in the sanctuary.

**67:19** You ascended to the height; You took captives; You received gifts among mankind, even among the rebellious, so that the LORD God may dwell there.

**67:20** Blessed be the Lord! Day by day He carries our burden—God, our salvation. Selah.

**67:21** The God who is for us is a God of saving acts, and to GOD, the Lord, belong ways of escape from death.

**67:22** Surely God will smash the head of His enemies, the hairy scalp of the one who walks on in his trespasses.

**67:23** The Lord said, "From Bashan I will bring them back; I will bring them back from the depths of the sea,

**67:24** so that you may strike your foot in blood, and the tongue of your dogs may have its share from the enemies."

**67:25** They have seen Your processions, God, the processions of my God, my King, into the sanctuary.

**67:26** The singers went ahead, the musicians behind, in the midst of young women beating tambourines.

**67:27** In the assemblies bless God, the Lord, you who are from the fountain of Israel.

**67:28** There is Benjamin, the youngest, leading them, the leaders of Judah with their throng, the leaders of Zebulun, the leaders of Naphtali.

**67:29** Your God has ordained your strength; be strong, God, You who have worked for us.

**67:30** Because of Your temple above Jerusalem, kings will carry tribute to You.

**67:31** Rebuke the beasts of the reeds, the herd of bulls with the calves of the peoples, prostrating themselves with pieces of silver; He has scattered the peoples who delight in battles.

**67:32** Ambassadors will come from Egypt; Cush will swiftly stretch out its hands to God.

**67:33** Kingdoms of the earth, sing to God; sing praise to the Lord. Selah.

**67:34** To the One who rides in the highest heavens of old—look, He gives forth His voice, a voice of strength.

**67:35** Ascribe strength to God! Over Israel is His majesty, and His strength is in the clouds.

**67:36** Awe-inspiring are You, God, from Your sanctuaries! The God of Israel is the One who gives strength and might to the people. Blessed be God!

## Translator Notes

- **67:5** — The Qal imperative sollu literally means "cast up a highway," and aravot in the Masoretic text denotes "desert plains" (though some compare a cognate term for "clouds"); be-Yah shemo uses the shortened divine name Yah with the preposition be expressing identity ("His name is the LORD").
- **67:9** — The archaic expression zeh Sinai (compare Judges 5:5) literally reads "this Sinai" and functions either as a determinative title ("the One of Sinai") in apposition to God or as an emphatic exclamation ("even Sinai").
- **67:14** — This poetic verse is syntactically elliptical without a finite verb in the second clause; shefattayim is a dual noun traditionally rendered "sheepfolds" (or "hearthstones"), and yeraqraq charuts literally denotes "greenish-yellow gold," describing the sheen of fine gold.
- **67:21** — The divine title YHWH Adonai reverses the usual order of Adonai YHWH and carries the vowels of Elohim so that Adonai is not repeated, rendered here as "GOD, the Lord."
- **67:31** — The Hithpael participle mitrappes (from raphas) means "prostrating/humbling oneself" (compare Proverbs 6:3) or "trampling down," and the Masoretic text reads the Piel perfect bizzar ("He has scattered") rather than an imperative.
