## Text

**38:1** For the music director, for Jeduthun. A psalm of David.

**38:2** I said, "I will keep watch over my ways so as not to sin with my tongue; I will keep a muzzle on my mouth while a wicked man is before me."

**38:3** I was mute in stillness; I kept silent from good, and my pain was stirred up.

**38:4** My heart grew hot inside me; in my musing, a fire blazed; I spoke with my tongue:

**38:5** "Make known to me, LORD, my end, and what the measure of my days is; may I know how transient I am.

**38:6** Look, You have made my days handbreadths, and my lifespan is like nothing before You; surely every person standing firm is altogether a breath. Selah.

**38:7** Surely as a shadow a man walks about; surely for a breath they are in commotion; he heaps things up and does not know who will gather them.

**38:8** And now, what am I waiting for, Lord? My expectation is in You.

**38:9** Rescue me from all my transgressions; do not make me the reproach of a fool.

**38:10** I am mute; I will not open my mouth, because You Yourself have acted.

**38:11** Take Your blow away from me; by the assault of Your hand I am spent.

**38:12** With rebukes for iniquity You discipline a man, and like a moth You dissolve what he treasures; surely every person is a breath. Selah.

**38:13** Hear my prayer, LORD, and listen closely to my cry for help; do not remain silent at my tears, for I am a sojourner with You, a temporary resident like all my fathers.

**38:14** Look away from me so that I may brighten up, before I go away and am no more."

## Translator Notes

- **38:1** — Ketiv reads "Jedithun" while Qere reads "Jeduthun", a Levitical music leader under David; the translation follows the Qere.
- **38:3** — The Hebrew phrase "kept silent from good" is terse, conveying either refraining even from speaking good words or keeping silent with no good result.
- **38:11** — The Hebrew noun translated "assault" occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and derives from a root denoting hostility or striking.
- **38:14** — The imperative translated "Look away" is a shortened form associated with turning the gaze away, followed by a Hiphil cohortative meaning "brighten up" or "recover cheerfulness."
