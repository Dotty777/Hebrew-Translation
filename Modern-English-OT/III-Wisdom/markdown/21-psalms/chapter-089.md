## Text

**89:1** A prayer of Moses, the man of God. Lord, You have been a dwelling place for us from generation to generation.

**89:2** Before the mountains were born and You brought the earth and the world to birth, from eternity to eternity You are God.

**89:3** You turn humanity back to dust, and You say, "Turn back, sons of man."

**89:4** For a thousand years in Your eyes are like yesterday once it has passed, and like a watch in the night.

**89:5** You sweep them away in a torrent; they become like sleep, in the morning like grass that grows anew.

**89:6** In the morning it blossoms and grows anew; by evening it wilts and dries up.

**89:7** For we come to an end in Your anger, and in Your fury we are terrified.

**89:8** You have placed our iniquities in front of You, our hidden wrongdoing in the light of Your face.

**89:9** For all our days decline in Your wrath; we bring our years to a close like a sigh.

**89:10** The days of our years amount to seventy years, and if through strength, eighty years; yet their boast is toil and sorrow, for it passes swiftly and we fly away.

**89:11** Who knows the strength of Your anger, and Your wrath corresponding to the fear due You?

**89:12** Thus teach us to count our days, so that we may acquire a heart of wisdom.

**89:13** Turn back, LORD! How long? And relent toward Your servants.

**89:14** Satisfy us in the morning with Your loyal love, so that we may shout for joy and be glad throughout all our days.

**89:15** Make us glad in proportion to the days You have afflicted us, the years we have seen calamity.

**89:16** May Your work appear to Your servants, and Your splendor upon their children.

**89:17** And may the favor of the Lord our God be upon us; establish for us the work of our hands, and the work of our hands, establish it.

## Translator Notes

- **89:2** — MT vocalizes the verb as Polel 2ms active ("You brought ... to birth") from the root chul (to writhe in labor or give birth), taking "earth and world" as its direct object; some ancient versions reflect a passive reading ("were brought forth").
- **89:5** — The Qal verb chalaph ("grows anew") here and in 89:6 (MT 90:6) can mean either "sprout anew / renew itself" or "pass away"; the morning-versus-evening contrast in 89:6 (MT 90:6) supports the sense of fresh growth.
- **89:10** — The Hebrew noun rohab ("boast" or "pride") occurs only here in the Old Testament and denotes what is proudest or best in a human lifespan.
- **89:11** — The second clause is concise in Hebrew and may be read either as a second object of "Who knows" or as an independent statement: "as the fear due You is, so is Your wrath."
