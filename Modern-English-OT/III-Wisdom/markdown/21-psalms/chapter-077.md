## Text

**77:1** A Maskil of Asaph. Listen closely, my people, to my instruction; incline your ear to the sayings of my mouth.

**77:2** With a parable I will open my mouth; I will pour forth riddles from ancient times,

**77:3** things we have heard and come to know, and that our fathers have recounted to us.

**77:4** We will not conceal them from their children, recounting to a future generation the praises of the LORD, and His might, and His wondrous deeds that He has done.

**77:5** He established a testimony in Jacob and placed a law in Israel, which He commanded our fathers to make known to their children,

**77:6** so that a future generation would know them, children yet to be born, and would arise and recount them to their children,

**77:7** and place their confidence in God, and not forget the acts of God, but observe His commandments,

**77:8** and not be like their fathers, a stubborn and rebellious generation, a generation that did not make its heart steadfast and whose spirit was not faithful with God.

**77:9** The sons of Ephraim, armed and shooting with the bow, turned back on the day of battle.

**77:10** They did not keep the covenant of God, and in His law they refused to walk.

**77:11** And they forgot His acts and His wondrous deeds that He had shown them.

**77:12** In the presence of their fathers He worked wonders in the land of Egypt, the field of Zoan.

**77:13** He split the sea and brought them across, and He caused the waters to stand like a heap.

**77:14** He guided them with the cloud by day, and all through the night with the light of fire.

**77:15** He split open rocks in the wilderness and gave them drink as from the great depths.

**77:16** He brought out flowing streams from the crag and made waters run down like rivers.

**77:17** Yet they continued even more to sin against Him, rebelling against the Most High in the dry land.

**77:18** And they tested God in their heart by asking for food for their appetite.

**77:19** And they spoke against God; they said, "Is God able to set a table in the wilderness?

**77:20** Look, He struck the rock and waters gushed out, and torrents overflowed; is He able to give bread as well, or will He prepare meat for His people?"

**77:21** Therefore the LORD heard and grew furious, so a fire was kindled against Jacob, and anger also rose against Israel,

**77:22** because they did not believe in God, nor did they trust in His deliverance.

**77:23** Nevertheless, He commanded the clouds on high, and He opened the doors of heaven;

**77:24** He rained down manna on them to eat, and the grain of heaven He gave them.

**77:25** Man ate the bread of the mighty ones; He sent them provisions to the full.

**77:26** He sent forth the east wind in the heavens, and by His might He drove on the south wind.

**77:27** And He rained down on them meat like dust, and winged birds like the sand of the seas;

**77:28** and He caused them to fall in the middle of their camp, all around their dwellings.

**77:29** So they ate and were thoroughly satisfied, and their craving He brought to them.

**77:30** They had not turned away from their craving, and their food was still in their mouths,

**77:31** when the anger of God rose against them, and He killed some of their sturdiest ones and brought low the young men of Israel.

**77:32** Despite all this, they sinned still more and did not believe in His wondrous deeds.

**77:33** So He ended their days in emptiness, and their years in terror.

**77:34** Whenever He killed them, they would seek Him; they would turn back and eagerly seek God.

**77:35** Then they remembered that God was their rock, and God Most High was their redeemer.

**77:36** Yet they flattered Him with their mouths, and with their tongues they lied to Him.

**77:37** And their heart was not steadfast with Him, and they were not faithful in His covenant.

**77:38** Yet He was compassionate; He pardoned iniquity and did not destroy them, and many times He turned back His anger and did not rouse all His wrath.

**77:39** And He remembered that they were flesh, a wind that goes away and does not return.

**77:40** How many times they rebelled against Him in the wilderness; they grieved Him in the wasteland!

**77:41** Again they tested God, and they caused pain to the Holy One of Israel.

**77:42** They did not remember His hand, the day when He ransomed them from the adversary,

**77:43** when He set His signs in Egypt and His portents in the field of Zoan.

**77:44** And He turned their canals into blood, and their flowing streams they could not drink.

**77:45** He sent against them swarms of flies that devoured them, and frogs that destroyed them.

**77:46** He gave their produce to the devouring locust, and their labor to the locust.

**77:47** He killed their vines with hail, and their sycamore trees with frost.

**77:48** He handed over their cattle to the hail, and their herds to lightning bolts.

**77:49** He sent against them the fierceness of His anger: fury, indignation, and distress, a troop of messengers of disaster.

**77:50** He made a level path for His anger; He did not hold back their soul from death, and their life He handed over to the plague.

**77:51** And He struck every firstborn in Egypt, the firstfruits of vigor in the tents of Ham.

**77:52** Then He brought forth His people like sheep and led them along like a flock in the wilderness.

**77:53** He guided them in safety, and they did not fear, while the sea covered their enemies.

**77:54** And He brought them to His holy territory, this mountain that His right hand acquired.

**77:55** He drove out nations from before them, allotted them by a measuring line as an inheritance, and caused the tribes of Israel to dwell in their tents.

**77:56** Yet they tested and rebelled against God Most High, and His testimonies they did not keep.

**77:57** They drew back and dealt treacherously like their fathers; they twisted aside like a deceitful bow.

**77:58** And they angered Him with their high places, and with their carved images they stirred Him to jealousy.

**77:59** God heard and grew furious, and He completely rejected Israel.

**77:60** He abandoned the tabernacle of Shiloh, the tent He had set up among humankind.

**77:61** He gave His might into captivity, and His splendor into the hand of the adversary.

**77:62** And He handed over His people to the sword, and with His inheritance He grew furious.

**77:63** Fire consumed their young men, and their young women were not celebrated with wedding songs.

**77:64** Their priests fell by the sword, and their widows did not weep.

**77:65** Then the Lord awoke like one asleep, like a warrior shouting aloud from wine.

**77:66** He struck His adversaries backward; He gave them everlasting disgrace.

**77:67** And He rejected the tent of Joseph, and the tribe of Ephraim He did not choose;

**77:68** rather, He chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion, which He loved.

**77:69** He built His sanctuary like the heights, like the earth that He established forever.

**77:70** And He chose His servant David and took him from the sheep pens;

**77:71** from behind the nursing ewes He brought him, to shepherd His people Jacob and His inheritance Israel.

**77:72** And he shepherded them according to the integrity of his heart, and with the skill of his hands he guided them.

## Translator Notes

- **77:9** — The Hebrew phrase uses two successive Qal construct participles (noshqei romei-qashet, literally "armed ones, shooters of the bow") to describe equipped archers.
- **77:15** — Hebrew pairs the plural noun tehomot ("depths") with the feminine singular adjective rabbah ("great" or "abundant"), functioning either as a poetic collective ("the great depths") or adverbially ("abundantly, like the depths").
- **77:25** — Literally "bread of mighty ones" (lechem abbirim), referring poetically to heavenly beings; the Septuagint interprets the phrase as "bread of angels."
- **77:28** — Hebrew uses masculine singular possessive suffixes (literally "its/his camp" and "its/his dwellings") despite plural pronouns in verses 27 and 29; the singular refers collectively to Israel, though some read it as referring to God.
- **77:41** — The rare Hiphil verb hitwu is commonly derived from a cognate root meaning "to cause pain" or "provoke," paralleling "grieved Him" in verse 40; others connect it to Hebrew tawah ("to mark") and render "set limits on."
- **77:47** — The Hebrew word chanamal occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible; ancient versions and the parallel with "hail" support translating it as "frost" or "sleet."
- **77:63** — The Pual verb hullalu literally means "were praised," referring idiomatically to bridal wedding songs; here and in verse 64, the Hebrew masculine singular possessive suffixes refer collectively to "His people" in verse 62.
- **77:69** — Hebrew kemo-ramim literally means "like the heights" (referring to the lofty heavens), whereas the Septuagint translates a similar-looking word meaning "wild oxen."
