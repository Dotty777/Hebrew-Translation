## Text

**94:1** Come, let us sing with joy to the LORD; let us shout in triumph to the rock of our salvation.

**94:2** Let us approach His presence with thanksgiving; with songs let us shout in triumph to Him.

**94:3** For a great God is the LORD, and a great King over all gods.

**94:4** In His hand are the deep recesses of the earth, and the summits of the mountains belong to Him.

**94:5** His is the sea, and He Himself made it; and the dry land His hands shaped.

**94:6** Come, let us bow down and bend low; let us kneel before the LORD who made us.

**94:7** For He is our God, and we are the people of His pasture and the flock of His hand. Today, if only you would listen to His voice!

**94:8** "Do not harden your heart as at Meribah, like the day of Massah in the wilderness,

**94:9** where your forefathers tested Me, put Me to the proof, even though they saw My work.

**94:10** For forty years I was disgusted with that generation, and I said, 'They are a people wandering in heart, and they have not known My ways.'

**94:11** So I swore in My anger, 'They will certainly not come into My rest.'"

## Translator Notes

- **94:4** — "Deep recesses" translates the hapax legomenon mechqerey (from chaqar, "search out, fathom"), and "summits" translates the rare plural toafot, whose exact etymology is debated but whose meaning "heights/peaks" is clear from the merism with the earth's depths.
- **94:7** — The clause "if only you would listen to His voice" reflects the Hebrew optative use of im ("if") without a stated consequence, expressing an urgent wish that introduces God's direct words in vv. 8-11.
- **94:8** — The place names Meribah ("Quarreling") and Massah ("Testing") recall Israel's rebellion in the wilderness (Exod 17:7; Num 20:13), with Massah echoed by the verb "tested" (nissuni, from nasah) in v. 9.
- **94:10** — The Hebrew literally reads "with a generation" (bedor) without a definite article or demonstrative, understood contextually in Hebrew poetry as "that generation."
- **94:11** — "They will certainly not come" renders the emphatic Hebrew negative oath formula im followed by an imperfect verb with paragogic nun (yevo'un), literally "if they enter."
