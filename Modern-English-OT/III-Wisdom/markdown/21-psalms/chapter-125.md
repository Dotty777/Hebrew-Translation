## Text

**125:1** A song of the ascents. When the LORD restored Zion's fortunes, we were like dreamers.

**125:2** Then our mouth was filled with laughter, and our tongue with jubilant shouting; then they said among the nations, "The LORD has done great deeds for these people."

**125:3** The LORD has done great deeds for us; we were glad.

**125:4** LORD, restore our fortunes, like watercourses in the Negev.

**125:5** Those who sow in tears will reap with jubilant shouting.

**125:6** The one who walks along weeping, carrying the bag of seed, will surely come in with jubilant shouting, carrying his sheaves.

## Translator Notes

- **125:1** — The noun shivat ("fortunes/restoration") is a hapax legomenon from the root shuv ("return, turn back"), parallel to the idiom shuv shevut/shevit ("restore fortunes" or "bring back captivity").
- **125:4** — The Qere (read form) is shevitenu and the Ketiv (written form) is shevutenu; both belong to the Hebrew idiom shuv shevut/shevit ("restore fortunes" or "turn back captivity"). The noun afiqim refers to watercourses or seasonal streambeds in the arid Negev that fill rapidly after rain.
- **125:6** — The phrase meshekh-hazzara ("bag of seed") literally denotes a "drawing" or "pouch" of seed (often linked to the leather pouch carried while sowing, or to the trail of scattered seed; cf. Amos 9:13). Both clauses use the Hebrew infinitive absolute construction (halokh yelekh uvakho and bo-yavo) to convey continuous weeping while walking and certainty of coming in with joy.
