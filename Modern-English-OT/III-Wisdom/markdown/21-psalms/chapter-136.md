## Text

**136:1** Beside the rivers of Babylon, there we sat and also wept as we remembered Zion.

**136:2** On the poplars in her midst, we hung our lyres.

**136:3** For there our captors asked us for words of song, and our tormentors for joy: "Sing for us from the songs of Zion!"

**136:4** How can we sing the song of the LORD on foreign soil?

**136:5** If I forget you, Jerusalem, may my right hand forget.

**136:6** May my tongue cling to my palate if I do not remember you, if I do not lift Jerusalem above the peak of my joy.

**136:7** Remember, LORD, against the sons of Edom the day of Jerusalem, those who said, "Strip her bare, strip her bare, down to her foundation!"

**136:8** Daughter of Babylon, the devastated one, happy is the one who pays you back the treatment you dealt to us.

**136:9** Happy is the one who seizes and smashes your infants against the rock.

## Translator Notes

- **136:2** — Hebrew 'aravim refers to Euphrates poplars, and kinnorot specifically denotes stringed lyres.
- **136:3** — The noun tolalenu ("our tormentors") occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible; scholars debate whether it derives from yalal ("those who make us wail") or shalal ("our plunderers").
- **136:5** — The Masoretic text reads tishkach (Qal imperfect or jussive of shachach, "forget") with no stated object, repeating the verb from the first clause and implying that the musician's right hand would forget its skill.
- **136:8** — Hebrew hashshdudah (Qal passive participle of shadad, "devastate") literally means "the devastated one," used proleptically of Babylon's assured ruin.
