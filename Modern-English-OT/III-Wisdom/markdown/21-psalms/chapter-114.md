## Text

**114:1** I love that the LORD hears my voice, my pleas for grace.

**114:2** Because He has bent His ear to me, throughout my days I will call.

**114:3** The cords of death surrounded me, and the straits of Sheol found me; distress and grief I found.

**114:4** And I called on the name of the LORD: "Please, LORD, rescue my soul!"

**114:5** Gracious and righteous is the LORD, and our God shows compassion.

**114:6** The LORD guards the simple; I sank low, and He saved me.

**114:7** Return, my soul, to your rest, for the LORD has dealt generously with you.

**114:8** For You have freed my soul from death, my eye from tears, my foot from falling.

**114:9** I will walk about before the LORD in the lands of the living.

## Translator Notes

- **114:1** — Hebrew has no explicit direct object after the verb 'I love,' taking the following clause either as its content ('that the LORD hears') or causally with an implied object ('I love Him, because the LORD hears').
- **114:3** — The verse pairs repeated Hebrew roots for poetic effect: 'straits' and 'distress' share the root meaning narrowness or confinement, and the verb 'found' (Qal of matsa) recurs in both halves.
- **114:6** — The verb 'saved' (yehoshia) is an unusual uncontracted Hiphil imperfect of yasha that retains the characteristic he prefix; 'I sank low' renders the stative Qal of dalal ('become low or depleted').
- **114:7** — Both 'your rest' (plural of abstraction) and 'with you' display the poetic, Aramaic-influenced feminine singular suffix -ayki addressing the psalmist's soul.
- **114:9** — The verb 'walk about' renders the Hithpael of halak, conveying continual or free movement, and the Hebrew noun 'lands' (artsot) is plural rather than singular.
