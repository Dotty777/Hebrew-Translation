## Text

**72:1** A psalm of Asaph. Indeed, God is good to Israel, to those pure of heart.

**72:2** Yet as for me, my feet had almost given way; my steps had all but slipped.

**72:3** For I envied the boastful, seeing the prosperity of the wicked.

**72:4** For there are no pangs at their death, and their body is plump.

**72:5** They are not in the trouble of mortals, nor are they stricken along with mankind.

**72:6** Therefore pride encircles them like a necklace; violence wraps them as a garment.

**72:7** Their eyes protrude from fatness; the imaginations of their heart overflow.

**72:8** They scoff and maliciously speak of oppression; from on high they speak.

**72:9** They set their mouth in the heavens, and their tongue walks across the earth.

**72:10** Therefore His people return here, and waters of fullness are drained by them.

**72:11** And they say, "How does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High?"

**72:12** Look, these are the wicked, and forever carefree, they have increased their wealth.

**72:13** Indeed, in vain I have purified my heart and washed my hands in innocence.

**72:14** And I have been stricken all day long, and my chastisement comes every morning.

**72:15** If I had said, "I will speak like this," then I would have dealt treacherously with the generation of Your children.

**72:16** So when I pondered how to know this, it was trouble in my eyes,

**72:17** until I entered the sanctuaries of God; then I understood their end.

**72:18** Indeed, You set them on slippery ground; You cause them to fall into ruins.

**72:19** How they become a desolation in an instant! They are swept away, wholly consumed by terrors!

**72:20** Like a dream upon waking, Lord, when You stir Yourself, You will despise their image.

**72:21** When my heart grew embittered and I was pierced in my inward parts,

**72:22** then I was senseless and did not know; I was a brute beast with You.

**72:23** Yet I am continually with You; You have grasped my right hand.

**72:24** By Your counsel You will lead me, and afterward You will take me to glory.

**72:25** Whom do I have in heaven? And alongside You, I desire nothing on earth.

**72:26** My flesh and my heart waste away, yet God is the rock of my heart and my portion forever.

**72:27** For look, those far from You will perish; You wipe out all who prove unfaithful to You.

**72:28** But as for me, the nearness of God is good for me; I have set my refuge in the Lord GOD, to recount all Your works.

## Translator Notes

- **72:2** — Two Ketiv/Qere variants occur here: Qere natayu (Qal perfect plural of natah, "had turned aside") vs. Ketiv ntwy (singular passive participle), and Qere shuppeku (Pual perfect plural of shafak, literally "were poured out," i.e., "slipped") vs. Ketiv shfkh (feminine singular Pual shuppekah agreeing collectively with "my steps").
- **72:4** — The Masoretic Text reads lemotam ("at their death"), alongside two rare nouns: hartsubbot ("pangs" or "bonds") and 'ulam ("their body" or "strength"). The consonants can also be divided as lamo tam ("for them; sound [is their body]"), which departs from the Masoretic division.
- **72:10** — Qere yashuv (Qal imperfect of shuv, "return") is translated here; Ketiv yshyb represents the Hiphil yashiv ("he brings back"). The second clause literally reads "and waters of fullness are drained by them," an idiom whose sense is debated (either drinking deeply of prosperity or draining a full cup of bitterness).
- **72:16** — Qere hu' (masculine singular pronoun agreeing with masculine 'amal, "trouble") vs. Ketiv hy' (feminine singular hi', agreeing with feminine zo't, "this").
- **72:20** — Hebrew ba'ir is generally understood as a contracted Hiphil infinitive construct of 'ur (for beha'ir, "when You stir/arouse Yourself," parallel to "upon waking"), though the same consonantal and vocalized form can literally mean "in the city."
