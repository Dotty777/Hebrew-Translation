## Text

**40:1** For the music director. A psalm of David.

**40:2** Blessed is the one who is attentive to the weak; in a day of calamity the LORD will deliver him.

**40:3** The LORD will guard him and preserve his life, and he will be pronounced blessed in the land; and do not hand him over to the desire of his enemies.

**40:4** The LORD will uphold him on a bed of illness; You transform his entire bed in his sickness.

**40:5** I myself said, "LORD, show me grace; heal my soul, because I have sinned against You."

**40:6** My enemies speak evil concerning me: "When will he die and his name vanish?"

**40:7** And if one comes to visit, he speaks empty words; his heart collects wickedness for itself; he goes outside and speaks.

**40:8** Together all who hate me whisper against me; against me they scheme evil for me:

**40:9** "A ruinous thing has been poured into him; and now that he has lain down, he will not rise again."

**40:10** Even my close companion whom I trusted, the one who ate my bread, has raised his heel high against me.

**40:11** But You, LORD, show me grace and raise me up, so that I may pay them back.

**40:12** From this I know that You take delight in me, because my enemy does not raise a triumphant shout over me.

**40:13** And as for me, in my integrity You have held me fast, and You have stationed me before You forever.

**40:14** Blessed is the LORD, God of Israel, from eternity to eternity. Amen and Amen.

## Translator Notes

- **40:3** — Ketiv reads an imperfect passive form ("he will be called blessed"), whereas Qere reads a Pual weqatal ("and he will be pronounced blessed"); the translation follows the Qere. The clause "do not hand him over" abruptly shifts to second-person address to God, and "desire" literally translates Hebrew "soul/appetite."
- **40:4** — Literally "all his bed You turn/transform in his sickness", an idiom denoting either tending a sick person's bed or transforming their illness into recovery, again shifting to second-person address to God.
- **40:9** — The Hebrew phrase "a ruinous thing" (literally "a thing of worthlessness/destruction") is paired with the Qal passive participle "poured into him", picturing a lethal affliction or curse fixed upon the sufferer.
- **40:10** — "My close companion" renders the Hebrew idiom "the man of my peace", denoting a trusted ally, and "has raised his heel high" literally renders the Hiphil verb "has magnified the heel", an image of treacherous betrayal.
