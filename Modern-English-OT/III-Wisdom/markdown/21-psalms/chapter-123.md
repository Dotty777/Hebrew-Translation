## Text

**123:1** A song of the ascents, of David. "Had it not been the LORD who was for us," now let Israel say,

**123:2** "had it not been the LORD who was for us when people rose against us,

**123:3** then they would have swallowed us alive when their wrath burned against us;

**123:4** then the waters would have swept us away, a torrent would have passed over our soul;

**123:5** then the seething waters would have passed over our soul."

**123:6** Blessed is the LORD, who did not give us over as prey to their teeth.

**123:7** Our soul escaped like a bird from the snare of trappers; the snare was broken, and we escaped.

**123:8** Our help lies in the name of the LORD, Maker of heaven and earth.

## Translator Notes

- **123:3** — Azay is an emphatic poetic form of az ("then") attested only in vv. 3-5 of this psalm. The phrase "when their wrath burned" translates the active Qal infinitive construct of charah ("burn, grow hot").
- **123:4** — Nachlah appends an archaic poetic -ah ending to the masculine noun nachal ("torrent, wadi"), as confirmed by the masculine singular verb avar ("passed"). In drowning imagery, "our soul" (nephesh) can also evoke the throat or life itself.
- **123:5** — Hazzeydonim ("the seething") is a hapax legomenon from the root zid ("boil up, seethe; act insolently"), depicting turbulent, surging waters.
