## Text

**20:1** For the music director. A psalm of David.

**20:2** LORD, in Your strength the king is glad, and in Your deliverance how exceedingly he exults!

**20:3** The longing of his heart You have given to him, and the request of his lips You have not withheld. Selah.

**20:4** For You come to meet him with blessings of goodness; You place on his head a crown of fine gold.

**20:5** Life he asked from You; You gave him length of days forever and ever.

**20:6** Great is his glory in Your deliverance; majesty and splendor You lay on him.

**20:7** For You make him blessings forever; You gladden him with joy in Your presence.

**20:8** For the king trusts in the LORD, and by the loyal love of the Most High he will not be shaken.

**20:9** Your hand will find all your enemies; your right hand will find those who hate you.

**20:10** You will make them like a furnace of fire at the time of your appearing; the LORD in His anger will swallow them up, and fire will devour them.

**20:11** Their fruit you will destroy from the earth, and their seed from among the sons of man.

**20:12** Though they directed evil against you and devised a scheme, they will not prevail.

**20:13** For you will cause them to turn their backs; with your bowstrings you will take aim at their faces.

**20:14** Be exalted, LORD, in Your strength! We will sing and make music to Your might.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — Ketiv/Qere: the written ketiv has the full Qal imperfect form yagil, while the read qere has the shortened form yagel (both from gil, "exult/rejoice," with identical sense in this exclamation).
- **20:7** — The Hebrew literally reads "You make him blessings forever" (Qal imperfect of shit with double accusative), an idiom signifying both that the king is richly blessed and that he serves as an enduring channel of blessing.
- **20:9** — Verses 9-13 shift to address the king directly (hence lowercase "you/your"), as indicated by the third-person reference to the LORD in v. 10 and the renewed direct address to the LORD in v. 14; some interpreters instead read these verses as addressed to the LORD.
- **20:13** — The clause literally reads "you will set them [as] a shoulder/back" (shekhem), an idiom for putting enemies to flight. The Polel imperfect tekhonen (from kun) denotes making ready or aiming arrows upon the bowstrings.
