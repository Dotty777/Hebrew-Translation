## Text

**85:1** A prayer of David. Bend Your ear, LORD, answer me, for I am afflicted and needy.

**85:2** Guard my soul, for I am loyal; You are my God—save Your servant who trusts in You.

**85:3** Be gracious to me, Lord, for to You I call all day long.

**85:4** Bring gladness to the soul of Your servant, for to You, Lord, I lift my soul.

**85:5** For You, Lord, are good and forgiving, and abundant in loyal love to all who call on You.

**85:6** Give ear to my prayer, LORD, and pay attention to the voice of my pleas for grace.

**85:7** On the day of my distress I call to You, for You will answer me.

**85:8** No one among the gods is like You, Lord, and nothing is like Your deeds.

**85:9** All nations that You have made will come and bow down before You, Lord, and honor Your name.

**85:10** For You are great and perform wonders; You are God alone.

**85:11** Instruct me, LORD, in Your way; I will walk in Your truth. Unify my heart to fear Your name.

**85:12** I will thank You, Lord my God, with all my heart, and I will honor Your name forever.

**85:13** For Your loyal love is great toward me, and You have rescued my soul from lowest Sheol.

**85:14** God, insolent men have risen against me, and a company of ruthless men have sought my life, and they have not placed You before them.

**85:15** But You, Lord, are a God compassionate and gracious, slow to anger and abundant in loyal love and truth.

**85:16** Turn toward me and be gracious to me; grant Your strength to Your servant, and save the son of Your female servant.

**85:17** Perform a sign for good on my behalf, so that those who hate me may see and be shamed, because You, LORD, have helped me and consoled me.

## Translator Notes

- **85:5** — The adjective sallach ("forgiving") uses an intensive pattern from salach ("forgive") and appears only here in the Hebrew Bible, denoting an abiding disposition to forgive.
- **85:11** — The verb yached ("unify") is a Piel imperative of yachad ("be one, unite"), asking for an undivided heart, whereas the Septuagint reads a form of chadah ("rejoice").
- **85:13** — Literally "lowest Sheol," using the feminine adjective tachtiyyah with feminine Sheol to denote the deepest realm of the dead.
- **85:17** — Literally "perform with me a sign for good," reflecting the Hebrew idiom asah im ("act on behalf of, deal with").
