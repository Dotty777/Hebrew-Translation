## Text

**4:1** For the music leader, on stringed instruments. A psalm of David.

**4:2** When I call, answer me, God of my righteousness; in distress You have made room for me. Show me favor and hear my prayer.

**4:3** Sons of men, how long will my glory be turned to disgrace? How long will you love emptiness and seek falsehood? Selah.

**4:4** And know that the LORD has set apart a faithful one for Himself; the LORD will hear when I call to Him.

**4:5** Tremble and do not sin; speak in your heart on your bed, and keep silent. Selah.

**4:6** Offer sacrifices of righteousness, and place your trust in the LORD.

**4:7** Many say, "Who will let us see good?" Lift up the light of Your face over us, LORD.

**4:8** You have placed joy in my heart, more than at the time when their grain and their new wine multiply.

**4:9** In peace I will at once lie down and sleep, for You alone, LORD, cause me to dwell in security.

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The verb hiflah (Hiphil of palah) means to set apart or distinguish, highlighting God's special regard for the chasid (faithful one, one bound in covenant loyalty).
- **4:5** — Rigzu (Qal imperative of ragaz) primarily means tremble or be agitated (rendered be angry in the Septuagint); speak in your heart is a Hebrew idiom for silent inward reflection.
- **4:7** — Nesah is an unusual Qal imperative form of nasa (lift up), spelled with samekh instead of sin and echoing the priestly blessing of Numbers 6:25-26.
- **4:9** — Levahdad (alone) is linked by the Masoretic atnah accent with You, LORD, though syntactically it may also modify the psalmist's dwelling (cause me to dwell alone in security).
