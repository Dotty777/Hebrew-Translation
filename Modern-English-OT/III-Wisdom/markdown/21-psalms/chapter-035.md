## Text

**35:1** For the music director. Of the servant of the LORD, of David.

**35:2** An oracle of transgression concerning the wicked is within my heart: there is no dread of God before his eyes.

**35:3** For he flatters himself in his own eyes regarding his iniquity being discovered and hated.

**35:4** The words of his mouth are wrongdoing and deceit; he has stopped acting wisely, doing good.

**35:5** He plans wrongdoing on his bed; he takes his stand on a way that is not good; he does not spurn evil.

**35:6** LORD, Your loyal love is in the heavens; Your faithfulness reaches up to the skies.

**35:7** Your righteousness is like God's mountains; Your judgments are a vast deep; mankind and animals You deliver, LORD.

**35:8** How precious is Your loyal love, God! And the sons of mankind seek refuge in the shadow of Your wings.

**35:9** They have their fill from the richness of Your house, and You let them drink from the stream of Your delights.

**35:10** For with You is the wellspring of life; by Your light we see light.

**35:11** Extend Your loyal love to those who know You, and Your righteousness to those upright of heart.

**35:12** Do not let the foot of arrogance come against me, nor the hand of the wicked send me wandering.

**35:13** There the workers of wrongdoing fell; they were thrust down and could not rise.

## Translator Notes

- **35:2** — The Masoretic Text reads "within my heart" (libbi), indicating that deep within himself the psalmist perceives an oracle (neum, normally a prophetic term) of transgression concerning the wicked; some ancient versions read "his heart."
- **35:3** — Literally "for he makes smooth to himself in his eyes, to find his iniquity, to hate," a compressed clause expressing how the wicked smoothly reassures himself concerning his guilt being detected and hated, or flatters himself until it is exposed.
- **35:7** — The construct phrase harrei-el ("God's mountains") serves in Hebrew both literally and as a superlative idiom for towering, mighty mountains.
