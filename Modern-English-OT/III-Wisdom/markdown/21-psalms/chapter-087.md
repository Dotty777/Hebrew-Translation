## Text

**87:1** A song, a psalm of the sons of Korah. For the music director, set to Mahalath Leannoth. A Maskil of Heman the Ezrahite.

**87:2** LORD, God of my salvation, by day I have cried out, and at night before You.

**87:3** May my prayer come into Your presence; turn Your ear to my outcry.

**87:4** For my soul has had its fill of calamities, and my life has reached Sheol.

**87:5** I am numbered with those going down to the pit; I have become like a man with no strength,

**87:6** turned loose among the dead, like those killed who lie in the grave, whom You no longer remember, and they have been severed from Your hand.

**87:7** You have placed me in the lowest pit, in dark regions, in the deeps.

**87:8** Upon me Your wrath has pressed heavily, and with all Your breakers You have afflicted me. Selah.

**87:9** You have put my acquaintances far from me; You have made me an utter abomination to them. I am confined and cannot go out.

**87:10** My eye languishes from affliction; I have called to You, LORD, every day; I have spread out my palms to You.

**87:11** Will You work a wonder for the dead? Or will the shades rise up and praise You? Selah.

**87:12** Will Your loyal love be recounted in the grave, or Your faithfulness in Abaddon?

**87:13** Will Your wonder become known in the darkness, and Your righteousness in a land of forgetfulness?

**87:14** But I have cried to You for help, LORD, and in the morning my prayer comes to meet You.

**87:15** Why, LORD, do You spurn my soul and hide Your face from me?

**87:16** I am afflicted and perishing from youth; I have borne Your terrors; I am numb.

**87:17** Over me Your fierce anger has swept; Your horrors have annihilated me.

**87:18** Like water they have surrounded me all day long; they have closed around me as one.

**87:19** You have put loved one and companion far from me; my acquaintances are darkness.

## Translator Notes

- **87:1** — Mahalath Leannoth transliterates Hebrew machalat leannot, a musical or liturgical heading of uncertain sense (possibly a tune title or direction meaning "for singing responsively" or "concerning affliction"); Maskil transliterates maskil (Hiphil participle of skl), a psalm genre term often taken as a contemplative or skillful poem.
- **87:6** — Hebrew chophshi ("turned loose") normally denotes a freed slave released from service, used here ironically of being cut loose among the dead beyond God's active care.
- **87:8** — The Hebrew clause omits both "with" and "me," literally reading "and all Your breakers You have afflicted" (Piel perfect of anah II), understood instrumentally as "with all Your breakers You have afflicted me."
- **87:16** — Hebrew aphunah ("I am numb") is a biblical hapax legomenon, usually parsed as a cohortative Qal imperfect of pun with an uncertain meaning variously rendered "numb," "helpless," or "at a loss."
- **87:19** — Hebrew meyudda'ay machshak is a stark verbless clause literally reading "my acquaintances, darkness," conveying either that darkness is the psalmist's only remaining company or that his acquaintances have vanished into darkness.
