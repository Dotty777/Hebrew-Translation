## Text

**2:1** Why are the nations in tumult, and why do the peoples plot emptiness?

**2:2** The kings of the earth station themselves, and the rulers confer together against the LORD and against His anointed one.

**2:3** "Let us snap their bonds and fling their ropes away from us!"

**2:4** The One seated in the heavens laughs; the Lord mocks them.

**2:5** Then He will speak to them in His anger, and in His burning wrath He will terrify them:

**2:6** "Yet I Myself have established My king on Zion, My holy mountain."

**2:7** I will recount the decree: the LORD said to me, "You are My son; today I Myself have fathered you.

**2:8** Ask Me, and I will give the nations as your inheritance, and as your possession the ends of the earth.

**2:9** You will smash them with an iron rod; like a potter's vessel you will shatter them."

**2:10** And now, kings, act wisely; accept correction, judges of the earth.

**2:11** Serve the LORD with reverence, and exult with trembling.

**2:12** Kiss the son, or He will grow angry and you will perish along the path, for His anger may soon blaze up. Blessed are all who seek refuge in Him.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Qal verb rendered "are in tumult" occurs only here in Biblical Hebrew, denoting restless commotion or thronging. "Plot" uses the same verbal root (hagah) translated "meditates" in 1:2, here taking the noun "emptiness" as its direct object.
- **2:7** — Following the Masoretic accentuation, the pause falls after "decree" (preceded by the preposition el, "concerning"), making "the LORD" the subject of "said"; some instead construe "the decree of the LORD: He said to me." "Have fathered you" translates the Qal perfect of yalad, here expressing royal adoption at enthronement.
- **2:9** — The Masoretic vocalization reads this Qal verb from the root ra'a ("break, smash"), matching the parallel Piel verb "shatter"; the Septuagint instead read the same consonants from the root "shepherd/rule."
- **2:12** — The phrase "Kiss the son" uses the Piel imperative of "kiss" (a gesture of royal homage) with bar, widely taken as the poetic or Aramaic word for "son" (cf. Proverbs 31:2, echoing 2:7), though some understand it from Hebrew bar ("purely" or "in purity"). "Along the path" renders an adverbial accusative echoing 1:6.
