## Text

**12:1** For the music director. A psalm of David.

**12:2** How long, LORD, will You forget me forever? How long will You conceal Your face from me?

**12:3** How long must I set counsels in my soul, with grief in my heart daily? How long will my enemy rise high over me?

**12:4** Look, answer me, LORD my God; brighten my eyes, so that I do not sleep in death,

**12:5** so that my enemy does not say, "I have overpowered him," nor my adversaries exult if I am shaken.

**12:6** Yet I have trusted in Your loyal love; my heart will exult in Your deliverance. I will sing to the LORD, for He has dealt generously with me.

## Translator Notes

- **12:2** — The juxtaposition of ad-anah ("how long") with netzach ("forever" or "perpetually") creates a deliberate lament paradox expressing feelings of endless abandonment.
- **12:3** — Hebrew ashit etzot benafshi ("set counsels in my soul") depicts anxious inward deliberation. The adverb yomam means "by day" or "daily," with no mention of night in the Masoretic Text.
- **12:5** — Hebrew yecholtiv combines the 1st-person Qal perfect of yachol ("be able, prevail") directly with a 3rd-person singular object suffix, an idiom meaning "I have overpowered him."
- **12:6** — The verb yagel is a shortened Qal jussive form of gil ("exult"), paired with the 1st-person cohortative ashirah ("I will sing"), while gamal alai idiomatically denotes dealing generously or graciously with someone.
