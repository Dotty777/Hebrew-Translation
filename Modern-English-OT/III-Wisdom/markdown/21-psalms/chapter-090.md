## Text

**90:1** The one who dwells in the hiding place of the Most High will lodge in the shadow of the Almighty.

**90:2** I say to the LORD, "My refuge and my stronghold, my God; in Him I will trust."

**90:3** For He Himself will rescue you from the snare of a fowler, from devastating pestilence.

**90:4** With His pinions He will shelter you, and beneath His wings you will take refuge; His faithfulness is a large shield and rampart.

**90:5** You will not fear the dread of night, nor an arrow that flies by day,

**90:6** nor pestilence that walks in thick darkness, nor a scourge that devastates at midday.

**90:7** A thousand may fall by your side, and ten thousand at your right hand, yet it will not draw near to you.

**90:8** Only with your eyes will you look on, and you will see the repayment of the wicked.

**90:9** For You, LORD, are my refuge; you have made the Most High your dwelling place.

**90:10** No calamity will befall you, and no plague will approach your tent.

**90:11** For He will command His angels on your behalf, to watch over you in all your ways.

**90:12** Upon their palms they will lift you up, so that you do not strike your foot on a stone.

**90:13** Upon the lion and the cobra you will tread; you will trample the young lion and the serpent.

**90:14** "Because he has set his affection on Me, I will deliver him; I will set him high in safety, because he knows My name.

**90:15** He will call to Me, and I will answer him; I am with him in distress; I will free him and honor him.

**90:16** With length of days I will satisfy him, and I will let him look upon My salvation."

## Translator Notes

- **90:2** — MT reads Qal imperfect 1cs ("I say"), whereas the Septuagint reflects a participle ("saying") linking smoothly to the third-person subject of 90:1.
- **90:4** — The noun socherah ("rampart") is a Hebrew hapax legomenon derived from the root sachar ("to surround"), signifying a protective surrounding wall or buckler.
- **90:9** — Following the Masoretic accentuation, the verse shifts from the psalmist's first-person confession ("For You, LORD, are my refuge") to second-person reassurance ("you have made the Most High your dwelling place"); some alternatively take the first "you" as the believer, reading "Because you have made the LORD, my refuge, the Most High, your dwelling place."
- **90:14** — God speaks in the first person in 90:14-16; the Piel verb sagab ("set high in safety") carries the spatial image of lifting someone onto an inaccessible height beyond reach of harm.
