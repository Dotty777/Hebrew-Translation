## Text

**80:1** For the music director, according to the Gittith. Of Asaph.

**80:2** Sing joyfully to God our strength; shout in triumph to the God of Jacob.

**80:3** Lift up a song and strike the tambourine, the pleasant lyre with the harp.

**80:4** Blow the ram's horn at the new moon, at the full moon, for the day of our festival.

**80:5** For it is a statute for Israel, a ruling of the God of Jacob.

**80:6** He set it as a testimony in Joseph when He went out against the land of Egypt. I hear a language I did not know:

**80:7** "I removed his shoulder from the burden; his hands were released from the basket.

**80:8** In distress you called out, and I rescued you; I answered you in the cover of thunder; I tested you at the waters of Meribah. Selah.

**80:9** Listen, My people, and let Me admonish you; Israel, if only you would listen to Me!

**80:10** There must be no strange god among you, and you must not bow down to a foreign god.

**80:11** I am the LORD your God, the One who brought you up from the land of Egypt. Open your mouth wide, that I may fill it.

**80:12** Yet My people did not listen to My voice, and Israel would not yield to Me.

**80:13** So I let them go in the stubbornness of their heart, to walk in their own counsels.

**80:14** If only My people were listening to Me, if Israel would walk in My ways!

**80:15** In a moment I would subdue their enemies, and against their adversaries I would turn back My hand.

**80:16** Those who hate the LORD would feign obedience to Him, and their time would last forever.

**80:17** And He would feed him with the choicest wheat, and from the rock I would satisfy you with honey."

## Translator Notes

- **80:1** — The musical or liturgical term ha-gittit ("the Gittith") is uncertain in origin, possibly denoting a melody or instrument linked to Gath or to winepresses (gat).
- **80:6** — The name Joseph is spelled uniquely here as Yehosef. The suffix on be-tseto ("when he/He went out") is ambiguous and can refer either to God marching out against Egypt at the Exodus (cf. Exod 11:4) or to Joseph going out over Egypt (cf. Gen 41:45).
- **80:16** — The Piel verb yekhachashu with le- literally means to act deceptively toward someone, hence to feign obedience or offer reluctant submission; Hebrew ittam is literally "their time," leaving open whether it denotes the enemies' lasting doom or Israel's enduring era.
- **80:17** — The Masoretic Text has the third-person masculine singular Hiphil verb and suffix wayya'akhilehu ("and He would feed him") before shifting back to first- and second-person singular in asbi'ekha ("I would satisfy you"), a poetic person shift characteristic of prophetic speech; "choicest wheat" is literally "fat of wheat" (chelev chittah).
