## Text

**34:1** Of David. Strive, LORD, with those who strive against me; battle those who battle against me.

**34:2** Grasp shield and large shield, and rise to my aid.

**34:3** Draw out the spear and bar the way to meet my pursuers; tell my soul, "I am your deliverance."

**34:4** May those who seek my life be ashamed and disgraced; may those who plan my harm be turned backward and humiliated.

**34:5** May they become like chaff before the wind, while the angel of the LORD thrusts them away.

**34:6** May their way be darkness and slippery places, while the angel of the LORD pursues them.

**34:7** For without cause they hid their net for me in a pit; without cause they dug for my life.

**34:8** May devastation come upon him when he does not know, and may his net that he hid trap him; in devastation may he fall into it.

**34:9** And my soul will exult in the LORD; it will be glad in His deliverance.

**34:10** All my bones will say, "LORD, who is like You, rescuing the afflicted from someone stronger than he, and the afflicted and the needy from the one who plunders him?"

**34:11** Witnesses of violence rise up; they question me concerning what I do not know.

**34:12** They repay me with evil in place of good—bereavement to my soul.

**34:13** Yet as for me, when they were ill, sackcloth was my garment; I afflicted my soul with fasting, and my prayer would return to my chest.

**34:14** As for a friend, as for a brother of mine, I walked about; like one mourning a mother, I bowed low in gloom.

**34:15** Yet at my stumbling they rejoiced and assembled; wretches assembled against me without my knowing; they tore at me and did not cease.

**34:16** As godless mockers for a cake, they gnashed their teeth at me.

**34:17** Lord, how long will You watch? Bring back my soul from their devastations, my only one from young lions.

**34:18** I will give thanks to You in a great assembly; among a mighty people I will praise You.

**34:19** Do not let those who are falsely my enemies rejoice over me, nor let those who hate me without cause wink an eye.

**34:20** For it is not peace that they speak; rather, against the quiet ones of the land they plan words of deceit.

**34:21** And they widened their mouths against me; they said, "Aha! Aha! Our own eyes have seen!"

**34:22** You have seen, LORD; do not remain silent. Lord, do not stay far from me.

**34:23** Rouse Yourself and wake up for my justice; my God and my Lord, for my case.

**34:24** Judge me in accordance with Your righteousness, LORD my God, and do not let them rejoice over me.

**34:25** May they not say in their hearts, "Aha, our longing!" May they not say, "We have swallowed him down!"

**34:26** May those who rejoice at my harm be ashamed and humiliated together; may those who make themselves great against me wear shame and disgrace.

**34:27** May those who delight in my righteousness shout for joy and rejoice; and may they say continually, "May the LORD be great, who delights in the peace of His servant!"

**34:28** And my tongue will utter Your righteousness—Your praise all day long.

## Translator Notes

- **34:3** — The word segor is pointed in the Masoretic Text as a Qal imperative of sagar ("shut" or "bar [the way]"), though some lexicons understand it as a noun denoting a weapon such as a javelin or battle-axe.
- **34:7** — In the Masoretic Text, shachat rishtam ("pit of their net" or "their net in a pit") stands before the midpoint pause, leaving the object ("a pit") implied with the verb chafru ("they dug") in the second clause.
- **34:13** — The idiom "my prayer would return to my chest" may describe either praying with head bowed low onto the chest in grief or a prayer of blessing returning to the one who offered it.
- **34:15** — The rare plural noun nekim ("wretches") derives from nakah ("strike") and is understood either passively as abject outcasts or actively as assailants.
- **34:16** — The obscure phrase bechanfei laagei maog (literally "as godless ones of mockers of a cake") is widely taken to refer to table-parasites who deride others for a scrap of bread; charoq ("gnashed") is a Qal infinitive absolute functioning as a finite verb.
