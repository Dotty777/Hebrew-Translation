## Text

**61:1** For the music director, according to Jeduthun. A psalm of David.

**61:2** Only toward God is my soul in stillness; from Him is my salvation.

**61:3** Only He is my rock and my salvation, my high fortress; I will not be severely shaken.

**61:4** How long will you assail a man? Will you batter him, all of you, like a leaning wall, a toppling fence?

**61:5** Only from his eminence do they plot to cast him down; they delight in falsehood. With their mouth they bless, yet in their inward parts they curse. Selah.

**61:6** Only for God be still, my soul, for from Him is my hope.

**61:7** Only He is my rock and my salvation, my high fortress; I will not be shaken.

**61:8** Upon God rest my salvation and my glory; the rock of my strength, my refuge, is in God.

**61:9** Trust in Him at every time, people; pour out your heart in His presence; God is a refuge to us. Selah.

**61:10** Only a breath are the sons of mankind, a falsehood are the sons of man; on the scales they rise; all together they are less than a breath.

**61:11** Do not trust in extortion, and in robbery do not put empty hope; when wealth flourishes, do not fix your heart on it.

**61:12** Once God spoke; twice I heard this: that strength belongs to God,

**61:13** and to You, Lord, belongs loyal love, for You Yourself repay each one according to his deed.

## Translator Notes

- **61:2** — The noun dumiyyah ('stillness, silence') stands in deliberate contrast with the Qal feminine singular imperative dommi ('be still!') in 61:6 (MT 62:6). The particle akh ('only') recurs six times in this psalm (verses 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10) as a unifying refrain.
- **61:4** — The Poel verb tehotetu ('assail') is a biblical hapax legomenon. The translation follows the vocalization of the critical text, teratsechu, a Piel active ('you batter/murder'); an alternate vocalization tradition (Ben Naphtali) reads a Pual passive ('you will be slain'). The simile of the leaning wall describes the man under attack.
- **61:5** — Hebrew befiv literally reads 'with his mouth' (a distributive singular suffix paired with the plural verb 'they bless'), meaning each adversary blesses with his own mouth.
- **61:10** — The parallel idioms 'sons of mankind' (bene-adam) and 'sons of man' (bene-ish) encompass all humanity, from the lowly to the prominent. The final clause uses comparative min elliptically with hevel ('less than a breath,' i.e., lighter than a puff of air).
