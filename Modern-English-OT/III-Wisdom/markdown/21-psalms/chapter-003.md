## Text

**3:1** A psalm of David, when he was fleeing from his son Absalom.

**3:2** LORD, how my foes have multiplied! Many rise against me.

**3:3** Many say about my soul, "He has no deliverance in God." Selah.

**3:4** Yet You, LORD, are a shield around me, my glory, and the One who raises my head.

**3:5** With my voice I call out to the LORD, and He answers me from His holy mountain. Selah.

**3:6** As for me, I lay down and slept; I woke up, because the LORD upholds me.

**3:7** I will not fear tens of thousands of people who have positioned themselves against me on every side.

**3:8** Rise up, LORD! Deliver me, my God! For You have struck all my enemies on the jaw; the teeth of the wicked You have shattered.

**3:9** To the LORD belongs deliverance; may Your blessing be upon Your people. Selah.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Following the Masoretic Hebrew versification, the psalm superscription is numbered as verse 1, so verses 2-9 correspond to verses 1-8 in many English editions.
- **3:3** — Hebrew yeshu'atah is a poetic lengthened form of "deliverance" (yeshu'ah), the same noun that recurs in 3:9. "Selah" (here and in 3:5, 3:9) is a transliterated liturgical or musical term of uncertain meaning.
- **3:8** — The Hiphil perfect "have struck" and Piel perfect "have shattered" follow the opening imperatives to ground the appeal either in God's past acts of rescue or in confident assurance of deliverance.
