## Text

**78:1** A psalm of Asaph. God, nations have entered Your inheritance; they have defiled the temple of Your holiness; they have made Jerusalem into heaps of ruins.

**78:2** They have given the corpses of Your servants as food to the birds of the sky, the flesh of Your faithful ones to the beasts of the earth.

**78:3** They have poured out their blood like water around Jerusalem, and there is no one to bury them.

**78:4** We have become a reproach to our neighbors, mockery and ridicule to those around us.

**78:5** How long, LORD? Will You remain angry forever? Will Your jealousy blaze like fire?

**78:6** Pour out Your fury on the nations that do not know You, and on kingdoms that do not call on Your name.

**78:7** For they have devoured Jacob and made his dwelling place desolate.

**78:8** Do not remember against us former iniquities; let Your mercies come quickly to meet us, for we have sunk very low.

**78:9** Help us, God of our salvation, on account of the glory of Your name; rescue us and make atonement for our sins for the sake of Your name.

**78:10** Why should the nations say, "Where is their God?" Let vengeance for the blood of Your servants that has been poured out be made known among the nations before our eyes.

**78:11** May the groaning of the prisoner come before You; according to the greatness of Your arm, spare those condemned to die.

**78:12** And pay back to our neighbors sevenfold into their lap their reproach, with which they reproached You, Lord.

**78:13** And we, Your people and the flock of Your pasture, will give thanks to You forever; from generation to generation we will declare Your praise.

## Translator Notes

- **78:2** — The phrase rendered beasts of the earth uses the archaic poetic construct ending -o on chayto (chayto-arets), literally "living creature of the earth," employed collectively for wild animals.
- **78:7** — The Masoretic Text has the collective third-person masculine singular Qal perfect verb akhal (literally "he has devoured") before shifting to the third-person plural Hiphil heshammu ("they have made desolate").
- **78:8** — The phrase avonot rishonim can be read either attributively as "former iniquities" (since avon is grammatically masculine despite its -ot plural ending) or as a construct chain meaning "iniquities of ancestors."
- **78:11** — Hebrew hoter is a Hiphil imperative of yatar ("cause to remain," hence "spare"), and bene temutah is literally "sons of death," an idiom for people condemned to die.
