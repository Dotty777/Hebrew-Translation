## Text

**91:1** A psalm, a song for the day of the Sabbath.

**91:2** It is good to give thanks to the LORD, and to make music to Your name, Most High,

**91:3** to declare Your loyal love in the morning, and Your faithfulness in the nights,

**91:4** with a ten-stringed instrument and with the harp, with resonant melody on the lyre.

**91:5** For You have made me glad, LORD, through Your work; at the works of Your hands I will shout for joy.

**91:6** How great Your works are, LORD! Exceedingly deep are Your thoughts!

**91:7** A senseless man does not know, and a fool does not understand this:

**91:8** that when the wicked flourish like grass and all workers of iniquity blossom, it is for them to be destroyed forever.

**91:9** Yet You are on high forever, LORD.

**91:10** For look, Your enemies, LORD, for look, Your enemies will perish; all workers of iniquity will be dispersed.

**91:11** Yet You have raised my horn like that of a wild ox; I have been drenched with fresh oil.

**91:12** And my eye has looked upon those lying in wait for me; my ears will hear of the wicked who rise against me.

**91:13** The righteous one will flourish like a palm tree; like a cedar in Lebanon he will grow tall.

**91:14** Planted in the house of the LORD, in the courts of our God they will flourish.

**91:15** They will still produce fruit in old age; they will be rich with sap and fresh,

**91:16** to declare that the LORD is upright; He is my rock, and in Him there is no injustice.

## Translator Notes

- **91:4** — The noun higgayon ("resonant melody") comes from the root hagah ("murmur, resound, meditate") and here denotes solemn or resonant instrumental sound on the lyre.
- **91:11** — MT vocalizes balloti as Qal perfect 1cs of balal ("mix, pour over, moisten"), yielding an intransitive or passive sense ("I have been drenched"); some modern versions emend to 2ms ("You have poured over me").
- **91:16** — The qere reads awlatah (plene spelling with medial vav, a poetic feminine form of awlah, "injustice/unrighteousness"), whereas the ketiv writes the same word defectively without the vav.
