## Text

**12:1** And remember your Creator in the days of your young manhood, before the days of adversity come and years arrive about which you will say, "I take no delight in them";

**12:2** before the sun, the light, the moon, and the stars turn dark, and the clouds come back after the rain;

**12:3** on the day when the guards of the house tremble, and the men of strength become stooped, and the grinding women fall idle because they have grown few, and those peering through the windows grow dark,

**12:4** and the double doors onto the street are closed when the sound of the mill drops low, and one rises at the sound of the bird, and all the daughters of song sink down.

**12:5** Moreover, they fear heights, and terrors are along the road; the almond tree blooms, the grasshopper drags itself heavily, and the caperberry fails, because man is going to his everlasting home, and the mourners walk around in the street.

**12:6** Before the cord of silver is snapped, and the bowl of gold is crushed, and a jar is broken at the spring, and the wheel is crushed into the cistern,

**12:7** and the dust goes back to the earth just as it was, and the spirit goes back to God, who gave it.

**12:8** "Vapor of vapors," said the Convener, "everything is vapor."

**12:9** And beyond being wise, the Convener also taught knowledge to the people; he weighed, investigated, and set in order many proverbs.

**12:10** The Convener sought to find words of delight, and written in uprightness were words of truth.

**12:11** Words of wise men are like goads, and like driven nails are the masters of collections; they were given from one Shepherd.

**12:12** And beyond these, my son, be warned: there is no end to making many books, and much study brings weariness to the flesh.

**12:13** The conclusion of the matter; everything has been heard. Fear God and observe His commandments, because this is the whole of man.

**12:14** For God will bring every deed to judgment, together with everything concealed, whether good or evil.

## Translator Notes

- **12:5** — Hebrew yane'ts is a Hiphil imperfect meaning 'blossoms' (spelled with an orthographic aleph), and ha-'aviyyonah (occurring only here) denotes the caperberry, an ancient stimulant, though some take it as 'desire' by connection with the root meaning 'to desire.'
- **12:6** — The written ketiv reads yirchaq ('is removed' or 'grows distant'), while the read qere followed here reads yerateq (Niphal imperfect, 'is snapped' or 'severed').
- **12:8** — Hebrew hevel literally means 'vapor' or 'breath,' used metaphorically for transience or futility; ha-Qoheleth ('the Convener') is a title derived from the root qhl ('to assemble').
- **12:10** — The Masoretic Text vocalizes katuv as a Qal passive participle ('written'), rather than an active finite verb ('wrote').
- **12:11** — Hebrew ba'ale asuppot literally means 'masters of collections,' referring either to compilers/sages of wisdom assemblies or idiomatically to 'collected sayings.'
