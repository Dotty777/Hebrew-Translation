## Text

**7:1** A good name is better than choice oil, and the day of death than the day of one's birth.

**7:2** Going to a house of mourning is better than going to a house of feasting, because that is the end of every person, and the living will take it to heart.

**7:3** Grief is better than laughter, because through a sad face the heart grows better.

**7:4** The heart of the wise is in a house of mourning, while the heart of fools is in a house of gladness.

**7:5** Listening to the rebuke of a wise person is better than being someone who listens to the song of fools.

**7:6** For like the sound of thorns under the pot, so is the laughter of the fool; and this too is futility.

**7:7** For extortion makes a fool of a wise person, and a bribe ruins the heart.

**7:8** Better is the end of a matter than its beginning; better is one who is patient in spirit than one who is haughty in spirit.

**7:9** Do not be hasty in your spirit to become angry, because anger rests in the bosom of fools.

**7:10** Do not say, "How is it that the earlier days were better than these?" for you do not ask about this out of wisdom.

**7:11** Wisdom is good alongside an inheritance, and it is an advantage for those who see the sun.

**7:12** For to be in the shelter of wisdom is like being in the shelter of money, yet the advantage of knowledge is that wisdom keeps its possessors alive.

**7:13** Look at the work of God, for who can straighten what He has made crooked?

**7:14** On a day of prosperity be glad, and on a day of adversity reflect: God has made the one alongside the other, so that man cannot find out anything after him.

**7:15** I have seen all things in the days of my futility: there is a righteous person who perishes in his righteousness, and there is a wicked person who prolongs his days in his evil.

**7:16** Do not be righteous to excess, and do not make yourself overly wise; why should you bring ruin on yourself?

**7:17** Do not act wickedly to excess, and do not be foolish; why die before your time?

**7:18** It is good that you take hold of this, and also do not let your hand drop from that, for the one who fears God will come through all of them.

**7:19** Wisdom gives greater strength to a wise person than ten rulers who are in a city.

**7:20** For there is no righteous person on earth who does good and does not sin.

**7:21** Also, do not take to heart all the words that people speak, so that you will not hear your servant cursing you.

**7:22** For many times your heart has known that you yourself have cursed others as well.

**7:23** All this I tested with wisdom; I said, "I will become wise," yet it was far from me.

**7:24** What has been is far away, and deep, so deep—who can find it?

**7:25** I turned, together with my heart, to know and to explore and to seek wisdom and a reckoning, and to know that wickedness is folly and that foolishness is madness.

**7:26** And I find more bitter than death the woman whose heart is traps and nets, whose hands are fetters; one who is good before God will escape from her, but a sinner will be caught by her.

**7:27** "See, this is what I have found," said the Preacher, "putting one thing with another to find a reckoning,

**7:28** which my soul has still sought, yet I have not found: one man out of a thousand I have found, yet a woman among all these I have not found.

**7:29** Only look, this I have found: that God made mankind upright, yet they themselves have sought many schemes."

## Translator Notes

- **7:12** — The Hebrew wording is compressed, literally "for in the shadow/shelter of wisdom, in the shadow/shelter of silver," using repeated prepositions to draw a parallel between the protection offered by wisdom and money.
- **7:18** — The idiom "will come through all of them" literally reads "will go out with/from all of them" (Qal imperfect of yatsa with the direct object marker), meaning either to emerge safely from both extremes or to fulfill both obligations.
- **7:22** — The Ketiv (written text) has the defective form of the second-person pronoun while the Qere (read text) supplies the standard masculine singular form "you"; both convey the same meaning.
- **7:27** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads the feminine singular perfect verb "said" to agree with the grammatical feminine form of the title Qohelet (literally "Assembler" or "Speaker," traditionally rendered "the Preacher").
