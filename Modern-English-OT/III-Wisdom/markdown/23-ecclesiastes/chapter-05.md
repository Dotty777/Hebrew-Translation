## Text

**5:1** Do not be hasty with your mouth, and do not let your heart hurry to bring forth a word before God; for God is in heaven, and you are on the earth, so let your words be few.

**5:2** For a dream comes through an abundance of preoccupation, and a fool's voice through an abundance of words.

**5:3** When you vow a vow to God, do not delay in paying it, for there is no delight in fools. What you vow, pay.

**5:4** Better that you not vow than that you vow and not pay.

**5:5** Do not allow your mouth to cause your flesh to sin, and do not say before the messenger, "It was an error." Why should God grow angry at your voice and ruin the work of your hands?

**5:6** For in an abundance of dreams there are also futilities, and words in abundance; rather, fear God.

**5:7** If you see oppression of the poor and the plundering of justice and righteousness in the province, do not be astonished at the matter; for one high official watches above another high official, and high ones are over them.

**5:8** Yet the profit of a land in every respect is this: a king for a tilled field.

**5:9** One who loves silver will not be satisfied with silver, nor whoever loves abundance with revenue. This too is futility.

**5:10** When goods multiply, those who consume them multiply; and what benefit is there for their owner except looking on with his eyes?

**5:11** The worker's sleep is sweet, whether he eats little or much; yet the fullness of the rich man does not permit him to sleep.

**5:12** There is a sickening evil I have seen under the sun: wealth kept by its owner to his own harm.

**5:13** And that wealth perishes in a bad business, and he fathers a son, yet there is nothing in his hand.

**5:14** Just as he came out of his mother's womb, naked he will depart again as he came, and he will take nothing from his toil that he may carry along in his hand.

**5:15** And this too is a sickening evil: in every way as he came, so he will go; and what profit is there for him that he toils for the wind?

**5:16** Moreover, all his days he eats in darkness, and he is greatly vexed, with his sickness and anger.

**5:17** Look, what I myself have seen is good and fitting: to eat and drink and see good in all his toil at which he toils under the sun during the numbered days of his life that God has given him, for that is his portion.

**5:18** Furthermore, as for every person to whom God has given wealth and possessions and granted power to eat from it and to receive his portion and to rejoice in his toil—this is a gift of God.

**5:19** For he will not often remember the days of his life, because God occupies him with the gladness of his heart.

## Translator Notes

- **5:6** — The Masoretic Hebrew is notably terse ("For in an abundance of dreams and futilities and words in abundance"), with the conjunction on "futilities" introducing the predicate; the second ki is adversative ("rather").
- **5:8** — The Qere reads masculine hu' (agreeing with masculine yitron, "profit"), while the Ketiv reads feminine hi' (agreeing with feminine 'erets, "land"). The concise closing phrase melekh lesadeh ne'evad is difficult: the Niphal participle ne'evad may modify "field" ("a king for a tilled field") or "king" ("a king is served by a field").
- **5:10** — The Qere reads re'ut ("looking, seeing"), a late Hebrew feminine noun form, while the Ketiv spells the rarer synonymous form re'it.
- **5:16** — The Masoretic text vocalizes vekha'as as a Qal perfect verb ("and he is vexed") modified by the adverb "greatly," followed by the abrupt nominal phrase "and his sickness and anger."
- **5:19** — The Hiphil participle ma'aneh derives from 'anah III ("to be occupied," cognate with Ecclesiastes' recurring noun 'inyan, "business/occupation"), hence "occupies him"; some derive it from 'anah I ("to answer"), yielding "answers him with the gladness of his heart."
