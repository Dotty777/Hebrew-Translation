## Text

**1:1** The words of the Convener, son of David, king in Jerusalem.

**1:2** "Vapor of vapors," says the Convener, "vapor of vapors! Everything is vapor."

**1:3** What advantage is there for man in all his toil at which he toils under the sun?

**1:4** One generation goes and another generation comes, yet the earth stands forever.

**1:5** The sun rises and the sun sets, and to its place it pants, rising there.

**1:6** Going toward the south and turning toward the north, turning and turning goes the wind, and upon its rounds the wind returns.

**1:7** All the streams go into the sea, yet the sea is not full; to the place where the streams go, there they return to go.

**1:8** All things are wearying; no one is able to speak of them. No eye is satisfied by seeing, and no ear is filled from hearing.

**1:9** What has been, that is what will be, and what has been done, that is what will be done; and there is not anything new under the sun.

**1:10** Is there a matter of which someone says, "Look, this is new"? It has already been in the ages that were before us.

**1:11** There is no remembrance of the former ones, and likewise for the later ones that will be, there will be no remembrance of them among those who will come afterward.

**1:12** I, the Convener, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.

**1:13** And I set my heart to inquire and to explore by wisdom concerning everything that is done under the heavens. It is a wretched task that God has given to the sons of man to be occupied with.

**1:14** I have seen all the deeds that are done under the sun, and look, everything is vapor and chasing wind.

**1:15** What is bent cannot become straight, and what is missing cannot be numbered.

**1:16** I spoke with my heart, saying, "Look, I have enlarged and increased wisdom beyond all who were before me over Jerusalem, and my heart has seen much wisdom and knowledge."

**1:17** And I set my heart to know wisdom and to know madness and folly; I came to know that this too is a striving for wind.

**1:18** For in an abundance of wisdom is an abundance of vexation, and one who adds knowledge adds pain.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Hebrew Qoheleth is a feminine singular Qal participle of qahal ("to assemble, convene"), used as an official title for one who convenes or addresses an assembly (traditionally rendered "the Preacher" or "the Teacher").
- **1:2** — Hebrew hevel literally means "vapor" or "breath," used throughout the book for what is fleeting, ungraspable, and empty (often rendered "vanity" or "futility"); "vapor of vapors" is a Hebrew superlative construction.
- **1:13** — Hebrew la'anot bo (Qal infinitive construct of 'anah, "to be occupied with," cognate with 'inyan, "task") may also play on the homonymous root 'anah ("to be afflicted, humbled"), conveying a burdensome occupation.
- **1:14** — Hebrew re'ut ruach derives either from a late Hebrew/Aramaic root meaning "striving, pursuit" ("chasing wind") or from ra'ah ("to shepherd, herd," as in Hosea 12:1, MT 12:2), depicting a futile attempt to capture the wind.
- **1:17** — Hebrew sikluth ("folly") is here spelled with sin rather than samekh (contrast 2:3, 12, 13), an orthographic variant that also creates a subtle visual echo of sekel ("insight, prudence").
