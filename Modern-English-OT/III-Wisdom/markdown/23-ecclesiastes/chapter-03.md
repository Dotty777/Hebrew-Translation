## Text

**3:1** For everything there is an appointed season, and a time for every affair under the heavens:

**3:2** a time to give birth and a time to die; a time to plant and a time to pull up what has been planted;

**3:3** a time to kill and a time to heal; a time to break down and a time to build;

**3:4** a time to weep and a time to laugh; a time to lament and a time to dance;

**3:5** a time to cast stones and a time to collect stones; a time to embrace and a time to keep far from embracing;

**3:6** a time to search and a time to lose; a time to keep and a time to cast aside;

**3:7** a time to tear and a time to sew; a time to remain silent and a time to speak;

**3:8** a time to love and a time to hate; a time of war and a time of peace.

**3:9** What advantage belongs to the worker in that at which he toils?

**3:10** I have seen the task that God has given to the sons of man to be occupied with.

**3:11** He has made everything fitting in its time; He has also placed eternity in their heart, yet so that man cannot discover the work that God has done from beginning even to end.

**3:12** I know that there is nothing better for them than to be glad and to do good during one's life.

**3:13** Moreover, that every man should eat and drink and see good in all his toil—that is a gift of God.

**3:14** I know that all that God does will endure forever; to it nothing can be added, and from it nothing can be subtracted. And God has done this so that people should fear before Him.

**3:15** What has been already is, and what is to be has already been; and God seeks out what is pursued.

**3:16** And further I saw under the sun: in the place of justice, there was wickedness; and in the place of righteousness, there was wickedness.

**3:17** I said in my heart, "God will judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every affair and for every deed there."

**3:18** I said in my heart concerning the sons of man, "It is for God to test them, and for them to see that they are beasts in themselves."

**3:19** For the fate of the sons of man and the fate of the beast—one fate belongs to them: as the death of the one, so is the death of the other, and all have one breath. And man has no advantage over the beast, for everything is vapor.

**3:20** All are going to one place; all came into being from the dust, and all are returning to the dust.

**3:21** Who knows whether the spirit of the sons of man ascends upward and whether the spirit of the beast descends downward to the earth?

**3:22** So I saw that there is nothing better than that a man should be glad in his works, for that is his portion. For who will bring him to see what will be after him?

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — Hebrew laledet is the Qal active infinitive construct of yalad, literally "to give birth," though often rendered passively as "to be born" (contrast the Niphal form for being born in 7:1).
- **3:11** — Hebrew yafeh means "beautiful" or "fitting"; ha'olam ("eternity" or "perpetuity") denotes a sense of timeless duration beyond human grasp (some also connect it with later senses of "the world" or "obscurity").
- **3:15** — Hebrew nirdaf is a Niphal participle of radaf ("to pursue"), literally "what is pursued" or "what is driven away"; it is variously understood of past events that God brings back again or of the persecuted person whom God seeks out to vindicate (linking to 3:16-17).
- **3:17** — The Masoretic text ends with sham ("there," echoing shammah in 3:16 and referring to the sphere of God's judgment); some scholars repoint the consonants as sam ("He has appointed").
- **3:21** — The Masoretic vocalization of ha'olah and hayyoredet matches the definite article ("that ascends... that descends"), though the following pronoun hi ("it") and comparable Masoretic interrogative vocalizations (e.g., Lev 10:19; Num 16:22) support the interrogative sense "whether it ascends... whether it descends."
