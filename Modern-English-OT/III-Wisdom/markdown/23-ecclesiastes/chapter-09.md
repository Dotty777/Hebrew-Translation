## Text

**9:1** For all this I took to my heart, examining all of it: that the righteous and the wise and their deeds are in the hand of God. Whether it is love or hatred, man does not know; everything lies before them.

**9:2** Everything is the same for all: there is one fate for the righteous and for the wicked, for the good, and for the clean and the unclean, for the one who sacrifices and for the one who does not sacrifice. As the good person is, so is the sinner; the one who swears an oath is like the one who fears an oath.

**9:3** This is an evil in everything that is done under the sun: that one fate belongs to all. Moreover, the heart of the sons of man is full of evil, and madness is in their hearts while they live, and afterward—to the dead.

**9:4** For anyone who is joined to all the living has confidence, because a living dog is better than a dead lion.

**9:5** For the living know that they will die, yet the dead know nothing at all; they no longer have any reward, because their memory has been forgotten.

**9:6** Their love, along with their hatred and their envy, has already perished, and they no longer have any portion forever in all that is done under the sun.

**9:7** Go, eat your bread in gladness, and drink your wine with a cheerful heart, because God has already accepted your works.

**9:8** At all times let your clothes be white, and never let oil be missing from your head.

**9:9** Enjoy life with the wife you love throughout all the days of your life of vapor that He has given you under the sun—all your days of vapor—for that is your portion in life and in your toil as you toil under the sun.

**9:10** Everything your hand finds to do, do with your strength, because there is no work or reckoning or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol, where you are going.

**9:11** Once more I saw under the sun that the race does not belong to the swift, nor the battle to the mighty, nor bread to the wise, nor wealth to those of understanding, nor favor to the knowledgeable, for time and chance happen to all of them.

**9:12** Indeed, man does not even know his time: like fish caught in a cruel net and like birds caught in a snare, like them the sons of man are ensnared at an evil time, when it suddenly falls on them.

**9:13** This too I saw as wisdom under the sun, and it appeared great to me:

**9:14** There was a small city, and the men in it were few; and a great king came against it, surrounded it, and built great siegeworks against it.

**9:15** And a poor wise man was found in it, and he rescued the city by his wisdom; still, no one remembered that poor man.

**9:16** So I said, "Wisdom is better than might, yet the wisdom of the poor man is scorned, and his words are not heard."

**9:17** Words of the wise are better heard in quietness than the outcry of a ruler among fools.

**9:18** Wisdom is better than weapons of battle, yet a single sinner ruins much good.

## Translator Notes

- **9:2** — The Masoretic Text lists only three terms in the middle series ("for the good, and for the clean and the unclean") without adding "and for the evil/bad," which some ancient versions supply for symmetry.
- **9:4** — The Qere (read text) has the Pual imperfect of chavar ("is joined"), translated here, while the Ketiv (written text) reads the root bachar ("chooses" or "is chosen").
- **9:9** — The Masoretic Text repeats the phrase "all your days of vapor" for emphasis, whereas some ancient versions omit the repetition.
- **9:15** — The opening verb is an impersonal Qal active third-person singular ("one found," idiomatically "was found"), and the Piel perfect "he rescued" can also be taken modally as "he could have rescued."
