## Text

**8:1** Who is like the wise person, and who knows the interpretation of a matter? A person's wisdom brightens his face, and the sternness of his face is transformed.

**8:2** I say: Keep a king's command, and that because of the oath of God.

**8:3** Do not rush to go from his presence, and do not persist in an evil matter, for he does all that he desires.

**8:4** For a king's word has authority, and who can say to him, "What are you doing?"

**8:5** The one who keeps a command will know nothing evil, and a wise person's heart will know time and judgment.

**8:6** For every undertaking has a time and a judgment, though man's misery weighs heavily upon him.

**8:7** For he does not know what will happen; indeed, who will tell him how it will happen?

**8:8** No man has mastery over the spirit to hold back the spirit, and there is no authority over the day of death; there is no discharge in war, nor will wickedness rescue its possessors.

**8:9** All this I have seen, and I set my heart on every deed that is done under the sun—a time when man has ruled over man to his harm.

**8:10** And so I saw wicked people buried—those who used to enter and walk away from the holy place—and they were forgotten in the city where they had done so. This too is futility.

**8:11** Because a verdict against an evil deed is not carried out swiftly, the heart of the sons of man is filled within them to do evil.

**8:12** Even though a sinner does evil a hundred times and prolongs his days, yet I know that it will go well for those who fear God, who fear before Him.

**8:13** Yet it will not go well for the wicked person, and he will not prolong his days, like a shadow, because he does not fear before God.

**8:14** There is a futility that is done on the earth: there are righteous people to whom things come according to the deeds of the wicked, and there are wicked people to whom things come according to the deeds of the righteous. I said that this too is futility.

**8:15** So I praised gladness, since there is nothing good for a person under the sun except to eat and to drink and to be glad, and this will accompany him in his toil during the days of his life that God has given him under the sun.

**8:16** When I set my heart to know wisdom and to see the task that is done on the earth—even though neither by day nor by night does one see sleep with his eyes—

**8:17** then I saw all the work of God, that man is not able to find out the work that is done under the sun. No matter how much man toils to seek it, he will not find it; and even if the wise person claims to know, he is not able to find it.

## Translator Notes

- **8:2** — The opening independent pronoun "I" stands elliptically without an explicit verb of speaking ("I [say]"), and "command" is literally "mouth" of a king.
- **8:8** — The Hebrew noun ruach can mean either "spirit" (the life-breath departing at death, matching "the day of death") or "wind" (impossible to confine).
- **8:10** — This verse has famously compressed Hebrew syntax; the Masoretic verb is a Hithpael imperfect of shakach ("were forgotten"), whereas some ancient versions reflect a similar root meaning "were praised."
- **8:17** — The rare Late Biblical Hebrew compound conjunction beshel asher (literally "in that which") functions concessively here to mean "no matter how much" or "however hard."
