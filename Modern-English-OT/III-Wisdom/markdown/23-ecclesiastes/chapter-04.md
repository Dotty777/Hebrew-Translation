## Text

**4:1** Then I turned again and saw all the oppressions that are carried out under the sun. Look, the tears of the oppressed, and there was no comforter for them; power lay in the hand of their oppressors, and there was no comforter for them.

**4:2** So I praised the dead, who have already died, above the living, who are still alive.

**4:3** Yet better than both of them is the one who has not yet come into being, who has not seen the evil work that is done under the sun.

**4:4** Then I saw all toil and all success in work, that it is a man's envy toward his neighbor. This too is vapor and chasing wind.

**4:5** The fool clasps his hands and devours his own flesh.

**4:6** Better is a palmful of rest than two handfuls of toil and chasing wind.

**4:7** Then I turned again and saw vapor under the sun.

**4:8** There is one alone without a second; indeed, he has neither son nor brother. Yet there is no end to all his toil, nor is his eye satisfied with wealth. "And for whom am I toiling and depriving my soul of good?" This too is vapor, and it is a wretched business.

**4:9** Better are two than one, since there is a good reward for them in their toil.

**4:10** For if they fall, the one will raise up his partner; yet woe to the one who falls when there is no second to raise him up.

**4:11** Moreover, if two lie down, they become warm; but for one, how can he become warm?

**4:12** And if someone overpowers the one, the two will stand against him; and a three-ply cord is not swiftly snapped.

**4:13** Better is a poor and wise youth than an aged and foolish king who no longer knows how to be warned.

**4:14** For from prison he went out to reign, even though in his kingdom he was born poor.

**4:15** I saw all the living who walk about under the sun alongside the second youth who would stand in his place.

**4:16** There is no end to all the people, to all who existed before them; yet even those who come afterward will not rejoice in him. Surely this too is vapor and striving for wind.

**4:17** Guard your foot when you go to the house of God; drawing near to listen is better than fools offering a sacrifice, for they do not realize that they are doing evil.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The Hebrew repeats the exact form ha'ashuqim in two senses—first as the abstract plural "oppressions" and second as the Qal passive participle "the oppressed"—alongside the Qal active participle "their oppressors" from the same root ('ashaq).
- **4:4** — The phrase re'ut ruach ("chasing wind") may derive from Hebrew ra'ah ("to shepherd, herd") or from an Aramaic cognate sense of "striving, longing" (see 1:14).
- **4:8** — The Qere reads singular 'eno ("his eye," matching the feminine singular verb tisba'), whereas the Ketiv has plural 'enav ("his eyes"). The Hebrew shifts abruptly without an introductory verb into the first-person question, "And for whom am I toiling...?"
- **4:16** — The clause "to all who existed before them" (literally "to all that was before them") mirrors Ecclesiastes' idiom in 1:16 and 2:9, though some take the suffix on lifnehem as resumptive, yielding "all those before whom he stood," that is, all whom he led.
- **4:17** — The Qere reads singular raglekha ("your foot"), whereas the Ketiv reads the plural form ("your feet"). The Qal infinitive absolute qarov ("drawing near") forms an elliptical comparison before the preposition min ("than"), and la'asot ra' (literally "to do evil") serves as the complement of the participle "knowing."
