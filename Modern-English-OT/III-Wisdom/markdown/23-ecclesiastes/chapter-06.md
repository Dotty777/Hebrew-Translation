## Text

**6:1** There is an evil I have seen under the sun, and it is heavy upon humanity:

**6:2** A man to whom God gives wealth, possessions, and honor, and he lacks nothing for his soul of all that he craves, yet God does not grant him power to eat from it; instead, a stranger eats it. This is futility, and it is an evil sickness.

**6:3** If a man fathers a hundred children and lives many years—however numerous the days of his years may be—yet his soul is not satisfied with good things, and he has no burial either, I say, "Better than he is a stillborn child."

**6:4** For in futility it comes, and in darkness it departs, and in darkness its name is shrouded.

**6:5** Moreover, it has neither seen nor known the sun; yet there is more rest for this one than for that one.

**6:6** And even if he lived a thousand years twice over, yet did not see good—is it not to one place that all are going?

**6:7** All a person's toil is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is not filled.

**6:8** For what advantage is there for the wise person over the fool? What is there for the poor person who knows how to walk in the presence of the living?

**6:9** Better is what the eyes see than the roving of appetite. This too is futility and herding wind.

**6:10** What has been has already been called by name, and it is known what a human being is; and he cannot contend with one who is mightier than he.

**6:11** For there are many words that multiply futility; what advantage is there for a person?

**6:12** For who knows what is good for a person in life, throughout the numbered days of his life of futility, which he spends like a shadow? For who can declare to a person what will happen after him under the sun?

## Translator Notes

- **6:7** — Hebrew nefesh ("soul, life") carries its concrete sense of "appetite" or "craving" here (paired with "his mouth") and in 6:9 ("roving of appetite"), and the Niphal verb timmale' literally means "be filled."
- **6:10** — The Qere reads shettaqqif ("one who is mightier") without the definite article, whereas the Ketiv includes the article ("the one who is mightier"). The preceding clause ("it is known what a human being is") may also be rendered "it is known that he is human."
- **6:12** — The Qal verb ya'asem (literally "he makes them," with plural suffix referring to "days") is an idiom for spending or passing time, here compared to a fleeting shadow.
