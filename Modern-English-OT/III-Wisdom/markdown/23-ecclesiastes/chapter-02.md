## Text

**2:1** I said in my heart, "Come now, let me test you with gladness, and experience what is good." Yet look, that too was vapor.

**2:2** Of laughter I said, "Madness!" and of gladness, "What does this do?"

**2:3** I explored in my heart how to refresh my flesh with wine, while my heart guided me with wisdom, and how to grasp folly, until I might see what is good for the sons of man to do under the heavens for the number of the days of their life.

**2:4** I made my works great; I built houses for myself; I planted vineyards for myself.

**2:5** I made gardens and orchards for myself, and I planted in them trees of every kind of fruit.

**2:6** I made pools of water for myself, to irrigate from them a forest sprouting trees.

**2:7** I bought male and female servants, and I had servants born in my house; I also had livestock of cattle and flocks in abundance, more than all who were before me in Jerusalem.

**2:8** I also collected for myself silver and gold, and the treasure of kings and of the provinces. I obtained for myself male and female singers, and the delights of the sons of man, concubine after concubine.

**2:9** Thus I grew great and increased beyond all who were before me in Jerusalem; moreover, my wisdom stood by me.

**2:10** And whatever my eyes asked for, I did not withhold from them; I did not keep my heart back from any gladness, for my heart was glad in all my toil, and this was my portion from all my toil.

**2:11** Then I turned toward all my works that my hands had done and toward the toil that I had toiled to do, and look, everything was vapor and chasing wind, and there was no advantage under the sun.

**2:12** And I turned to see wisdom, madness, and folly. For what can the man do who comes after the king? Only that which they have already done.

**2:13** And I saw that there is an advantage to wisdom over folly, like the advantage of light over darkness.

**2:14** The wise man has his eyes in his head, whereas the fool walks in darkness; yet I too came to know that one fate befalls all of them.

**2:15** Then I said in my heart, "Like the fate of the fool, it will befall me as well; why then have I been exceedingly wise?" And I spoke in my heart that this too is vapor.

**2:16** For there is no remembrance forever of the wise man, just as of the fool, seeing that in the coming days everything will already have been forgotten; and how the wise man dies even as the fool!

**2:17** So I hated life, because the work that is done under the sun was distressing to me, for everything is vapor and chasing wind.

**2:18** And I hated all my toil at which I am toiling under the sun, because I must leave it behind to the man who will be after me.

**2:19** And who knows whether he will be wise or foolish? Yet he will rule over all my toil at which I toiled and acted wisely under the sun. This too is vapor.

**2:20** So I turned about to make my heart despair over all the toil at which I had toiled under the sun.

**2:21** For there is a man whose toil is with wisdom, knowledge, and skill, yet to a man who did not toil at it he must give it as his portion. This too is vapor and a great evil.

**2:22** For what comes to a man in all his toil and in the striving of his heart at which he toils under the sun?

**2:23** For all his days are pains, and his task is vexation; even in the night his heart does not lie still. This too is vapor.

**2:24** There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink and let his soul see good in his toil. This too I saw, that it is from the hand of God.

**2:25** For who can eat and who can have enjoyment apart from me?

**2:26** For to a man who is good before Him, He has given wisdom, knowledge, and gladness; but to the sinner He has given the task of gathering and collecting, to give to the one who is good before God. This too is vapor and chasing wind.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — Hebrew limshokh (Qal infinitive construct of mashakh, literally "to draw, pull, sustain") with "wine" and "my flesh" idiomatically expresses refreshing, stimulating, or cheering the body with wine.
- **2:8** — Hebrew shiddah weshiddot ("a shiddah and shiddot," denoting multiplicity) occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible; its exact sense is uncertain, most commonly understood in context as "many concubines" (though rabbinic Hebrew uses shiddah for a chest or litter).
- **2:24** — The Masoretic text lacks an explicit comparative particle ("than"), reading literally "there is nothing good for a man that he should eat and drink..."; it is usually understood elliptically as "nothing better... than" (compare 3:12, 22), though some read it as "it is not in a man's own power to eat and drink."
- **2:25** — The Masoretic text reads mimmenni ("apart from me"), whereas the Septuagint and Syriac reflect mimmennu ("apart from Him"); the rare verb yachush probably means "have enjoyment" or "feel" (from a late Hebrew/Aramaic root), though the homonymous verb chush means "hasten."
