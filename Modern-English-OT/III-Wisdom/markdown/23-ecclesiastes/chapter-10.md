## Text

**10:1** Dead flies cause a perfumer's oil to stink and ferment; a little folly is heavier than wisdom and honor.

**10:2** The heart of a wise man is toward his right, and the heart of a fool is toward his left.

**10:3** And even along the road, when a fool walks, his heart is lacking, and he says to all that he is a fool.

**10:4** If the spirit of the ruler rises against you, do not give up your post, for calmness puts great offenses to rest.

**10:5** There is an evil I saw under the sun, like an error going out from before the ruler:

**10:6** Folly is placed in many lofty positions, while the wealthy sit in a low place.

**10:7** I saw slaves on horses, and officials walking like slaves on the ground.

**10:8** One who digs a pit will fall into it, and a snake will bite one who breaches a wall.

**10:9** One who quarries stones will be injured by them; one who splits logs will be put in danger by them.

**10:10** If the iron becomes dull and he has not sharpened the edge, then he must exert greater strength, yet wisdom has the advantage of bringing success.

**10:11** If the snake bites without a charm, then there is no advantage for the master of the tongue.

**10:12** The words of a wise man's mouth are grace, while the lips of a fool swallow him up.

**10:13** The start of the words of his mouth is folly, and the end of his speech is wicked madness.

**10:14** And the fool multiplies words. Man does not know what will be, and what will be after him, who can tell him?

**10:15** The toil of fools wearies him, because he does not know how to go to a city.

**10:16** Woe to you, land, whose king is a youth and whose officials eat in the morning!

**10:17** Happy are you, land, whose king is a son of nobles and whose officials eat at the right time, for strength and not for drunkenness!

**10:18** Through sheer laziness the roof beams sag, and through slackness of hands the house leaks.

**10:19** For laughter they prepare a meal, and wine makes life glad, and money answers it all.

**10:20** Even in your mind, do not curse a king, and in your bedchambers, do not curse a wealthy man; for a bird of the sky will carry the voice, and a winged creature will report the matter.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — Hebrew joins two Hiphil imperfect verbs without a conjunction (yav'ish yabbia', 'cause to stink, cause to bubble/ferment') and uses yaqar ('heavy/weighty') to describe a little folly outweighing wisdom and honor.
- **10:3** — The written ketiv reads 'when the fool' (with the definite article), while the read qere followed here reads 'when a fool' (indefinite). The final clause can alternatively mean he says concerning everyone else that he is a fool.
- **10:10** — Hebrew panim ('face') refers to the blade's cutting edge, paired with the Pilpel verb qilqal ('sharpen'). The terse closing clause uses the Hiphil infinitive of kshr ('bringing success') to state wisdom's advantage.
- **10:15** — The Hebrew shifts from plural 'fools' (ha-kesilim) to distributive singular 'him' and 'he,' and pairs the normally masculine noun 'toil' (amal) with a feminine Piel verb (teyagge'ennu).
- **10:20** — The written ketiv reads 'the wings' (with the definite article), while the read qere followed here omits the article ('wings'; literally 'a master of wings').
