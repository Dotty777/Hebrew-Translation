## Text

**11:1** Send out your bread across the surface of the waters, for you will find it when many days have passed.

**11:2** Give a share to seven, and even to eight, because you do not know what calamity will happen on the earth.

**11:3** If the clouds are filled with rain, they empty onto the earth; and if a tree falls toward the south or toward the north, in the place where the tree falls, there it will be.

**11:4** One who observes the wind will not sow, and one who gazes at the clouds will not harvest.

**11:5** Just as you do not know what the way of the wind is, nor how bones form in the womb of a pregnant woman, in the same way you do not know the work of God, who makes all things.

**11:6** In the morning sow your seed, and in the evening do not let your hand rest, because you do not know which one will succeed, this or that, or whether both of them will be equally good.

**11:7** And sweet is the light, and seeing the sun is good for the eyes.

**11:8** For if a man lives many years, he should rejoice in all of them, yet remember the days of darkness, because they will be many. All that is coming is vapor.

**11:9** Rejoice, young man, in your youth, and let your heart make you glad in the days of your young manhood; walk in the paths of your heart and in the sights of your eyes, yet know that concerning all these things God will bring you to judgment.

**11:10** And remove vexation from your heart, and put trouble away from your flesh, because youth and dark hair are vapor.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — Hebrew yehu' is a rare Aramaizing Qal imperfect form of the verb hwh ('to be'), written with a final silent aleph.
- **11:5** — Hebrew ha-ruach can mean either 'the wind' (as in 11:4) or 'the spirit/breath,' and the elliptical phrase ka'atsamim literally reads 'like the bones,' understood either as a second parallel mystery or as how the spirit enters the bones.
- **11:10** — Hebrew shacharut occurs only here and is derived either from shachor ('blackness,' referring to the dark hair of youth) or from shachar ('dawn,' i.e., the dawn of life).
