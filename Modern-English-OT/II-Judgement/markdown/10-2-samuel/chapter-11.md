## Text

**11:1** It came about at the turn of the year, at the time when kings go out, that David sent Joab and his servants with him, along with all Israel, and they devastated the sons of Ammon and besieged Rabbah. But David remained in Jerusalem.

**11:2** It happened at the time of evening that David got up from his bed and walked about on the roof of the king's house; from the roof he saw a woman bathing, and the woman was very lovely in appearance.

**11:3** David sent and made inquiry about the woman, and someone said, "Is this not Bathsheba, daughter of Eliam, wife of Uriah the Hittite?"

**11:4** Then David sent messengers and took her. She came to him, and he lay with her. Now she had been purifying herself from her uncleanness. Then she returned to her house.

**11:5** And the woman conceived; so she sent and informed David, and said, "I am pregnant."

**11:6** So David sent to Joab, "Send Uriah the Hittite to me." And Joab sent Uriah to David.

**11:7** When Uriah came to him, David inquired how Joab was faring, how the people were faring, and how the war was faring.

**11:8** Then David said to Uriah, "Go down to your house and wash your feet." So Uriah went out from the king's house, and a gift from the king went out after him.

**11:9** But Uriah lay down at the entrance of the king's house with all his lord's servants, and he did not go down to his house.

**11:10** And they reported to David, saying, "Uriah has not gone down to his house." So David said to Uriah, "Are you not coming from a journey? Why have you not gone down to your house?"

**11:11** Uriah said to David, "The ark and Israel and Judah are staying in booths, and my lord Joab and my lord's servants are encamped on the open field; should I then go to my house to eat and to drink and to lie with my wife? By your life and by the life of your soul, I will not do this thing."

**11:12** Then David said to Uriah, "Remain here today as well, and tomorrow I will send you away." So Uriah remained in Jerusalem that day and the following day.

**11:13** And David invited him, and he ate and drank in his presence, and David made him drunk. In the evening he went out to lie down on his bed with his lord's servants, but to his house he did not go down.

**11:14** It came about in the morning that David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by Uriah's hand.

**11:15** And he wrote in the letter, saying, "Place Uriah directly at the front of the fierce battle, and pull back from behind him, so that he will be struck down and die."

**11:16** And it came about, as Joab kept watch over the city, that he assigned Uriah to the place where he knew men of valor were.

**11:17** Then the men of the city came out and fought with Joab, and some of the people, from among David's servants, fell; Uriah the Hittite died as well.

**11:18** Then Joab sent and informed David of all the events of the battle.

**11:19** And he commanded the messenger, saying, "When you have finished telling all the events of the battle to the king,

**11:20** then if the king's fury rises and he says to you, 'Why did you draw near to the city to fight? Did you not know that they would shoot from atop the wall?

**11:21** Who struck down Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth? Did not a woman throw an upper millstone on him from atop the wall so that he died at Thebez? Why did you draw near to the wall?' then you shall say, 'Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead as well.'"

**11:22** So the messenger went, arrived, and reported to David everything for which Joab had sent him.

**11:23** The messenger said to David, "The men overpowered us and came out against us into the field, yet we pressed upon them as far as the entrance of the gate.

**11:24** Then the archers shot at your servants from atop the wall, and some of the king's servants died; and your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead as well."

**11:25** Then David said to the messenger, "This is what you shall say to Joab, 'Do not let this thing be evil in your eyes, for the sword devours this one as well as that; strengthen your battle against the city and tear it down.' And encourage him."

**11:26** When Uriah's wife heard that her husband Uriah had died, she lamented over her husband.

**11:27** When the mourning had passed, David sent and brought her into his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. Yet the thing that David had done was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — The Masoretic Text spells this word with a silent aleph while vocalizing it as hammelakhim ("the kings"), rather than hammal'akhim ("the messengers").
- **11:4** — The circumstantial clause uses the Hithpael participle mitqaddeshet ("purifying herself") to indicate Bathsheba's ritual purification from menstrual uncleanness, explaining both her bathing and the timing of her conception.
- **11:21** — The Masoretic Text reads "Jerubbesheth," substituting bosheth ("shame") for Baal in the name Jerubbaal (cf. Judges 9:53).
- **11:24** — Both the verb "shot" (wayyoru) and the participle "archers" (hammorim) are Hiphil forms of yarah ("shoot") written with a quiescent orthographic aleph.
- **11:25** — The particle 'et introduces the subject "this thing" with the intransitive jussive verb yera' ("be evil"), creating a pointed lexical contrast with 11:27; the final Piel imperative "encourage him" shifts back from Joab to the messenger.
