## Text

**10:1** Afterward it came about that the king of the sons of Ammon died, and his son Hanun reigned in his place.

**10:2** David said, "I will show loyal kindness to Hanun son of Nahash, just as his father showed loyal kindness to me." So David sent by the hand of his servants to comfort him regarding his father, and David's servants arrived in the land of the sons of Ammon.

**10:3** Then the officials of the sons of Ammon said to their lord Hanun, "Is David honoring your father in your eyes because he has sent comforters to you? Has David not sent his servants to you in order to examine the city, to spy it out, and to overthrow it?"

**10:4** So Hanun seized David's servants, shaved off half of their beards, cut off their garments in half up to their buttocks, and sent them away.

**10:5** When they told David, he sent to meet them, for the men were deeply humiliated. And the king said, "Remain in Jericho until your beards grow out, and then return."

**10:6** And the sons of Ammon saw that they had made themselves odious to David, so the sons of Ammon sent and hired the Arameans of Beth-rehob and the Arameans of Zobah, twenty thousand foot soldiers, and the king of Maacah with a thousand men, and the men of Tob, twelve thousand men.

**10:7** When David heard, he sent Joab and the entire army, the mighty warriors.

**10:8** The sons of Ammon came out and formed battle lines at the entrance of the gate, while the Arameans of Zobah and Rehob, and the men of Tob and Maacah, were by themselves in the field.

**10:9** When Joab saw that the battle front was against him both in front and behind, he selected from all the chosen men of Israel and formed lines against the Arameans.

**10:10** The rest of the people he placed into the hand of his brother Abishai, and he formed lines against the sons of Ammon.

**10:11** He said, "If the Arameans are too strong for me, then you shall be a help to me; and if the sons of Ammon are too strong for you, then I will go to help you.

**10:12** Be strong, and let us show ourselves strong for our people and for the cities of our God; and may the LORD do what is good in His eyes."

**10:13** Then Joab and the people who were with him drew near for battle against the Arameans, and they fled from before him.

**10:14** When the sons of Ammon saw that the Arameans had fled, they fled from before Abishai and entered the city. Then Joab turned back from the sons of Ammon and came to Jerusalem.

**10:15** When the Arameans saw that they had been routed before Israel, they gathered together.

**10:16** Hadadezer sent and brought out the Arameans who were beyond the River, and they came to Helam, with Shobach, commander of Hadadezer's army, before them.

**10:17** When it was reported to David, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, and came to Helam. And the Arameans formed lines to meet David and fought with him.

**10:18** And the Arameans fled from before Israel, and David killed of the Arameans seven hundred charioteers and forty thousand horsemen; and he struck down Shobach, the commander of their army, so that he died there.

**10:19** When all the kings, the servants of Hadadezer, saw that they had been routed before Israel, they made peace with Israel and served them. So the Arameans feared to help the sons of Ammon anymore.

## Translator Notes

- **10:6** — The Niphal verb nib'ashu literally means "they had become a stench," an idiom for becoming thoroughly odious and provoking hostility.
- **10:7** — The Masoretic Text places two definite nouns in apposition, literally "all the army, the mighty warriors" (contrast the construct phrase in 1 Chronicles 19:8).
- **10:9** — The ketiv reads "in Israel" (b-yisra'el), whereas the qere reads "Israel" (yisra'el), yielding the construct phrase "the chosen men of Israel."
- **10:18** — Hebrew literally reads "seven hundred chariotry" (rekhev), a collective noun used metonymically with the verb "killed" to designate chariot fighters or crews.
