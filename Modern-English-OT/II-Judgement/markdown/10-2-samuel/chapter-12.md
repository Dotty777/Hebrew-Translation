## Text

**12:1** Then the LORD sent Nathan to David. He came to him and said to him, "Two men were in the same city, one wealthy and one poor.

**12:2** The wealthy man had flocks and herds in great abundance.

**12:3** But the poor man had nothing at all except one small ewe lamb that he had bought. He nourished her, and she grew up together with him and his children; she would eat from his own piece of bread, drink from his cup, and lie in his arms, and she was like a daughter to him.

**12:4** Now a traveler came to the wealthy man, and he begrudged taking from his own flock or his own herd to prepare for the wayfarer who had come to him; instead, he took the poor man's ewe lamb and prepared her for the man who had come to him."

**12:5** Then David's anger flared fiercely against the man, and he said to Nathan, "As the LORD lives, the man who did this is worthy of death!

**12:6** And he must repay the ewe lamb fourfold, because he did this thing and because he had no compassion."

**12:7** Then Nathan said to David, "You are the man! This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'I Myself anointed you as king over Israel, and I Myself rescued you from the hand of Saul.

**12:8** I gave you your master's house and your master's wives into your arms, and I gave you the house of Israel and Judah; and if that were too little, I would have added for you as much again and more.

**12:9** Why have you despised the word of the LORD, by doing what is evil in My eyes? You struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword, you took his wife for yourself as a wife, and you killed him by the sword of the sons of Ammon.

**12:10** And now, the sword will not depart from your house forever, because you despised Me and took the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be a wife for yourself.'

**12:11** This is what the LORD says: 'Look, I am raising up calamity against you out of your own house; and I will take your wives before your very eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he will lie with your wives before the eyes of this sun.

**12:12** For you acted in secret, but I Myself will do this thing in front of all Israel and in front of the sun.'"

**12:13** Then David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the LORD." And Nathan replied to David, "The LORD also has removed your sin; you will not die.

**12:14** However, because by this deed you have caused the enemies of the LORD to show utter contempt, the son who has been born to you will also surely die."

**12:15** Then Nathan went to his house. And the LORD struck the child that Uriah's wife had borne to David, and he fell gravely ill.

**12:16** So David sought God on behalf of the boy; David kept a fast, and he would go inside and spend the night, lying upon the ground.

**12:17** The elders of his house stood over him to lift him up from the ground, but he was unwilling, and he would not eat food with them.

**12:18** Then on the seventh day it happened that the child died. David's servants were afraid to tell him that the child had died, for they said, "Look, while the child was alive, we spoke to him and he did not listen to our voice. How then can we say to him, 'The child is dead'? He may do harm!"

**12:19** And David saw that his servants were whispering to one another, and David understood that the child was dead. Then David said to his servants, "Is the child dead?" And they replied, "He is dead."

**12:20** Then David got up from the ground, washed, anointed himself, and changed his garments; and he went into the house of the LORD and worshiped. Then he came to his own house, and when he asked, they set food before him, and he ate.

**12:21** Then his servants said to him, "What is this thing that you have done? On account of the child while he was alive, you fasted and wept, yet when the child died, you got up and ate food."

**12:22** He answered, "While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept; for I said, 'Who knows? The LORD may be gracious to me, and the child may live.'

**12:23** Now, however, he is dead; why then am I fasting? Am I able to bring him back again? I am going to him, but he will not come back to me."

**12:24** Then David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and went in to her and lay with her. She bore a son, and she called his name Solomon; and the LORD loved him.

**12:25** And He sent word by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and he called his name Jedidiah, for the sake of the LORD.

**12:26** Now Joab fought against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and he captured the royal city.

**12:27** Then Joab sent messengers to David and said, "I have fought against Rabbah, and I have also captured the city of waters.

**12:28** So now, gather the rest of the people, encamp against the city, and capture it; otherwise I myself will capture the city, and my name will be called over it."

**12:29** Then David gathered all the people and went to Rabbah; he fought against it and captured it.

**12:30** And he took the crown of their king from his head, and its weight was a talent of gold with a precious stone, and it was on David's head. He also brought out the spoil of the city in great abundance.

**12:31** And he brought out the people who were in it and set them to the saw, and to iron picks, and to iron axes, and made them pass through the brickkiln; and thus he did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **12:9** — Qere reads "in My eyes," matching the LORD's first-person speech in verses 7-12, whereas Ketiv reads "in His eyes."
- **12:14** — The Hebrew reads "you have caused the enemies of the LORD to show utter contempt" (taking the Piel infinitive absolute plus finite verb of na'ats causatively); the phrase "the enemies of the LORD" is widely viewed as a scribal euphemism to avoid speaking directly of scorning the LORD.
- **12:24** — Qere reads "and she called" (Bathsheba), whereas Ketiv reads "and he called" (David).
- **12:30** — The Masoretic vocalization reads "their king" (malkam); the same consonants can also be vocalized as Milcom (or Malcam), the national deity of the Ammonites.
- **12:31** — Qere reads "the brickkiln" (or "brick-mold"), whereas Ketiv reads a form of uncertain sense ("bmlkn"). The idioms "set them to" and "made them pass through" are understood by some as forced labor and by others as severe punishment.
