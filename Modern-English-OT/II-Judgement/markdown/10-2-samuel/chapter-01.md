## Text

**1:1** Now it happened after Saul's death, once David had returned from striking down the Amalekites, that David stayed in Ziklag two days.

**1:2** On the third day, look, a man was coming from the camp, from Saul, with his garments torn and earth on his head. When he came to David, he fell to the ground and bowed down.

**1:3** David said to him, "Where are you coming from?" He answered him, "I escaped from Israel's camp."

**1:4** David said to him, "What took place? Please tell me." And he said, "The people fled from the battle, and many from the people also fell and died; Saul and his son Jonathan are dead as well."

**1:5** David said to the young man reporting to him, "How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan have died?"

**1:6** The young man reporting to him said, "I happened by chance to be on Mount Gilboa, and there was Saul leaning on his spear, and look, the chariots and horsemen were overtaking him.

**1:7** He turned around, saw me, and called to me, and I answered, 'Here I am.'

**1:8** He asked me, 'Who are you?' and I told him, 'I am an Amalekite.'

**1:9** Then he said to me, 'Please stand over me and put me to death, for agony has gripped me, because my life is still wholly in me.'

**1:10** So I stood over him and put him to death, because I knew that he would not live after his fall. Then I took the crown that was on his head and the armlet that was on his arm, and brought them here to my lord."

**1:11** Then David grasped his garments and tore them, as did all the men who were with him.

**1:12** They lamented and wept and fasted until evening over Saul and over his son Jonathan, and over the people of the LORD and over the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

**1:13** David asked the young man reporting to him, "Where are you from?" He replied, "I am the son of a resident alien, an Amalekite."

**1:14** David asked him, "How were you not afraid to reach out your hand to destroy the LORD's anointed?"

**1:15** Then David called one of the young men and said, "Step forward and strike him." So he struck him, and he died.

**1:16** And David said to him, "Your blood is on your own head, because your mouth has testified against you, saying, 'I myself put the LORD's anointed to death.'"

**1:17** Then David chanted this dirge over Saul and over his son Jonathan,

**1:18** and he directed that the sons of Judah be taught the Bow; see, it is written in the Book of Jashar:

**1:19** "Your splendor, Israel, lies pierced on your heights! How the warriors have fallen!

**1:20** Do not report it in Gath, do not announce it in the streets of Ashkelon, so that the daughters of the Philistines will not rejoice, so that the daughters of the uncircumcised will not triumph.

**1:21** Mountains in Gilboa, let no dew and no rain be on you, nor fields of offerings, for there the shield of warriors was polluted, the shield of Saul, unanointed with oil.

**1:22** From the blood of the pierced, from the fat of warriors, Jonathan's bow did not draw back, and Saul's sword would not return empty.

**1:23** Saul and Jonathan, loved and delightful in their lives, and in their death they were not separated; swifter than eagles were they, mightier than lions.

**1:24** Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who dressed you in scarlet with luxuries, who placed gold ornaments on your clothing.

**1:25** How the warriors have fallen in the middle of the battle! Jonathan lies pierced on your heights.

**1:26** I am in distress over you, my brother Jonathan; you were very delightful to me. More marvelous to me was your love than the love of women.

**1:27** How the warriors have fallen, and the weapons of war have perished!"

## Translator Notes

- **1:8** — Ketiv reads "and he said," whereas Qere reads "and I said" (translated here, matching the Amalekite's reply).
- **1:9** — The rare Hebrew noun hashavats (a hapax legomenon, from a root meaning to interweave or bind tightly) denotes a seizure, cramp, or death agony. The following clause literally reads "for all-still my life is in me," expressing that his life force had not yet left him.
- **1:16** — Ketiv reads plural "your bloods" (an idiom for bloodguilt), whereas Qere reads singular "your blood" (translated here).
- **1:18** — Hebrew qashet ("bow") is traditionally taken as the title of David's lament (cf. Jonathan's bow in v. 22), though some understand it literally as instruction in archery. The Book of Jashar (lit. "the Book of the Upright") was an ancient collection of Israelite songs (cf. Josh 10:13).
- **1:21** — Hebrew "fields of offerings" (sede terumot) translates the Masoretic Text directly and refers to fertile fields yielding agricultural offerings. The Niphal verb nig'al means "was polluted" or "was defiled."
