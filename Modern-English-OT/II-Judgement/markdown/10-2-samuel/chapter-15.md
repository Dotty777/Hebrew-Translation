## Text

**15:1** Now after this, Absalom provided for himself a chariot and horses, along with fifty men running before him.

**15:2** Absalom would rise early and stand beside the road to the gate. Whenever any man had a dispute to come to the king for judgment, Absalom would call to him and say, "From which city are you?" And he would reply, "Your servant is from one of the tribes of Israel."

**15:3** Then Absalom would say to him, "See, your claims are good and right, yet there is no one from the king to hear you."

**15:4** Moreover, Absalom would say, "If only someone would make me judge in the land! Then every man who has a dispute or case would come to me, and I would give him justice."

**15:5** And whenever a man approached to bow down to him, he would stretch out his hand, take hold of him, and kiss him.

**15:6** In this way Absalom treated all of Israel who came to the king for judgment, and so Absalom stole the heart of the men of Israel.

**15:7** At the end of forty years, Absalom said to the king, "Please let me go and pay my vow, which I vowed to the LORD, in Hebron.

**15:8** For your servant vowed a vow while I lived at Geshur in Aram, saying, 'If the LORD indeed brings me back to Jerusalem, then I will serve the LORD.'"

**15:9** The king said to him, "Go in peace." So he arose and went to Hebron.

**15:10** Then Absalom sent spies throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "As soon as you hear the sound of the trumpet, you shall say, 'Absalom has become king in Hebron!'"

**15:11** Now two hundred men from Jerusalem went with Absalom; they were invited guests who went in their innocence, and they did not know anything.

**15:12** While he was offering the sacrifices, Absalom sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's counselor, from his city, from Giloh. And the conspiracy grew strong, for the people with Absalom kept increasing.

**15:13** Then a messenger came to David, saying, "The heart of the men of Israel has gone after Absalom."

**15:14** So David said to all his servants who were with him in Jerusalem, "Arise and let us flee, for there will be no escape for us from Absalom. Hurry to leave, or else he will hurry and overtake us, bring disaster down upon us, and strike the city with the edge of the sword."

**15:15** The king's servants said to the king, "According to everything my lord the king chooses, here are your servants."

**15:16** So the king went out with all his household following him; however, the king left ten concubines to keep the house.

**15:17** The king went out with all the people following him, and they stopped at the distant house.

**15:18** All his servants passed by beside him, and all the Cherethites, all the Pelethites, and all the Gittites, six hundred men who had come after him from Gath, passed on before the king.

**15:19** Then the king said to Ittai the Gittite, "Why should you also go with us? Return and stay with the king, for you are a foreigner and also an exile from your own place.

**15:20** Yesterday was your arrival, and today should I make you wander about with us while I am going wherever I am going? Return and take your brothers back with you; may loyal love and faithfulness be yours."

**15:21** Ittai answered the king and said, "As the LORD lives, and as my lord the king lives, indeed in the place where my lord the king is, whether for death or for life, surely there your servant will be."

**15:22** David said to Ittai, "Go and pass on." So Ittai the Gittite passed on, with all his men and all the little ones who were with him.

**15:23** The whole countryside was weeping with a loud voice while all the people passed by. The king crossed the Kidron Valley, and all the people passed on toward the road to the wilderness.

**15:24** Now Zadok too, and all the Levites with him, were carrying the ark of the covenant of God. They set down the ark of God, and Abiathar came up until all the people had finished passing out of the city.

**15:25** Then the king said to Zadok, "Return the ark of God to the city. If I find favor in the eyes of the LORD, He will bring me back and show me both it and His dwelling place.

**15:26** But if He says this, 'I take no delight in you,' here I am; let Him do to me what is good in His eyes."

**15:27** The king also said to Zadok the priest, "Are you a seer? Return to the city in peace, along with Ahimaaz your son and Jonathan son of Abiathar, your two sons with you.

**15:28** See, I will wait in the plains of the wilderness until word comes from you to inform me."

**15:29** So Zadok and Abiathar returned the ark of God to Jerusalem, and they stayed there.

**15:30** Meanwhile David went up the ascent of the Olives, weeping as he went up, with his head covered, and walking barefoot. All the people who were with him each covered his head, and they went up, weeping as they went up.

**15:31** Now someone told David, "Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom." And David said, "Please turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness, LORD."

**15:32** When David arrived at the summit, where God was worshiped, there to meet him was Hushai the Archite, with his tunic torn and earth on his head.

**15:33** David said to him, "If you pass on with me, you will be a burden to me.

**15:34** But if you return to the city and say to Absalom, 'I will be your servant, King; just as I was your father's servant in time past, so now I will be your servant,' then you will frustrate the counsel of Ahithophel for me.

**15:35** Are not Zadok and Abiathar the priests there with you? Therefore, everything that you hear from the king's house, you shall report to Zadok and Abiathar the priests.

**15:36** Look, their two sons are there with them—Ahimaaz, Zadok's son, and Jonathan, Abiathar's son—and by their hand you shall send to me everything that you hear."

**15:37** So Hushai, David's friend, came into the city, just as Absalom was entering Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **15:7** — The Masoretic Text reads "forty years," translated literally here without emendation.
- **15:20** — Qere reads the Hiphil imperfect "make you wander" (Ketiv writes a form with waw); the closing blessing follows the concise Masoretic Text without expansion.
- **15:21** — The particle im after ki is written in the consonantal text but omitted in the Qere reading (Ketiv velo Qere); both forms introduce an emphatic oath affirmation.
- **15:27** — The Masoretic Text reads haroeh attah, literally "Are you a seer?" (or "Do you see?"), translated faithfully as written.
- **15:28** — Qere reads "in the plains of the wilderness" (from aravah), whereas Ketiv reads "at the fords of the wilderness" (from avarah).
- **15:31** — The Masoretic Text reads the active Hiphil verb "told" (higgid) with "David" fronted without a preposition, understood impersonally as "someone told David."
