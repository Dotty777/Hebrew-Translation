## Text

**13:1** Now it happened after this that Absalom son of David had a beautiful sister whose name was Tamar, and Amnon son of David loved her.

**13:2** And Amnon was so tormented that he made himself ill on account of Tamar his sister, for she was a virgin, and it seemed impossible in Amnon's eyes to do anything to her.

**13:3** Now Amnon had a friend whose name was Jonadab, son of Shimeah, David's brother; and Jonadab was a very shrewd man.

**13:4** He said to him, "Why are you so gaunt, son of the king, morning after morning? Will you not tell me?" And Amnon said to him, "It is Tamar, the sister of my brother Absalom, whom I love."

**13:5** Then Jehonadab said to him, "Lie down on your bed and pretend to be ill. When your father comes to see you, say to him, 'Please let my sister Tamar come and give me food to eat, and prepare the meal before my eyes, so that I may watch and eat from her hand.'"

**13:6** So Amnon lay down and pretended to be ill. When the king came to see him, Amnon said to the king, "Please let my sister Tamar come and make two cakes in front of my eyes, so that I may eat from her hand."

**13:7** Then David sent word to Tamar at home, saying, "Please go to the house of your brother Amnon and prepare the meal for him."

**13:8** So Tamar went to the house of her brother Amnon, while he was lying down. She took the dough, kneaded it, shaped the cakes before his eyes, and cooked the cakes.

**13:9** Then she took the pan and poured them out before him, but he refused to eat. And Amnon said, "Send every man out from my presence." So every man went out from his presence.

**13:10** Then Amnon said to Tamar, "Bring the meal into the inner room, that I may eat from your hand." So Tamar took the cakes she had made and brought them into the inner room to her brother Amnon.

**13:11** When she brought them close for him to eat, he seized hold of her and said to her, "Come, lie with me, my sister."

**13:12** But she said to him, "No, my brother, do not violate me! For such a thing is not done in Israel; do not commit this outrageous folly.

**13:13** As for me, where could I carry my disgrace? And as for you, you would be like one of the fools in Israel. Now please speak to the king, for he will not withhold me from you."

**13:14** Yet he was unwilling to listen to her voice, and he overpowered her, violated her, and lay with her.

**13:15** Then Amnon hated her with an exceedingly great hatred, for greater was the hatred with which he hated her than the love with which he had loved her. And Amnon said to her, "Rise up, go!"

**13:16** And she said to him, "No, because this great evil of sending me away is greater than the other that you did to me!" But he was unwilling to listen to her.

**13:17** Then he summoned his young man, his attendant, and said, "Send this one away from my presence outside, and bolt the door behind her."

**13:18** Now she was wearing a long-sleeved tunic, for such robes the virgin daughters of the king used to wear. And his attendant brought her outside and bolted the door behind her.

**13:19** Then Tamar put ashes on her head, and she tore the long-sleeved tunic that was on her; she placed her hand on her head and went away, crying aloud as she went.

**13:20** Then her brother Absalom said to her, "Has your brother Aminon been with you? Now, my sister, keep silent; he is your brother. Do not take this matter to heart." So Tamar remained, desolate, in the house of her brother Absalom.

**13:21** Now King David heard all these things, and he grew very angry.

**13:22** But Absalom did not speak with Amnon, either bad or good, for Absalom hated Amnon because he had violated his sister Tamar.

**13:23** Then after two full years it happened that Absalom had sheepshearers at Baal-hazor, which is near Ephraim, and Absalom invited all the king's sons.

**13:24** And Absalom came to the king and said, "Look now, your servant has sheepshearers; please let the king and his servants go with your servant."

**13:25** But the king said to Absalom, "No, my son, let us not all go, so that we do not become a burden to you." He pressed him, but he was unwilling to go, and he blessed him.

**13:26** Then Absalom said, "If not, then please let my brother Amnon go with us." And the king replied to him, "Why should he go with you?"

**13:27** But Absalom pressed him, so he sent Amnon and all the king's sons with him.

**13:28** Then Absalom commanded his young men, saying, "Watch now: when Amnon's heart is merry with wine and I say to you, 'Strike Amnon,' then put him to death. Do not be afraid; have I not myself commanded you? Be strong and be men of valor!"

**13:29** So Absalom's young men did to Amnon just as Absalom had commanded. Then all the king's sons rose up, each man mounted his mule, and they fled.

**13:30** Now while they were on the way, the report came to David, saying, "Absalom has struck down all the king's sons, and not one of them is left!"

**13:31** Then the king rose, tore his garments, and lay on the ground; and all his servants stood by with their garments torn.

**13:32** Then Jonadab, son of Shimeah, David's brother, answered and said, "Let not my lord say, 'They have put to death all the young men, the king's sons,' for Amnon alone is dead; for by Absalom's command this has been determined since the day he violated his sister Tamar.

**13:33** So now, let not my lord the king take such a thing to heart, saying, 'All the king's sons are dead,' for Amnon alone is dead."

**13:34** Now Absalom fled. And the young man keeping watch lifted up his eyes and saw: look, many people were coming from the road behind him, from the side of the mountain.

**13:35** Then Jonadab said to the king, "Look, the king's sons have come! According to the word of your servant, so it has happened."

**13:36** And just as he finished speaking, look, the king's sons came, and they lifted up their voice and wept; and the king and all his servants also wept with a very great weeping.

**13:37** Now Absalom fled and went to Talmai son of Ammihud, king of Geshur. And he mourned for his son day after day.

**13:38** So Absalom fled and went to Geshur, and he was there three years.

**13:39** And King David longed to go out to Absalom, for he was comforted concerning Amnon, since he was dead.

## Translator Notes

- **13:16** — The Masoretic syntax of Tamar's protest is compressed, opening with "No!" followed by an elliptical causal expression ("on account of / because") and a comparative clause ("this evil ... greater than the other ... in sending me away").
- **13:20** — The Hebrew spells the name "Aminon" here rather than "Amnon," widely understood as a scornful diminutive spoken by Absalom.
- **13:33** — Qere omits the written particle "im" (Ketiv "ki im", "rather / but only"), reading simply "ki" ("for").
- **13:37** — Qere reads "Ammihud," whereas Ketiv reads "Ammihur." The unstated subject of "mourned" in the second clause is David.
- **13:39** — The verb translated "longed" is pointed as a feminine singular Piel form ("vattekhal") with the masculine subject "King David," commonly understood with an implied feminine subject such as "his soul" or "his spirit" (in the sense of Qal kalah, "pine/long for"), though some interpret the Piel as "ceased" (going out against Absalom).
