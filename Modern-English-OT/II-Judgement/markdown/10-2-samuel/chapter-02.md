## Text

**2:1** Now after this, David inquired of the LORD, saying, "Should I go up into one of the cities of Judah?" The LORD answered him, "Go up." David asked, "Where should I go up?" And He replied, "To Hebron."

**2:2** So David went up there, and his two wives as well: Ahinoam the Jezreelite and Abigail, the wife of Nabal the Carmelite.

**2:3** And David brought up his men who were with him, each man and his household, and they settled in the cities of Hebron.

**2:4** Then the men of Judah came and anointed David there as king over the house of Judah. And they reported to David, saying, "The men of Jabesh-gilead are the ones who buried Saul."

**2:5** So David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh-gilead and told them, "Blessed are you by the LORD, because you have shown this loyal kindness to your lord, to Saul, and buried him.

**2:6** And now, may the LORD show you loyal kindness and faithfulness, and I myself will also do this good for you, because you have done this thing.

**2:7** So now let your hands grow strong, and be men of valor; for your lord Saul is dead, and the house of Judah has also anointed me as king over them."

**2:8** Meanwhile, Abner son of Ner, commander of Saul's army, had taken Ish-bosheth son of Saul and brought him across to Mahanaim.

**2:9** He made him king over Gilead, over the Ashurites, over Jezreel, over Ephraim, over Benjamin, and over all of Israel.

**2:10** Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he began reigning over Israel, and he reigned for two years. However, the house of Judah followed David.

**2:11** The number of days that David was king in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

**2:12** Now Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul marched out from Mahanaim to Gibeon.

**2:13** Joab son of Zeruiah and the servants of David marched out and met them together by the pool of Gibeon. They sat down, these on one side of the pool and those on the other side of the pool.

**2:14** Abner said to Joab, "Please let the young men get up and compete in front of us." Joab answered, "Let them get up."

**2:15** So they got up and crossed over by number: twelve for Benjamin and for Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve from David's servants.

**2:16** Each man grasped his opponent by the head and drove his sword into his opponent's side, and they fell together. So that place was called Helkath-hazzurim, which is in Gibeon.

**2:17** The battle was exceedingly severe on that day, and Abner and the men of Israel were defeated before David's servants.

**2:18** Now the three sons of Zeruiah were there: Joab, Abishai, and Asahel. Asahel was as swift on his feet as one of the gazelles in the field.

**2:19** Asahel pursued Abner, and he did not turn aside to go to the right or to the left from following Abner.

**2:20** Then Abner turned around and asked, "Is that you, Asahel?" He replied, "It is I."

**2:21** Abner told him, "Turn aside to your right or to your left, seize one of the young men for yourself, and take his armor for yourself." But Asahel was unwilling to turn away from following him.

**2:22** Once again Abner said to Asahel, "Turn away from following me! Why must I strike you to the ground? And how could I raise my face to Joab your brother?"

**2:23** Yet he refused to turn away, so Abner struck him in the abdomen with the back end of the spear, and the spear came out behind him. He fell there and died on the spot. And everyone who came to the place where Asahel had fallen and died stood still.

**2:24** However, Joab and Abishai pursued Abner. When the sun went down, they came as far as the hill of Ammah, which faces Giah on the road to the wilderness of Gibeon.

**2:25** The sons of Benjamin gathered themselves behind Abner and formed one troop, and they stood on the top of a hill.

**2:26** Abner called to Joab and said, "Is the sword to devour forever? Do you not know that it will end in bitterness? How long until you tell the people to turn back from following their brothers?"

**2:27** Joab replied, "As God lives, if you had not spoken, then surely in the morning the people would have withdrawn, each man from following his brother."

**2:28** So Joab blew the ram's horn, and all the people stood still; they did not pursue Israel anymore, nor did they fight again.

**2:29** Abner and his men marched through the Arabah all that night; they crossed the Jordan, marched through the whole of the Bithron, and came to Mahanaim.

**2:30** Now Joab turned back from following Abner and gathered all the people together, and from David's servants nineteen men and Asahel were missing.

**2:31** Yet the servants of David had struck down some from Benjamin and from Abner's men, so that three hundred and sixty men died.

**2:32** They carried Asahel and buried him in his father's tomb, which was in Bethlehem. Then Joab and his men marched all night, and daylight broke for them at Hebron.

## Translator Notes

- **2:9** — The Masoretic Text reads "the Ashurites" (ha'ashuri), possibly referring to the territory of Asher or a northern district; the prepositions "to" (el) and "over" (al) alternate interchangeably in this list.
- **2:14** — The Piel verb yesahaqu literally means "play" or "make sport," used here as a grim military euphemism for a representative contest of combat.
- **2:16** — Helkath-hazzurim is traditionally understood as "Field of Sword-edges" (or "Field of Flint-knives" or "Field of Rocks"), from Hebrew helqah ("plot, field") and tsurim ("flints, sharp edges, rocks").
- **2:27** — Joab's oath is syntactically ambiguous: Hebrew mehabboqer (lit. "from the morning") may refer either to the morning just past (implying the people would have withdrawn immediately had Abner not issued his challenge in v. 14) or to the following morning (implying the pursuit would have continued until dawn had Abner not called for a halt in v. 26).
- **2:29** — Hebrew habbitron ("the Bithron," from a root meaning "cut" or "divide") likely designates a ravine or district east of the Jordan, though some interpreters take it temporally as "the half-day" or "forenoon."
