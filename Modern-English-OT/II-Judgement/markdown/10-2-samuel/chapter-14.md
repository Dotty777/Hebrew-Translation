## Text

**14:1** Now Joab son of Zeruiah knew that the king's heart was toward Absalom.

**14:2** So Joab sent to Tekoa, took a wise woman from there, and told her, "Please act as a mourner, put on garments of mourning, and do not anoint yourself with oil; be like a woman who has been mourning over a dead person these many days.

**14:3** Go in to the king and speak to him in this way." Then Joab put the words into her mouth.

**14:4** The Tekoite woman spoke to the king; she fell on her face to the ground, bowed down, and said, "Help, King!"

**14:5** The king said to her, "What is the matter with you?" She answered, "Truly, I am a widow, for my husband has died.

**14:6** Your maidservant had two sons, and the two of them fought in the field with no one to intervene between them; the one struck the other and killed him.

**14:7** And now the entire clan has risen up against your maidservant, saying, 'Hand over the one who struck his brother, so that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed, and destroy the heir as well!' So they will extinguish my ember that remains, leaving my husband neither name nor remnant on the face of the earth."

**14:8** The king said to the woman, "Go to your house, and I will give orders concerning you."

**14:9** The Tekoite woman said to the king, "My lord the king, let the guilt be on me and on my father's house, and may the king and his throne be guiltless."

**14:10** The king said, "Whoever speaks to you, bring him to me, and he will not touch you again."

**14:11** She said, "Please let the king remember the LORD your God, so that the avenger of blood may not increase the ruin, and so that they do not destroy my son." He answered, "As the LORD lives, not a single hair of your son will fall to the ground."

**14:12** Then the woman said, "Please let your maidservant speak a word to my lord the king." He said, "Speak."

**14:13** The woman said, "Why then have you devised such a thing against the people of God? In speaking this word, the king is like one who is guilty, because the king does not bring back his banished one.

**14:14** For we must surely die, and we are like water poured out on the ground that cannot be gathered up; yet God does not take away a life, but He devises plans so that a banished one will not remain banished from Him.

**14:15** Now the reason I have come to speak this matter to the king my lord is that the people made me afraid; so your maidservant said, 'Let me please speak to the king; perhaps the king will carry out the request of his maidservant.

**14:16** For the king will hear and rescue his maidservant from the hand of the man who would destroy both me and my son together from the inheritance of God.'

**14:17** And your maidservant said, 'Please let the word of my lord the king bring rest,' for my lord the king is like the angel of God in hearing good and evil. And may the LORD your God be with you."

**14:18** Then the king answered and said to the woman, "Please do not hide from me anything that I am asking you." And the woman said, "Please let my lord the king speak."

**14:19** The king said, "Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?" The woman answered and said, "As your soul lives, my lord the king, there is no turning to the right or to the left from anything that my lord the king has spoken. Indeed, it was your servant Joab who commanded me, and it was he who put all these words into the mouth of your maidservant.

**14:20** In order to change the face of the matter, your servant Joab has done this thing. Yet my lord is wise, like the wisdom of the angel of God, to know everything that is in the land."

**14:21** Then the king said to Joab, "Look now, I have done this thing; go and bring back the young man Absalom."

**14:22** Joab fell on his face to the ground, bowed down, and blessed the king. Then Joab said, "Today your servant knows that I have found favor in your eyes, my lord the king, because the king has carried out the request of your servant."

**14:23** So Joab arose, went to Geshur, and brought Absalom to Jerusalem.

**14:24** However, the king said, "Let him turn aside to his own house, and let him not see my face." So Absalom turned aside to his own house and did not see the face of the king.

**14:25** Now in all Israel there was no man as handsome as Absalom, so greatly praised; from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head, there was no blemish in him.

**14:26** When he shaved his head—for at the end of every year he would shave it because it became heavy on him, so he shaved it—he weighed the hair of his head at two hundred shekels by the king's weight.

**14:27** To Absalom were born three sons and one daughter, whose name was Tamar; she was a woman beautiful in appearance.

**14:28** Absalom lived in Jerusalem two full years, yet he did not see the face of the king.

**14:29** Then Absalom sent for Joab to send him to the king, but he was unwilling to come to him. So he sent again a second time, and he was unwilling to come.

**14:30** Therefore he said to his servants, "Look, Joab's field is next to mine, and he has barley there; go and set it on fire." So Absalom's servants set the field on fire.

**14:31** Then Joab arose, came to Absalom at the house, and said to him, "Why have your servants set my field on fire?"

**14:32** Absalom said to Joab, "Look, I sent to you, saying, 'Come here so that I may send you to the king to say, "Why have I come from Geshur? It would be better for me if I were still there."' Now therefore, let me see the king's face; and if there is guilt in me, let him put me to death."

**14:33** So Joab went to the king and told him. Then the king summoned Absalom, and he came to the king and bowed down to him on his face to the ground before the king. And the king kissed Absalom.

## Translator Notes

- **14:4** — The Masoretic Text reads "spoke" (Qal of amar) at the start of the verse before the prostration and the second "said"; translated faithfully as written without emending to "came."
- **14:14** — Literally "God does not lift/take away a life," followed by the Qal intransitive imperfect and Niphal participle of nadach ("so that a banished one will not remain banished").
- **14:19** — The rare form ish here is a variant spelling of the existential particle yesh ("there is"), paired with the oath negative im to mean "there is no turning to the right or to the left."
- **14:22** — Qere reads "your servant," matching "your servant knows" earlier in the verse, whereas Ketiv reads "his servant."
- **14:30** — Qere reads the Hiphil plural imperative with feminine suffix ("set it on fire," matching plural "go"), whereas Ketiv writes an irregular spelling with waw.
