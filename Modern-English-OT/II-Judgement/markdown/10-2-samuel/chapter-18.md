## Text

**18:1** David mustered the people who were with him and placed over them captains of thousands and captains of hundreds.

**18:2** David sent out the people, a third under the hand of Joab, a third under the hand of Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab's brother, and a third under the hand of Ittai the Gittite. And the king said to the people, "I too will certainly go out with you."

**18:3** But the people said, "You must not go out! For if we indeed flee, they will not set their mind on us; and if half of us die, they will not set their mind on us, for now there are ten thousand like us. So now it is better that you be there for us to help from the city."

**18:4** The king said to them, "Whatever is good in your eyes I will do." So the king stood at the side of the gate, while all the people went out by hundreds and by thousands.

**18:5** The king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, "Be gentle for my sake with the young man Absalom." And all the people heard when the king commanded all the captains concerning Absalom.

**18:6** Then the people went out into the field to meet Israel, and the battle was in the forest of Ephraim.

**18:7** There the people of Israel were routed before the servants of David, and the slaughter there on that day was great, twenty thousand.

**18:8** And the battle there was spread out across the face of all the land, and the forest devoured more of the people than the sword devoured on that day.

**18:9** And Absalom happened to come before the servants of David. Absalom was riding on the mule, and the mule went under the tangled branches of the great terebinth, and his head caught fast in the terebinth; so he was left between heaven and earth, while the mule that was under him passed on.

**18:10** A certain man saw it and told Joab, saying, "Look, I saw Absalom hanging in the terebinth."

**18:11** Joab said to the man who had told him, "Look, you saw him! Why then did you not strike him there to the ground? It would have been my part to give you ten pieces of silver and a belt."

**18:12** The man said to Joab, "Even if I weighed a thousand pieces of silver in my palms, I would not stretch out my hand against the king's son; for the king commanded you, Abishai, and Ittai in our hearing, saying, 'Protect the young man Absalom, whoever you are.'

**18:13** Otherwise, had I acted deceitfully against my own life—and nothing is hidden from the king—you yourself would have stood at a distance."

**18:14** Joab said, "I will not linger like this before you." And he took three javelins in his hand and drove them into the heart of Absalom while he was still alive in the heart of the terebinth.

**18:15** Ten young men who carried Joab's weapons circled around, struck Absalom, and put him to death.

**18:16** Joab sounded the ram's horn, and the people turned back from pursuing Israel, for Joab held the people back.

**18:17** They took Absalom and threw him into the great pit in the forest, and they raised up over him a very great heap of stones. And all Israel fled, every man to his tent.

**18:18** Now during his lifetime Absalom had taken and raised up for himself the pillar that is in the King's Valley, for he had said, "I have no son to keep my name in memory." He named the pillar after his own name, and to this day it is called the Monument of Absalom.

**18:19** And Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, "Let me run, please, and bring news to the king that the LORD has vindicated him from the hand of his enemies."

**18:20** Joab said to him, "You are not a bearer of news this day. You may bring news on another day, but on this day you must not bring news, because the king's son is dead."

**18:21** Then Joab said to the Cushite, "Go, report to the king what you saw." So the Cushite bowed down to Joab and ran.

**18:22** Yet again Ahimaaz son of Zadok spoke and said to Joab, "Come what may, please let me also run after the Cushite." And Joab said, "Why would you run, my son, when there is no reward for news coming to you?"

**18:23** "Come what may, I will run." And he said to him, "Run!" So Ahimaaz ran along the way of the plain and went past the Cushite.

**18:24** Now David was sitting between the two gates, and the watchman went to the roof of the gate by the wall. He lifted his eyes and looked, and there was a man running alone.

**18:25** The watchman called out and reported to the king. And the king said, "If he is alone, news is in his mouth." And he kept coming nearer and nearer.

**18:26** Then the watchman saw another man running; and the watchman called out to the gatekeeper and said, "Look, a man is running alone!" And the king said, "This one also is bringing news."

**18:27** The watchman said, "I see that the running of the first one is like the running of Ahimaaz son of Zadok." And the king said, "This one is a good man, and he is coming with good news."

**18:28** Then Ahimaaz called out and said to the king, "All is well!" He bowed down to the king with his face to the ground and said, "Blessed be the LORD your God, who has handed over the men who lifted up their hand against my lord the king."

**18:29** The king said, "Is all well with the young man Absalom?" And Ahimaaz said, "I saw the great commotion when Joab sent the king's servant and your servant, yet I did not know what it was."

**18:30** Then the king said, "Turn aside; take your stand here." So he turned aside and stood.

**18:31** And now the Cushite arrived, and the Cushite said, "May my lord the king receive news, for the LORD has vindicated you today from the hand of all who rose up against you."

**18:32** The king said to the Cushite, "Is all well with the young man Absalom?" And the Cushite said, "May the enemies of my lord the king and all who rose up against you for harm be like the young man."

## Translator Notes

- **18:3** — Qere reads the Qal infinitive construct "to help" for Ketiv's Hiphil form. The MT also reads the adverb "now" ("for now there are ten thousand like us"), whereas several ancient versions and some Hebrew manuscripts read the pronoun "you" ("for you are like ten thousand of us").
- **18:8** — Qere reads the Niphal feminine singular participle "spread out," agreeing with singular "battle," whereas Ketiv has a feminine plural form.
- **18:12** — Qere reads the conditional particle "even if" for Ketiv's variant spelling. In the embedded quote, MT has the indefinite pronoun mi ("whoever you are") after "protect," whereas several ancient versions read li ("for me").
- **18:13** — Qere reads "against my own life," whereas Ketiv reads "against his life" (referring to Absalom).
- **18:22** — Literally "and for you there is no news/reward finding [you]"; the Hebrew noun besorah can mean either "news" or "reward for bringing news" (cf. 2 Sam 4:10), with the Qal participle indicating a reward coming to him.
- **18:29** — The Hebrew syntax places the subject "Joab" between the two direct objects "the king's servant" (the Cushite) and "your servant" (Ahimaaz); some instead construe "the king's servant Joab" as the subject sending Ahimaaz.
