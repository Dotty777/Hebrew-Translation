## Text

**16:1** Now David had passed a short distance beyond the summit, and there was Ziba, Mephibosheth's servant, coming to meet him with a pair of saddled donkeys, and on them were two hundred loaves of bread, a hundred clusters of raisins, a hundred summer fruits, and a skin of wine.

**16:2** The king said to Ziba, "What do you have these for?" And Ziba replied, "The donkeys are for the king's household to ride on, the bread and the summer fruit are for the young men to eat, and the wine is for the weary to drink in the wilderness."

**16:3** The king asked, "And where is your master's son?" Ziba said to the king, "Look, he is staying in Jerusalem, for he said, 'Today the house of Israel will restore my father's kingdom to me.'"

**16:4** Then the king said to Ziba, "Look, everything that belongs to Mephibosheth is yours." And Ziba said, "I bow down; may I find favor in your sight, my lord the king."

**16:5** When King David reached Bahurim, a man from the clan of the house of Saul came out from there; his name was Shimei son of Gera, and he came out cursing continuously.

**16:6** He threw stones at David and at all the servants of King David, while all the people and all the mighty men were on his right and on his left.

**16:7** This is what Shimei said as he cursed: "Get out! Get out, man of bloodshed and worthless man!

**16:8** The LORD has brought back upon you all the bloodshed of the house of Saul, in whose place you reigned, and the LORD has given the kingship into the hand of your son Absalom. Now look, you are in your own calamity, because you are a man of bloodshed!"

**16:9** Then Abishai son of Zeruiah said to the king, "Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Please let me cross over and take off his head."

**16:10** The king replied, "What is there between me and you, sons of Zeruiah? So let him curse, because the LORD has told him, 'Curse David.' Who then can say, 'Why have you done this?'"

**16:11** David also said to Abishai and to all his servants, "Look, my own son, who came from my own body, is seeking my life! How much more now this Benjamite! Leave him alone and let him curse, for the LORD has told him to.

**16:12** Perhaps the LORD will look upon my eye, and the LORD will return good to me in place of his cursing this day."

**16:13** So David and his men continued on the road, while Shimei walked along the hillside opposite him, cursing as he went, throwing stones across at him, and flinging dust.

**16:14** The king and all the people who were with him arrived weary, and he refreshed himself there.

**16:15** Meanwhile, Absalom and all the people, the men of Israel, came to Jerusalem, and Ahithophel was with him.

**16:16** Now when Hushai the Archite, David's friend, came to Absalom, Hushai said to Absalom, "Long live the king! Long live the king!"

**16:17** Absalom said to Hushai, "Is this your loyal love to your friend? Why did you not go with your friend?"

**16:18** Hushai answered Absalom, "No! Rather, the one whom the LORD and this people and all the men of Israel have chosen—his I will be, and with him I will stay.

**16:19** And secondly, whom should I serve? Should it not be before his son? Just as I served before your father, so I will be before you."

**16:20** Then Absalom said to Ahithophel, "Give your counsel: what should we do?"

**16:21** Ahithophel said to Absalom, "Go in to your father's concubines, whom he left behind to keep the house; then all Israel will hear that you have made yourself a stench to your father, and the hands of all who are with you will grow strong."

**16:22** So they pitched a tent for Absalom on the roof, and Absalom went in to his father's concubines in the sight of all Israel.

**16:23** Now the counsel of Ahithophel that he gave in those days was as if someone inquired of the word of God; so was all the counsel of Ahithophel, both to David and to Absalom.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — The Qere reads 'and the bread' (we-ha-lechem), whereas the Ketiv contains an apparent scribal dittography of the prefix lamed (w-l-h-l-ch-m).
- **16:10** — The Qere reads 'So let him curse, because' (koh ... ki), whereas the Ketiv reads 'If he curses, and because/if' (ki ... we-ki).
- **16:12** — Following the Qere, 'upon my eye' (be-eini, traditionally understood as David's weeping eye or tears); the Ketiv (b-'-w-n-y) can be read 'upon my affliction' or 'upon the wrong done to me.'
- **16:18** — The Qere reads 'his' (lo), while the Ketiv writes the homophonous spelling l-a ('not').
- **16:23** — The word 'someone' (Hebrew ish, 'a man') is a Masoretic Qere without Ketiv, read aloud in the traditional text though absent from the written consonants.
