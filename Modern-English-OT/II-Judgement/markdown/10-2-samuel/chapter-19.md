## Text

**19:1** The king was shaken, and he went up to the upper chamber of the gate and wept. As he walked, he said, "My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! If only I myself had died in your place! Absalom, my son, my son!"

**19:2** It was reported to Joab, "Look, the king is weeping and mourning over Absalom."

**19:3** So the victory on that day became mourning for all the people, because on that day the people heard it said, "The king is grieved over his son."

**19:4** And the people slipped quietly into the city on that day, just as humiliated people slip quietly away when they flee in battle.

**19:5** Meanwhile, the king had covered his face, and the king cried out with a loud voice, "My son Absalom! Absalom, my son, my son!"

**19:6** Then Joab came to the king in the house and said, "Today you have shamed the faces of all your servants who rescued your life today, and the lives of your sons and your daughters, and the lives of your wives, and the lives of your concubines,

**19:7** by loving those who hate you and hating those who love you. For you have declared today that commanders and servants mean nothing to you; for I know today that if Absalom were alive and all of us were dead today, then it would be right in your eyes.

**19:8** So now get up, go out, and speak to the heart of your servants. For I swear by the LORD, if you do not go out, surely not one man will stay the night with you tonight; and this will be worse for you than all the calamity that has come upon you from your youth until now."

**19:9** So the king got up and sat in the gate. And they informed all the people, "Look, the king is sitting in the gate." Then all the people came before the king. Now Israel had fled, each man to his tents.

**19:10** And all the people were arguing throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "The king delivered us from the hand of our enemies, and he rescued us from the hand of the Philistines; yet now he has fled from the land away from Absalom.

**19:11** And Absalom, whom we anointed over us, has died in battle. So now, why are you staying silent about bringing the king back?"

**19:12** Then King David sent to Zadok and to Abiathar the priests, saying, "Speak to the elders of Judah, saying, 'Why should you be the last to bring the king back to his house, since the word of all Israel has come to the king, to his house?

**19:13** You are my brothers; you are my bone and my flesh. So why should you be the last to bring the king back?'

**19:14** And to Amasa say, 'Are you not my bone and my flesh? May God do so to me, and may He add to it, if you are not commander of the army before me continually in place of Joab!'"

**19:15** And he inclined the heart of all the men of Judah as one man, so they sent word to the king, "Return, you and all your servants."

**19:16** Then the king returned and came as far as the Jordan. And Judah came to Gilgal to go to meet the king, to bring the king across the Jordan.

**19:17** And Shimei son of Gera, the Benjaminite who was from Bahurim, hurried and went down with the men of Judah to meet King David.

**19:18** With him were a thousand men from Benjamin, along with Ziba, the servant of the house of Saul, and his fifteen sons and his twenty servants with him; and they rushed across the Jordan before the king.

**19:19** And the ferryboat crossed to bring the king's household over and to do what was good in his eyes. And Shimei son of Gera fell down before the king as he was crossing the Jordan.

**19:20** And he said to the king, "May my lord not count iniquity against me, and do not remember how your servant acted perversely on the day my lord the king went out from Jerusalem, so that the king would take it to heart.

**19:21** For your servant knows that I have sinned; so look, I have come today as the first of all the house of Joseph to go down to meet my lord the king."

**19:22** Then Abishai son of Zeruiah answered and said, "Should Shimei not be put to death for this, because he cursed the LORD's anointed?"

**19:23** But David said, "What is there between me and you, sons of Zeruiah, that you should become an adversary to me today? Should any man be put to death in Israel today? For do I not know that today I am king over Israel?"

**19:24** And the king said to Shimei, "You shall not die." And the king swore to him.

**19:25** Now Mephibosheth son of Saul went down to meet the king. He had neither tended his feet nor trimmed his mustache, nor had he washed his clothes, from the day the king departed until the day he came back in peace.

**19:26** And when he came to Jerusalem to meet the king, the king said to him, "Why did you not go with me, Mephibosheth?"

**19:27** He answered, "My lord the king, my servant deceived me. For your servant said, 'I will saddle the donkey for myself and ride on it and go with the king,' because your servant is lame.

**19:28** Moreover, he has slandered your servant to my lord the king. Yet my lord the king is like the angel of God; therefore do what is good in your eyes.

**19:29** For all my father's house were nothing but men doomed to death before my lord the king, yet you set your servant among those who eat at your table. What further rightful claim do I have, then, to cry out any longer to the king?"

**19:30** Then the king said to him, "Why do you speak any more of your affairs? I have decided: you and Ziba shall divide the land."

**19:31** And Mephibosheth said to the king, "Let him even take it all, now that my lord the king has come back in peace to his house."

**19:32** Now Barzillai the Gileadite had come down from Rogelim, and he crossed the Jordan with the king to escort him across the Jordan.

**19:33** Now Barzillai was very old, eighty years of age; and he had provided for the king during his stay at Mahanaim, for he was a very great man.

**19:34** And the king said to Barzillai, "You, cross over with me, and I will provide for you with me in Jerusalem."

**19:35** But Barzillai said to the king, "How many are the days of the years of my life, that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem?

**19:36** I am eighty years old today. Can I distinguish between good and bad? Can your servant taste what I eat and what I drink? Can I still hear the voice of singing men and singing women? Why then should your servant be a further burden to my lord the king?

**19:37** Your servant will go just a short way across the Jordan with the king; so why should the king repay me with this reward?

**19:38** Please let your servant return, so that I may die in my own city, beside the grave of my father and my mother. Yet here is your servant Chimham; let him cross over with my lord the king, and do for him what is good in your eyes."

**19:39** And the king said, "Chimham shall cross over with me, and I will do for him what is good in your eyes; and whatever you choose to ask of me, I will do for you."

**19:40** Then all the people crossed the Jordan, and the king also crossed over. And the king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and he returned to his place.

**19:41** Then the king went on to Gilgal, and Chimham went on with him. And all the people of Judah had brought the king across, along with half the people of Israel.

**19:42** Now all the men of Israel were coming to the king, and they said to the king, "Why have our brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away and brought the king and his household across the Jordan, together with all David's men with him?"

**19:43** Then all the men of Judah answered the men of Israel, "Because the king is a close relative to us. Why then are you angry over this matter? Have we eaten at all at the king's expense, or has any gift been given to us?"

**19:44** Then the men of Israel answered the men of Judah and said, "We have ten shares in the king, and in David as well we have more than you. Why then did you treat us lightly? Was it not our word first to bring back our king?" Yet the speech of the men of Judah was harsher than the speech of the men of Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **19:7** — For "if," the qere reads lu ("if"), introducing the counterfactual condition, while the ketiv is spelled lo ("not," possibly an orthographic variant of lu).
- **19:32** — For "across the Jordan" at the end of the verse, the qere reads et-hayyarden ("across the Jordan"), while the ketiv reads bayyarden ("at the Jordan").
- **19:41** — For "had brought [the king] across," the qere reads the Hiphil perfect he'eviru following the fronted subject, while the ketiv reads a prefixed form with waw (wy'brw).
- **19:43** — "Has any gift been given to us" translates nisse't nissa' lanu, combining nisse't (either a noun meaning "gift/portion" or an unusual Niphal infinitive absolute) with nissa' (Niphal perfect of nasa').
- **19:44** — "In David as well we have more than you" translates the terse Masoretic Hebrew wegam-bedawid ani mimmekha ("and also in David I [have more] than you"), where the men of Israel speak collectively in the first-person singular.
