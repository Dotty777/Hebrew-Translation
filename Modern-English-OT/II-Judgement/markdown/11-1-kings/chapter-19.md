## Text

**19:1** Ahab reported to Jezebel everything that Elijah had done, and all about how he had killed all the prophets with the sword.

**19:2** So Jezebel sent a messenger to Elijah, saying, "May the gods do so and add still more; surely about this time tomorrow I will make your life like the life of one of them."

**19:3** When he saw this, he rose and went for his life, came to Beersheba, which belongs to Judah, and left his attendant there.

**19:4** He himself walked a day's journey into the wilderness, and came and sat beneath a single broom bush. He asked for himself to die, and said, "Enough! Now, LORD, take my life, because I am no better than my fathers."

**19:5** He lay down and fell asleep beneath a single broom bush. And look, an angel was touching him, and said to him, "Get up, eat."

**19:6** He looked, and there at his head was a bread cake baked on hot stones and a jug of water. So he ate and drank, and lay down again.

**19:7** The angel of the LORD returned a second time and touched him, and said, "Get up, eat, because the journey is too much for you."

**19:8** So he got up, ate, and drank, and walked in the strength of that meal for forty days and forty nights as far as the mountain of God, Horeb.

**19:9** There he came into the cave and spent the night there. And look, the word of the LORD came to him, and He said to him, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

**19:10** He said, "I have been deeply zealous for the LORD, God of hosts, because the sons of Israel have abandoned Your covenant, demolished Your altars, and killed Your prophets with the sword. I myself alone remain, and they are seeking my life to take it."

**19:11** And He said, "Go out and stand on the mountain before the LORD." And look, the LORD was passing by, and a great and powerful wind was tearing mountains apart and shattering rocks before the LORD; the LORD was not in the wind. And after the wind, an earthquake; the LORD was not in the earthquake.

**19:12** And after the earthquake, a fire; the LORD was not in the fire. And after the fire, a sound of delicate silence.

**19:13** When Elijah heard this, he covered his face with his cloak, went out, and stood at the opening of the cave. And look, a voice came to him and said, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

**19:14** He said, "I have been deeply zealous for the LORD, God of hosts, because the sons of Israel have abandoned Your covenant, demolished Your altars, and killed Your prophets with the sword. I myself alone remain, and they are seeking my life to take it."

**19:15** Then the LORD said to him, "Go, return along your way to the wilderness of Damascus; when you arrive, anoint Hazael as king over Aram.

**19:16** You shall also anoint Jehu son of Nimshi as king over Israel, and you shall anoint Elisha son of Shaphat, from Abel-meholah, as prophet in place of you.

**19:17** And it will happen that whoever escapes from Hazael's sword, Jehu will put to death, and whoever escapes from Jehu's sword, Elisha will put to death.

**19:18** Yet I will leave seven thousand remaining in Israel: all the knees that have not knelt to Baal, and every mouth that has not kissed him."

**19:19** So he departed from there and found Elisha son of Shaphat while he was plowing; twelve yokes of oxen were ahead of him, and he himself was with the twelfth. Elijah crossed over to him and cast his cloak onto him.

**19:20** He left the oxen, ran after Elijah, and said, "Please let me kiss my father and my mother, and then I will go after you." And he said to him, "Go, return, for what have I done to you?"

**19:21** So he turned back from following him, took the yoke of oxen and slaughtered them, boiled their meat with the gear of the oxen, and gave it to the people, and they ate. Then he got up, went after Elijah, and served him.

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — The plural verbs ya'asun and yosifun treat elohim as plural ('the gods') in Jezebel's oath. The Masoretic Text omits 'to me' and uses the asseverative oath particle ki ('surely') rather than a negative conditional.
- **19:3** — The Masoretic Text points wayyar' as Qal of ra'ah ('he saw'); some Hebrew manuscripts and ancient versions reflect yare' ('he was afraid').
- **19:4** — Ketiv reads the feminine numeral 'ahat ('one'), while Qere reads masculine 'ehad ('one') to agree with the masculine noun rotem ('broom bush').
- **19:12** — Hebrew qol demamah daqqah literally means 'a sound of thin/fine stillness' or 'delicate silence,' contrasting sharply with the violent wind, earthquake, and fire.
- **19:21** — Hebrew bishshelam habbasar ('boiled them, the meat') places a proleptic plural object suffix on the Piel verb bishshel referring to the oxen, clarified in apposition by habbasar ('the meat').
