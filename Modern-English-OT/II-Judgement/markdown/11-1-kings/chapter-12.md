## Text

**12:1** And Rehoboam went to Shechem, because all Israel had come to Shechem to make him king.

**12:3** And they sent and called for him, and Jeroboam and all the assembly of Israel came and spoke to Rehoboam, saying,

**12:4** "Your father made our yoke harsh; as for you, now lighten your father's harsh labor and his heavy yoke that he laid on us, and we will serve you."

**12:5** And he said to them, "Go away for three more days, then return to me." So the people went away.

**12:6** Then King Rehoboam consulted with the elders who had stood before his father Solomon while he was alive, saying, "How do you advise bringing back an answer to this people?"

**12:7** And they spoke to him, saying, "If today you will be a servant to this people and serve them, and answer them and speak good words to them, then they will be servants to you always."

**12:8** But he abandoned the counsel of the elders that they had counseled him, and consulted with the young men who had grown up with him and who stood before him.

**12:9** And he said to them, "What do you advise that we bring back as an answer to this people who have spoken to me, saying, 'Lighten the yoke that your father put on us'?"

**12:10** And the young men who had grown up with him spoke to him, saying, "Thus you shall say to this people who spoke to you, saying, 'Your father made our yoke heavy, but you, lighten it from upon us'—thus you shall speak to them: 'My little finger is thicker than my father's loins.

**12:11** And now, my father loaded a heavy yoke upon you, yet I will add to your yoke; my father disciplined you with whips, yet I will discipline you with scorpions.'"

**12:12** So Jeroboam and all the people came to Rehoboam on the third day, just as the king had spoken, saying, "Return to me on the third day."

**12:13** And the king answered the people harshly, and he abandoned the counsel of the elders that they had counseled him.

**12:14** And he spoke to them according to the counsel of the young men, saying, "My father made your yoke heavy, yet I will add to your yoke; my father disciplined you with whips, yet I will discipline you with scorpions."

**12:15** So the king did not listen to the people, for it was a turn of events from the LORD, in order to establish His word that the LORD had spoken by the hand of Ahijah the Shilonite to Jeroboam the son of Nebat.

**12:16** And all Israel saw that the king had not listened to them, so the people brought back an answer to the king, saying, "What share do we have in David? And there is no inheritance in the son of Jesse! To your tents, Israel! Now see to your own house, David!" So Israel went to their tents.

**12:18** Then King Rehoboam sent Adoram, who was over the forced labor, and all Israel stoned him with stones, and he died. And King Rehoboam exerted himself to mount the chariot to flee to Jerusalem.

**12:19** And Israel has been in revolt against the house of David to this day.

**12:20** Now it happened when all Israel heard that Jeroboam had returned, that they sent and called him to the congregation and made him king over all Israel. None followed the house of David except the tribe of Judah alone.

**12:21** And Rehoboam came to Jerusalem and assembled all the house of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin, one hundred eighty thousand chosen fighting men, to fight against the house of Israel, to restore the kingship to Rehoboam the son of Solomon.

**12:22** Then the word of God came to Shemaiah, the man of God, saying,

**12:23** "Say to Rehoboam the son of Solomon, king of Judah, and to all the house of Judah and Benjamin, and to the rest of the people, saying,

**12:24** 'This is what the LORD says: You shall not go up, nor shall you fight against your brothers, the sons of Israel. Return, every man to his house, for this matter has come about from Me.'" So they listened to the word of the LORD and turned back to depart according to the word of the LORD. At that time Abijah the son of Jeroboam fell ill. And Jeroboam said to his wife, "Arise now and disguise yourself so that they will not know that you are the wife of Jeroboam, and go to Shiloh. Look, Ahijah the prophet is there, the one who spoke about me to be king over this people. Take in your hand ten loaves of bread, cakes, and a jar of honey, and go to him. He will tell you what will happen to the boy." And the wife of Jeroboam did so; she arose and went to Shiloh, and came to the house of Ahijah. Now Ahijah could not see, because his eyes had grown dim from his old age. Now the LORD had said to Ahijah, "Look, the wife of Jeroboam is coming to inquire a word from you concerning her son, for he is ill. Thus and thus you shall speak to her; and it will be, as she enters, that she will pretend to be a stranger." And it happened when Ahijah heard the sound of her feet coming in at the doorway, that he said, "Come in, wife of Jeroboam! Why is it that you are pretending to be a stranger? For I have been sent to you with harsh news."

**12:25** Then Jeroboam built Shechem in the hill country of Ephraim and settled in it; then he went out from there and built Penuel.

**12:26** Then Jeroboam said in his heart, "Now the kingdom will return to the house of David.

**12:27** If this people goes up to offer sacrifices in the house of the LORD in Jerusalem, then the heart of this people will return to their lord, to Rehoboam king of Judah, and they will kill me and return to Rehoboam king of Judah."

**12:28** Then the king sought counsel and made two golden calves, and he said to them, "It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem; here are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt."

**12:29** He placed the one in Bethel, and the other he set in Dan.

**12:30** Now this thing became a sin, for the people went before the one as far as Dan.

**12:31** And he made a house of high places, and he made priests from every part of the people who were not of the sons of Levi.

**12:32** And Jeroboam instituted a festival in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day of the month, like the festival that is in Judah, and he went up to the altar; so he did in Bethel, sacrificing to the calves that he had made. And he stationed in Bethel the priests of the high places that he had made.

**12:33** And he went up to the altar that he had made in Bethel on the fifteenth day in the eighth month, in the month that he had devised out of his own heart; and he instituted a festival for the sons of Israel and went up to the altar to burn incense.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — Ketiv reads plural "they came" while the translated Qere reads singular "he came," agreeing grammatically with the first element of the compound subject; the same Ketiv/Qere occurs in 12:21.
- **12:7** — Ketiv reads singular "and he spoke," while the translated Qere reads plural "and they spoke," agreeing with the elders who advise Rehoboam.
- **12:15** — The noun sibbah ("turn of events"), derived from the root savav ("turn around"), occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and denotes a divinely directed turning of affairs.
- **12:24** — The narrative from "At that time Abijah the son of Jeroboam fell ill" to the end of the verse is Masoretic 14:1-6, carried here following the Saints (Greek) verse arrangement; Masoretic 14:7-20 follow as additional verses. In Jeroboam's instructions to his wife, Ketiv preserves the archaic feminine pronoun atti while Qere reads standard att ("you").
- **12:33** — Ketiv reads mlbd ("apart/alone"), likely a scribal interchange of dalet and vav, while the translated Qere reads millibbo ("out of his own heart," i.e. of his own devising).
- **12:2 *(additional)*** — In the closing clause, the Hebrew verb is the Qal wayyiqtol of yashav ("stayed" or "dwelt") rather than shuv ("returned"), depicting Jeroboam as remaining in Egypt until summoned.
- **14:10 *(additional)*** — "Every male" renders the blunt Hebrew idiom "one who urinates against a wall," and "both bound and free" translates a merism of Qal passive participles meaning everyone without exception.
- **14:14 *(additional)*** — The closing Hebrew clause is abruptly elliptical—literally "this day, and what? even now"—stressing that the judgment is already imminent.
- **14:15 *(additional)*** — Here "the River" refers to the Euphrates.
