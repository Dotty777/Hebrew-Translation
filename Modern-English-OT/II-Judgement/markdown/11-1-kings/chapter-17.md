## Text

**17:1** And Elijah the Tishbite, from among the settlers of Gilead, said to Ahab, "As the LORD, the God of Israel, lives, before whom I stand, there will be no dew or rain during these years, except according to my word."

**17:2** Then the word of the LORD came to him, saying,

**17:3** "Depart from here, turn eastward, and hide yourself in the Wadi Cherith, which lies east of the Jordan.

**17:4** And it will be that you will drink from the wadi, and I have commanded the ravens to sustain you there."

**17:5** So he went and acted according to the word of the LORD; he went and stayed in the Wadi Cherith, which lies east of the Jordan.

**17:6** And the ravens kept bringing him bread and meat in the morning, and bread and meat in the evening, and he would drink from the wadi.

**17:7** Then it happened after some days that the wadi dried up, because there had been no rain in the land.

**17:8** Then the word of the LORD came to him, saying,

**17:9** "Rise, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and stay there. Look, I have commanded a widow there to sustain you."

**17:10** So he rose and went to Zarephath. When he came to the entrance of the city, look, a widow was there gathering sticks. He called out to her and said, "Please get me a little water in a vessel so that I may drink."

**17:11** As she went to get it, he called out to her and said, "Please bring me a morsel of bread in your hand."

**17:12** But she said, "As the LORD your God lives, I have no baked cake, only a handful of flour in the jar and a little oil in the flask. Look, I am gathering two sticks so that I may go in and prepare it for myself and my son, that we may eat it and die."

**17:13** Then Elijah said to her, "Do not be afraid; go, do as you said. Only make a small cake for me from it first and bring it out to me, and afterward make some for yourself and your son.

**17:14** For this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'The jar of flour will not be used up, and the flask of oil will not run short, until the day the LORD gives rain on the face of the ground.'"

**17:15** So she went and acted according to the word of Elijah, and she and he and her household ate for days.

**17:16** The jar of flour was not used up, and the flask of oil did not run short, according to the word of the LORD that He spoke through Elijah.

**17:17** Now it happened after these things that the son of the woman, the owner of the house, fell ill, and his illness became very severe, until no breath remained in him.

**17:18** So she said to Elijah, "What is there between me and you, man of God? Have you come to me to call my iniquity to remembrance and to put my son to death?"

**17:19** And he said to her, "Give me your son." Then he took him from her arms, carried him up to the upper room where he was staying, and laid him on his bed.

**17:20** He called out to the LORD and said, "LORD my God, have You also brought calamity upon the widow with whom I am lodging, by putting her son to death?"

**17:21** Then he stretched himself out over the child three times, and called out to the LORD and said, "LORD my God, please let the life of this child return into him."

**17:22** And the LORD listened to the voice of Elijah, and the life of the child returned into him, and he lived.

**17:23** Then Elijah took the child and brought him down from the upper room into the house, and gave him to his mother. And Elijah said, "See, your son is alive!"

**17:24** Then the woman said to Elijah, "Now indeed I know that you are a man of God, and that the word of the LORD in your mouth is truth."

## Translator Notes

- **17:1** — The Masoretic vocalization reads "from the settlers of Gilead" (from toshav, resident alien/settler) rather than a place name Tishbe. In Hebrew oaths, the conditional particle im followed by ki im ("except") expresses an emphatic negation.
- **17:14** — Ketiv reads ttn (an unusual infinitive or imperfect form of natan, "give"), while the Qere followed here reads tet, the standard Qal infinitive construct ("giving").
- **17:15** — Ketiv reads "he and she," while the Qere followed here reverses the order to "she and he," agreeing with the preceding feminine singular verb ("ate").
- **17:21** — "Stretched himself out" translates the Hithpolel of madad ("measure"); "life" renders nefesh ("life/soul"), and "into him" literally reads "upon his inward parts."
