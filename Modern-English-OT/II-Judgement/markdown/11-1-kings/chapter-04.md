## Text

**4:1** And King Solomon was king over all Israel.

**4:2** And these were the officials whom he had: Azariah son of Zadok was the priest;

**4:3** Elihoreph and Ahijah, sons of Shisha, were scribes; Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder;

**4:4** and Benaiah son of Jehoiada was in charge of the army, and Zadok and Abiathar were priests;

**4:5** and Azariah son of Nathan was in charge of the governors, and Zabud son of Nathan was a priest, the king's friend;

**4:6** and Ahishar was in charge of the household, and Adoniram son of Abda was in charge of the forced labor.

**4:7** Now Solomon had twelve governors over all Israel, who provided for the king and his household; a month in the year fell to each one to provide.

**4:8** And these were their names: Ben-hur, in the hill country of Ephraim;

**4:9** Ben-deker, in Makaz, in Shaalbim, Beth-shemesh, and Elon-beth-hanan;

**4:10** Ben-hesed, in Arubboth—his were Socoh and all the land of Hepher;

**4:11** Ben-abinadab, over all the highland of Dor—Taphath daughter of Solomon was his wife;

**4:12** Baana son of Ahilud, over Taanach and Megiddo and all Beth-shean, which is beside Zarethan below Jezreel, from Beth-shean to Abel-meholah, as far as beyond Jokmeam;

**4:13** Ben-geber, in Ramoth-gilead—his were the tent villages of Jair son of Manasseh, which are in Gilead, and his was the region of Argob, which is in Bashan, sixty large cities with walls and bars of bronze;

**4:14** Ahinadab son of Iddo, in Mahanaim;

**4:15** Ahimaaz, in Naphtali—he also took Basemath daughter of Solomon as a wife;

**4:16** Baana son of Hushai, in Asher and in Aloth;

**4:17** Shimei son of Ela, in Benjamin;

**4:18** Geber son of Uri, in the land of Gilead, the land of Sihon king of the Amorites and Og king of Bashan—and there was one governor who was in the land;

**4:19** Jehoshaphat son of Paruah, in Issachar.

## Translator Notes

- **4:5** — Hebrew kohen usually means "priest," though some take it here as a royal administrative title; re'eh ha-melekh ("the king's friend") is a technical court title for a trusted royal adviser.
- **4:7** — Following the Masoretic qere ha'echad ("each one," with the distributive definite article); the written ketiv echad omits the article ("one"). Both occurrences of "provide" translate the Pilpel stem of kul ("sustain" or "supply provisions").
- **4:16** — Hebrew u-ve'alot can be parsed either as "and in Aloth" (repeating the preposition be-, as in 4:9) or as "and Bealoth" (taking the initial letter as part of the place name).
- **4:18** — Hebrew netsiv echad asher ba'arets ("one governor who was in the land") is elliptical, either stressing that a single governor oversaw this vast Transjordanian region or referring to one governor appointed in Judah.
