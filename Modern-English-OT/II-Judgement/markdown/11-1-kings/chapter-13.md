## Text

**13:1** Now look, a man of God came from Judah by the word of the LORD to Bethel, and Jeroboam was standing beside the altar to burn incense.

**13:2** He cried out against the altar by the word of the LORD and said, "Altar, altar, this is what the LORD says: 'Look, a son will be born to the house of David, Josiah is his name; and on you he will sacrifice the priests of the high places who burn incense on you, and human bones will be burned on you.'"

**13:3** And on that day he gave a sign, saying, "This is the sign that the LORD has spoken: Look, the altar will be split open, and the ashes that are on it will be spilled out."

**13:4** When the king heard the word of the man of God that he cried out against the altar in Bethel, Jeroboam stretched out his hand from the altar, saying, "Seize him!" Then his hand that he had stretched out against him withered, and he could not bring it back to himself.

**13:5** The altar also was split open, and the ashes were spilled out from the altar, according to the sign that the man of God had given by the word of the LORD.

**13:6** Then the king answered and said to the man of God, "Please implore the favor of the LORD your God and pray on my behalf, so that my hand may be restored to me." So the man of God implored the favor of the LORD, and the king's hand was restored to him, and it became as it was at first.

**13:7** Then the king spoke to the man of God, "Come with me to the house and refresh yourself, and I will give you a gift."

**13:8** But the man of God said to the king, "Even if you give me half of your house, I will not go with you, and I will not eat bread or drink water in this place.

**13:9** For in this way I was commanded by the word of the LORD, saying, 'You must not eat bread and you must not drink water, and you must not return by the road that you traveled.'"

**13:10** So he went by another road, and he did not return by the road on which he had come to Bethel.

**13:11** Now a certain old prophet was living in Bethel; and his son came and recounted to him every deed that the man of God had done that day in Bethel, and the words that he had spoken to the king they also recounted to their father.

**13:12** Their father spoke to them, "By which road did he go?" Now his sons had seen the road on which the man of God who came from Judah had gone.

**13:13** Then he said to his sons, "Saddle the donkey for me." So they saddled the donkey for him, and he rode on it.

**13:14** He went after the man of God and found him sitting under the terebinth tree. And he said to him, "Are you the man of God who came from Judah?" And he said, "I am."

**13:15** Then he said to him, "Come with me to the house and eat bread."

**13:16** He said, "I cannot return with you or go in with you, and I will not eat bread nor drink water with you in this place.

**13:17** For a word came to me by the word of the LORD, 'You must not eat bread and you must not drink water there; you must not return to travel by the road on which you went.'"

**13:18** He said to him, "I too am a prophet like you, and an angel spoke to me by the word of the LORD, saying, 'Bring him back with you to your house so that he may eat bread and drink water.'" He lied to him.

**13:19** So he turned back with him, and he ate bread in his house and drank water.

**13:20** While they were sitting at the table, the word of the LORD came to the prophet who had brought him back.

**13:21** He cried out to the man of God who had come from Judah, saying, "This is what the LORD says: Because you have rebelled against the command of the LORD and have not kept the commandment that the LORD your God commanded you,

**13:22** but turned back and ate bread and drank water in the place about which He spoke to you, 'Do not eat bread and do not drink water,' your corpse will not come to the tomb of your fathers."

**13:23** After he had eaten bread and after he had drunk, he saddled the donkey for him, for the prophet whom he had brought back.

**13:24** So he departed, and a lion met him on the road and killed him. His corpse was thrown on the road, while the donkey stood beside it, and the lion stood beside the corpse.

**13:25** Look, men were passing by, and they saw the corpse thrown on the road and the lion standing beside the corpse. They came and spoke about it in the city where the old prophet lived.

**13:26** When the prophet who had brought him back from the road heard, he said, "It is the man of God who rebelled against the command of the LORD; so the LORD handed him over to the lion, which mauled him and killed him, according to the word of the LORD that He spoke to him."

**13:28** He went and found his corpse thrown on the road, with the donkey and the lion standing beside the corpse. The lion had not eaten the corpse, nor had it mauled the donkey.

**13:29** Then the prophet lifted up the corpse of the man of God, laid it on the donkey, and brought it back; and he came into the city of the old prophet to mourn and to bury him.

**13:30** He laid his corpse in his own tomb, and they mourned over him, "Alas, my brother!"

**13:31** After he had buried him, he said to his sons, "When I die, bury me in the tomb where the man of God is buried; lay my bones beside his bones.

**13:32** For the word that he cried out by the word of the LORD against the altar that is in Bethel and against all the houses of the high places that are in the cities of Samaria will surely come about."

**13:33** After this event Jeroboam did not turn back from his evil way, but again he made priests of the high places from every part of the people; whoever wished, he would consecrate him so that he became one of the priests of the high places.

**13:34** And through this matter it became the sin of the house of Jeroboam, so as to wipe it out and destroy it from the face of the earth.

## Translator Notes

- **13:2** — The verb translated 'will be burned' is an impersonal third-person plural Qal imperfect (yisrefu, lit. 'they will burn'), a standard Hebrew construction expressing a passive sense.
- **13:6** — The phrase 'implore the favor of' renders the Piel idiom chillah et-peney (lit. 'soften' or 'appease the face of'), used of seeking divine mercy or goodwill.
- **13:11** — The Masoretic Text shifts from singular ('his son came and recounted to him') to plural ('they also recounted to their father') within the verse; this shift is retained rather than harmonized to the Septuagint.
- **13:12** — The Masoretic Text points the verb as a Qal (vayyir'u, 'saw' or 'had seen') rather than a Hiphil ('showed'), and is translated directly as pointed.
- **13:33** — 'Consecrate him' translates the priestly installation idiom yemalle et-yado (Piel of male', lit. 'fill his hand'). The final clause pairs a singular verb (vihi) with a plural predicate ('priests of the high places'), rendered as 'one of the priests of the high places.'
