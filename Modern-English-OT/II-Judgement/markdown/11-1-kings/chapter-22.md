## Text

**22:1** They remained for three years; there was no war between Aram and Israel.

**22:2** Now it happened in the third year that Jehoshaphat king of Judah went down to the king of Israel.

**22:3** The king of Israel said to his servants, "Do you know that Ramoth-Gilead is ours, yet we remain quiet rather than taking it from the hand of the king of Aram?"

**22:4** Then he said to Jehoshaphat, "Will you go with me to war at Ramoth-Gilead?" And Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "I am like you, my people are like your people, my horses are like your horses."

**22:5** And Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "First, please inquire for the word of the LORD."

**22:6** So the king of Israel assembled the prophets, around four hundred men, and asked them, "Should I go against Ramoth-Gilead to war, or should I hold back?" They replied, "Go up, and the Lord will give it into the king's hand."

**22:7** But Jehoshaphat asked, "Is there not still a prophet of the LORD here, so that we may inquire from him?"

**22:8** The king of Israel answered Jehoshaphat, "There is still one man from whom to inquire of the LORD, yet I hate him, because he does not prophesy good about me, only evil—Micaiah son of Imlah." Jehoshaphat said, "Let the king not say that."

**22:9** So the king of Israel summoned a court official and said, "Bring Micaiah son of Imlah quickly."

**22:10** Now the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were each seated on his throne, clothed in robes, at a threshing floor at the entrance of Samaria's gate, while all the prophets were prophesying in front of them.

**22:11** Zedekiah son of Chenaanah made iron horns for himself and said, "This is what the LORD says: 'With these you will gore Aram until you destroy them completely.'"

**22:12** And all the prophets were prophesying in the same way, saying, "Go up to Ramoth-Gilead and succeed, and the LORD will give it into the king's hand."

**22:13** Now the messenger who went to call Micaiah spoke to him, saying, "Look now, the words of the prophets are, with one mouth, good toward the king. Let your word, please, be like the word of one of them, and speak what is good."

**22:14** Micaiah answered, "As the LORD lives, whatever the LORD says to me, that is what I will speak."

**22:15** When he came to the king, the king said to him, "Micaiah, should we go to Ramoth-Gilead to war, or should we hold back?" And he said to him, "Go up and succeed, and the LORD will give it into the king's hand."

**22:16** But the king said to him, "How many times must I put you under oath that you will speak to me nothing except truth in the name of the LORD?"

**22:17** So he said, "I saw all Israel scattered across the mountains, like sheep with no shepherd. And the LORD said, 'There is no master for these; let each man return to his house in peace.'"

**22:18** Then the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Did I not say to you, 'He does not prophesy good about me, only evil'?"

**22:19** And he said, "Therefore, listen to the word of the LORD: I saw the LORD seated on His throne, with the whole host of heaven standing beside Him at His right hand and at His left.

**22:20** And the LORD asked, 'Who will entice Ahab so that he will go up and fall at Ramoth-Gilead?' One said one thing, while another was saying something else.

**22:21** Then the spirit came out, stood before the LORD, and said, 'I will entice him.' And the LORD said to him, 'By what means?'

**22:22** And he said, 'I will go out and become a spirit of falsehood in the mouth of all his prophets.' Then He said, 'You will entice him and also prevail. Go out and do so.'

**22:23** And now, look, the LORD has placed a spirit of falsehood in the mouth of all these prophets of yours, and the LORD has spoken disaster against you."

**22:24** Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah stepped up, struck Micaiah on the cheek, and asked, "Which way did the Spirit of the LORD pass from me to speak with you?"

**22:25** Micaiah replied, "Look, you will see on that day when you go into a room within a room to hide yourself."

**22:26** Then the king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah and return him to Amon, commander of the city, and to Joash, the king's son,

**22:27** and say, 'This is what the king says: Put this man in prison and feed him bread of distress and water of distress until I come back in peace.'"

**22:28** But Micaiah said, "If you really return in peace, then the LORD has not spoken through me." And he also said, "Listen, all you peoples!"

**22:29** Then the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-Gilead.

**22:30** The king of Israel told Jehoshaphat, "I will disguise myself and enter the battle, while you put on your robes." So the king of Israel disguised himself and entered the battle.

**22:31** Now the king of Aram had ordered his thirty-two chariot commanders, saying, "Do not fight with small or great, except with the king of Israel alone."

**22:32** Now when the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, "Indeed, this is the king of Israel!" And they turned aside against him to fight, so Jehoshaphat cried out.

**22:33** When the chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from following after him.

**22:34** But a man drew a bow at random and struck the king of Israel between the joints and the breastplate. So he said to his chariot driver, "Turn your hand and bring me out of the army, because I have been wounded."

**22:35** The battle grew fierce on that day, and the king was kept standing in the chariot facing Aram; he died in the evening, and the blood of the wound poured into the hollow of the chariot.

**22:36** Then the shout went through the army as the sun went down, saying, "Each man to his city, and each man to his land!"

**22:37** Thus the king died and was brought into Samaria, and they buried the king in Samaria.

**22:38** And the chariot was rinsed at the pool of Samaria, and the dogs licked up his blood, while the prostitutes bathed, according to the word of the LORD that He had spoken.

**22:39** Now the rest of the deeds of Ahab, and everything that he did, and the house of ivory that he built, and all the cities that he built, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

**22:40** So Ahab lay down with his fathers, and his son Ahaziah reigned in his place.

**22:41** And Jehoshaphat son of Asa began to reign over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab, king of Israel.

**22:42** Jehoshaphat was thirty-five years old when he became king, and for twenty-five years he reigned in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Azubah daughter of Shilhi.

**22:43** And he walked in the entire way of his father Asa; he did not turn aside from it, doing what was right in the eyes of the LORD.

**22:44** However, the high places were not removed; the people were still sacrificing and burning incense at the high places.

**22:45** And Jehoshaphat made peace with the king of Israel.

**22:46** Now the rest of the deeds of Jehoshaphat, and his might that he demonstrated, and how he fought, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**22:51** Then Jehoshaphat lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of his father David, and his son Jehoram reigned in his place.

**22:52** Ahaziah son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria in the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, and he reigned over Israel for two years.

**22:53** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD; he walked in the way of his father, and in the way of his mother, and in the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who made Israel sin.

**22:54** He served Baal and bowed down to him, and he provoked the LORD, the God of Israel, to anger, in accordance with everything that his father had done.

## Translator Notes

- **22:6** — The Hebrew text here reads Adonai ('the Lord') rather than the Tetragrammaton ('the LORD'), contrasting with verses 12 and 15. The Hebrew also omits an explicit direct object for 'give' here and in verses 12 and 15, leaving the oracle's wording open-ended.
- **22:13** — Ketiv/Qere variant: the written ketiv is plural ('your words'), while the read qere translated here is singular ('your word'), agreeing with the singular verb 'let ... be' and the following singular 'word of one of them'.
- **22:21** — Hebrew uses the definite article ('the spirit'), likely pointing to a specific spirit in the heavenly vision or the spirit of prophecy rather than an indefinite 'a spirit'.
- **22:30** — Hebrew uses two infinitives absolute (Hithpael 'disguising' and Qal 'entering') with first-person force ('I will disguise myself and enter'), immediately contrasted with the emphatic second-person imperative 'while you put on your robes'.
- **22:38** — Hebrew literally has a concise circumstantial clause 'while the prostitutes bathed' (waw + subject + Qal perfect) without stating a location or object, following the rinsing of the chariot at the pool of Samaria.
- **22:49 *(additional)*** — Following the qere: "made" (the ketiv reads the consonants for "ten") and the plural "were wrecked" (the ketiv reads a feminine singular, agreeing collectively with "ships").
