## Text

**10:1** Now when the queen of Sheba heard the report of Solomon regarding the name of the LORD, she came to test him with riddles.

**10:2** She came to Jerusalem with a very great entourage, camels carrying spices, a great quantity of gold, and precious stones. And she came to Solomon and spoke to him all that was in her heart.

**10:3** Solomon explained to her all her questions; there was not a single matter hidden from the king that he did not explain to her.

**10:4** When the queen of Sheba saw all the wisdom of Solomon and the house that he had built,

**10:5** the food of his table, the seating of his servants, the station of his attendants and their robes, his cupbearers, and his burnt offering that he offered up at the house of the LORD, there was no breath left in her.

**10:6** She said to the king, "The report that I heard in my land was true, regarding your affairs and your wisdom.

**10:7** Yet I did not believe the reports until I came and my eyes saw, and look, half of it was not told to me; you have added wisdom and prosperity beyond the report that I heard.

**10:8** Happy are your men, and happy are these servants of yours, who stand before you continually, hearing your wisdom.

**10:9** May the LORD your God be blessed, who delighted in you to place you on the throne of Israel! Because the LORD loves Israel forever, He has appointed you king to carry out justice and righteousness."

**10:10** She gave to the king one hundred twenty talents of gold, spices in very great abundance, and precious stones; never again did spices come in such quantity as those that the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.

**10:11** Moreover, Hiram's fleet, which carried gold from Ophir, brought from Ophir a very great quantity of almug timber and precious stones.

**10:12** From the almug timber the king made supports for the house of the LORD and for the house of the king, along with lyres and harps for the singers. No such almug timber has come, nor has any been seen, to this day.

**10:13** King Solomon gave to the queen of Sheba all her desire that she asked for, besides what he gave her according to the bounty of King Solomon. Then she turned and went to her land, she and her servants.

**10:14** The weight of the gold that came to Solomon in a single year was six hundred sixty-six talents of gold,

**10:15** besides that of the traveling merchants and the commerce of the traders, and of all the kings of the mixed peoples and the governors of the land.

**10:16** King Solomon made two hundred large shields of beaten gold; six hundred shekels of gold went onto each large shield.

**10:17** He also made three hundred shields of beaten gold; three minas of gold went onto each shield. And the king placed them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.

**10:18** The king also made a large ivory throne and overlaid it with refined gold.

**10:19** The throne had six steps, and the throne had a rounded top at its back, with armrests on either side of the place of the seat, and two lions standing beside the armrests.

**10:20** Twelve lions stood there on the six steps, on one side and on the other; nothing like this had been made for any kingdom.

**10:21** All the drinking vessels of King Solomon were gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were pure gold. None was of silver; it was not reckoned as anything in the days of Solomon.

**10:22** Now this is the account of the forced labor that King Solomon levied to build the house of the LORD, his house, the Millo, the wall of Jerusalem, Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer. Solomon built Gezer, Lower Beth-horon, Baalath, Tadmor in the wilderness in the land, all the storage cities that belonged to Solomon, the chariot cities, the cities for horsemen, and Solomon's desire that he desired to build in Jerusalem, in Lebanon, and in all the land of his dominion. As for all the people who were left of the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites, who were not of the sons of Israel—their descendants who were left after them in the land, whom the sons of Israel had not been able to devote to destruction—Solomon levied them as forced laborers, to this day. Yet of the sons of Israel Solomon made no one a slave, for they were the men of war, his servants, his commanders, his captains, and the commanders of his chariots and his horsemen. For the king had a fleet of Tarshish at sea with the fleet of Hiram; once every three years the fleet of Tarshish would come carrying gold and silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.

**10:23** Thus King Solomon grew greater than all the kings of the earth in wealth and in wisdom.

**10:24** And the whole earth kept seeking the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom, which God had placed in his heart.

**10:25** And each of them would bring his tribute—articles of silver and articles of gold, garments, weapons, spices, horses, and mules—a set amount year by year.

**10:26** Solomon had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots and twelve thousand horsemen. And Solomon assembled chariots and horsemen; he had one thousand four hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, and he stationed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.

**10:27** The king made silver like stones in Jerusalem, and cedars he made as abundant as the sycamores that are in the lowland.

**10:28** Now the export of horses for Solomon was from Egypt and from Kue; the king's traders would acquire them from Kue for a price.

**10:29** A chariot came up and went out from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for one hundred fifty; and in the same way these were exported by their hand to all the kings of the Hittites and to the kings of Aram.

## Translator Notes

- **10:5** — The Masoretic vocalization olato ("his burnt offering") with the verb yaaleh ("offered up") is followed here; the parallel in 2 Chronicles 9:4 reads "his ascent" (stairway), which some interpreters read here as well.
- **10:12** — The Hebrew noun translated "supports" (misad) occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible; derived from the verb meaning "to support," it may denote structural supports, railings, or pillars (compare "steps" in 2 Chronicles 9:11).
- **10:15** — The Masoretic Hebrew vocalizes ha-erev ("the mixed peoples"; compare Jeremiah 25:24), whereas the parallel in 2 Chronicles 9:14 reads ha-arav ("Arabia").
- **10:22** — For the desert city, the qere reads "Tadmor" (compare 2 Chronicles 8:4) while the ketiv reads "Tamar" (compare Ezekiel 47:19). The final exotic import translated "peacocks" (tukkiyyim) is traditionally so rendered, though some scholars understand it as "baboons" or a species of monkey.
- **10:28** — The Hebrew miqweh is read here as the preposition "from" prefixed to the region Kue (ancient Cilicia, renowned for horse breeding), though older traditions took it as a common noun meaning "a drove" or "linen yarn."
