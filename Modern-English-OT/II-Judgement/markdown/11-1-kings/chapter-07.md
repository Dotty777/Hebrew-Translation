## Text

**7:1** Then King Solomon sent and fetched Hiram from Tyre.

**7:2** He was the son of a widowed woman from the tribe of Naphtali, and his father was a man of Tyre, an artisan in bronze. He was filled with wisdom, understanding, and knowledge to do every kind of work in bronze. So he came to King Solomon and carried out all his work.

**7:3** He fashioned the two pillars of bronze: eighteen cubits was the height of one pillar, and a cord of twelve cubits encircled the second pillar.

**7:4** He also made two capitals of cast bronze to place on the tops of the pillars: five cubits was the height of one capital, and five cubits was the height of the second capital.

**7:5** There were lattices of latticework and twisted cords of chainwork for the capitals that were on top of the pillars: seven for one capital and seven for the second capital.

**7:6** So he made the pillars, and two rows all around upon the one latticework to cover the capitals that were on top of the pomegranates, and he did the same for the second capital. There were capitals on the two pillars also above, close by the bulge that was beside the latticework; and the pomegranates were two hundred, in rows all around on the second capital.

**7:7** He erected the pillars for the portico of the main hall. He erected the right pillar and called its name Jachin, and he erected the left pillar and called its name Boaz.

**7:8** Now the capitals that were on top of the pillars were of lily work in the portico, four cubits. On top of the pillars was lily work, and the work of the pillars was completed.

**7:10** Then he made the cast sea, ten cubits from its rim to its rim, circular all around, and its height was five cubits; and a line of thirty cubits encircled it all around.

**7:11** Underneath its rim all around were gourds encircling it, ten to a cubit, surrounding the sea all around; the gourds were in two rows, cast in its casting.

**7:12** Its thickness was a handbreadth, and its rim was like the workmanship of a cup's rim, a lily blossom; it held two thousand baths.

**7:13** It stood on twelve oxen: three facing north, three facing west, three facing south, and three facing east. The sea was upon them above, and all their hindquarters faced inward.

**7:14** Then he made the ten stands of bronze: the length of each stand was four cubits, its width four cubits, and its height three cubits.

**7:15** This was the workmanship of the stand: they had panels, and there were panels between the frames.

**7:16** On the panels that were between the frames were lions, oxen, and cherubim; on the frames above was a pedestal, and beneath the lions and the oxen were wreaths of hanging work.

**7:17** Each stand had four bronze wheels and bronze axles, and its four feet had shoulder supports; beneath the basin the shoulder supports were cast, with wreaths beside each one.

**7:18** Its opening inside the crown and upward was one cubit; its opening was circular, of pedestal workmanship, a cubit and a half, and on its opening also were carvings, while their panels were square, not circular. The four wheels were underneath the panels, and the mounts of the wheels were in the stand; the height of each wheel was a cubit and a half.

**7:19** The workmanship of the wheels was like the workmanship of a chariot wheel: their mounts, their rims, their spokes, and their hubs were all cast.

**7:20** There were four shoulder supports at the four corners of each stand; its shoulder supports were part of the stand.

**7:21** On top of the stand was a height of half a cubit, circular all around; and on top of the stand, its stays and its panels were part of it.

**7:22** He engraved on the plates of its stays and on its panels cherubim, lions, and palm trees, according to the open space on each, with wreaths all around.

**7:23** In this way he made the ten stands: all of them had one casting, one measurement, and one form.

**7:24** He also made ten bronze basins: each basin held forty baths, each basin was four cubits, and there was one basin on each stand for the ten stands.

**7:25** He placed the stands, five on the right side of the house and five on the left side of the house; and he placed the sea on the right side of the house, toward the east, facing south.

**7:26** Hiram also made the basins, the shovels, and the sprinkling bowls. Thus Hiram finished doing all the work that he performed for King Solomon for the house of the LORD:

**7:27** two pillars, the two bowls of the capitals that were on top of the pillars, and the two lattices to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on top of the pillars;

**7:28** and the four hundred pomegranates for the two lattices, two rows of pomegranates for each lattice, to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were upon the pillars;

**7:29** and the ten stands, and the ten basins on the stands;

**7:30** and the one sea, and the twelve oxen underneath the sea;

**7:31** and the pots, the shovels, and the sprinkling bowls. All these articles that Hiram made for King Solomon for the house of the LORD were of burnished bronze.

**7:32** Solomon left all the articles alone because of their exceedingly great abundance; the weight of the bronze was not ascertained.

**7:33** In the plain of the Jordan the king cast them, in the clay soil between Succoth and Zarethan.

**7:34** Then Solomon made all the articles that were in the house of the LORD: the altar of gold, and the table of gold on which was the bread of the Presence;

**7:35** the lampstands of pure gold, five on the right and five on the left in front of the inner sanctuary, along with the blossoms, the lamps, and the tongs of gold;

**7:36** the cups, the wick trimmers, the sprinkling bowls, the dishes, and the firepans of pure gold; and the hinges for the doors of the inner house, the Holy of Holies, and for the doors of the house, the main hall, of gold.

**7:37** So all the work that King Solomon performed for the house of the LORD was finished. Then Solomon brought in the consecrated things of his father David; the silver, the gold, and the articles he placed in the treasuries of the house of the LORD.

**7:38** As for his own house, Solomon built it over thirteen years, and he finished his entire house.

**7:39** He built the House of the Forest of Lebanon: its length was one hundred cubits, its width fifty cubits, and its height thirty cubits, upon four rows of cedar pillars, with cedar beams upon the pillars.

**7:40** It was paneled with cedar above over the chambers that were upon the pillars, forty-five in all, fifteen to a row.

**7:41** There were window frames in three rows, with window opposite window in three tiers.

**7:42** All the doorways and the doorposts were square in frame, and window stood opposite window in three tiers.

**7:43** He also made the Hall of Pillars: its length was fifty cubits and its width thirty cubits, with a portico in front of them, and pillars and a canopy in front of them.

**7:44** He also made the Hall of the Throne where he would judge, the Hall of Judgment; and it was paneled with cedar from floor to floor.

**7:45** His own house where he would dwell, in the other court inward from the hall, was of this same workmanship. He also made a house like this hall for Pharaoh's daughter, whom Solomon had married.

**7:46** All these were of costly stones, hewn to measure, sawed with a saw inside and outside, from the foundation to the coping, and on the outside as far as the great court.

**7:47** The foundation was laid with costly stones, large stones, stones of ten cubits and stones of eight cubits.

**7:48** And above were costly stones, hewn to measure, and cedar.

**7:49** The great court all around had three rows of hewn stone and a row of cedar beams, as did the inner court of the house of the LORD and the portico of the house.

## Translator Notes

- **7:6** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads 'the pillars' and 'on top of the pomegranates,' a well-known textual transposition of the two nouns (compare 7:27-28). At 'the latticework,' the qere adds the definite article omitted in the ketiv.
- **7:10** — For 'and a line,' the qere reads the standard spelling veqav, whereas the ketiv writes the longer orthographic form veqaveh with the same meaning.
- **7:22** — At 'and on its panels,' the qere omits a redundant conjunction vav present in the ketiv. The difficult phrase translated 'according to the open space on each' literally refers to the bare or clear space available on each section.
- **7:31** — For 'these,' the qere reads ha'elleh ('all these articles'), whereas the ketiv reads ha'ohel ('all the articles of the tent').
- **7:44** — The Masoretic Hebrew literally reads 'from the floor to the floor,' likely referring to the full vertical span from the lower floor to the upper floor or ceiling level.
