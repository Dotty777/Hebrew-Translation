## Text

**20:1** It happened after these things that there was a vineyard belonging to Naboth the Jezreelite, which was in Jezreel, next to the palace of Ahab, king of Samaria.

**20:2** Ahab spoke to Naboth, saying, "Give me your vineyard so that it may be a vegetable garden for me, because it is close next to my house, and I will give you a better vineyard than it in its place. If it is good in your eyes, I will give you silver as the price of this one."

**20:3** Naboth said to Ahab, "Far be it from me before the LORD to give the inheritance of my fathers to you."

**20:4** So Ahab went to his house, sullen and indignant over the word that Naboth the Jezreelite had spoken to him, for he had said, "I will not give you the inheritance of my fathers." He lay down on his bed, turned his face away, and ate no food.

**20:5** Then his wife Jezebel came to him and spoke to him, "Why is it that your spirit is sullen, and you are not eating food?"

**20:6** He spoke to her, "Because I spoke to Naboth the Jezreelite and said to him, 'Give me your vineyard for silver, or if you wish, I will give you a vineyard in its place.' Yet he said, 'I will not give you my vineyard.'"

**20:7** His wife Jezebel said to him, "Do you now exercise kingship over Israel? Get up, eat food, and let your heart be glad; I myself will give you the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite."

**20:8** So she wrote letters in the name of Ahab, sealed them with his seal, and sent letters to the elders and to the nobles who were in his city, those living with Naboth.

**20:9** She wrote in the letters, saying, "Call a fast, and seat Naboth at the head of the people.

**20:10** And seat two men, sons of worthlessness, opposite him, and let them testify against him, saying, 'You have cursed God and the king.' Then bring him out and stone him so that he dies."

**20:11** So the men of his city—the elders and the nobles residing in his city—carried out what Jezebel had sent to them, according to what was written in the letters that she had sent to them.

**20:12** They called a fast and seated Naboth at the head of the people.

**20:13** Then the two men, sons of worthlessness, came in and sat opposite him; and the men of worthlessness testified against Naboth before the people, saying, "Naboth has cursed God and the king." So they brought him outside the city and stoned him with stones, and he died.

**20:14** Then they sent word to Jezebel, saying, "Naboth has been stoned and has died."

**20:15** When Jezebel heard that Naboth had been stoned and had died, Jezebel said to Ahab, "Get up, take possession of the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite, which he refused to give you for silver, because Naboth is not alive—he is dead."

**20:16** When Ahab heard that Naboth had died, Ahab got up to go down to the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite to take possession of it.

**20:17** Then the word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite, saying,

**20:18** "Get up, go down to meet Ahab, king of Israel, who is in Samaria. Look, he is in the vineyard of Naboth, where he has gone down to take possession of it.

**20:19** And you shall speak to him, saying, 'This is what the LORD says: Have you murdered and also taken possession?' And you shall speak to him, saying, 'This is what the LORD says: In the place where the dogs licked Naboth's blood, the dogs will lick your blood, even yours.'"

**20:20** Ahab said to Elijah, "Have you found me, my enemy?" He answered, "I have found you, because you have sold yourself to do what is evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**20:21** Look, I am bringing calamity upon you; I will sweep clean after you, and I will cut off from Ahab every male, both confined and free in Israel.

**20:22** I will make your house like the house of Jeroboam son of Nebat and like the house of Baasha son of Ahijah, because of the provocation by which you have provoked anger and caused Israel to sin.

**20:23** And concerning Jezebel also, the LORD has spoken, saying, 'The dogs will eat Jezebel by the rampart of Jezreel.'

**20:24** Whoever belonging to Ahab dies in the city, the dogs will eat; and whoever dies in the field, the birds of the sky will eat."

**20:25** Indeed, there was no one like Ahab, who sold himself to do what is evil in the eyes of the LORD, whom his wife Jezebel incited.

**20:26** He behaved most detestably in going after idols, according to everything that the Amorites had done, whom the LORD had dispossessed from before the sons of Israel.

**20:27** When Ahab heard these words, he tore his garments, put sackcloth on his flesh, and fasted; he lay in sackcloth and walked about softly.

**20:28** Then the word of the LORD came to Elijah the Tishbite, saying,

**20:29** "Have you seen that Ahab has humbled himself before Me? Since he has humbled himself before Me, I will not bring the calamity during his days; in the days of his son I will bring the calamity upon his house."

## Translator Notes

- **20:8** — Ketiv reads hassefarim ('the letters') with the definite article, while Qere reads sefarim ('letters') without the article.
- **20:10** — Hebrew berekhta (Piel of barakh, literally 'you have blessed') is a reverential euphemism for 'cursed' (so also in 20:13) to avoid pairing 'curse' directly with 'God.' 'Sons of worthlessness' (bene-veliyya'al) is a Hebrew idiom for base, unscrupulous scoundrels.
- **20:21** — 'Every male' renders the coarse Hebrew idiom mashtin beqir (literally 'one who urinates against a wall'). 'Both confined and free' renders the merism 'atsur ve'azuv (literally 'restrained and released/abandoned'), encompassing everyone without exception.
- **20:23** — The Masoretic Text reads behel ('by the rampart/outer wall'), distinct from beheleq ('in the plot/territory') in 2 Kings 9:10, 36.
- **20:27** — Hebrew wayhallekh 'at (Piel of halakh with 'at, literally 'walked about softly/gently') denotes a slow, subdued manner expressing humility and mourning.
