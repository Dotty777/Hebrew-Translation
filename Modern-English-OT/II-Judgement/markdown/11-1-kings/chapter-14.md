## Text

**14:21** Now Rehoboam son of Solomon reigned in Judah. Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city that the LORD had chosen from all the tribes of Israel to place His name there. His mother's name was Naamah the Ammonite.

**14:22** Judah did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and they provoked Him to jealousy more than all that their fathers had done, with the sins that they committed.

**14:23** They too built for themselves high places, standing stones, and Asherah poles on every high hill and beneath every flourishing tree.

**14:24** Male shrine prostitutes were also in the land; they acted in accordance with all the abominations of the nations that the LORD had dispossessed before the sons of Israel.

**14:25** In the fifth year of King Rehoboam, Shishak king of Egypt marched up against Jerusalem.

**14:26** He took the treasures of the house of the LORD and the treasures of the king's house; he took all of it, and he took all the shields of gold that Solomon had made.

**14:27** Then King Rehoboam made shields of bronze in their place and entrusted them to the charge of the commanders of the guards who watched over the entrance of the king's house.

**14:28** Whenever the king went into the house of the LORD, the guards would carry them and bring them back to the chamber of the guards.

**14:29** Now the rest of the deeds of Rehoboam and everything that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**14:30** And there was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all the time.

**14:31** Then Rehoboam lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of David. His mother's name was Naamah the Ammonite. And Abijam his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **14:24** — Hebrew qadesh is a collective masculine singular noun (lit. 'consecrated one') denoting male shrine prostitutes. The phrase 'the abominations of the nations' reflects an unusual construction in which both nouns carry the definite article (hatto'evot haggoyim).
- **14:25** — Ketiv reads Shushaq (closer to Egyptian Shoshenq); Qere reads Shishak, which is followed in the translation.
- **14:27** — 'The guards' translates haratsim (Qal active participle of ruts, lit. 'the runners'), the standard title for the royal footguards and escorts.
