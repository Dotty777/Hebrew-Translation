## Text

**4:1** Now the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin heard that the sons of the exile were building a temple for the LORD, the God of Israel.

**4:2** So they came near to Zerubbabel and to the heads of the fathers' houses and said to them, "Let us build with you, for like you we seek your God, and to Him we have been sacrificing from the days of Esarhaddon king of Assyria, who brought us up here."

**4:3** But Zerubbabel and Jeshua and the rest of the heads of the fathers' houses of Israel said to them, "It is not for you and us to build a house for our God; rather, we together will build for the LORD, the God of Israel, as King Cyrus, king of Persia, commanded us."

**4:4** Then the people of the land kept weakening the hands of the people of Judah and terrifying them from building,

**4:5** and hiring counselors against them to thwart their counsel all the days of Cyrus king of Persia and until the reign of Darius king of Persia.

**4:6** And during the reign of Ahasuerus, at the outset of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the residents of Judah and Jerusalem.

**4:7** And in the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of his associates wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia; and the text of the letter was written in Aramaic and translated into Aramaic.

**4:8** Rehum the chancellor and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes as follows:

**4:9** Then Rehum the chancellor and Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their associates, the Dinaites and the Apharsathchites, the Tarpelites, the Apharsites, the Archevites, the Babylonians, the Susanchites, the Dehaites, the Elamites,

**4:10** and the rest of the nations whom the great and noble Osnappar deported and settled in the city of Samaria and the rest of Beyond the River, and now.

**4:11** This is the copy of the letter that they sent to him: "To King Artaxerxes: Your servants, the men of Beyond the River, and now.

**4:12** Let it be known to the king that the Jews who came up from you have come to us at Jerusalem; they are building the rebellious and wicked city, and they are completing the walls and repairing the foundations.

**4:13** Now let it be known to the king that if this city is built and the walls are completed, they will not pay tribute, tax, or toll, and in the end it will bring loss to kings.

**4:14** Now, since we eat the salt of the palace and it is not proper for us to see the king's dishonor, for this reason we have sent and informed the king,

**4:15** so that a search may be made in the book of the records of your fathers. And you will find in the book of the records and know that this city is a rebellious city, bringing loss to kings and provinces, and that they have practiced sedition within it from ancient days; for this reason this city was devastated.

**4:16** We are informing the king that if this city is built and the walls are completed, because of this you will have no portion in Beyond the River."

**4:17** The king sent a reply to Rehum the chancellor and Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their associates who live in Samaria, and to the rest of Beyond the River: "Peace, and now.

**4:18** The letter that you sent to us has been read distinctly before me.

**4:19** And by me a decree was issued, and they searched and found that from ancient days this city has risen up against kings, and rebellion and sedition have been practiced in it.

**4:20** And mighty kings have been over Jerusalem, exercising dominion over all of Beyond the River, and tribute, tax, and toll were paid to them.

**4:21** Now issue a decree to halt those men, and this city shall not be built until a decree is issued from me.

**4:22** And be careful not to act negligently regarding this; why should harm increase to bring loss to kings?"

**4:23** Then, once the copy of the letter of King Artaxerxes was read before Rehum and Shimshai the scribe and their associates, they went in haste to Jerusalem, to the Jews, and halted them with force and power.

**4:24** At that time the work of the house of God that is in Jerusalem halted, and it remained halted until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — The Qere reads welo ('and to Him'), matching the speakers' claim of worshipping Israel's God; the Ketiv reads wl' ('and not').
- **4:4** — The Qere reads umebahalim (Piel participle of bahal, 'terrifying/frightening'), whereas the Ketiv reads wmblhym (a metathesized form from balah, 'wearing down/troubling').
- **4:8** — The text of Ezra switches from Hebrew to Biblical Aramaic here (continuing through 6:18); 'chancellor' renders the official title be'el-te'em (literally 'master of decree').
- **4:9** — The Masoretic Text vocalizes all nine group names with Aramaic gentilic endings, though the first four may represent Persian official titles. Two Qere forms are followed here: 'Archevites' ('arkewaye' vs. Ketiv 'rkwy) and 'Dehaites' (dehaye' vs. Ketiv dhw', sometimes read as 'that is').
- **4:12** — The Qere reads weshurayya' shaklilu ('and they are completing the walls', Shaphel perfect of kll used inceptively or conatively in light of 4:13), whereas the Ketiv reads wshwry 'shkllw.
- **4:13** — 'In the end' translates the rare Old Persian loanword 'appetom (alternatively understood as 'revenue' or 'treasury'), paired with the Haphel verb tehanziq ('bring loss to', cf. 4:15, 22).
