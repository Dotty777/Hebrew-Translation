## Text

**6:1** Then King Darius issued a decree, and they searched in the house of the records where they deposited the treasures in Babylon.

**6:2** And in Ecbatana, in the citadel which is in the province of Media, a scroll was found, and inside it a memorandum was written as follows:

**6:3** "In the first year of King Cyrus, King Cyrus issued a decree: Concerning the house of God in Jerusalem, let the house be built, the place where they offer sacrifices, and let its foundations be laid, its height sixty cubits, its width sixty cubits,

**6:4** three courses of massive stones and a course of new timber; and let the expense be paid from the king's house.

**6:5** Also let the gold and silver vessels of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar brought out from the temple in Jerusalem and carried to Babylon, be restored, and let each go to the temple in Jerusalem, to its place, and you shall deposit them in the house of God."

**6:6** "Now, Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai, and their colleagues, the Apharsachites who are in Beyond the River, stay far away from there.

**6:7** Leave the work of that house of God alone; let the governor of the Jews and the elders of the Jews build that house of God on its site.

**6:8** Moreover, a decree is issued by me concerning what you shall do for those elders of the Jews for the building of that house of God: from the king's resources, namely the tribute of Beyond the River, the expense shall be paid in full to those men without interruption.

**6:9** And whatever is needed—young bulls, rams, and lambs for burnt offerings to the God of heaven, and wheat, salt, wine, and oil—according to the word of the priests who are in Jerusalem, let it be given to them day by day without fail,

**6:10** so that they may offer pleasing offerings to the God of heaven and pray for the life of the king and his sons.

**6:11** Also a decree is issued by me that if any man alters this edict, let a timber be pulled out from his house, and let him be lifted up and impaled upon it, and let his house be made a refuse heap on account of this.

**6:12** And may the God who caused His name to dwell there overthrow any king or people who stretches out his hand to alter this or to destroy that house of God which is in Jerusalem. I, Darius, have issued a decree; let it be carried out with full diligence."

**6:13** Then Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai, and their colleagues carried it out with full diligence, just as King Darius had sent word.

**6:14** And the elders of the Jews kept building and prospering through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo. And they built and completed it by the decree of the God of Israel and by the decree of Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes king of Persia.

**6:15** And this house was finished by the third day of the month of Adar, which was the sixth year of the reign of King Darius.

**6:16** And the sons of Israel—the priests, the Levites, and the rest of the people of the exile—observed the dedication of this house of God with joy.

**6:17** And they offered for the dedication of this house of God one hundred bulls, two hundred rams, four hundred lambs, and twelve male goats as a sin offering for all Israel, according to the number of the tribes of Israel.

**6:18** And they appointed the priests in their divisions and the Levites in their orders for the service of God in Jerusalem, according to what is written in the book of Moses.

**6:19** And the people of the exile observed the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month.

**6:20** For the priests and the Levites had purified themselves as one; all of them were pure. So they slaughtered the Passover offering for all the people of the exile, for their brothers the priests, and for themselves.

**6:21** And the sons of Israel who had returned from the exile ate it, along with everyone who had separated himself from the uncleanness of the nations of the land to join them, to seek the LORD, the God of Israel.

**6:22** And they observed the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days with gladness, for the LORD had made them glad and had turned the heart of the king of Assyria toward them to strengthen their hands in the work of the house of God, the God of Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **6:3** — Aramaic mesobelin is a Poel passive participle of the root sbl ("bear, support"), understood here of foundations being solidly laid or retained.
- **6:4** — MT reads hadat ("new"), describing fresh timber; the Septuagint reflects had ("one"), as in "one course of timber."
- **6:6** — Aramaic apharsekhaye is an Old Persian administrative title meaning "investigators" or "officials," traditionally transliterated as "Apharsachites." MT also reads the third-person suffix "their colleagues" before shifting to second-person address.
- **6:17** — Following the Qere lehatta'ah ("for a sin offering", singular emphatic); the Ketiv writes lhty', a variant spelling or plural ("for sin offerings").
- **6:22** — MT literally reads "the king of Assyria," a traditional designation here applied to the Persian king Darius as successor to the earlier Mesopotamian empires.
