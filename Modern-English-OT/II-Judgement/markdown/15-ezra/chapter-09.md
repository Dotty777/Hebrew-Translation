## Text

**9:1** When these things were finished, the officials came near to me, saying, "The people of Israel, the priests, and the Levites have not separated themselves from the peoples of the lands regarding their abominations, those of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Jebusites, the Ammonites, the Moabites, the Egyptians, and the Amorites.

**9:2** For they have taken some of their daughters for themselves and for their sons, so the holy seed has intermingled with the peoples of the lands. And the hand of the officials and the deputies has been first in this unfaithfulness."

**9:3** When I heard this matter, I tore my garment and my cloak, plucked some of the hair of my head and my beard, and sat down stunned.

**9:4** Then everyone who trembled at the words of the God of Israel gathered to me over the unfaithfulness of the exiles, while I sat stunned until the evening offering.

**9:5** And at the evening offering I rose from my fasting, with my garment and my cloak torn, and I knelt on my knees and spread out my palms to the LORD my God.

**9:6** And I said, "My God, I am ashamed and humiliated to lift my face to You, my God, for our iniquities have multiplied above our heads, and our guilt has grown up to the heavens.

**9:7** From the days of our fathers until this day we have been in great guilt; and because of our iniquities, we, our kings, and our priests have been handed over into the hand of the kings of the lands, to the sword, to captivity, to plunder, and to shame of face, as it is today.

**9:8** Yet now, for a short moment, favor has come from the LORD our God to leave us an escaped remnant and to give us a peg in His holy place, so that our God may give light to our eyes and give us a little reviving in our slavery.

**9:9** For we are slaves, yet in our slavery our God has not forsaken us; instead, He has extended loyal love to us in the presence of the kings of Persia, to give us reviving, to raise up the house of our God and restore its ruins, and to give us a wall in Judah and in Jerusalem.

**9:10** And now, what can we say, our God, after this? For we have forsaken Your commandments,

**9:11** which You commanded by the hand of Your servants the prophets, saying, 'The land you are entering to possess is a land of impurity through the impurity of the peoples of the lands, with their abominations by which they have filled it from one end to the other with their uncleanness.

**9:12** Therefore now, do not give your daughters to their sons, and do not take their daughters for your sons, and never seek their peace or their well-being, so that you may grow strong, eat the good of the land, and pass it on as an inheritance to your sons forever.'

**9:13** And after all that has come upon us because of our evil deeds and our great guilt, since You, our God, have held back, punishing us less than our iniquity deserves, and have given us an escaped remnant like this,

**9:14** should we break Your commandments once more and intermarry with the peoples of these abominations? Would You not be angry with us to the point of complete destruction, so that there would be no remnant or survivor?

**9:15** LORD, God of Israel, You are righteous, for we have been left as an escaped remnant, as it is today. Here we are before You in our guilt, for no one is able to stand before You on account of this."

## Translator Notes

- **9:5** — 'My fasting' translates the Hebrew hapax legomenon ta'anit, derived from the root 'anah ('humble/afflict oneself', cf. 8:21), denoting self-humbling or fasting. 'With my garment and my cloak torn' renders the Hebrew preposition be- with the Qal infinitive construct of qara' ('in my tearing').
- **9:8** — The noun techinnah usually means 'supplication for favor,' but here (as in Josh 11:20) denotes 'favor' shown by God. In 'so that our God may give light to our eyes,' 'our God' functions syntactically as the subject of the Hiphil infinitive construct of 'or ('give light').
- **9:13** — Literally 'You have held back below our iniquity' (Qal of chasak, 'hold back, spare'), an elliptical idiom meaning that God restrained His judgment to less than their iniquity deserved.
