## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The written ketiv spells the king's name with an extra waw before the final resh (Nebuchadnezzor), while the read qere followed here is Nebuchadnezzar. Hebrew literally reads "the sons of the province," an idiom for the inhabitants of the Persian province of Judah.
- **2:6** — The preposition lamed ("of") specifies subdivisions within the clan of Pahath-moab, as also in 2:16 ("of Hezekiah"). Hebrew juxtaposes "Jeshua" and "Joab" without a conjunction (asyndeton), smoothly rendered "Jeshua and Joab."
- **2:21** — Beginning here, the census includes place names alongside family clans, alternating between Hebrew "sons of" (2:21, 24-26, 29, 33-35) and "men of" (2:22-23, 27-28). Both expressions denote residents or citizens associated with those towns.
- **2:25** — The Masoretic Text reads "Kiriath-arim," a shortened form of the town name "Kiriath-jearim" found in the parallel list at Nehemiah 7:29.
- **2:43** — Hebrew ha-netinim (from the root natan, "to give") literally means "the given ones," designating a hereditary class of servants assigned to temple duties (also in 2:58, 70).
- **2:46** — The written ketiv reads "Shamlai," while the read qere followed here transposes the middle consonants to "Shalmai."
- **2:50** — The written ketiv reads "Nephisim" (with yod), while the read qere followed here reads "Nephusim" (with waw).
- **2:62** — The Pual verb wayyego'alu (from ga'al II, "to defile") with the preposition "from" forms a pregnant construction meaning "they were disqualified as ceremonially unclean and excluded from the priesthood." Unlike Nehemiah 7:64 ("it was not found"), the Masoretic Text here uses the plural Niphal verb "they were not found."
- **2:63** — Hebrew ha-tirshata is an Old Persian official title designating the provincial governor of Judah.
