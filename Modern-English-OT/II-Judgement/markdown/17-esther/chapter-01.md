## Text

**1:1** Now it happened in the days of Ahasuerus, the very Ahasuerus who reigned from India to Cush over one hundred twenty-seven provinces,

**1:2** in those days, as King Ahasuerus sat on his royal throne, which was in Susa the citadel,

**1:3** in the third year of his reign he gave a banquet for all his officials and his servants; the army of Persia and Media, the nobles, and the officials of the provinces were before him

**1:4** as he displayed the riches of the glory of his kingdom and the splendor of the majesty of his greatness for many days, one hundred eighty days.

**1:5** When these days were completed, the king gave a seven-day banquet for all the people present in Susa the citadel, from greatest to least, in the courtyard of the garden of the king's pavilion.

**1:6** Hangings of white cotton and violet were secured with cords of fine linen and purple onto silver rings and pillars of white marble; couches of gold and silver stood upon a pavement of porphyry, white marble, mother-of-pearl, and dark stone.

**1:7** Drinks were served in golden vessels, each vessel differing from the next, and royal wine was abundant, in keeping with the king's bounty.

**1:8** And the drinking followed the law, with no one compelling another, for so the king had directed every chief officer of his house, to act according to each man's wish.

**1:9** Queen Vashti also gave a banquet for the women in the royal palace belonging to King Ahasuerus.

**1:10** On the seventh day, when the king's heart was glad with wine, he ordered Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar, and Carcas, the seven eunuchs who attended in the presence of King Ahasuerus,

**1:11** to bring Queen Vashti before the king wearing the royal crown, in order to show the peoples and the officials her beauty, for she was lovely in appearance.

**1:12** Yet Queen Vashti refused to come at the king's word conveyed by the eunuchs. Then the king grew intensely angry, and his fury burned within him.

**1:13** Then the king spoke to the wise men who knew the times, for that was the king's procedure before all who knew law and justice,

**1:14** and those nearest to him were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena, and Memucan, the seven officials of Persia and Media who saw the king's face and sat first in the kingdom:

**1:15** "According to the law, what is to be done with Queen Vashti, because she has not carried out the command of King Ahasuerus conveyed by the eunuchs?"

**1:16** Then Memucan said in the presence of the king and the officials, "Queen Vashti has not wronged the king alone, but also all the officials and all the peoples who are in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus.

**1:17** For the queen's conduct will go out to all the women, making their husbands despised in their eyes as they say, 'King Ahasuerus commanded that Queen Vashti be brought before him, yet she did not come.'

**1:18** This very day the noble ladies of Persia and Media who have heard of the queen's conduct will speak out to all the king's officials, and there will be plenty of contempt and anger.

**1:19** If it pleases the king, let a royal order go out from his presence, and let it be written among the laws of Persia and Media so that it cannot be repealed, that Vashti is not to come before King Ahasuerus, and let the king give her royal position to another who is better than she.

**1:20** When the decree that the king makes is heard throughout all his kingdom, vast though it is, all the women will give honor to their husbands, from greatest to least."

**1:21** This proposal was pleasing in the eyes of the king and the officials, and the king acted according to the word of Memucan.

**1:22** He sent letters to all the king's provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, that every man should rule in his own household and speak in the language of his own people.

## Translator Notes

- **1:6** — The Hebrew fabric term karpas, a hapax legomenon of Indo-Iranian origin, denotes fine cotton. Several of the mosaic pavement terms (bahat, dar, and soheret) are also unique in Biblical Hebrew and are rendered approximately as porphyry, mother-of-pearl, and dark stone.
- **1:7** — The idiom 'in keeping with the king's bounty' translates the literal Hebrew 'according to the hand of the king' (also in 2:18). 'Drinks were served' renders the Hiphil infinitive construct of 'give to drink' used substantivally.
- **1:16** — Ketiv reads 'Mumcan'; Qere reads 'Memucan', matching the spelling in 1:14 and 1:21.
- **1:22** — The closing clause translates the Masoretic Hebrew literally ('and speaking in the language of his own people'), an unusual phrase often understood as asserting the husband's linguistic authority in a mixed-language household.
