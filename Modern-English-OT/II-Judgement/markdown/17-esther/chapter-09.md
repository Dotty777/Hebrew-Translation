## Text

**9:1** Now in the twelfth month, that is, the month of Adar, on its thirteenth day, when the king's command and his edict were about to be carried out, on the day when the enemies of the Jews had hoped to gain mastery over them, the reverse happened, so that the Jews themselves gained mastery over those who hated them.

**9:2** The Jews assembled in their cities throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus to lay hands on those who sought their harm. And no one stood before them, for the dread of them had fallen upon all the peoples.

**9:3** And all the officials of the provinces, the satraps, the governors, and those who did the king's business were supporting the Jews, because the dread of Mordecai had fallen upon them.

**9:4** For Mordecai was great in the king's house, and his fame spread throughout all the provinces, for the man Mordecai kept growing greater.

**9:6** And in the citadel of Susa the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men.

**9:7** And Parshandatha, and Dalphon, and Aspatha,

**9:8** and Poratha, and Adalia, and Aridatha,

**9:9** and Parmashta, and Arisai, and Aridai, and Vaizatha—

**9:10** the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the adversary of the Jews—they killed; but they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**9:11** On that day the number of those killed in the citadel of Susa came before the king.

**9:12** And the king said to Queen Esther, "In the citadel of Susa the Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman. What have they done in the rest of the king's provinces? Now what is your petition? It will be given to you. And what is your further request? It will be done."

**9:13** Then Esther said, "If it seems good to the king, let it be granted to the Jews who are in Susa tomorrow also to act according to today's edict, and let them hang the ten sons of Haman on the wooden pole."

**9:14** So the king commanded that this be done. An edict was issued in Susa, and they hanged the ten sons of Haman.

**9:15** The Jews who were in Susa assembled also on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and killed three hundred men in Susa, but they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**9:16** Now the rest of the Jews who were in the king's provinces assembled and defended their lives, and gained rest from their enemies, and killed seventy-five thousand of those who hated them; but they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**9:17** This took place on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar, and on its fourteenth day they rested and made it a day of banqueting and gladness.

**9:18** But the Jews who were in Susa assembled on its thirteenth day and on its fourteenth day, and on its fifteenth day they rested and made it a day of banqueting and gladness.

**9:19** Therefore the rural Jews, who live in the unwalled towns, observe the fourteenth day of the month of Adar as a day of gladness and banqueting, a festive day, and for sending food portions to one another.

**9:20** Then Mordecai wrote down these things and sent letters to all the Jews who were in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, both near and far,

**9:21** establishing for them that they should observe the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and its fifteenth day every single year,

**9:22** as the days on which the Jews rested from their enemies, and as the month that was turned for them from sorrow into gladness and from mourning into a festive day, to make them days of banqueting and gladness, and of sending food portions to one another and gifts to the needy.

**9:23** So the Jews accepted what they had begun to do and what Mordecai had written to them.

**9:24** For Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the adversary of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them, and had cast Pur, that is, the lot, to crush them and to destroy them.

**9:25** But when it came before the king, he commanded in writing that his wicked plot, which he had plotted against the Jews, should return upon his own head, and they hanged him and his sons on the wooden pole.

**9:26** Therefore they called these days Purim, after the name of Pur. Therefore, because of all the words of this letter, and what they had seen concerning this matter, and what had come upon them,

**9:27** the Jews established and accepted upon themselves, upon their descendants, and upon all who joined themselves to them, without fail, to keep observing these two days according to what was written for them and at their appointed time every single year.

**9:28** And these days are to be remembered and observed throughout every generation, every family, every province, and every city; and these days of Purim will not pass away from among the Jews, nor will their remembrance come to an end among their descendants.

**9:29** Then Queen Esther daughter of Abihail and Mordecai the Jew wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter of Purim.

**9:31** to establish these days of Purim at their appointed times, just as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had established for them, and just as they had established for themselves and for their descendants the matters of the fasts and their outcry.

**9:32** And the command of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim, and it was written in the book.

## Translator Notes

- **9:1** — Hebrew venahafoch hu uses a Niphal infinitive absolute of haphach ("turn, overturn") with the pronoun hu, literally "and it was overturned," emphasizing the complete reversal.
- **9:16** — As in 8:8, 9:6, 9:12, and 9:17-18, Late Biblical Hebrew frequently continues a finite verb with a chain of infinitives absolute (here Qal amod, noach, and harog) functioning as past-tense narrative verbs.
- **9:19** — The ketiv reads happeruzim and the qere reads happerazim, variant plural forms meaning "rural dwellers" or "inhabitants of unwalled towns," matching perazot ("unwalled towns") in the same clause.
- **9:25** — The feminine suffix in bevo'ah ("when it came" or "when she came") may refer either to the matter of Haman's plot (feminine machashavah) or to Esther herself. Hebrew im-hassepher literally reads "with the letter," meaning by written order.
- **9:27** — The ketiv reads the singular Piel perfect veqibbel ("and accepted," treating "the Jews" collectively as in 9:23), whereas the qere reads the plural veqibbelu ("and they accepted") in agreement with the preceding plural qiyyemu.
- **9:30 *(additional)*** — The Hebrew verb is masculine singular, referring to Mordecai.
