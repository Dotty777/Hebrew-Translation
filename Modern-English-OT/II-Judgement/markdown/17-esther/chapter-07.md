## Text

**7:1** Then the king and Haman came to drink with Queen Esther.

**7:2** On the second day also, at the wine banquet, the king said to Esther, "What is your request, Queen Esther? It will be given to you. And what is your petition? Up to half the kingdom, it will be done."

**7:3** Then Queen Esther answered and said, "If I have found favor in your eyes, King, and if it seems good to the king, let my life be given to me as my request, and my people as my petition.

**7:4** For we have been sold, I and my people, to be exterminated, to be killed, and to be destroyed. Now if we had been sold as male and female slaves, I would have kept silent, for the distress would not be worth the harm to the king."

**7:5** Then King Ahasuerus spoke and said to Queen Esther, "Who is he, and where is he, whose heart has emboldened him to do so?"

**7:6** And Esther said, "An adversary and an enemy—this wicked Haman!" Then Haman was gripped with terror before the king and the queen.

**7:7** The king rose in his fury from the wine banquet and went into the palace garden, while Haman remained to plead for his life with Queen Esther, for he saw that disaster had been determined against him by the king.

**7:8** When the king returned from the palace garden to the hall of the wine banquet, Haman was falling onto the couch where Esther was. And the king said, "Will he even assault the queen with me in the house?" As the word left the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

**7:9** Then Harbonah, one of the eunuchs before the king, said, "Moreover, look, the wooden pole that Haman made for Mordecai, who spoke good concerning the king, is standing at Haman's house, fifty cubits high." And the king said, "Hang him on it!"

**7:10** So they hanged Haman on the wooden pole that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the king's fury subsided.

## Translator Notes

- **7:4** — The final clause is a noted Hebrew crux: the noun hatzar can mean either "the distress" or "the adversary," and nezeq (a late Hebrew/Aramaic loanword) means "harm, damage, or loss," yielding either "the distress would not be worth the harm to the king" or "the adversary could not compensate for the king's loss."
- **7:5** — Hebrew idiom "whose heart has filled him to do so" (Qal perfect of male), expressing bold presumption. The Masoretic Text also repeats the verb "said" ("spoke and said") for dramatic emphasis.
- **7:9** — Hebrew ha'etz literally means "the wood" or "the tree," here referring to the fifty-cubit-high wooden stake or gallows Haman erected for execution and public display.
