## Text

**10:1** And King Ahasuerus placed a tax on the land and the coastlands of the sea.

**10:2** And all the deeds of his power and his might, and the detailed account of the greatness of Mordecai, whom the king made great, are they not written down in the scroll of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia?

**10:3** For Mordecai the Jew was second to King Ahasuerus, and great among the Jews, and favored by the multitude of his brothers, seeking good for his people and speaking peace for all his offspring.

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — The ketiv writes the king's name defectively without its two waws (אחשרש), while the qere reads the standard spelling אֲחַשְׁוֵרוֹשׁ ("Ahasuerus"). The noun מַס denotes forced labor in earlier Hebrew, but in this Late Biblical Hebrew context it refers to a tax or tribute.
- **10:2** — The Piel verb גִּדְּלוֹ ("made him great") plays on the cognate noun גְּדֻלָּה ("greatness") here and the adjective גָּדוֹל ("great") in 10:3; the relative clause אֲשֶׁר גִּדְּלוֹ הַמֶּלֶךְ may also be rendered "to which the king elevated him." The phrase סֵפֶר דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים is literally "the scroll of the events of the days," denoting royal chronicles or annals.
- **10:3** — The Qal passive participle רָצוּי ("favored, accepted") pairs with רֹב אֶחָיו, which denotes "the multitude of his brothers" and can also be understood as "the majority of his brothers." The Qal active participle phrase וְדֹבֵר שָׁלוֹם לְכׇל־זַרְעוֹ literally means "speaking peace for [or to] all his seed/offspring," referring to advocating the peace and welfare of all his descendants or wider kin.
