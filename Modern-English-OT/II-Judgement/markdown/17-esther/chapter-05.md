## Text

**5:1** Now it happened on the third day that Esther put on royal attire and stood in the inner courtyard of the king's palace, facing the king's palace, while the king was sitting on his royal throne in the royal palace, facing the entrance of the palace.

**5:2** When the king saw Queen Esther standing in the courtyard, she gained favor in his eyes, and the king extended to Esther the gold scepter that was in his hand. So Esther approached and touched the tip of the scepter.

**5:3** Then the king said to her, "What is the matter, Queen Esther, and what is your request? Even up to half the kingdom, it will be granted to you."

**5:4** Esther said, "If it seems good to the king, let the king and Haman come today to the banquet that I have prepared for him."

**5:5** Then the king said, "Hurry Haman here, to carry out Esther's word." So the king and Haman came to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

**5:6** And the king said to Esther during the wine banquet, "What is your petition? It will be granted to you. And what is your request? Even up to half the kingdom, it will be done."

**5:7** Esther answered and said, "My petition and my request:

**5:8** If I have found favor in the eyes of the king, and if it seems good to the king to grant my petition and to carry out my request, let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I will prepare for them, and tomorrow I will do according to the king's word."

**5:9** So Haman went out on that day joyful and cheerful at heart. Yet when Haman saw Mordecai at the king's gate, that he neither stood up nor trembled at his presence, Haman was filled with fury against Mordecai.

**5:10** Yet Haman held himself in check and went to his house. Then he sent and brought his friends and his wife Zeresh.

**5:11** And Haman recounted to them the splendor of his wealth, the great number of his sons, all the ways the king had magnified him, and how he had exalted him above the officials and the king's servants.

**5:12** And Haman said, "Furthermore, Queen Esther brought no one with the king to the banquet she had prepared except me, and for tomorrow also I am invited by her along with the king.

**5:13** Yet all of this counts for nothing to me whenever I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king's gate."

**5:14** Then his wife Zeresh and all his friends said to him, "Let them make a wooden pole fifty cubits high, and in the morning speak to the king so that they may hang Mordecai on it; then go joyfully with the king to the banquet." The proposal seemed good to Haman, and he made the wooden pole.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Literally 'put on royalty' (wattilbash Ester malkhut), an elliptical expression for royal robes or attire.
- **5:7** — Hebrew opens with an abrupt suspended subject ('My petition and my request') that builds suspense before Esther completes her thought with the conditional clauses in verse 8.
- **5:14** — Hebrew ets literally means 'wood' or 'tree,' here referring to a tall wooden stake or pole (fifty cubits, about 75 feet) for public hanging or impalement, traditionally rendered 'gallows.'
