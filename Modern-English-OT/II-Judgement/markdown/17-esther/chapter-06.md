## Text

**6:1** On that night, the king's sleep fled, and he gave orders to bring the book of records, the chronicles, and they were being read before the king.

**6:2** And it was found written how Mordecai had reported concerning Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs from among the guards of the threshold, who had sought to stretch out a hand against King Ahasuerus.

**6:3** Then the king said, "What honor and distinction has been done for Mordecai because of this?" And the king's attendants who served him said, "Not a thing has been done for him."

**6:4** So the king said, "Who is in the courtyard?" Now Haman had come into the outer courtyard of the king's palace to speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on the wooden pole that he had prepared for him.

**6:5** The king's attendants said to him, "Look, Haman is standing in the courtyard." And the king said, "Let him come in."

**6:6** So Haman came in, and the king said to him, "What should be done for the man in whose honor the king delights?" Now Haman said in his heart, "To whom would the king delight to show honor more than to me?"

**6:7** Then Haman said to the king, "For a man in whose honor the king delights,

**6:8** let them bring a royal robe that the king has worn, and a horse that the king has ridden and on whose head a royal crown has been set.

**6:9** And let the robe and the horse be placed in the hand of one of the king's noble officials, and let them clothe the man in whose honor the king delights, and have him ride on the horse through the city square, and proclaim before him, 'This is how it shall be done for the man in whose honor the king delights.'"

**6:10** Then the king said to Haman, "Hurry, take the robe and the horse, just as you have spoken, and do so for Mordecai the Jew, who sits at the king's gate. Do not leave out a single thing from all that you have spoken."

**6:11** So Haman took the robe and the horse, and clothed Mordecai, and had him ride through the city square, and proclaimed before him, "This is how it shall be done for the man in whose honor the king delights."

**6:12** Then Mordecai returned to the king's gate, while Haman hurried to his house, mourning, his head covered.

**6:13** And Haman recounted to his wife Zeresh and to all his friends everything that had happened to him. Then his wise men and his wife Zeresh said to him, "If Mordecai, before whom you have begun to fall, is of the offspring of the Jews, you will not prevail against him, but you will certainly fall before him."

**6:14** While they were still speaking with him, the king's eunuchs arrived and rushed to bring Haman to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

## Translator Notes

- **6:1** — Hebrew nadedah shenat hammelekh literally personifies sleep as fleeing, and the periphrastic construction wayyihyu niqra'im (Qal wayyiqtol of hayah plus Niphal participle of qara) conveys continuous action ('were being read').
- **6:8** — The relative clause 'and on whose head a royal crown has been set' (wa-asher nittan keter malkhut be-ro'sho, Niphal perfect of natan) syntactically parallels the preceding clause modifying the horse, reflecting royal Persian horse ornaments; verse 9 likewise specifies only the robe and the horse for the honoree.
- **6:9** — The Qal infinitive absolute we-naton ('and let ... be placed') functions jussively, a characteristic Late Biblical Hebrew construction in Esther; happartemim ('noble') is an Old Persian loanword denoting the highest-ranking nobles.
- **6:13** — The pairing of the Qal infinitive absolute with the imperfect (nafol tippol, 'you will certainly fall') intensifies the certainty of Haman's downfall.
