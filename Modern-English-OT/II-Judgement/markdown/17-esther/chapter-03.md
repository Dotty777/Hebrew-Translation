## Text

**3:1** After these events, King Ahasuerus promoted Haman, son of Hammedatha the Agagite, and elevated him, placing his seat above all the officials who were with him.

**3:2** And all the king's servants who were at the king's gate were kneeling and bowing low to Haman, for so the king had commanded concerning him. Yet Mordecai would neither kneel nor bow low.

**3:3** Then the king's servants who were at the king's gate said to Mordecai, "Why are you transgressing the king's command?"

**3:4** Now it happened, as they spoke to him day after day and he would not listen to them, that they reported it to Haman to see whether Mordecai's words would stand, for he had told them that he was a Jew.

**3:5** When Haman saw that Mordecai was neither kneeling nor bowing low to him, Haman was filled with fury.

**3:6** Yet in his eyes he disdained to lay a hand on Mordecai alone, since they had told him of Mordecai's people; so Haman sought to destroy all the Jews who were throughout the whole kingdom of Ahasuerus, the people of Mordecai.

**3:7** In the first month, which is the month of Nisan, in the twelfth year of King Ahasuerus, they cast Pur, that is, the lot, before Haman from day to day and from month to month, to the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar.

**3:8** Then Haman said to King Ahasuerus, "There is a certain people scattered and separated among the peoples throughout all the provinces of your kingdom. Their laws differ from those of every people, and they do not keep the king's laws, so it does not profit the king to leave them alone.

**3:9** If it pleases the king, let it be written to annihilate them, and I will weigh out ten thousand talents of silver into the hands of those who carry out the business, to bring into the king's treasuries."

**3:10** Then the king removed his signet ring from his hand and gave it to Haman, son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the adversary of the Jews.

**3:11** And the king said to Haman, "The silver is given to you, and the people also, to do with them as is good in your eyes."

**3:12** Then the king's scribes were summoned in the first month, on its thirteenth day, and everything Haman commanded was written to the king's satraps, to the governors over each province, and to the officials of each people, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language; it was written in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed with the king's signet ring.

**3:13** Letters were dispatched by couriers to all the king's provinces to destroy, to kill, and to annihilate all the Jews, from young to old, little children and women, on a single day, the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar, and to plunder their possessions.

**3:14** A copy of the document was to be issued as law in every province, made public to all the peoples, so that they would be ready for that day.

**3:15** The couriers set out in haste at the king's word, and the law was issued in Susa the citadel. The king and Haman sat down to drink, while the city of Susa was bewildered.

## Translator Notes

- **3:4** — Ketiv reads 'in their speaking' (be'omram); Qere reads 'as they spoke' (ke'omram), a minor orthographic difference with essentially the same temporal sense.
- **3:7** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads elliptically 'from day to day and from month to the twelfth month' (with an impersonal Hiphil verb 'one cast' for 'they cast'), indicating that the casting of the lot (pur, an Akkadian loanword) progressed through the calendar up to the twelfth month, Adar.
- **3:13** — 'Letters were dispatched' renders a Niphal infinitive absolute (nishloah) functioning as a finite passive verb, a stylistic feature of Late Biblical Hebrew.
