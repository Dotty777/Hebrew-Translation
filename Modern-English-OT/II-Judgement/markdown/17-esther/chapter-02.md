## Text

**2:1** After these events, when the fury of King Ahasuerus had subsided, he remembered Vashti, both what she had done and what had been decreed against her.

**2:2** Then the king's young men who attended him said, "Let young virgin women who are lovely in appearance be sought for the king.

**2:3** And let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom, so that they may gather every young virgin woman who is lovely in appearance to Susa the citadel, to the house of the women, under the charge of Hegai, the king's eunuch, keeper of the women; and let their cosmetic treatments be given to them.

**2:4** Then let the young woman who is pleasing in the king's eyes reign as queen in place of Vashti." This proposal was pleasing in the king's eyes, and he did so.

**2:5** There was a Jewish man in Susa the citadel whose name was Mordecai, son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite,

**2:6** who had been carried into exile from Jerusalem among the exiles who were exiled with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had exiled.

**2:7** He was raising Hadassah, that is, Esther, his uncle's daughter, for she had neither father nor mother. Now the young woman was beautiful in form and lovely in appearance, and when her father and her mother died, Mordecai took her as his own daughter.

**2:8** So it happened, when the king's order and his law were heard and when many young women were gathered to Susa the citadel under the charge of Hegai, that Esther was taken into the king's palace, under the charge of Hegai, keeper of the women.

**2:9** The young woman was pleasing in his eyes and won kindness before him, so he hurried to supply her with her cosmetic treatments and her portions, as well as the seven suitable young women to be given to her from the king's palace, and he moved her and her young women to the best part of the house of the women.

**2:10** Esther did not disclose her people or her ancestry, for Mordecai had commanded her not to disclose it.

**2:11** Every single day Mordecai walked back and forth in front of the courtyard of the house of the women to learn of Esther's well-being and what was being done with her.

**2:12** Now when each young woman's turn arrived to go in to King Ahasuerus, at the end of her twelve months under the regulation for the women—for that was how the days of their purifying treatments were completed, six months with oil of myrrh and six months with perfumes and cosmetic treatments for the women—

**2:13** in this manner the young woman would go in to the king: whatever she asked for would be given to her to take with her from the house of the women to the king's palace.

**2:14** In the evening she would go in, and in the morning she would return to the second house of the women, under the charge of Shaashgaz, the king's eunuch, keeper of the concubines. She would not go in to the king again unless the king delighted in her and she was summoned by name.

**2:15** Now when the turn arrived for Esther, daughter of Abihail the uncle of Mordecai, who had taken her as his own daughter, to go in to the king, she asked for nothing except what Hegai, the king's eunuch, keeper of the women, advised. And Esther kept winning favor in the eyes of all who saw her.

**2:16** So Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus, into his royal palace, in the tenth month, which is the month of Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign.

**2:17** And the king loved Esther more than all the women, and she won favor and kindness before him more than all the virgins; so he placed the royal crown on her head and made her queen in place of Vashti.

**2:18** Then the king gave a great banquet for all his officials and his servants, Esther's banquet; he also granted relief to the provinces and distributed gifts in keeping with the king's bounty.

**2:19** Now when virgins were gathered a second time, Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate.

**2:20** Esther still did not disclose her ancestry or her people, just as Mordecai had commanded her; for Esther kept carrying out Mordecai's command, just as when she was being raised by him.

**2:21** In those days, while Mordecai was sitting at the king's gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs from among the guards of the threshold, grew angry and sought to lay a hand on King Ahasuerus.

**2:22** The matter became known to Mordecai, and he told Queen Esther, and Esther reported it to the king in Mordecai's name.

**2:23** When the matter was investigated and found to be so, both of them were hanged on a wooden pole. And this was written in the book of the chronicles in the king's presence.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — The Masoretic Text spells the eunuch's name 'Hege' here, whereas 2:8 and 2:15 spell it 'Hegai'; both refer to the same official. The final clause uses a Qal infinitive absolute ('giving') with jussive force.
- **2:18** — The Hebrew hapax legomenon hanahah (from the root meaning 'to rest/grant rest') denotes relief or release, variously understood as a public holiday or a remission of taxes and compulsory service.
- **2:23** — 'Wooden pole' translates Hebrew ets (literally 'wood' or 'tree'), referring in Persian penal practice to a wooden stake for impalement or a gallows.
