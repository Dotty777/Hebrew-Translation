## Text

**8:1** On that day King Ahasuerus gave to Queen Esther the house of Haman, the adversary of the Jews. And Mordecai came before the king, for Esther had told what he was to her.

**8:2** Then the king took off his signet ring, which he had taken away from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai. And Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman.

**8:3** Then Esther spoke again before the king, fell at his feet, wept, and pleaded with him to avert the evil of Haman the Agagite and his plot that he had plotted against the Jews.

**8:4** The king held out the golden scepter to Esther. So Esther rose and stood before the king.

**8:5** And she said, "If it seems good to the king, and if I have found favor before him, and the matter seems proper before the king, and I am pleasing in his eyes, let it be written to revoke the letters, the plot of Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, which he wrote to destroy the Jews who are in all the king's provinces.

**8:6** For how can I bear to look upon the disaster that will overtake my people? And how can I bear to look upon the destruction of my relatives?"

**8:7** Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther and to Mordecai the Jew, "Look, I have given the house of Haman to Esther, and they have hanged him on the wooden pole because he stretched out his hand against the Jews.

**8:8** Now you yourselves write concerning the Jews whatever seems good in your eyes, in the king's name, and seal it with the king's signet ring; for a document that is written in the king's name and sealed with the king's signet ring cannot be revoked."

**8:9** So the king's scribes were summoned at that time, in the third month, that is, the month of Sivan, on its twenty-third day; and it was written according to all that Mordecai commanded to the Jews, and to the satraps, the governors, and the officials of the provinces from India to Cush, one hundred twenty-seven provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, and to the Jews in their own script and in their own language.

**8:10** He wrote in the name of King Ahasuerus, sealed it with the king's signet ring, and sent letters by couriers on horses, riding royal steeds, the offspring of stud mares,

**8:11** by which the king allowed the Jews who were in every city to assemble and to defend their lives, to exterminate, to kill, and to destroy any armed force of any people or province that would attack them, children and women, and to plunder their spoil,

**8:12** on a single day in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, that is, the month of Adar.

**8:13** A copy of the document was to be issued as an edict in every province and made public to all the peoples, so that the Jews would be ready for that day to avenge themselves on their enemies.

**8:14** The couriers, riding the royal steeds, went out hurried and pressed onward by the king's command. And the edict was issued in the citadel of Susa.

**8:15** Then Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal apparel of blue and white, with a large golden crown and a cloak of fine linen and purple; and the city of Susa cheered and rejoiced.

**8:16** To the Jews came light and gladness, joy and honor.

**8:17** And in every single province and in every single city, wherever the king's command and his edict arrived, there was gladness and joy for the Jews, a banquet and a festive day. And many from the peoples of the land declared themselves Jews, because the dread of the Jews had fallen upon them.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Here and in 8:7, 8:13, 9:15, and 9:18, the written ketiv spells "the Jews" with a double yod (hayyehudiyyim), while the read qere simplifies the form to hayyehudim, with no change in meaning.
- **8:10** — The rare Persian loanwords achashteranim ("royal") and rammakim (hapax legomenon, "stud mares" or "breeding herds") underscore the elite speed of the imperial postal relay.
- **8:13** — The ketiv reads atudim and the qere reads atidim, orthographic variants of the adjective meaning "ready" or "prepared."
- **8:17** — The verb mityahadim is a hapax legomenon Hithpael participle derived from the gentilic "Jew," meaning either "declared themselves Jews" or "became Jews." Also, "a festive day" literally translates Hebrew yom tov ("a good day").
