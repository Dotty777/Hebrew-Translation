## Text

**4:1** Now Mordecai learned everything that had been done, and Mordecai tore his garments and put on sackcloth and ashes. Then he went out into the middle of the city and cried out a great and bitter cry.

**4:2** He came up to the front of the king's gate, for no one was allowed to enter the king's gate in sackcloth attire.

**4:3** In every single province, wherever the king's command and his decree reached, there was great mourning among the Jews, with fasting, weeping, and wailing; and sackcloth and ashes were spread out for many.

**4:4** Then Esther's female attendants and her eunuchs came and told her, and the queen was greatly anguished. She sent garments to clothe Mordecai and to remove his sackcloth from him, but he would not accept them.

**4:5** So Esther summoned Hathach, one of the king's eunuchs whom he had stationed before her, and instructed him concerning Mordecai, to find out what this was and what it was about.

**4:7** And Mordecai told him everything that had happened to him, as well as the exact sum of silver that Haman had promised to weigh out into the king's treasuries for the Jews, to destroy them.

**4:8** He also gave him a copy of the written decree that had been issued in Susa to annihilate them, to show Esther and inform her, and to charge her to go in to the king to implore his favor and to appeal before him on behalf of her people.

**4:9** Then Hathach came and reported Mordecai's words to Esther.

**4:10** Then Esther spoke to Hathach and gave him instructions for Mordecai:

**4:11** "All the king's servants and the people of the king's provinces know that for any man or woman who goes in to the king into the inner courtyard without being summoned, there is one law: to be put to death, except the one to whom the king extends the gold scepter so that he may live. Yet I myself have not been summoned to go in to the king these thirty days."

**4:12** So they reported Esther's words to Mordecai.

**4:13** Then Mordecai gave orders to reply to Esther, "Do not imagine within yourself that in the king's house you will escape more than all the Jews.

**4:14** For if you keep completely silent at this time, relief and rescue will arise for the Jews from another place, but you and your father's house will perish. And who knows whether it was for a time like this that you reached royalty?"

**4:15** Then Esther gave orders to reply to Mordecai:

**4:16** "Go, assemble all the Jews who are found in Susa, and fast on my behalf; do not eat and do not drink for three days, night and day. I and my female attendants will also fast in the same way. And then I will go in to the king, which is not according to the law; and should I perish, I perish."

**4:17** Then Mordecai went on his way and did according to everything Esther had charged him.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Hophal imperfect yutsa (from yatsa, 'spread out') literally describes sackcloth and ashes being spread out beneath mourners as bedding (cf. Isaiah 14:11; 58:5).
- **4:4** — Qere reads wattavo'nah ('and they came'), normalizing the orthographic Ketiv spelling; both have the same meaning. The verb wattitchalchal ('was greatly anguished') is a unique Hithpalpel form of chul/chil ('writhe, tremble in anguish').
- **4:7** — Qere reads bayyehudim ('for the Jews'), normalizing the Ketiv's lengthened gentilic plural spelling; parashat ('exact sum', also in 10:2) denotes a precise specification.
- **4:16** — Literally 'and as I perish, I perish' (we-kha-asher avadti avadti), a Hebrew idiom of resolute acceptance using repeated Qal perfects (cf. Genesis 43:14).
