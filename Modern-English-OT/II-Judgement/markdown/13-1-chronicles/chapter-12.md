## Text

**12:1** These are the men who came to David at Ziklag while he was still kept in restraint on account of Saul the son of Kish; they were among the mighty warriors, helpers in the battle.

**12:2** They were equipped with the bow, using both the right hand and the left with stones and with arrows from the bow; they were from Saul's kinsmen, of Benjamin.

**12:3** The chief was Ahiezer, then Joash, the sons of Shemaah the Gibeathite; and Jeziel and Pelet, the sons of Azmaveth; and Beracah, and Jehu the Anathothite;

**12:4** and Ishmaiah the Gibeonite, a mighty warrior among the Thirty and over the Thirty;

**12:5** and Jeremiah, Jahaziel, Johanan, and Jozabad the Gederathite.

**12:6** Eluzai, Jerimoth, Bealiah, Shemariah, and Shephatiah the Haruphite;

**12:7** Elkanah, Isshiah, Azarel, Joezer, and Jashobeam, the Korahites;

**12:8** and Joelah and Zebadiah, the sons of Jeroham from Gedor.

**12:9** From the Gadites, mighty warriors of valor separated themselves to David at the stronghold in the wilderness, men of the army for battle, wielding shield and spear; their faces were like the faces of lions, and they were as swift as gazelles upon the mountains:

**12:10** Ezer was the chief, Obadiah the second, Eliab the third,

**12:11** Mishmannah the fourth, Jeremiah the fifth,

**12:12** Attai the sixth, Eliel the seventh,

**12:13** Johanan the eighth, Elzabad the ninth,

**12:14** Jeremiah the tenth, Machbannai the eleventh.

**12:15** These from the sons of Gad were chiefs of the army; the least was one to a hundred, and the greatest to a thousand.

**12:16** These are the men who crossed the Jordan in the first month, when it was overflowing all its banks, and they forced into flight all those of the valleys, toward the east and toward the west.

**12:17** And some from the sons of Benjamin and Judah came up to the stronghold to David.

**12:18** David went out to meet them, and he answered and said to them, "If you have come to me in peace to help me, my heart will be united toward you; but if to betray me to my adversaries, though there is no violence in my hands, may the God of our fathers see and pass judgment."

**12:19** Then the Spirit clothed Amasai, chief of the officers: "We are yours, David, and with you, son of Jesse! Peace, peace to you, and peace to your helper, for your God has helped you!" So David received them and placed them among the chiefs of the troop.

**12:20** Some from Manasseh also defected to David when he came with the Philistines against Saul for battle, yet they did not help them, for after consultation the lords of the Philistines sent him away, saying, "At the cost of our heads he will defect to his master Saul."

**12:21** As he went to Ziklag, there defected to him from Manasseh Adnah, Jozabad, Jediael, Michael, Jozabad, Elihu, and Zillethai, chiefs of the thousands belonging to Manasseh.

**12:22** And they helped David against the raiding band, for all of them were mighty warriors of valor, and they became commanders in the army.

**12:23** For day by day men kept coming to David to help him, until there was a great camp, like the camp of God.

**12:24** Now these are the numbers of the divisions armed for the army who came to David at Hebron to turn the kingdom of Saul over to him, according to the command of the LORD:

**12:25** The sons of Judah bearing shield and spear were six thousand eight hundred, armed for the army.

**12:26** From the sons of Simeon, mighty warriors of valor for the army, seven thousand one hundred.

**12:27** From the sons of Levi, four thousand six hundred;

**12:28** and Jehoiada, the leader of the Aaronites, and with him three thousand seven hundred,

**12:29** and Zadok, a young man, a mighty warrior of valor, with twenty-two commanders from his father's house.

**12:30** From the sons of Benjamin, Saul's kinsmen, three thousand, for until that time the majority of them had kept guard for the house of Saul.

**12:31** From the sons of Ephraim, twenty thousand eight hundred, mighty warriors of valor, men of renown in their fathers' houses.

**12:32** From the half-tribe of Manasseh, eighteen thousand, who were designated by name to come and make David king.

**12:33** From the sons of Issachar, men with discernment for the times, knowing what Israel should do; their chiefs were two hundred, and all their kinsmen were at their command.

**12:34** From Zebulun, those going out to the army, drawing up for battle with all weapons of war, fifty thousand, to keep rank without a double heart.

**12:35** From Naphtali, one thousand commanders, and with them thirty-seven thousand with shield and spear.

**12:36** From the Danites, drawing up for battle, twenty-eight thousand six hundred.

**12:37** From Asher, those going out to the army to draw up for battle, forty thousand.

**12:38** And from beyond the Jordan, from the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh, with all weapons for military warfare, one hundred twenty thousand.

**12:39** All these men of war, keeping battle formation, came to Hebron with a whole heart to make David king over all Israel; and all the rest of Israel as well were of one heart to make David king.

**12:40** They were there with David three days, eating and drinking, for their kinsmen had made preparation for them.

**12:41** Moreover, those who were near to them, even as far as Issachar, Zebulun, and Naphtali, were bringing food on donkeys, camels, mules, and oxen: provisions of flour, fig cakes, raisin cakes, wine, oil, oxen, and sheep in abundance, for there was joy in Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — Ketiv/Qere: the qere reads we-Yiziel ("and Jeziel"), whereas the ketiv writes a form corresponding to "and Jezuel."
- **12:6** — Ketiv/Qere: the qere reads ha-Charufi ("the Haruphite"), whereas the ketiv writes a form corresponding to "the Hariphite."
- **12:15** — Hebrew echad le-me'ah ha-qatan we-ha-gadol le-alef ("lit. one to a hundred the least, and the greatest to a thousand") can denote either martial prowess (a match for a hundred or a thousand) or rank (commanding a hundred or a thousand).
- **12:19** — Ketiv/Qere: the qere reads ha-shalishim ("the officers"), whereas the ketiv writes ha-sheloshim ("the thirty"). Also, the Qal verb laveshah literally means "clothed," a vivid metaphor for the Spirit empowering a person (cf. Judg 6:34).
- **12:34** — The rare Qal infinitive construct la'ador means to keep battle rank (as in 12:39, oderei ma'arakhah), though some connect it with an Aramaizing form of "help"; Hebrew be-lo lev wa-lev is literally "without a heart and a heart," meaning undivided loyalty.
