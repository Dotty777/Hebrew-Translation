## Text

**20:1** Now it happened at the turn of the year, at the time when kings go out, that Joab led the force of the army and devastated the land of the sons of Ammon; he came and besieged Rabbah, while David stayed in Jerusalem. Then Joab struck Rabbah and demolished it.

**20:2** David took the crown of their king off his head, and he found it to be the weight of a talent of gold, with a precious stone in it; and it was on David's head. He also brought out the spoil of the city in a very great amount.

**20:3** He brought out the people who were in it and cut them with the saw, with sharp iron tools, and with saws. So David did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

**20:4** After this, war arose at Gezer with the Philistines. At that time Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Sippai, one of the descendants of the Rephaim, and they were subdued.

**20:5** Again there was war with the Philistines, and Elhanan the son of Jair struck down Lahmi, the brother of Goliath the Gittite, and the shaft of his spear was like a weavers' beam.

**20:6** Once more there was war at Gath, and there was a man of immense stature whose fingers and toes were six and six, twenty-four in all; he too was born to Rapha.

**20:7** When he taunted Israel, Jonathan the son of Shimea, David's brother, struck him down.

**20:8** These were born to Rapha in Gath; they fell at the hand of David and at the hand of his servants.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — Hebrew malkam means "their king," though the same consonants can also be vocalized as Milcom (or Malcam), the national deity of the Ammonites.
- **20:3** — The rare Qal verb wayyasar ("and he cut" or "sawed") differs from the parallel in 2 Samuel 12:31, which reads wayyasem ("and he set"). The Masoretic Text also repeats "with saws" (bamgerot) at the end of the list, where 2 Samuel 12:31 reads "with axes."
- **20:4** — Hebrew ha-repha'im ("the Rephaim") is plural here, whereas 20:6 and 20:8 use the singular with the definite article, ha-rapha ("Rapha" or "the Rephaite"), denoting a lineage of giant warriors.
- **20:5** — Ketiv reads "Jaor" (or "Jaur"); Qere reads "Jair" (ya'ir), which is translated here.
