## Text

**25:1** David and the commanders of the army separated for the service the sons of Asaph, of Heman, and of Jeduthun, who prophesied with lyres, harps, and cymbals. And the number of them, the men of work for their service, was:

**25:2** Of the sons of Asaph: Zaccur, Joseph, Nethaniah, and Asarelah, the sons of Asaph, under the direction of Asaph, who prophesied under the direction of the king.

**25:3** Of Jeduthun, the sons of Jeduthun: Gedaliah, Zeri, Jeshaiah, Hashabiah, and Mattithiah, six, under the direction of their father Jeduthun with the lyre, who prophesied in thanking and praising the LORD.

**25:4** Of Heman, the sons of Heman: Bukkiah, Mattaniah, Uzziel, Shebuel, Jerimoth, Hananiah, Hanani, Eliathah, Giddalti, Romamti-ezer, Joshbekashah, Mallothi, Hothir, and Mahazioth.

**25:5** All these were sons of Heman, the king's seer in the words of God, to lift up a horn; and God gave Heman fourteen sons and three daughters.

**25:6** All these were under the direction of their father for singing in the house of the LORD, with cymbals, harps, and lyres, for the service of the house of God. Asaph, Jeduthun, and Heman were under the direction of the king.

**25:7** And the number of them along with their brothers who were trained in singing to the LORD, all who were skilled, was two hundred eighty-eight.

**25:8** And they cast lots for their duties equally, the small just like the great, teacher together with student.

## Translator Notes

- **25:1** — Ketiv reads the plural noun 'the prophets,' while the translated Qere reads the Niphal participle 'who prophesied' (compare vv. 2-3).
- **25:3** — The Masoretic Hebrew lists five sons of Jeduthun here before the total 'six'; the sixth son, Shimei, appears in the lot list at v. 17.
- **25:5** — 'To lift up a horn' (Hiphil infinitive construct of rum with qeren) is a Hebrew idiom for exalting honor or strength, though some understand it literally of sounding a horn.
- **25:8** — 'Teacher' renders the Hiphil participle mebin (one who imparts understanding or is skilled), paired with talmid ('student'), which occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible.
- **25:9** — The Masoretic Hebrew omits the tally phrase ('he and his sons and his brothers, twelve') after Joseph, stating it only for Gedaliah.
