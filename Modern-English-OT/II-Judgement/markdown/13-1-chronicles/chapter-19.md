## Text

**19:1** After this it happened that Nahash king of the sons of Ammon died, and his son reigned in his place.

**19:2** Then David said, "I will show loyal love to Hanun son of Nahash, because his father showed loyal love to me." So David sent messengers to comfort him concerning his father. And David's servants came into the land of the sons of Ammon, to Hanun, to comfort him.

**19:3** But the officials of the sons of Ammon said to Hanun, "Is David honoring your father in your eyes, because he has sent comforters to you? Have his servants not come to you in order to explore, to overthrow, and to spy out the land?"

**19:4** So Hanun took David's servants, shaved them, cut off their garments in half up to the hips, and sent them away.

**19:5** And some went and told David about the men, so he sent to meet them, for the men were deeply humiliated. And the king said, "Stay at Jericho until your beards grow out, and then return."

**19:6** When the sons of Ammon saw that they had made themselves a stench to David, Hanun and the sons of Ammon sent one thousand talents of silver to hire for themselves chariots and horsemen from Aram-naharaim, from Aram-maacah, and from Zobah.

**19:7** So they hired for themselves thirty-two thousand chariots, along with the king of Maacah and his people, who came and encamped before Medeba. Meanwhile, the sons of Ammon gathered from their cities and came for battle.

**19:8** When David heard of it, he sent Joab and the entire army of mighty men.

**19:9** The sons of Ammon marched out and drew up in battle array at the entrance of the city, while the kings who had come were by themselves in the open field.

**19:10** When Joab saw that the battle front was facing him both in front and behind, he selected from all the select men in Israel and drew them up in array against the Arameans.

**19:11** The remainder of the people he placed under the hand of his brother Abishai, and they drew up in array against the sons of Ammon.

**19:12** He said, "If the Arameans prove too strong for me, then you shall come to my rescue; and if the sons of Ammon prove too strong for you, then I will rescue you.

**19:13** Be strong, and let us prove ourselves strong on behalf of our people and on behalf of the cities of our God; and may the LORD do what is good in His eyes."

**19:14** So Joab and the people who were with him advanced before the Arameans for battle, and they fled from before him.

**19:15** When the sons of Ammon saw that the Arameans had fled, they too fled from before his brother Abishai and went into the city. Then Joab came to Jerusalem.

**19:16** When the Arameans saw that they had been routed before Israel, they sent messengers and brought out the Arameans who were beyond the River, with Shophach, the commander of Hadadezer's army, at their head.

**19:17** When it was reported to David, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, came up to them, and drew up in array against them. So David drew up in battle array to meet the Arameans, and they fought against him.

**19:18** But the Arameans fled from before Israel, and David killed seven thousand charioteers and forty thousand foot soldiers of the Arameans; and he put Shophach, the commander of the army, to death.

**19:19** When the servants of Hadadezer saw that they had been routed before Israel, they made peace with David and served him. So the Arameans were no longer willing to rescue the sons of Ammon.

## Translator Notes

- **19:4** — Hebrew ha-mifsa'ah is a biblical hapax legomenon from the root pasa' ("to stride, step"), denoting the fork of the legs, i.e., the hips or crotch.
- **19:6** — Hebrew hitba'ashu is the Hithpael of ba'ash ("to stink"), literally "made themselves a stench," i.e., thoroughly offensive.
- **19:13** — Hebrew chazaq we-nitchazzeqah pairs the Qal imperative ("be strong") with the Hithpael cohortative ("let us prove ourselves strong") of the root chazaq.
- **19:18** — Hebrew rekhev (literally "chariots, chariotry") here denotes chariot crews ("charioteers") as the direct object of the verb "killed" (harag).
