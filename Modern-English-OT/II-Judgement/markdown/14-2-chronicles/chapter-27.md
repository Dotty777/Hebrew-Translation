## Text

**27:1** Jotham was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned in Jerusalem sixteen years. His mother's name was Jerushah daughter of Zadok.

**27:2** He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, according to all that his father Uzziah had done, only he did not enter the temple of the LORD. Yet the people were still acting corruptly.

**27:3** It was he who built the Upper Gate of the house of the LORD, and on the wall of Ophel he built extensively.

**27:4** He also built cities in the hill country of Judah, and in the forests he built fortresses and towers.

**27:5** He also fought with the king of the sons of Ammon and prevailed over them. So the sons of Ammon gave him in that year one hundred talents of silver, ten thousand kors of wheat, and ten thousand kors of barley. The sons of Ammon rendered this to him both in the second year and in the third.

**27:6** Thus Jotham grew strong, because he established his ways before the LORD his God.

**27:7** Now the rest of the deeds of Jotham, and all his wars and his ways, see, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah.

**27:9** Then Jotham lay down with his fathers, and they buried him in the city of David; and his son Ahaz became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **27:5** — A kor is a dry measure equal to ten ephahs (roughly 220 liters or 6 bushels); the Hiphil heshivu ("rendered," literally "returned") is an idiom for paying recurring tribute.
- **27:6** — The Hithpael vayyitchazzeq ("grew strong") echoes the recurring chazaq ("strong") motif across chapters 25-27, while the Hiphil hekhin ("established," literally "made firm/prepared") denotes steadfastly directing his conduct before God.
