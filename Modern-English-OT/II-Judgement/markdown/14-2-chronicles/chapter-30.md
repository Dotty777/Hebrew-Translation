## Text

**30:1** Then Hezekiah sent word across all Israel and Judah, and he wrote letters to Ephraim and Manasseh as well, to come to the house of the LORD in Jerusalem to observe Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**30:2** The king, his officials, and the entire assembly in Jerusalem had consulted together to observe the Passover in the second month.

**30:3** For they were unable to observe it at that time, because the priests had not consecrated themselves sufficiently and the people had not gathered to Jerusalem.

**30:4** The matter was right in the eyes of the king and in the eyes of the entire assembly.

**30:5** So they established a decree to circulate a proclamation throughout all Israel, from Beersheba even to Dan, to come to observe Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel, in Jerusalem, for they had not observed it in great numbers as it was written.

**30:6** So the couriers went across all Israel and Judah with the letters from the hand of the king and his officials, and in accordance with the king's command, saying, "Sons of Israel, return to the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, so that He may return to the escaped remnant left to you from the grasp of the kings of Assyria.

**30:7** Do not be like your fathers and like your brothers, who acted treacherously against the LORD, the God of their fathers, so that He handed them over to desolation, just as you see.

**30:8** Now do not harden your neck as your fathers did. Give your hand to the LORD and enter His sanctuary, which He has consecrated forever, and serve the LORD your God, so that His burning anger may turn back from you.

**30:9** For when you return to the LORD, your brothers and your children will find compassion before those who captured them and will return to this land; for gracious and compassionate is the LORD your God, and He will not avert His face from you if you return to Him."

**30:10** So the couriers were passing from city to city through the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, even as far as Zebulun, yet people kept laughing at them and scoffing at them.

**30:11** However, some men from Asher, Manasseh, and Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem.

**30:12** In Judah as well, the hand of God was present to give them one heart to carry out the command of the king and the officials by the word of the LORD.

**30:13** Many people gathered at Jerusalem to observe the Feast of Unleavened Bread in the second month, an exceedingly vast assembly.

**30:14** They rose up and removed the altars that were in Jerusalem; they also removed all the incense altars and cast them into the Kidron Valley.

**30:15** Then they slaughtered the Passover offering on the fourteenth day of the second month. The priests and the Levites were put to shame, so they consecrated themselves and brought burnt offerings into the house of the LORD.

**30:16** They stood at their posts according to their regulation, in keeping with the law of Moses the man of God, while the priests dashed the blood from the hand of the Levites.

**30:17** For there were many in the assembly who had not consecrated themselves; therefore the Levites were in charge of slaughtering the Passover offerings for everyone who was not clean, in order to consecrate them to the LORD.

**30:18** For a majority of the people, many from Ephraim and Manasseh, Issachar and Zebulun, had not purified themselves; instead, they ate the Passover contrary to what is written. Yet Hezekiah prayed on their behalf, saying, "May the good LORD make atonement for

**30:19** everyone who sets his heart to seek God, the LORD, the God of his fathers, even if not according to the purification of the sanctuary."

**30:20** So the LORD listened to Hezekiah and healed the people.

**30:21** The sons of Israel present in Jerusalem observed the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days with great gladness, while the Levites and the priests praised the LORD day by day with instruments of might to the LORD.

**30:22** Hezekiah spoke to the heart of all the Levites who demonstrated good insight for the LORD. So they ate the appointed feast throughout the seven days, sacrificing peace offerings and making confession to the LORD, the God of their fathers.

**30:23** Then the entire assembly took counsel together to observe seven more days, so they observed seven days in gladness.

**30:24** For Hezekiah king of Judah set apart for the assembly one thousand bulls and seven thousand sheep, and the officials set apart for the assembly one thousand bulls and ten thousand sheep; and priests consecrated themselves in great numbers.

**30:25** The entire assembly of Judah rejoiced, together with the priests and the Levites, and the entire assembly that came out of Israel, and the resident foreigners who came from the land of Israel and those living in Judah.

**30:26** So there was great gladness in Jerusalem, for since the days of Solomon son of David, king of Israel, nothing like this had happened in Jerusalem.

**30:27** Then the Levitical priests rose up and blessed the people, and their voice was heard, and their prayer came to His holy dwelling place, to heaven.

## Translator Notes

- **30:3** — The compound adverbial form lemadday (literally 'for what is enough') occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and denotes 'sufficiently' or 'in sufficient numbers.'
- **30:18** — The Masoretic verse division separates the preposition be'ad ('for') at the end of verse 18 from its object ('everyone who sets his heart...') at the start of verse 19. The Piel verb yekhapper with be'ad literally means 'make atonement for' and carries the sense of granting pardon.
- **30:21** — The phrase 'instruments of might' (kele-'oz) is a literal rendering of the Hebrew expression denoting loud or resounding musical instruments used in temple praise.
- **30:22** — The Masoretic text reads wayyo'khelu ('and they ate') with hammo'ed ('the appointed feast'), referring metonymically to eating the sacrificial festival meals over the seven days. The Hithpael participle mitwaddim ('making confession') differs in stem from the Hiphil 'giving thanks' in 31:2, though in this context it may encompass both confession and grateful acknowledgment.
