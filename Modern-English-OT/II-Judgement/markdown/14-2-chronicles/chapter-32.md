## Text

**32:1** After these acts and this faithfulness, Sennacherib king of Assyria came and entered Judah; he encamped against the fortified cities and determined to breach them for himself.

**32:2** And Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come and that his face was set for war against Jerusalem.

**32:3** So he took counsel with his officers and his mighty men to stop up the waters of the springs that were outside the city, and they helped him.

**32:4** A large crowd of people gathered together, and they stopped up all the springs and the stream that flowed through the middle of the land, saying, "Why should the kings of Assyria come and find abundant water?"

**32:5** He strengthened himself and rebuilt the entire wall that had been broken down, raised it up to the towers, and built another wall on the outside; he also strengthened the Millo in the City of David and made weapons in abundance and shields.

**32:6** He appointed battle commanders over the people, gathered them to himself in the square of the city gate, and spoke to their hearts, saying,

**32:7** "Be strong and resolute; do not fear and do not be dismayed before the king of Assyria or before all the multitude that is with him, for there is more with us than with him.

**32:8** With him is an arm of flesh, but with us is the LORD our God to help us and to fight our battles." And the people relied on the words of Hezekiah king of Judah.

**32:9** After this, Sennacherib king of Assyria sent his servants to Jerusalem, while he himself was before Lachish and all his power was with him, to Hezekiah king of Judah and to all Judah who were in Jerusalem, saying,

**32:10** "This is what Sennacherib king of Assyria says: On what are you trusting, that you remain under siege in Jerusalem?

**32:11** Is not Hezekiah misleading you to hand you over to die by famine and by thirst, saying, 'The LORD our God will rescue us from the grasp of the king of Assyria'?

**32:12** Was it not this same Hezekiah who removed His high places and His altars and spoke to Judah and Jerusalem, saying, 'Before one altar you shall worship, and on it you shall burn incense'?

**32:13** Do you not know what I and my fathers have done to all the peoples of the lands? Were the gods of the nations of the lands at all able to rescue their land from my hand?

**32:14** Who among all the gods of these nations that my fathers utterly destroyed was able to rescue his people from my hand, that your God should be able to rescue you from my hand?

**32:15** And now, do not let Hezekiah deceive you, and do not let him mislead you in this way, and do not believe him; for no god of any nation or kingdom has been able to rescue his people from my hand or from the hand of my fathers. How much less will your God rescue you from my hand!"

**32:16** His servants spoke even more against the LORD God and against His servant Hezekiah.

**32:17** He also wrote letters to taunt the LORD, the God of Israel, and to speak against Him, saying, "Just as the gods of the nations of the lands have not rescued their people from my hand, so the God of Hezekiah will not rescue His people from my hand."

**32:18** They called out in a loud voice in the language of Judah to the people of Jerusalem who were on the wall, to frighten them and throw them into panic, so that they might capture the city.

**32:19** And they spoke about the God of Jerusalem as about the gods of the peoples of the earth, the work of human hands.

**32:20** Then King Hezekiah and Isaiah son of Amoz, the prophet, prayed about this and cried out to heaven.

**32:21** And the LORD sent an angel who annihilated every mighty warrior, leader, and commander in the camp of the king of Assyria. So he returned with shame of face to his own land. And when he entered the house of his god, some of those who came from his own body struck him down there with the sword.

**32:22** So the LORD saved Hezekiah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib king of Assyria and from the hand of all others, and He cared for them on every side.

**32:23** Many brought gifts to the LORD at Jerusalem and precious things to Hezekiah king of Judah, and he was exalted in the sight of all the nations from that time on.

**32:24** In those days Hezekiah became ill to the point of death, and he prayed to the LORD; and He spoke to him and gave him a sign.

**32:25** But Hezekiah did not make a return according to the benefit bestowed on him, because his heart became proud; therefore wrath came upon him and upon Judah and Jerusalem.

**32:26** Then Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart, he and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so the wrath of the LORD did not come upon them in the days of Hezekiah.

**32:27** Now Hezekiah had exceedingly great wealth and honor, and he made treasuries for himself for silver, gold, precious stones, spices, shields, and every kind of desirable article,

**32:28** and storehouses for the produce of grain, new wine, and fresh oil, and stalls for every kind of livestock, and flocks for the folds.

**32:29** He also made cities for himself, along with flocks and herds in abundance, for God had given him very great possessions.

**32:30** It was this same Hezekiah who stopped up the upper outlet of the waters of Gihon and directed them straight down to the west side of the City of David. And Hezekiah succeeded in all his work.

**32:31** Even so, in the matter of the envoys of the rulers of Babylon who sent to him to inquire about the sign that had occurred in the land, God left him to test him, to know all that was in his heart.

**32:32** Now the rest of the acts of Hezekiah and his acts of loyal love, see, they are written in the vision of Isaiah son of Amoz, the prophet, in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.

**32:33** So Hezekiah lay down with his fathers, and they buried him on the ascent of the tombs of the sons of David; and all Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem paid him honor at his death. And Manasseh his son became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **32:5** — The Hebrew clause literally reads "and he raised up upon/to the towers," using an apocopated wayyiqtol of alah (interpreted as Hiphil with the wall as implied object) and Late Biblical Hebrew al in the sense of "up to."
- **32:15** — The Hiphil verb translated "will rescue" (yatsilu) is masculine plural with "your God" (eloheikhem), contrasting with the singular verb in v. 14 and reflecting the plural form of elohim or the Assyrian speaker's polytheistic perspective.
- **32:21** — Translated from the qere (umiytsiei meav), combining partitive min with the plural construct of the adjective yatsi ("those who come forth") before "his inward parts/body," meaning some of his own offspring; the ketiv writes a variant ending with waw.
- **32:22** — The Masoretic Text reads a Piel wayyiqtol of nahal ("guided/cared for them on every side"), whereas the Septuagint and Vulgate reflect a different Hebrew reading meaning "gave them rest on every side" (from Hiphil nuach).
- **32:28** — The Masoretic Text literally reads "and flocks for the folds" (using a metathesized spelling, averot, beside uravot "stalls" earlier in the verse), rather than reversing the terms to "folds for the flocks."
