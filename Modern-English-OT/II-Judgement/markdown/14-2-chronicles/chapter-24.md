## Text

**24:1** Joash was seven years old when he became king, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Zibiah from Beersheba.

**24:2** Joash did what was right in the eyes of the LORD all the days of Jehoiada the priest.

**24:3** Jehoiada took two wives for him, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**24:4** After this, Joash had it in his heart to restore the house of the LORD.

**24:5** He gathered the priests and the Levites and said to them, "Go out to the cities of Judah and gather silver from all Israel to repair the house of your God year by year, and you must act quickly in the matter." But the Levites did not act quickly.

**24:6** So the king called Jehoiada the chief and said to him, "Why have you not required of the Levites to bring in from Judah and from Jerusalem the levy of Moses the servant of the LORD, and of the assembly of Israel, for the tent of the testimony?"

**24:7** For the sons of Athaliah, that wicked woman, had broken into the house of God, and also all the holy things of the house of the LORD they had used for the Baals.

**24:8** Then the king commanded, and they made one chest and placed it outside at the gate of the house of the LORD.

**24:9** And they made a proclamation in Judah and in Jerusalem to bring to the LORD the levy of Moses the servant of God upon Israel in the wilderness.

**24:10** All the officials and all the people rejoiced, and they brought it and dropped it into the chest until they had finished.

**24:11** Whenever the chest was brought to the king's office by the hand of the Levites, and when they saw that there was much silver, the king's scribe and the officer of the chief priest would come and empty the chest, lift it up, and return it to its place. They did this day by day and collected silver in abundance.

**24:12** Then the king and Jehoiada gave it to those who did the work of the service of the house of the LORD, and they hired stonecutters and craftsmen to restore the house of the LORD, and also craftsmen in iron and bronze to repair the house of the LORD.

**24:13** So those who did the work labored, and restoration progressed in the work by their hand; they established the house of God according to its proper design and strengthened it.

**24:14** When they had finished, they brought the rest of the silver before the king and Jehoiada, and it was made into vessels for the house of the LORD—vessels for ministering and for offering up, dishes, and vessels of gold and silver. And they offered up burnt offerings in the house of the LORD continually all the days of Jehoiada.

**24:15** Then Jehoiada grew old and full of days, and he died; he was one hundred and thirty years old when he died.

**24:16** They buried him in the City of David with the kings, because he had done good in Israel, and toward God and His house.

**24:17** Now after the death of Jehoiada, the officials of Judah came and bowed down to the king. Then the king listened to them.

**24:18** They abandoned the house of the LORD, the God of their fathers, and served the Asherim and the idols; so wrath came upon Judah and Jerusalem because of this guilt of theirs.

**24:19** Yet He sent prophets among them to bring them back to the LORD; they testified against them, but they did not pay attention.

**24:20** Then the Spirit of God clothed Zechariah son of Jehoiada the priest, and he stood above the people and said to them, "This is what God says: 'Why are you transgressing the commandments of the LORD so that you will not prosper? Because you have forsaken the LORD, He has forsaken you.'"

**24:21** So they conspired against him and stoned him with stones by the commandment of the king in the court of the house of the LORD.

**24:22** King Joash did not remember the loyal kindness that Jehoiada his father had done for him, but he killed his son. And as he died, he said, "May the LORD see and require it!"

**24:23** At the turn of the year, the army of Aram marched up against him; they came to Judah and Jerusalem, destroyed all the officials of the people from among the people, and sent all their spoil to the king of Damascus.

**24:24** Although the army of Aram had come with a small number of men, the LORD gave a very great army into their hand, because they had forsaken the LORD, the God of their fathers. Thus they executed judgments against Joash.

**24:25** When they departed from him—for they left him with severe illnesses—his servants conspired against him because of the blood of the sons of Jehoiada the priest, and they killed him on his bed, and he died. They buried him in the City of David, but they did not bury him in the tombs of the kings.

**24:26** Now these are the ones who conspired against him: Zabad son of Shimeath the Ammonite woman, and Jehozabad son of Shimrith the Moabite woman.

**24:27** As for his sons, how the oracle against him increased, and the rebuilding of the house of God—look, they are written in the commentary of the Book of the Kings. And Amaziah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **24:7** — The phrase 'that wicked woman' translates hammirsha'at, a unique abstract feminine noun literally meaning 'wickedness,' used concretely to characterize Athaliah.
- **24:13** — The clause 'restoration progressed' renders watta'al arukha, an idiom literally meaning 'healing went up' (like new flesh closing a wound), applied to repair work.
- **24:14** — The expression 'for offering up' translates the Masoretic Hiphil infinitive construct weha'alot (from alah), coordinated with sharet ('ministering') to designate utensils used in offering sacrifices.
- **24:25** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads plural bene ('sons') in 'the blood of the sons of Jehoiada,' though only Zechariah is named in verses 20-22; some ancient versions harmonize to the singular.
- **24:27** — Ketiv/Qere: the ketiv (written text) reads w-r-b ('and the multitude/greatness of'), whereas the qere (read text) translated here reads yireb, an apocopated Qal imperfect of rabah ('increased' or 'grew great'). The noun massa can mean either 'oracle' or 'burden.'
