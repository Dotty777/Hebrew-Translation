## Text

**33:1** Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem.

**33:2** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, according to the abominations of the nations whom the LORD had dispossessed before the sons of Israel.

**33:3** He rebuilt the high places that his father Hezekiah had demolished, erected altars for the Baals, made Asherahs, and bowed down to all the host of heaven and served them.

**33:4** He also built altars in the house of the LORD, of which the LORD had said, "In Jerusalem My name shall be forever."

**33:5** And he built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the house of the LORD.

**33:6** He also made his sons pass through the fire in the Valley of Ben-hinnom; he practiced soothsaying, divination, and sorcery, and dealt with mediums and spiritists. He did much that was evil in the eyes of the LORD, provoking Him to anger.

**33:7** Then he placed the carved image of the idol that he had made in the house of God, of which God had said to David and to Solomon his son, "In this house and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen from all the tribes of Israel, I will put My name forever;

**33:8** and I will not again remove the foot of Israel from the land that I appointed for your fathers, if only they are careful to do all that I have commanded them, according to all the law, the statutes, and the ordinances by the hand of Moses."

**33:9** So Manasseh led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem astray to do more evil than the nations whom the LORD had destroyed before the sons of Israel.

**33:10** The LORD spoke to Manasseh and to his people, but they paid no attention.

**33:11** Therefore the LORD brought against them the commanders of the army of the king of Assyria, and they captured Manasseh with hooks, bound him with bronze shackles, and led him to Babylon.

**33:12** And when he was in distress, he implored the favor of the LORD his God and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers.

**33:13** And he prayed to Him, and He accepted his plea and heard his supplication, and He restored him to Jerusalem to his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the LORD is God.

**33:14** After this he built an outer wall for the City of David west of Gihon in the valley, extending to the entrance of the Fish Gate; he ran it around Ophel and raised it very high. He also stationed army commanders in all the fortified cities in Judah.

**33:15** He also removed the foreign gods and the idol from the house of the LORD, along with all the altars that he had built on the mountain of the house of the LORD and in Jerusalem, and he threw them outside the city.

**33:16** And he rebuilt the altar of the LORD and sacrificed on it sacrifices of peace offerings and thanksgiving; and he commanded Judah to serve the LORD, the God of Israel.

**33:17** Nevertheless, the people were still sacrificing at the high places, yet only to the LORD their God.

**33:18** Now the rest of the acts of Manasseh, and his prayer to his God, and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the LORD, the God of Israel, see, they are in the records of the kings of Israel.

**33:19** His prayer also, and how his plea was accepted, and all his sin and his unfaithfulness, and the sites where he built high places and set up the Asherahs and the carved images before he humbled himself, see, they are written in the records of Hozai.

**33:20** So Manasseh lay down with his fathers, and they buried him in his own house. And Amon his son became king in his place.

**33:21** Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem.

**33:22** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, just as Manasseh his father had done; and Amon sacrificed to all the carved images that Manasseh his father had made, and he served them.

**33:23** And he did not humble himself before the LORD as Manasseh his father had humbled himself; instead, this same Amon multiplied guilt.

**33:24** Then his servants conspired against him and put him to death in his own house.

**33:25** But the people of the land struck down all those who had conspired against King Amon, and the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **33:7** — The Masoretic Text preserves the unique spelling le-eilom, an Aramaizing or variant form of le-olam ("forever").
- **33:13** — "He accepted his plea" translates the Niphal wayyiqtol of atar (meaning to let oneself be favorably moved by a plea); the same verb recurs as a Niphal infinitive construct in v. 19.
- **33:16** — Translated from the qere vayyiven (Qal wayyiqtol of banah, "built/rebuilt"); the ketiv reads vayyakhen (Hiphil wayyiqtol of kun, "restored/established").
- **33:19** — The Masoretic Text vocalizes Chozai as a proper name ("Hozai"), whereas the Septuagint and some interpreters take the word as "the seers" or "his seers" (compare v. 18).
