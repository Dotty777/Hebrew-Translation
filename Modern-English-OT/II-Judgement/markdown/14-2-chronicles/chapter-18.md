## Text

**18:1** Now Jehoshaphat had wealth and honor in abundance, and he entered into a marriage alliance with Ahab.

**18:2** After some years he went down to Ahab in Samaria. Ahab slaughtered sheep and cattle in abundance for him and for the people with him, and he urged him to go up against Ramoth-gilead.

**18:3** Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, "Will you go with me to Ramoth-gilead?" He said to him, "I am just as you are, and my people are as your people, and we are with you in the war."

**18:4** Then Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "First, please inquire for the word of the LORD."

**18:5** So the king of Israel gathered the prophets—four hundred men—and said to them, "Should we go to Ramoth-gilead for battle, or should I hold back?" They replied, "Go up, and God will give it into the king's hand."

**18:6** But Jehoshaphat asked, "Is there not another prophet of the LORD here so that we may inquire of him?"

**18:7** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is still one man through whom to inquire of the LORD, yet I hate him, because he does not prophesy good about me, but disaster all his days—he is Micaiah the son of Imla." Jehoshaphat said, "May the king not say that."

**18:8** Then the king of Israel summoned a court official and said, "Quickly bring Micaiah the son of Imla."

**18:9** Now the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were seated, each on his own throne, dressed in robes; they were seated at the threshing floor by the entrance to the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets were prophesying in front of them.

**18:10** Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah made iron horns for himself and said, "This is what the LORD says: 'With these you will gore the Arameans until you completely destroy them.'"

**18:11** All the prophets were prophesying in the same manner, saying, "Go up to Ramoth-gilead and prosper, and the LORD will give it into the king's hand."

**18:12** Now the messenger who had gone to call Micaiah spoke to him, saying, "Look, the words of the prophets with one voice are good toward the king. Please let your word be like one of theirs, and speak what is good."

**18:13** Micaiah replied, "As the LORD lives, whatever my God says, that is what I will speak."

**18:14** When he came to the king, the king asked him, "Micaiah, should we go to Ramoth-gilead for battle, or should I hold back?" He replied, "Go up and prosper, and they will be given into your hand."

**18:15** The king said to him, "How many times must I place you under oath that you speak nothing to me except the truth in the name of the LORD?"

**18:16** He replied, "I saw all Israel scattered across the mountains like sheep without a shepherd; and the LORD said, 'These have no master—let each man return to his house in peace.'"

**18:17** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Did I not say to you that he does not prophesy good about me, only disaster?"

**18:18** Then Micaiah said, "Therefore, listen to the word of the LORD: I saw the LORD seated on His throne, and all the host of heaven standing at His right and at His left.

**18:19** And the LORD asked, 'Who will lure Ahab king of Israel so that he will go up and fall at Ramoth-gilead?' One was saying one thing, and another was saying something else.

**18:20** Then the spirit stepped forward, stood before the LORD, and said, 'I will lure him.' The LORD asked him, 'In what way?'

**18:21** He answered, 'I will go out and become a spirit of falsehood in the mouth of all his prophets.' Then He said, 'You will lure him and prevail as well; go out and do it.'

**18:22** So now, look—the LORD has placed a spirit of falsehood in the mouth of these prophets of yours, and the LORD has spoken disaster against you."

**18:23** Then Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah approached, struck Micaiah on the cheek, and asked, "By which way did the Spirit of the LORD pass from me to speak with you?"

**18:24** Micaiah replied, "Look, you will see on that day when you go into a room within a room to hide yourself."

**18:25** Then the king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah and bring him back to Amon, governor of the city, and to Joash, the king's son,

**18:26** and say, 'This is what the king says: Put this man in prison and feed him bread of hardship and water of hardship until I return in peace.'"

**18:27** Micaiah replied, "If you truly return in peace, then the LORD has not spoken through me." And he said, "Listen, all you peoples!"

**18:28** Then the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead.

**18:29** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will disguise myself and enter the battle, while you wear your own robes." So the king of Israel disguised himself, and they entered the battle.

**18:30** Now the king of Aram had given orders to his chariot commanders, saying, "Do not fight with small or great, only with the king of Israel alone."

**18:31** When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, "That is the king of Israel!" So they closed in around him to fight. But Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD helped him, and God turned them away from him.

**18:32** When the chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from going after him.

**18:33** Then a man drew a bow at random and struck the king of Israel between the joints and the armor. So he said to the chariot driver, "Turn your hand around and take me out of the army, for I have been wounded."

**18:34** The battle grew fierce on that day, and the king of Israel held himself upright in the chariot facing the Arameans until the evening; and he died at the time the sun went down.

## Translator Notes

- **18:8** — The qere (read form) is Micaiah (Mikayehu), matching 18:7; the ketiv (written consonants) displays the shorter spelling mykhw (Micahu).
- **18:14** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads the shortened form "Micah" (Mikah) here, rendered "Micaiah" for consistency with the rest of the chapter.
- **18:29** — Hebrew uses two infinitives absolute (Hithpael hithchappes and Qal bo) functioning as finite first-person verbs ("I will disguise myself and enter"), immediately contrasted with the explicit imperative addressed to Jehoshaphat.
- **18:33** — The qere (read form) is singular "your hand"; the ketiv (written consonants) is plural/dual "your hands."
- **18:34** — The Masoretic Hebrew uses the Hiphil participle maamid ("holding himself upright"), in contrast to the passive Hophal participle in 1 Kgs 22:35.
