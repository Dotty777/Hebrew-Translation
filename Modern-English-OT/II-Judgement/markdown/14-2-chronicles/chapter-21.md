## Text

**21:1** Then Jehoshaphat lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the City of David, and Jehoram his son became king in his place.

**21:2** He had brothers, the sons of Jehoshaphat: Azariah, Jehiel, Zechariah, Azariahu, Michael, and Shephatiah. All these were sons of Jehoshaphat king of Israel.

**21:3** Their father gave them many gifts of silver, gold, and precious things, along with fortified cities in Judah; but he gave the kingdom to Jehoram, because he was the firstborn.

**21:4** When Jehoram had risen over his father's kingdom and strengthened himself, he killed all his brothers with the sword, and also some of the officials of Israel.

**21:5** Jehoram was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.

**21:6** He walked in the way of the kings of Israel, just as the house of Ahab had done, for a daughter of Ahab was his wife; and he did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**21:7** Yet the LORD was not willing to destroy the house of David, on account of the covenant that He had made with David, and just as He had promised to give a lamp to him and to his sons always.

**21:8** In his days Edom revolted from under the hand of Judah and set a king over themselves.

**21:9** Then Jehoram crossed over with his commanders and all the chariots with him. He rose by night and struck Edom, which had surrounded him, and the commanders of the chariots.

**21:10** So Edom has been in revolt from under the hand of Judah to this day. Then Libnah revolted at that time from under his hand, because he had forsaken the LORD, the God of his fathers.

**21:11** Moreover, he made high places in the mountains of Judah, and he led the inhabitants of Jerusalem into prostitution and drove Judah astray.

**21:12** Then a letter came to him from Elijah the prophet, saying, "This is what the LORD, the God of your father David, says: 'Because you have not walked in the ways of your father Jehoshaphat or in the ways of Asa king of Judah,

**21:13** but have walked in the way of the kings of Israel, and have led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem into prostitution just as the house of Ahab promoted prostitution, and have also killed your brothers, your father's household, who were better than you,

**21:14** look, the LORD is about to strike a severe blow against your people, your sons, your wives, and all your possessions.

**21:15** And you yourself will be in many sicknesses, with a disease of your bowels, until your bowels come out because of the sickness, day after day.'"

**21:16** Then the LORD stirred up against Jehoram the spirit of the Philistines and of the Arabs who were beside the Cushites.

**21:17** They came up against Judah and broke into it, and they carried off all the possessions found in the king's house, as well as his sons and his wives, so that no son was left to him except Jehoahaz, the youngest of his sons.

**21:18** After all this, the LORD struck him in his bowels with a sickness for which there was no healing.

**21:19** In the course of time, when the end of two years arrived, his bowels came out because of his sickness, and he died in severe diseases. And his people did not make a fire for him like the fire of his fathers.

**21:20** He was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem. He departed unregretted, and they buried him in the City of David, but not in the tombs of the kings.

## Translator Notes

- **21:2** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads "king of Israel," a title the Chronicler occasionally applies to kings of Judah (cf. 21:4; 28:19). The first and fourth sons are spelled Azariah (Azaryah) and Azariahu (Azaryahu), variant forms of the same name.
- **21:17** — Jehoahaz (Yeho'achaz) is an alternate form of the name Ahaziah (Achazyahu, 22:1), reversing the position of the divine name element.
- **21:19** — Literally "to days from days, and at the time of the going out of the end to two days," an idiomatic construction in which "days" (yamim) denotes years.
- **21:20** — Literally "without desire" (be-lo chemdah), an idiom meaning no one lamented or regretted his death.
