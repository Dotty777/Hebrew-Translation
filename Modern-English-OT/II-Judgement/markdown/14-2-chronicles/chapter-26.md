## Text

**26:1** Then all the people of Judah took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah.

**26:2** It was he who built Eloth and restored it to Judah after the king lay down with his fathers.

**26:3** Uzziah was sixteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned in Jerusalem fifty-two years. His mother's name was Jecoliah from Jerusalem.

**26:4** He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, according to all that his father Amaziah had done.

**26:5** He persisted in seeking God during the days of Zechariah, who had understanding in the visions of God; and during the days that he sought the LORD, God made him prosper.

**26:6** He went out and fought against the Philistines, and broke down the wall of Gath, the wall of Jabneh, and the wall of Ashdod; and he built cities in Ashdod and among the Philistines.

**26:7** God helped him against the Philistines, against the Arabs who lived in Gur-baal, and against the Meunites.

**26:8** The Ammonites paid tribute to Uzziah, and his fame went as far as the entrance of Egypt, for he became exceedingly strong.

**26:9** Moreover, Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem at the Corner Gate, at the Valley Gate, and at the Angle, and fortified them.

**26:10** He built towers in the wilderness and hewed out many cisterns, for he had much livestock, both in the lowland and on the plain, as well as farmers and vinedressers in the hills and in the fertile lands, for he was a lover of the soil.

**26:11** Uzziah also had an army of fighting men who went out to war in troops, according to the number of their muster by the hand of Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the official, under the direction of Hananiah, one of the king's commanders.

**26:12** The total number of the heads of the fathers' houses of the mighty warriors was two thousand six hundred.

**26:13** Under their direction was a military force of three hundred and seven thousand five hundred, waging war with mighty power to help the king against the enemy.

**26:14** Uzziah prepared for them, for the entire army, shields, spears, helmets, body armor, bows, and slingstones.

**26:15** In Jerusalem he made machines, the design of a skilled designer, to be on the towers and on the corners to shoot arrows and large stones. His fame went out far and wide, for he was marvelously helped until he became strong.

**26:16** Yet when he became strong, his heart grew proud to his ruin, and he acted unfaithfully against the LORD his God: he entered the temple of the LORD to burn incense on the altar of incense.

**26:17** Then Azariah the priest went in after him, and with him eighty priests of the LORD, men of valor.

**26:18** They stood against King Uzziah and said to him, "It is not for you, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD, but for the priests, the sons of Aaron, who are consecrated to burn incense. Go out from the sanctuary, for you have acted unfaithfully, and it will not be to your honor from the LORD God."

**26:19** Then Uzziah grew furious, with a censer in his hand to burn incense; and while he was furious with the priests, leprosy broke out on his forehead before the priests in the house of the LORD, beside the altar of incense.

**26:20** Azariah the chief priest and all the priests turned toward him, and look, he was leprous on his forehead! They rushed him out from there, and he himself hurried to go out as well, because the LORD had struck him.

**26:21** King Uzziah was a leper until the day of his death, and he lived in a separate house as a leper, because he was cut off from the house of the LORD. Meanwhile, his son Jotham was over the king's house, judging the people of the land.

**26:22** Now the rest of the deeds of Uzziah, the first and the last, Isaiah the prophet, son of Amoz, has written.

**26:23** So Uzziah lay down with his fathers, and they buried him with his fathers in the burial field that belonged to the kings, for they said, "He is a leper." And his son Jotham became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **26:3** — Follows the Qere Yekolyah ("Jecoliah"); the Ketiv is spelled Yekilyah.
- **26:5** — Literally "in the seeing/visions of God" (bi-r'ot ha-elohim, Qal infinitive construct of ra'ah, "see"); the Hiphil participle ha-mevin ("having understanding") with the preposition be- parallels Daniel 1:17.
- **26:7** — Follows the Qere ha-arvim ("the Arabs"); the Ketiv writes the longer gentilic plural form ha-arviyim.
- **26:11** — Follows the Qere Ye'i'el ("Jeiel"); the Ketiv reads Ye'u'el ("Jeuel").
- **26:21** — Follows the Qere ha-chofshit ("a separate house," literally "house of separation/isolation"); the Ketiv reads the abstract feminine noun ha-chofshut.
