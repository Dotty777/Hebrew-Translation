## Text

**9:1** Now the queen of Sheba heard the report of Solomon, and she came to test Solomon with riddles in Jerusalem, with a very large entourage and camels carrying spices, gold in abundance, and precious stones. So she came to Solomon and spoke with him about all that was in her heart.

**9:2** And Solomon explained to her all her questions; not one matter was hidden from Solomon that he did not explain to her.

**9:3** The queen of Sheba saw the wisdom of Solomon and the house that he had built,

**9:4** as well as the food of his table, the seating of his servants, the attendance of his ministers and their garments, his cupbearers and their garments, and his ascent by which he went up to the house of the LORD; and there was no spirit left in her.

**9:5** Then she said to the king, "True was the report that I heard in my land regarding your words and your wisdom.

**9:6** Yet I did not believe their words until I came and my own eyes saw; and look, half the greatness of your wisdom had not been told to me; you have gone beyond the report that I heard.

**9:7** Happy are your men, and happy are these servants of yours, who stand in your presence continually and hear your wisdom!

**9:8** May the LORD your God be blessed, who delighted in you to set you on His throne as king for the LORD your God. Because of your God's love for Israel to establish them forever, He has set you over them as king to carry out justice and righteousness."

**9:9** Then she gave to the king one hundred twenty talents of gold, spices in very great abundance, and precious stones; never had there been spice like that which the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.

**9:10** In addition, the servants of Huram and the servants of Solomon, who brought gold from Ophir, brought algum timber and precious stones.

**9:11** From the algum timber the king made walkways for the house of the LORD and for the house of the king, as well as lyres and harps for the singers; nothing like them had been seen before in the land of Judah.

**9:12** And King Solomon gave to the queen of Sheba all that she desired, whatever she requested, beyond what she had brought to the king. Then she turned around and went to her land, she and her servants.

**9:13** Now the weight of the gold that came in to Solomon in a single year was six hundred sixty-six talents of gold,

**9:14** apart from what the traveling traders and the merchants were bringing; and all the kings of Arabia and the governors of the land were bringing gold and silver to Solomon.

**9:15** And King Solomon made two hundred large shields of hammered gold; six hundred shekels of hammered gold went onto each large shield.

**9:16** And he made three hundred shields of hammered gold; three hundred shekels of gold went onto each shield. Then the king placed them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.

**9:17** The king also made a great throne of ivory, and he plated it with pure gold.

**9:18** There were six steps for the throne and a footstool of gold, fastened to the throne, with armrests on this side and on that side of the seat, and two lions standing beside the armrests.

**9:19** And twelve lions were standing there on the six steps, on this side and on that side; such a thing had not been made for any kingdom.

**9:20** And all the drinking vessels of King Solomon were gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were refined gold; silver was not regarded as anything in the days of Solomon.

**9:21** For the king had ships going to Tarshish with the servants of Huram; once every three years the ships of Tarshish would arrive, carrying gold and silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.

**9:22** Thus King Solomon grew greater than all the kings of the earth in wealth and wisdom.

**9:23** All the kings of the earth kept seeking Solomon's presence to hear his wisdom, which God had placed in his heart.

**9:24** And they were bringing, each man his tribute: articles of silver and articles of gold, garments, weapons and spices, horses and mules, a set amount year after year.

**9:25** Solomon possessed four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, together with twelve thousand horsemen; he placed them in the cities for the chariots and with the king in Jerusalem.

**9:26** And he was ruler over all the kings from the River to the land of the Philistines, and all the way to the border of Egypt.

**9:27** And the king made silver in Jerusalem like stones, and cedars he made like the sycamore trees in the lowland in abundance.

**9:28** And horses were brought out from Egypt for Solomon, and from all the lands.

**9:29** As for the remaining deeds of Solomon, the first and the last, are they not written in the accounts of Nathan the prophet, and in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the vision of Iddo the seer regarding Jeroboam son of Nebat?

**9:30** For forty years Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel.

**9:31** Then Solomon lay down with his fathers, and they buried him in the city of David his father; and his son Rehoboam became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **9:4** — The Masoretic text reads aliyyato ("his ascent" or "his stairway," noun aliyyah), matching the Qal imperfect ya'aleh ("he went up"), whereas 1 Kings 10:5 and some ancient versions reflect olato ("his burnt offering") with the homographic Hiphil imperfect ya'aleh ("he offered up").
- **9:10** — Ketiv/Qere variant on the Tyrian king's name: the Qere reads Huram (Chronicles' usual spelling), while the Ketiv reads Hiram (as in Kings).
- **9:12** — Literally "beyond what she had brought to the king," an elliptical Chronicles idiom meaning Solomon gave her all she requested in addition to reciprocating beyond the gifts she had brought him.
- **9:15** — The weight unit "shekels" is implicit in the Hebrew idiom (literally "six hundred of hammered gold," and likewise "three hundred of gold" in 9:16); zahav shahut denotes beaten, hammered, or alloyed gold.
- **9:29** — Ketiv/Qere variant on the seer's name: the Qere reads Ye'do (traditionally rendered "Iddo," compare Iddo in 2 Chronicles 12:15 and 13:22), while the Ketiv reads Ye'di.
