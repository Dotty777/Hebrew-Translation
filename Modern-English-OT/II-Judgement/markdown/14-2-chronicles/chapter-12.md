## Text

**12:1** When the kingdom of Rehoboam was established and he had grown strong, he abandoned the law of the LORD, and all Israel with him.

**12:2** In the fifth year of King Rehoboam, Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, because they had acted unfaithfully against the LORD,

**12:3** with one thousand two hundred chariots and sixty thousand horsemen, and the people who came with him from Egypt were without number: Libyans, Sukkiim, and Cushites.

**12:4** He captured the fortified cities that belonged to Judah and came as far as Jerusalem.

**12:5** Then Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam and the officials of Judah who had gathered to Jerusalem because of Shishak, and he said to them, "This is what the LORD says: You have abandoned Me, and I too have abandoned you into the hand of Shishak."

**12:6** So the officials of Israel and the king humbled themselves and said, "The LORD is righteous."

**12:7** When the LORD saw that they had humbled themselves, the word of the LORD came to Shemaiah, saying, "They have humbled themselves; I will not destroy them, and I will give them a little deliverance, and My wrath will not be poured out on Jerusalem by the hand of Shishak.

**12:8** Yet they will become his servants, so that they may know My service and the service of the kingdoms of the lands."

**12:9** So Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem and took the treasures of the house of the LORD and the treasures of the king's house. He took everything. He also took the shields of gold that Solomon had made.

**12:10** Then King Rehoboam made shields of bronze in their place and entrusted them into the charge of the officers of the guards who kept the entrance of the king's house.

**12:11** Whenever the king went into the house of the LORD, the guards would come and carry them, and then bring them back to the guards' chamber.

**12:12** When he humbled himself, the anger of the LORD turned away from him, so as not to destroy him completely; moreover, there were good things in Judah.

**12:13** So King Rehoboam strengthened himself in Jerusalem and reigned. For Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city that the LORD had chosen from all the tribes of Israel to put His name there. His mother's name was Naamah the Ammonite.

**12:14** He did evil, because he did not prepare his heart to seek the LORD.

**12:15** Now the acts of Rehoboam, from first to last, are they not written in the records of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer, for genealogical registration? And there were wars between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually.

**12:16** Rehoboam lay down with his fathers and was buried in the city of David, and Abijah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **12:7** — Hebrew kim'at lifletah literally means "as a little for escape," indicating either a partial measure of deliverance (as qualified in v. 8) or deliverance in a short time.
- **12:10** — Hebrew haratsim literally means "the runners," a term for the royal foot-guards who escorted the king and guarded the palace entrance.
- **12:15** — Hebrew lehityaches is the Hithpael infinitive construct of yachas ("to be enrolled by genealogy"), referring here to genealogical registration or records.
