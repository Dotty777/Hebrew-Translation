## Text

**13:1** In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam, Abijah became king over Judah.

**13:2** He reigned three years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Micaiah daughter of Uriel of Gibeah. And there was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.

**13:3** Abijah joined battle with an army of mighty men of war, four hundred thousand chosen men, while Jeroboam drew up battle lines against him with eight hundred thousand chosen men, mighty men of valor.

**13:4** Then Abijah stood on Mount Zemaraim, which is in the hill country of Ephraim, and said, "Listen to me, Jeroboam and all Israel!

**13:5** Should you not know that the LORD, the God of Israel, gave kingship over Israel to David forever, to him and to his sons by a covenant of salt?

**13:6** Yet Jeroboam son of Nebat, a servant of Solomon son of David, rose up and rebelled against his master.

**13:7** Worthless men, scoundrels, gathered around him and proved too strong for Rehoboam son of Solomon, when Rehoboam was young and faint of heart, and he did not stand firm before them.

**13:8** And now you intend to stand firm before the kingdom of the LORD in the hand of the sons of David, and you are a great multitude, and with you are golden calves that Jeroboam made for you as gods.

**13:9** Have you not driven out the priests of the LORD, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites, and made priests for yourselves like the peoples of the lands? Whoever comes to consecrate himself with a young bull and seven rams becomes a priest of what are not gods.

**13:10** But as for us, the LORD is our God, and we have not abandoned Him. The priests ministering to the LORD are sons of Aaron, and the Levites are at the work.

**13:11** They offer up to the LORD burnt offerings morning by morning and evening by evening, and fragrant incense; the arrangement of bread is on the pure table, and the golden lampstand with its lamps is to be lit evening by evening; for we keep the charge of the LORD our God, but you have abandoned Him.

**13:12** Look, God is with us at our head, along with His priests and the alarm trumpets to sound the alarm against you. Sons of Israel, do not fight against the LORD, the God of your fathers, for you will not succeed."

**13:13** Meanwhile Jeroboam had sent an ambush around to come from behind them; so they were in front of Judah, and the ambush was behind them.

**13:14** When Judah turned around, look, the battle was against them in front and behind. So they cried out to the LORD, and the priests were sounding the trumpets.

**13:15** Then the men of Judah raised a battle shout; and as the men of Judah raised the battle shout, God struck Jeroboam and all Israel before Abijah and Judah.

**13:16** The sons of Israel fled before Judah, and God gave them into their hand.

**13:17** Abijah and his people struck them with a great slaughter, so five hundred thousand chosen men of Israel fell dead.

**13:18** So the sons of Israel were subdued at that time, while the sons of Judah prevailed because they relied on the LORD, the God of their fathers.

**13:19** Abijah pursued Jeroboam and captured cities from him: Bethel and its villages, Jeshanah and its villages, and Ephrain and its villages.

**13:20** Jeroboam no longer retained strength in the days of Abijah, and the LORD struck him, and he died.

**13:21** But Abijah grew strong, and took fourteen wives for himself, and fathered twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters.

**13:22** Now the rest of the acts of Abijah, his ways, and his sayings are written in the commentary of the prophet Iddo.

**13:23** Then Abijah lay down with his fathers, and they buried him in the city of David, and Asa his son reigned in his place. In his days the land was quiet for ten years.

## Translator Notes

- **13:2** — The Masoretic Text names Abijah's mother Micaiah daughter of Uriel of Gibeah, whereas 2 Chronicles 11:20 calls her Maacah daughter of Absalom.
- **13:9** — Hebrew lemalle yado (Piel infinitive construct of male) literally means "to fill his hand," the standard biblical idiom for priestly consecration or ordination.
- **13:14** — Ketiv preserves the fuller denominative participle with doubled tsade (from chatsotserah, "trumpet"), while qere reads the shortened Hiphil participle machtserim; both mean "sounding the trumpets."
- **13:19** — Qere reads the town name Ephrain against ketiv Ephron. Hebrew benoteha literally means "its daughters," an idiom for surrounding dependent villages.
- **13:22** — Hebrew midrash (from darash, "to inquire, study") occurs only here and in 2 Chronicles 24:27 in the Hebrew Bible, denoting an investigative record, treatise, or commentary.
