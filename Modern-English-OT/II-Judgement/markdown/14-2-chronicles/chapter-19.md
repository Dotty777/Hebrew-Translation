## Text

**19:1** Then Jehoshaphat king of Judah returned to his house in peace, to Jerusalem.

**19:2** Jehu the son of Hanani the seer went out to meet him and said to King Jehoshaphat, "Are you to help the wicked and love those who hate the LORD? On account of this, wrath is upon you from the presence of the LORD.

**19:3** Nevertheless, good things have been found in you, because you have purged the Asherah poles from the land and have directed your heart to seek God."

**19:4** Jehoshaphat resided in Jerusalem; then he went out once more among the people, from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim, and turned them back to the LORD, the God of their fathers.

**19:5** He installed judges throughout the land, in all the fortified cities of Judah, for each city.

**19:6** And he told the judges, "See what you are doing, for you are not judging for man, but for the LORD, and He is with you in the matter of judgment.

**19:7** So now, let the dread of the LORD be upon you. Take care as you act, for with the LORD our God there is no injustice, no partiality, and no taking of a bribe."

**19:8** In Jerusalem also, Jehoshaphat installed some of the Levites and the priests, as well as some of the heads of the fathers' houses of Israel, for the judgment of the LORD and for disputes; and they returned to Jerusalem.

**19:9** He charged them, saying, "In this manner you shall act: in the fear of the LORD, in faithfulness, and with a whole heart.

**19:10** In every dispute that comes to you from your brothers living in their cities—between bloodshed and bloodshed, between law and commandment, statutes and ordinances—you shall warn them so that they do not become guilty before the LORD and wrath does not come upon you and your brothers. In this manner you shall act, and you will not become guilty.

**19:11** Now see: Amariah the chief priest is over you in every matter of the LORD, and Zebadiah the son of Ishmael, the ruler of the house of Judah, in every matter of the king; and the Levites are officers before you. Be strong and act, and may the LORD be with the good."

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — Hebrew combines the interrogative particle with a modal infinitive construct (halarasha laazor), literally "Is it to help the wicked?", expressing moral rebuke.
- **19:8** — The Masoretic Text reads wayyashubu ("and they returned to Jerusalem," Qal of shub), referring to Jehoshaphat's company or the appointed judges; some ancient versions revocalize the text to read "for the inhabitants of Jerusalem."
- **19:11** — Hebrew hattob literally means "the good" (singular substantive), denoting the upright person who acts faithfully in judgment.
