## Text

**4:1** And he made an altar of bronze; its length was twenty cubits, its width was twenty cubits, and its height was ten cubits.

**4:2** He also made the cast sea, ten cubits from its rim to its rim, circular all around; its height was five cubits, and a line of thirty cubits encircled it all around.

**4:3** And beneath it was the likeness of oxen all the way around, surrounding it, ten to a cubit, encircling the sea all around; the oxen were in two rows, cast in its casting.

**4:4** It stood upon twelve oxen: three facing northward, three facing westward, three facing southward, and three facing eastward; and the sea was upon them above, and all their hindquarters were inward.

**4:5** Its thickness was a handbreadth, and its rim was like the workmanship of the rim of a cup, a lily blossom; it held and contained three thousand baths.

**4:6** He also made ten basins and placed five on the right side and five on the left side for washing in them; they would rinse in them what was prepared for the burnt offering, while the sea was for the priests to wash in.

**4:7** He also made the ten gold lampstands according to their prescribed manner, and he placed them in the temple hall, five on the right side and five on the left side.

**4:8** He also made ten tables and set them in the temple hall, five on the right side and five on the left side; and he made a hundred gold bowls.

**4:9** And he made the courtyard of the priests and the great court, and doors for the court; and their doors he overlaid with bronze.

**4:10** And the sea he placed on the right side, eastward, facing southward.

**4:11** Huram also made the pots, the shovels, and the bowls. Thus Huram finished doing the work that he did for King Solomon in the house of God:

**4:12** two pillars, the two globes and the capitals on the top of the pillars, and the two latticeworks to cover the two globes of the capitals that were on the top of the pillars;

**4:13** and the four hundred pomegranates for the two latticeworks, two rows of pomegranates for each latticework to cover the two globes of the capitals that were upon the pillars.

**4:14** He also made the stands, and he made the basins upon the stands;

**4:15** the one sea and the twelve oxen beneath it.

**4:16** The pots, the shovels, the forks, and all their utensils Huram, his master craftsman, made for King Solomon for the house of the LORD, of polished bronze.

**4:17** In the Jordan plain the king cast them, in the thick soil between Succoth and Zeredah.

**4:18** And Solomon made all these utensils in very great abundance, for the weight of the bronze was not ascertained.

**4:19** Solomon also made all the utensils that were in the house of God: the gold altar, and the tables on which was the bread of the Presence;

**4:20** and the lampstands and their lamps, to kindle them according to the prescribed manner before the inner sanctuary, of pure gold.

**4:21** And the blossoms, the lamps, and the tongs were of gold—that is, the finest gold.

**4:22** And the wick trimmers, the bowls, the dishes, and the firepans were of pure gold; and as for the entrance of the house, its inner doors for the Most Holy Place and the doors of the house for the temple hall were of gold.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Masoretic Text reads 'oxen' (beqarim), differing from 'gourds' (peqa'im) in 1 Kings 7:24. The closing phrase literally reads 'cast in its casting' (Qal passive participle of yatsaq), meaning cast together in one piece with the sea.
- **4:5** — The Masoretic Text uses a double-verb capacity idiom combining the Hiphil participle of chazaq ('holding') with the Hiphil imperfect of kul ('could contain') and records three thousand baths, compared with two thousand baths in 1 Kings 7:26.
- **4:11** — Ketiv reads 'Hiram' (chiram), while the Qere followed here reads 'Huram' (churam), matching the Chronicler's usual spelling earlier in this verse and in 4:16.
- **4:16** — Hebrew literally reads 'Huram his father' (churam 'aviv; compare 'Huram my father' in 2 Chronicles 2:12), using 'father' as an honorific title for a master craftsman or chief adviser.
- **4:21** — The phrase 'the finest gold' renders the unique expression mikhlot zahav, literally 'perfections of gold' (from kalah, 'to be complete'), denoting gold of the highest purity.
