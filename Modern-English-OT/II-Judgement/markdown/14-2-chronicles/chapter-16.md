## Text

**16:1** In the thirty-sixth year of Asa's reign, Baasha king of Israel marched up against Judah and built Ramah, in order to allow no one to go out or come in to Asa king of Judah.

**16:2** Asa then brought silver and gold out of the treasuries of the house of the LORD and the house of the king, and he sent them to Ben-hadad king of Aram, who resided in Damascus, saying,

**16:3** "Let there be a covenant between me and you, as between my father and your father. Look, I have sent you silver and gold; go, break your covenant with Baasha king of Israel, that he may withdraw from me."

**16:4** Ben-hadad listened to King Asa and sent the commanders of his armies against the cities of Israel; they struck Ijon, Dan, Abel-maim, and all the storehouses of the cities of Naphtali.

**16:5** When Baasha heard of it, he ceased building Ramah and halted his work.

**16:6** Then King Asa took all Judah, and they carried off the stones of Ramah and its timber with which Baasha had built, and he built Geba and Mizpah with them.

**16:7** At that time Hanani the seer came to Asa king of Judah and told him, "Because you leaned on the king of Aram and did not lean on the LORD your God, therefore the army of the king of Aram has escaped from your hand.

**16:8** Were not the Cushites and the Lubim a vast army with exceedingly great numbers of chariots and horsemen? Yet because you leaned on the LORD, He gave them into your hand.

**16:9** For the eyes of the LORD roam across all the earth to show Himself strong on behalf of those whose heart is undivided toward Him. You have behaved foolishly concerning this, for from now on there will be wars against you."

**16:10** Asa grew angry with the seer and placed him in the house of the stocks, because he was in a rage with him over this. At that time Asa also oppressed some of the people.

**16:11** Now the deeds of Asa, from first to last, see, they are written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.

**16:12** In the thirty-ninth year of his reign, Asa fell ill in his feet, and his illness was exceedingly severe. Yet even in his illness he did not seek the LORD, but rather the physicians.

**16:13** Then Asa lay down with his fathers and died in the forty-first year of his reign.

**16:14** They buried him in his tomb, which he had cut out for himself in the City of David, and they laid him on the bier that had been filled with spices of various kinds, blended in a crafted ointment; and they burned an exceedingly great fire for him.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — The Chronicler spells Damascus as Darmeseq, an Aramaic-influenced Late Biblical Hebrew orthography.
- **16:9** — The Polel participle meshotetot ("roam") conveys continuous scanning motion, while the Hithpael infinitive lehithchazzeq ("to show Himself strong") expresses God's active display of strength for those whose heart is shalem ("whole, undivided," echoing 15:17).
- **16:10** — Hebrew beit hammahpekhet ("the house of the stocks") refers to a prison cell fitted with stocks that twisted or confined a prisoner's body.
- **16:12** — The verb vayyechele ("fell ill") appears with a Late Biblical Hebrew III-Aleph spelling rather than the usual III-He form, creating a sonic wordplay with rofe'im ("physicians") at the end of the verse.
