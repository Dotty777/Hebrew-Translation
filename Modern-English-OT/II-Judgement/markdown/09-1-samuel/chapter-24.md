## Text

**24:1** Then David went up from there and stayed among the strongholds of En Gedi.

**24:2** Now when Saul returned from following after the Philistines, they reported to him, saying, "Look, David is in the wilderness of En Gedi."

**24:3** So Saul took three thousand picked men out of all Israel and went to seek David and his men before the Rocks of the Wild Goats.

**24:4** He came to the sheepfolds alongside the road, where there was a cave, and Saul went inside to relieve himself, while David and his men were sitting in the far recesses of the cave.

**24:5** Then David's men said to him, "Here is the day about which the LORD said to you, 'Look, I am giving your enemy into your hand, and you shall do to him as seems good in your eyes.'" So David rose and stealthily cut off the corner of Saul's robe.

**24:6** Afterward, David's heart struck him because he had cut off the corner belonging to Saul.

**24:7** He said to his men, "Far be it from me, before the LORD, that I should do this thing to my lord, to the anointed of the LORD, to extend my hand against him, for he is the anointed of the LORD."

**24:8** So David checked his men with these words and did not allow them to rise against Saul. Then Saul rose from the cave and went along the road.

**24:9** Afterward David rose, went out of the cave, and called after Saul, saying, "My lord the king!" When Saul looked behind him, David bent low with his face to the ground and bowed down.

**24:10** David said to Saul, "Why do you listen to the words of people saying, 'Look, David is seeking your harm'?

**24:11** Look, this day your eyes have seen how the LORD gave you into my hand today in the cave. Someone said to kill you, yet my eye spared you, and I said, 'I will not extend my hand against my lord, for he is the anointed of the LORD.'

**24:12** And my father, look! Yes, look at the corner of your robe in my hand! Because I cut off the corner of your robe and did not kill you, know and see that there is no evil or transgression in my hand, and I have not sinned against you, yet you are hunting for my life to take it.

**24:13** May the LORD judge between me and you, and may the LORD avenge me from you, yet my hand will not be against you.

**24:14** Just as the ancient proverb says, 'From wicked people comes wickedness,' yet my hand will not be against you.

**24:15** After whom has the king of Israel gone out? After whom are you pursuing? After a dead dog? After a single flea?

**24:16** So may the LORD be arbiter and judge between me and you; may He see and plead my cause, and vindicate me from your hand."

**24:17** Now it happened, as David finished speaking these words to Saul, that Saul said, "Is this your voice, my son David?" Then Saul raised his voice and wept.

**24:18** And he said to David, "You are more righteous than I am, for you have rendered good to me, while I have rendered evil to you.

**24:19** And you have made known today the good that you have done with me, in that the LORD turned me over into your hand, yet you did not kill me.

**24:20** For when a man finds his enemy, does he send him off on a good way? So may the LORD repay you with good in return for what you have done for me this day.

**24:21** And now, look, I know that you will surely reign, and the kingdom of Israel will stand firm in your hand.

**24:22** And now, swear to me by the LORD that you will not cut off my seed after me and that you will not wipe out my name from the house of my father."

**24:23** So David swore to Saul, and Saul went to his house, while David and his men went up to the stronghold.

## Translator Notes

- **24:4** — Hebrew literally reads "to cover his feet" (Hiphil infinitive construct of sakak), an idiom and euphemism for relieving oneself.
- **24:5** — The qere reads singular "your enemy," matching the following singular pronoun "to him," whereas the ketiv reads plural "your enemies."
- **24:8** — The verb "checked" renders the Piel of shasa', which literally means "to tear apart" or "to cleave," used here metaphorically for sharply restraining his men with words.
- **24:11** — Hebrew "Someone said" uses an indefinite third-person masculine singular verb, and "my eye spared you" translates the feminine singular verb wattahos (Qal wayyiqtol of hus) with its standard implied feminine subject "eye."
- **24:16** — Hebrew literally reads "judge me from your hand" (Qal imperfect of shaphat with min), a forensic idiom meaning to vindicate or deliver by rendering judgment.
