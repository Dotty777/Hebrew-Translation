## Text

**1:1** Now there was a certain man from Ramathaim-zophim, from the hill country of Ephraim, and his name was Elkanah, son of Jeroham, son of Elihu, son of Tohu, son of Zuph, an Ephrathite.

**1:2** He had two wives: the name of one was Hannah, and the name of the second was Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.

**1:3** Now that man used to go up from his town from year to year to worship and to sacrifice to the LORD of hosts in Shiloh; and there the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were priests to the LORD.

**1:4** When the day came and Elkanah sacrificed, he would give portions to his wife Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters.

**1:5** Yet to Hannah he would give a double portion, for he loved Hannah, even though the LORD had shut her womb.

**1:6** And her rival would provoke her harshly in order to distress her, because the LORD had shut up her womb.

**1:7** And he would do so year after year; as often as she went up to the house of the LORD, so her rival would provoke her, and she wept and would not eat.

**1:8** And her husband Elkanah said to her, "Hannah, why are you weeping? And why are you not eating? And why is your heart grieved? Am I not better to you than ten sons?"

**1:9** Then Hannah stood up after eating in Shiloh and after drinking. Now Eli the priest was sitting on the chair by the doorpost of the temple of the LORD.

**1:10** She was bitter in soul, and she prayed to the LORD and wept profusely.

**1:11** And she vowed a vow and said, "LORD of hosts, if You will truly look upon the affliction of Your maidservant and remember me, and not forget Your maidservant, but give to Your maidservant male offspring, then I will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor shall come upon his head."

**1:12** And it happened that as she kept on praying before the LORD, Eli was watching her mouth.

**1:13** Now Hannah was speaking in her heart; only her lips were moving, and her voice could not be heard. So Eli considered her to be drunk.

**1:14** And Eli said to her, "How long will you stay drunk? Remove your wine from yourself."

**1:15** Hannah answered and said, "No, my lord. I am a woman hard-pressed in spirit, and I have not drunk wine or strong drink, but I have poured out my soul before the LORD.

**1:16** Do not count your maidservant as a daughter of worthlessness, for out of the abundance of my complaint and my vexation I have spoken until now."

**1:17** And Eli answered and said, "Go in peace, and may the God of Israel grant your request that you have asked from Him."

**1:18** And she said, "May your female servant find favor in your eyes." Then the woman went on her way and ate, and her face was no longer downcast.

**1:19** In the morning they got up early and worshiped before the LORD; then they returned and came to their home in Ramah. And Elkanah knew his wife Hannah, and the LORD remembered her.

**1:20** And it happened in the course of time that Hannah conceived and bore a son; and she called his name Samuel, "Because I have asked for him from the LORD."

**1:21** And the man Elkanah and all his household went up to offer to the LORD the annual sacrifice and his vow.

**1:22** But Hannah did not go up, because she said to her husband, "Not until the boy is weaned; then I will bring him, so that he may appear before the LORD and remain there forever."

**1:23** And her husband Elkanah said to her, "Do what is good in your eyes. Stay until you have weaned him; only may the LORD uphold His word." So the woman stayed and nursed her son until she had weaned him.

**1:24** When she had weaned him, she took him up with her, along with three bulls, one ephah of flour, and a skin of wine, and she brought him to the house of the LORD at Shiloh; and the boy was still young.

**1:25** And they slaughtered the bull and brought the boy to Eli.

**1:26** And she said, "Please, my lord! As your soul lives, my lord, I am the woman who stood beside you here to pray to the LORD.

**1:27** For this boy I prayed, and the LORD has given me my request that I asked from Him.

**1:28** And I also have lent him to the LORD; all the days that he lives, he is lent to the LORD." And there he worshiped the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **1:5** — Hebrew מָנָה אַחַת אַפָּיִם (lit. 'one portion of two faces/nostrils') is a difficult dual expression traditionally understood as a double or special portion.
- **1:18** — Hebrew וּפָנֶיהָ לֹא־הָיוּ־לָהּ עוֹד (lit. 'and her face was no longer to her') is an idiom meaning her distressed countenance was gone.
- **1:24** — The Masoretic Text reads בְּפָרִים שְׁלֹשָׁה ('with three bulls'), while v. 25 has the singular הַפָּר ('the bull'); in this verse the Septuagint and 4QSam-a reflect 'a three-year-old bull'.
- **1:28** — The Hiphil הִשְׁאִלְתִּהוּ ('I have lent him') and Qal passive participle שָׁאוּל ('lent') play on the root שׁאל ('ask') in vv. 20 and 27; the Masoretic text ends with singular וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ ('and he worshiped').
