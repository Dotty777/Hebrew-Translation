## Text

**20:1** Then David fled from Naioth in Ramah, and he came and said before Jonathan, "What have I done? What is my iniquity, and what is my sin before your father, that he is seeking my life?"

**20:2** He said to him, "Far be it! You will not die. Look, my father does not do anything great or small without disclosing it to me. Why then would my father hide this matter from me? It is not so."

**20:3** Yet David swore further and said, "Your father knows very well that I have found favor in your eyes, and he has said, 'Let Jonathan not know this, or he will be grieved.' Yet truly, as the LORD lives and as your soul lives, there is merely a step between me and death."

**20:4** Then Jonathan said to David, "Whatever your soul says, I will do for you."

**20:5** So David said to Jonathan, "Look, tomorrow is the new moon, and I ought surely to sit with the king to eat; yet let me go, and I will hide in the field until the third evening.

**20:6** If your father misses me at all, then say, 'David earnestly asked my permission to run to Bethlehem, his city, because the yearly sacrifice for the whole family is there.'

**20:7** If he says this, 'Good,' then there is peace for your servant; but if he grows exceedingly angry, know that harm has been determined by him.

**20:8** Therefore show loyal love to your servant, for you have brought your servant into a covenant of the LORD with yourself. Yet if there is iniquity in me, put me to death yourself; why should you bring me to your father?"

**20:9** Jonathan said, "Far be it from you! For if I knew for certain that harm had been determined by my father to come upon you, would I not tell you of it?"

**20:10** Then David said to Jonathan, "Who will tell me, or what if your father answers you harshly?"

**20:11** Jonathan said to David, "Come, let us go out into the field." So the two of them went out into the field.

**20:12** Then Jonathan said to David, "By the LORD, the God of Israel, when I sound out my father about this time tomorrow or the third day, look, if things are good toward David and I do not then send to you and disclose it to you,

**20:13** may the LORD do so to Jonathan, and may He add still more! And if it pleases my father to bring harm upon you, I will disclose it to you and send you away, so that you may go in peace. May the LORD be with you, just as He was with my father.

**20:14** And if I am still alive, will you not show the loyal love of the LORD toward me, so that I do not die?

**20:15** And do not cut off your loyal love from my house forever, not even when the LORD cuts off every one of David's enemies from the face of the earth."

**20:16** So Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David: "May the LORD require it from the hand of David's enemies."

**20:17** Once again Jonathan had David swear by his love for him, because he loved him with the love of his own soul.

**20:18** Then Jonathan said to him, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and you will be missed, because your seat will be found empty.

**20:19** On the third day, go down quickly and come to the place where you hid yourself on the day of the deed, and remain beside the stone Ezel.

**20:20** And I myself will shoot three arrows to its side, as though I were shooting at a target.

**20:21** Now I will send the boy: 'Go, find the arrows.' If I expressly say to the boy, 'Look, the arrows are on this side of you; take them,' then come, for there is peace for you and nothing is wrong, as the LORD lives.

**20:22** But if I say this to the youth, 'Look, the arrows are beyond you,' then go, for the LORD has sent you away.

**20:23** And as for the matter of which you and I have spoken, see, the LORD is between me and you forever."

**20:24** So David hid in the field. When the new moon came, the king sat down to the meal to eat.

**20:25** The king sat on his seat as at other times, on the seat by the wall; Jonathan stood up, and Abner sat at Saul's side, but David's place was empty.

**20:26** Yet Saul did not speak anything on that day, for he thought, "Something has happened; he is not clean; surely he is not clean."

**20:27** On the day after the new moon, the second day, David's place was empty. So Saul said to his son Jonathan, "Why has the son of Jesse not come to the meal, either yesterday or today?"

**20:28** Jonathan answered Saul, "David earnestly asked my permission to go to Bethlehem.

**20:29** He said, 'Please let me go, because we have a family sacrifice in the city, and my brother himself has commanded me to come. Now then, if I have found favor in your eyes, please let me slip away and see my brothers.' That is why he has not come to the king's table."

**20:30** Then Saul's anger burned against Jonathan, and he said to him, "You son of a wayward, rebellious woman! Do I not know that you are choosing the son of Jesse to your own shame and to the shame of your mother's nakedness?

**20:31** For all the days that the son of Jesse lives upon the earth, neither you nor your kingdom will be established. So now, send and bring him to me, for he is doomed to die!"

**20:32** Jonathan answered his father Saul and said to him, "Why is he to be put to death? What has he done?"

**20:33** Then Saul hurled the spear at him to strike him down, and Jonathan knew that it had been determined by his father to put David to death.

**20:34** Jonathan rose from the table in fierce anger and ate no food on the second day of the new moon, because he was grieved over David, since his father had disgraced him.

**20:35** In the morning, Jonathan went out into the field for his appointment with David, and a young boy was with him.

**20:36** He said to his boy, "Run, please find the arrows that I am about to shoot." As the boy ran, he shot the arrow to pass beyond him.

**20:37** When the boy arrived at the place of the arrow that Jonathan had shot, Jonathan called out after the boy and said, "Is the arrow not beyond you?"

**20:38** And Jonathan called out after the boy, "Quickly! Hurry! Do not stop!" So Jonathan's boy gathered the arrows and came to his master.

**20:39** Yet the boy did not know anything; Jonathan and David alone knew the matter.

**20:40** Then Jonathan handed his weapons to his boy and said to him, "Go, carry them into the city."

**20:41** As soon as the boy had left, David rose from the south side, fell on his face to the ground, and bowed down three times. They kissed one another and wept with one another, and David wept all the more.

**20:42** Then Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace, since both of us have sworn in the name of the LORD, saying, 'The LORD will be between me and you, and between my offspring and your offspring forever.'"

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — The translation follows the Masoretic Qere lo-ya'aseh ("does not do," Qal imperfect); the written Ketiv reads lw 'sh ("did not do," spelling the negative lo as lw with the Qal perfect). The idiom "disclosing it to me" is literally "uncovering my ear" (also in vv. 12-13).
- **20:12** — The verse opens with an elliptical oath invocation (lit. "The LORD, the God of Israel"), and its closing negative clause connects directly to the self-imprecation at the beginning of v. 13 ("may the LORD do so to Jonathan...").
- **20:14** — The Masoretic Hebrew of this verse is highly compressed, using welo ("and not") three times: the opening rhetorical negative question is paused by the condition "if I am still alive," resumed before "show the loyal love of the LORD," and followed by the purpose/result clause "so that I do not die."
- **20:19** — The Piel verb shillashta (denominative from shalosh, "three") means "do on the third day," followed by tered me'od (lit. "go down greatly/quickly"). Both "the day of the deed" (yom hamma'aseh) and "the stone Ezel" (ha'even ha'azel) follow the Masoretic text directly.
- **20:38** — The translation follows the Masoretic Qere plural hachitzim ("the arrows"); the written Ketiv has the singular hachetzi ("the arrow"), matching the singular in vv. 36-37. In 20:24, the Qere reads the preposition el ("to") where the Ketiv reads al.
