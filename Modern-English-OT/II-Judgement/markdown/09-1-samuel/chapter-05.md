## Text

**5:1** Now the Philistines had captured the ark of God, and they brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.

**5:2** The Philistines took the ark of God, brought it into the house of Dagon, and placed it beside Dagon.

**5:3** When the Ashdodites got up early the next day, there was Dagon, fallen on his face to the ground before the ark of the LORD. So they took Dagon and returned him to his place.

**5:4** When they got up early in the morning the next day, there was Dagon, fallen on his face to the ground before the ark of the LORD, while the head of Dagon and both palms of his hands were cut off upon the threshold; only Dagon was left on him.

**5:5** For this reason the priests of Dagon and all who enter the house of Dagon do not step upon the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod until this day.

**5:6** Then the hand of the LORD was heavy upon the Ashdodites; He devastated them and struck them with tumors—Ashdod and its territories.

**5:7** When the men of Ashdod saw that it was so, they said, "The ark of the God of Israel must not stay with us, because His hand is harsh against us and against Dagon our god."

**5:8** So they sent and gathered all the lords of the Philistines to themselves and said, "What are we to do with the ark of the God of Israel?" And they said, "Let the ark of the God of Israel be transferred to Gath." So they transferred the ark of the God of Israel.

**5:9** And it came about after they had transferred it that the hand of the LORD was against the city with a very great panic. He struck the men of the city, from small to great, and tumors broke out on them.

**5:10** So they sent the ark of God away to Ekron. And it happened when the ark of God came to Ekron that the Ekronites cried out, saying, "They have transferred the ark of the God of Israel to me, to put me and my people to death!"

**5:11** Then they sent and gathered all the lords of the Philistines and said, "Send the ark of the God of Israel away, and let it return to its place, so that it does not put me and my people to death." For a deadly panic was throughout the entire city; the hand of God was exceedingly heavy there.

**5:12** And the men who did not die were struck with tumors, and the outcry of the city rose up to heaven.

## Translator Notes

- **5:4** — The Masoretic Text reads literally "only Dagon was left on him," referring to the remaining torso of the statue after its head and hands were severed; the Septuagint reads "the back/trunk of Dagon."
- **5:6** — Here and in 5:9, 12, the Ketiv reads opalim ("swellings/tumors") while the euphemistic Qere reads tehorim ("tumors/hemorrhoids"); the Qere is translated throughout.
- **5:9** — The verb "broke out" translates a Niphal hapax legomenon of the root satar (distinct from the homograph meaning "hide"), denoting an eruption of swellings.
- **5:10** — Here and in 5:11, the Masoretic Text uses collective first-person singular pronouns ("to me," "me and my people") for the speakers of Ekron as a community, whereas the Septuagint harmonizes to the plural ("us," "our").
