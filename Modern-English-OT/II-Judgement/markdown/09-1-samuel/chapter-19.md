## Text

**19:1** Saul spoke to his son Jonathan and to all his servants about putting David to death, yet Jonathan the son of Saul delighted greatly in David.

**19:2** So Jonathan told David, saying, "My father Saul is seeking to put you to death. Now then, please be on your guard in the morning; stay in a secret place and hide yourself.

**19:3** I will go out and stand at my father's side in the field where you are, and I myself will speak about you to my father; whatever I see, I will tell you."

**19:4** Then Jonathan spoke well of David to his father Saul and said to him, "Let the king not sin against his servant David, for he has not sinned against you, and his deeds have been very good for you.

**19:5** He placed his life in his hand and struck the Philistine, and the LORD accomplished a great deliverance for all Israel. You saw it and rejoiced; why then should you sin against innocent blood by putting David to death for no reason?"

**19:6** Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan, and Saul swore, "As the LORD lives, he will not be put to death."

**19:7** Then Jonathan called David, and Jonathan told him all these words. And Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence as previously.

**19:8** Once more war broke out, and David went out and fought against the Philistines; he dealt them a great blow, and they fled from before him.

**19:9** Then an evil spirit from the LORD came upon Saul as he was sitting in his house with his spear in his hand, while David was playing music with his hand.

**19:10** Saul sought to pin David to the wall with the spear, but David slipped away from before Saul, and Saul struck the spear into the wall. David fled and escaped that night.

**19:11** Then Saul sent messengers to David's house to watch him and to put him to death in the morning. But his wife Michal told David, saying, "If you do not save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be put to death."

**19:12** So Michal lowered David through the window, and he went away, fled, and escaped.

**19:13** Michal took the household idol and placed it in the bed; she put a cushion of goats' hair at its head and covered it with the cloth.

**19:14** Then Saul sent messengers to take David, but she said, "He is ill."

**19:15** So Saul sent the messengers to see David, saying, "Bring him up to me in the bed to put him to death."

**19:16** The messengers came in, and there was the household idol in the bed, with the cushion of goats' hair at its head.

**19:17** Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me like this and sent my enemy away, so that he escaped?" Michal said to Saul, "He himself said to me, 'Let me go! Why should I put you to death?'"

**19:18** Now David had fled and escaped; he came to Samuel at Ramah and told him all that Saul had done to him. Then he and Samuel went and stayed at Naioth.

**19:19** It was reported to Saul, "Look, David is at Naioth in Ramah."

**19:20** Then Saul sent messengers to take David; and when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing and presiding over them, the Spirit of God came upon Saul's messengers, and they also prophesied.

**19:21** When they told Saul, he sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. Once again Saul sent a third group of messengers, and they also prophesied.

**19:22** Then he himself also went to Ramah and came as far as the great cistern that is in Secu. He inquired and said, "Where are Samuel and David?" And someone said, "Look, they are at Naioth in Ramah."

**19:23** So he went there, to Naioth in Ramah. And the Spirit of God came upon him too, and he walked along continuously, prophesying until he arrived at Naioth in Ramah.

**19:24** He also stripped off his garments, and he also prophesied before Samuel, and fell down naked all that day and all night. Therefore people say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

## Translator Notes

- **19:9** — The construct phrase ruach [divine name] ra'ah (lit. "a spirit of the LORD, evil") places the feminine adjective ra'ah ("evil") after the construct chain to modify ruach ("spirit"), indicating an evil spirit sent from the LORD. The Piel participle menaggen beyad literally means "playing music with the hand."
- **19:13** — The noun teraphim is plural in form, denoting here a human-sized household idol or image. The rare phrase kevir ha'izzim (occurring only here and in v. 16) refers to a woven covering, quilt, or cushion of goats' hair.
- **19:18** — Here and in 19:19, 22, 23 (and 20:1), the translation follows the Masoretic Qere reading Nayot ("Naioth"); the written Ketiv preserves a different consonantal spelling (nwyt in ch. 19; mnwwt in 20:1).
- **19:20** — The Qal verb wayyar ("and he saw") is masculine singular, used collectively or distributively of Saul's messengers. The noun lahaqat ("company" or "band") is a hapax legomenon, occurring only here in the Hebrew Bible.
