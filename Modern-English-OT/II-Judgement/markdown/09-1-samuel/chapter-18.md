## Text

**18:6** Now as they were arriving, when David returned from striking down the Philistine, the women came out from all the cities of Israel, singing and dancing to meet King Saul, with tambourines, with gladness, and with three-stringed instruments.

**18:7** The celebrating women sang back and forth and said, "Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands."

**18:8** Saul grew very angry, and this saying was displeasing in his eyes; he said, "They have given David ten thousands, and to me they have given the thousands. Now what remains for him is only the kingdom."

**18:9** So Saul kept eyeing David from that day onward.

**18:12** Now Saul feared David, because the LORD was with him, while He had withdrawn from Saul.

**18:13** So Saul removed him from being with him and appointed him as commander of a thousand for himself; and he went out and came in before the people.

**18:14** David was succeeding in all his ways, and the LORD was with him.

**18:15** When Saul saw that he was succeeding greatly, he stood in dread of him.

**18:16** All Israel and Judah, however, loved David, because he was the one going out and coming in before them.

**18:20** Now Michal, the daughter of Saul, loved David; and they told Saul, and the matter was right in his eyes.

**18:21** Saul said, "I will give her to him, that she may become a snare for him and the hand of the Philistines may be against him." Then Saul said to David, "A second time you will become my son-in-law today."

**18:22** Saul commanded his servants, "Speak to David in secret, saying, 'See, the king takes pleasure in you, and all his servants love you; so now, become the king's son-in-law.'"

**18:23** Saul's servants spoke these words in David's ears, and David said, "Is it a light matter in your eyes to become the king's son-in-law, when I am a poor man and lightly regarded?"

**18:24** Then Saul's servants reported to him, saying, "David spoke words like these."

**18:25** Saul said, "This is what you are to say to David, 'The king has no desire for a bride-price, but rather for a hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to avenge himself on the king's enemies.'" Now Saul schemed to cause David to fall by the hand of the Philistines.

**18:26** His servants told David these words, and the matter was right in David's eyes to become the king's son-in-law; and the days had not yet expired.

**18:27** So David rose up and went, he and his men, and struck down two hundred men among the Philistines. David brought their foreskins, and they counted them out in full to the king so that he might become the king's son-in-law. Then Saul gave him his daughter Michal as a wife.

**18:28** Saul saw and knew that the LORD was with David, and that Michal, the daughter of Saul, loved him.

**18:29** So Saul feared David even more, and Saul remained an enemy to David always.

## Translator Notes

- **18:6** — Following the Qere 'lashir' (Qal infinitive construct of 'shir,' 'to sing'); the Ketiv writes 'lshwr.' The rare noun 'shalishim,' derived from the root for 'three,' refers to a musical instrument such as a three-stringed lute or sistrum.
- **18:9** — Following the Qere 'oyen' (Qal active participle of the denominative verb from 'ayin,' 'eye'), meaning to watch with suspicion or jealousy; the Ketiv writes 'wn' defectively without the yod.
- **18:14** — The Hiphil participle 'maskil' (from 'sakal,' also in verse 15) denotes both wise, prudent conduct and the success that results from it.
- **18:21** — Hebrew 'bishtayim' (literally 'with two,' i.e., 'a second time') stands inside Saul's speech in the Masoretic Text, referring to a second opportunity to marry one of his daughters.
- **18:29** — Hebrew 'lero'' is a rare form of the Qal infinitive construct of 'yare'' ('to fear'), paired with the Hiphil of 'yasaf' ('added') and 'od' ('still/more') to express intensifying fear.
