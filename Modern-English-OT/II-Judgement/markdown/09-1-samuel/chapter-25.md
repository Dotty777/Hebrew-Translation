## Text

**25:1** Now Samuel died, and all Israel gathered and lamented for him, and they buried him at his house in Ramah. Then David rose and went down to the wilderness of Paran.

**25:2** Now there was a man in Maon whose business was in Carmel. The man was very great; he had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats, and he was shearing his sheep in Carmel.

**25:3** Now the name of the man was Nabal, and the name of his wife was Abigail. The woman was of good insight and lovely in form, whereas the man was harsh and evil in his deeds, and he was a Calebite.

**25:4** Then David heard in the wilderness that Nabal was shearing his sheep.

**25:5** So David sent ten young men, and David said to the young men, "Go up to Carmel, go to Nabal, and ask after his welfare in my name.

**25:6** And thus you shall say: 'To life! Peace be to you, peace to your house, and peace to everything that belongs to you.

**25:7** And now I have heard that you have shearers. Now, your shepherds have been with us; we did not mistreat them, nor did anything of theirs go missing all the days they were in Carmel.

**25:8** Ask your young men, and they will tell you. May the young men then find favor in your eyes, since we have come on a festive day. Please give whatever your hand finds to your servants and to your son David.'"

**25:9** Then David's young men came, spoke to Nabal in accordance with all these words in the name of David, and waited.

**25:10** But Nabal answered the servants of David and said, "Who is David, and who is the son of Jesse? Today servants are numerous who are breaking away, each man from his master.

**25:11** Should I then take my bread and my water and my butchered meat that I have butchered for my shearers, and give them to men when I do not know where they are from?"

**25:12** So David's young men turned back along their way; they returned and came and told him in accordance with all these words.

**25:13** Then David said to his men, "Each man, strap on his sword!" So each man strapped on his sword, and David also strapped on his sword. And about four hundred men went up after David, while two hundred stayed by the baggage.

**25:14** Meanwhile, one of the young men reported to Abigail, Nabal's wife, saying, "Look, David sent messengers from the wilderness to bless our master, yet he lashed out at them.

**25:15** Yet the men were very good to us; we were not mistreated, nor did we miss anything all the days we moved about with them while we were in the field.

**25:16** They were a wall around us both by night and by day, all the days we were with them shepherding the sheep.

**25:17** And now, know and see what you should do, for harm is fully determined against our master and against all his house, and he is too worthless a man for anyone to speak to him."

**25:18** Then Abigail hurried and took two hundred loaves of bread, two skins of wine, five prepared sheep, five seahs of roasted grain, one hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs, and set them on the donkeys.

**25:19** She said to her young men, "Go on ahead of me; look, I am coming behind you." However, she did not tell her husband Nabal.

**25:20** And as she was riding on the donkey and going down under cover of the mountain, look, David and his men were coming down toward her, and she met them.

**25:21** Now David had said, "Surely for nothing I guarded everything belonging to this fellow in the wilderness, so that nothing of all that is his went missing, yet he has returned evil to me in exchange for good.

**25:22** May God do so to the enemies of David, and may He do even more, if I leave remaining by morning of all that belongs to him one who urinates against a wall."

**25:23** When Abigail saw David, she hurried and came down off the donkey, fell upon her face before David, and bowed down to the ground.

**25:24** She fell at his feet and said, "Upon me, upon me myself, my lord, be the guilt! Please let your maidservant speak in your hearing, and listen to the words of your maidservant.

**25:25** Please do not let my lord give his mind to this worthless man, to Nabal, for just as his name is, so is he: Nabal is his name, and folly is with him. Yet I, your maidservant, did not see the young men of my lord whom you sent.

**25:26** And now, my lord, as the LORD lives and as your soul lives, since the LORD has kept you back from incurring bloodguilt and from saving yourself by your own hand, now may your enemies and those who seek harm against my lord become like Nabal.

**25:27** And now, as for this blessing that your servant woman has brought to my lord, let it be given to the young men who walk in the footsteps of my lord.

**25:28** Please pardon the transgression of your maidservant; for the LORD will surely make a lasting house for my lord, since my lord fights the LORD's battles, and no evil will be found in you throughout your days.

**25:29** And if someone rises to pursue you and to seek your life, the life of my lord will be bound up in the bundle of the living with the LORD your God, whereas the life of your enemies He will sling out from within the pocket of the sling.

**25:30** And it will happen, when the LORD has done for my lord in accordance with all the good that He has spoken about you and has appointed you as leader over Israel,

**25:31** that this will not become for you a cause of faltering or a stumbling block of heart for my lord, by having shed blood without cause or by my lord having saved himself. And when the LORD has dealt well with my lord, then remember your maidservant."

**25:32** So David said to Abigail, "Blessed is the LORD, the God of Israel, who sent you to meet me this day!

**25:33** And blessed is your good sense, and blessed are you, who have restrained me this day from incurring bloodguilt and from saving myself by my own hand.

**25:34** For indeed, as the LORD, the God of Israel, lives, who has kept me back from harming you, had you not hurried and come to meet me, surely by the light of morning there would not have been left to Nabal one who urinates against a wall."

**25:35** Then David accepted from her hand what she had brought him, and he said to her, "Go up in peace to your house. See, I have listened to your voice and have granted you favor."

**25:36** Then Abigail came to Nabal, and look, he had a banquet in his house like the banquet of a king. Nabal's heart was cheerful within him, and he was exceedingly drunk; so she told him nothing, small or great, until the light of morning.

**25:37** And it happened in the morning, once the wine had gone out from Nabal, that his wife told him these matters; then his heart died inside him, and he became like a stone.

**25:38** Then it happened about ten days later that the LORD struck Nabal, and he died.

**25:39** When David heard that Nabal had died, he said, "Blessed is the LORD, who has pleaded the cause of my reproach from Nabal's hand and has held back His servant from evil; and the LORD has returned Nabal's evil upon his own head." Then David sent and spoke for Abigail, to take her to himself as a wife.

**25:40** So David's servants came to Abigail at Carmel and spoke to her, saying, "David has sent us to you to take you to himself as a wife."

**25:41** She rose, bowed down with her face to the ground, and said, "Here is your maidservant as a slave woman, to wash the feet of the servants of my lord."

**25:42** Then Abigail hurried and got up, and she rode on the donkey with her five young women who followed behind her; she went after the messengers of David and became his wife.

**25:43** David also took Ahinoam from Jezreel, and the two of them both became his wives.

**25:44** Meanwhile, Saul had given his daughter Michal, the wife of David, to Palti son of Laish, who was from Gallim.

## Translator Notes

- **25:3** — The qere reads "a Calebite" (a descendant of Caleb), whereas the ketiv consonants suggest kelibbo ("according to his own heart," i.e., self-willed) or a wordplay on "dog" (keleb).
- **25:6** — Hebrew koh lehai (literally "thus to life" or "to the living one") is an idiomatic greeting wishing long life and prosperity.
- **25:14** — The verb "lashed out" translates the Qal of 'it, literally "to swoop down upon" like a bird of prey, used figuratively here for raging at David's messengers.
- **25:22** — The Masoretic Text explicitly reads "to the enemies of David" as a euphemistic self-curse formula, and "one who urinates against a wall" (also in 25:34) is a blunt Hebrew idiom for any male.
- **25:25** — Hebrew plays on Nabal's name (nabal, meaning "fool") and the cognate noun "folly" (nebalah).
