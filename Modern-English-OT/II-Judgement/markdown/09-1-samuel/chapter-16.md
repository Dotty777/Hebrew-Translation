## Text

**16:1** Then the LORD said to Samuel, "How long will you mourn over Saul, seeing that I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and go; I am sending you to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for I have seen a king for Myself among his sons."

**16:2** Samuel said, "How can I go? Saul will hear and kill me." And the LORD said, "Take a heifer from the herd with you and say, 'I have come to sacrifice to the LORD.'

**16:3** Invite Jesse to the sacrifice, and I Myself will make known to you what you are to do; and anoint for Me the one whom I tell you."

**16:4** So Samuel did what the LORD had spoken and came to Bethlehem. The elders of the city came trembling to meet him and said, "Is your coming in peace?"

**16:5** He said, "In peace. I have come to sacrifice to the LORD. Consecrate yourselves and come with me to the sacrifice." Then he consecrated Jesse and his sons and invited them to the sacrifice.

**16:6** When they arrived, he saw Eliab and said, "Surely His anointed is before the LORD."

**16:7** But the LORD said to Samuel, "Do not gaze at his appearance or at the height of his stature, for I have rejected him. For it is not what a human sees; indeed, a human sees what meets the eyes, whereas the LORD sees into the heart."

**16:8** Then Jesse called Abinadab and had him pass before Samuel. And he said, "This one too the LORD has not chosen."

**16:9** Next Jesse had Shammah pass by. And he said, "This one too the LORD has not chosen."

**16:10** So Jesse had seven of his sons pass before Samuel. And Samuel said to Jesse, "The LORD has not chosen these."

**16:11** Samuel said to Jesse, "Are these all the young men?" And he said, "There still remains the youngest, and look, he is tending the sheep." Then Samuel said to Jesse, "Send and fetch him, for we will not sit down until he arrives here."

**16:12** So he sent and brought him in. He was ruddy, with handsome eyes and a pleasing appearance. And the LORD said, "Rise up, anoint him, for this is the one."

**16:13** Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers, and the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David from that day onward. Then Samuel rose and went to Ramah.

**16:14** Now the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the LORD terrified him.

**16:15** Saul's servants said to him, "Look now, an evil spirit from God is terrifying you.

**16:16** Let our lord please give the command, and your servants before you will seek out a man who knows how to play the lyre. Then whenever an evil spirit from God is upon you, he will play with his hand, and it will be well with you."

**16:17** So Saul said to his servants, "Please look for a man for me who plays well, and bring him to me."

**16:18** One of the young men answered and said, "Look, I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who knows how to play, a mighty man of strength, a man of war, discerning in speech, and a man of fine appearance; and the LORD is with him."

**16:19** So Saul sent messengers to Jesse and said, "Send me David your son, who is with the sheep."

**16:20** Then Jesse took a donkey loaded with bread, a skin of wine, and one young goat, and sent them by the hand of his son David to Saul.

**16:21** So David came to Saul and stood before him. Saul loved him greatly, and David became his armor-bearer.

**16:22** Then Saul sent word to Jesse, saying, "Please let David stand before me, for he has found favor in my eyes."

**16:23** So whenever the spirit from God came upon Saul, David would take the lyre and play with his hand; then Saul would be refreshed and it would be well with him, and the evil spirit would depart from him.

## Translator Notes

- **16:4** — The Masoretic Hebrew verb "and said" (wayyo'mer) is third-person masculine singular (literally "and he said"), likely indicating an individual elder speaking on behalf of the group.
- **16:7** — The Masoretic clause "for it is not what a human sees" is intentionally elliptical (literally "for not what man sees"), without the explicit verb supplied in the Septuagint; likewise, "what meets the eyes" is literally "to the eyes" (la'enayim), contrasted with "to the heart" (lallebab).
- **16:11** — The verb "remains" (sha'ar) is the only Qal occurrence of this root in the Hebrew Bible (elsewhere Niphal or Hiphil). The verb "sit down" (nasob, Qal of sbb, "turn, surround") refers here to gathering around the table for the sacrificial meal.
- **16:20** — The concise Hebrew phrase translated "a donkey loaded with bread" is literally "a donkey of bread" (chamor lechem), an idiomatic expression for a donkey carrying a load of bread.
