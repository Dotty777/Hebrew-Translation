## Text

**15:1** Samuel said to Saul, "It was me the LORD sent to anoint you as king over His people, over Israel. Now then, listen to the voice of the words of the LORD.

**15:2** This is what the LORD of hosts says: 'I have called to account what Amalek did to Israel, how he set himself against him on the way when Israel came up from Egypt.

**15:3** Now go and strike Amalek, and devote to complete destruction all that belongs to him; do not spare him, but put to death man and woman, young child and nursing infant, ox and sheep, camel and donkey.'"

**15:4** So Saul summoned the people and mustered them at Telaim, two hundred thousand foot soldiers and ten thousand men of Judah.

**15:5** Saul came as far as the city of Amalek and set an ambush in the valley.

**15:6** Then Saul said to the Kenites, "Go, depart, go down from among the Amalekites, so that I do not sweep you away with them; for you showed loyal kindness to all the sons of Israel when they came up from Egypt." So the Kenites departed from among Amalek.

**15:7** Then Saul struck Amalek from Havilah as one goes toward Shur, which lies east of Egypt.

**15:8** He seized Agag the king of Amalek alive, and all the people he devoted to complete destruction by the edge of the sword.

**15:9** But Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best of the sheep and the cattle, the second-born and the lambs, and all that was good, and they were not willing to devote them to complete destruction; yet all the property that was despised and worthless, that they devoted to complete destruction.

**15:10** Then the word of the LORD came to Samuel, saying,

**15:11** "I regret that I have made Saul king, for he has turned back from following Me and has not carried out My words." And Samuel became angry, and he cried out to the LORD all night.

**15:12** Samuel rose early in the morning to meet Saul, and it was reported to Samuel, saying, "Saul went to Carmel, and look, he has set up a monument for himself; then he turned around, passed on, and went down to Gilgal."

**15:13** Then Samuel came to Saul, and Saul said to him, "Blessed are you by the LORD! I have carried out the word of the LORD."

**15:14** But Samuel said, "Then what is this sound of sheep in my ears, and the sound of cattle that I am hearing?"

**15:15** Saul said, "They have brought them from the Amalekites, because the people spared the best of the sheep and the cattle in order to sacrifice to the LORD your God; and the rest we have devoted to complete destruction."

**15:16** Then Samuel said to Saul, "Stop, and let me tell you what the LORD spoke to me last night." And he said to him, "Speak."

**15:17** Samuel said, "Though you are small in your own eyes, are you not the head of the tribes of Israel? And the LORD anointed you as king over Israel.

**15:18** And the LORD sent you on a journey and said, 'Go and devote the sinners, Amalek, to complete destruction, and fight against them until they are completely consumed.'

**15:19** Why then did you not listen to the voice of the LORD, but swooped down upon the plunder and did what is evil in the eyes of the LORD?"

**15:20** Saul said to Samuel, "Indeed, I did listen to the voice of the LORD, and I went on the journey on which the LORD sent me; I brought Agag the king of Amalek, and Amalek I devoted to complete destruction.

**15:21** Yet the people took from the plunder sheep and cattle, the choicest of the devoted things, to sacrifice to the LORD your God at Gilgal."

**15:22** Then Samuel said, "Does the LORD take as much pleasure in burnt offerings and sacrifices as in listening to the voice of the LORD? Look, listening is better than sacrifice, and paying attention than the fat of rams.

**15:23** For rebellion is the sin of divination, and stubbornness is wickedness and household idols. Since you have rejected the word of the LORD, He has rejected you from being king."

**15:24** Then Saul said to Samuel, "I have sinned, for I have violated the command of the LORD and your words, because I feared the people and listened to their voice.

**15:25** Now then, please pardon my sin, and return with me so that I may bow in worship to the LORD."

**15:26** Samuel said to Saul, "I will not return with you, for you have rejected the word of the LORD, and the LORD has rejected you from being king over Israel."

**15:27** As Samuel turned around to go, Saul grasped the corner of his robe, and it tore.

**15:28** Then Samuel said to him, "The LORD has torn the kingship of Israel away from you today and has given it to your neighbor who is better than you.

**15:29** Moreover, the Splendor of Israel will not deal falsely and will not feel regret, for He is not a human being to feel regret."

**15:30** He said, "I have sinned; now please honor me before the elders of my people and before Israel, and return with me, and I will bow in worship to the LORD your God."

**15:31** So Samuel returned after Saul, and Saul bowed in worship to the LORD.

**15:32** Then Samuel said, "Bring Agag the king of Amalek here to me." And Agag walked to him confidently, and Agag said, "Surely the bitterness of death has departed."

**15:33** Samuel said, "Just as your sword has made women childless, so will your mother be childless among women." Then Samuel hacked Agag to pieces before the LORD at Gilgal.

**15:34** Then Samuel went to Ramah, while Saul went up to his house at Gibeah of Saul.

**15:35** Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death, for Samuel mourned over Saul. And the LORD regretted that He had made Saul king over Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **15:5** — The verb translated "set an ambush" (wayyareb) is a defective Hiphil form of the root 'rb ("lie in ambush"), though identical in spelling to the Qal of ryb ("contend"); the noun nachal denotes a seasonal streambed or wadi.
- **15:9** — The plural noun translated "second-born" (hammishnim, literally "the second ones") is uncertain in meaning, likely referring to prime animals of the second birth or second year. The word translated "despised" (nemibzah) is an unusual passive participle form of the root bzh, paired with nāmēs (נָמֵס), a Niphal participle of the root mss ("melt, waste away"), translated "worthless."
- **15:16** — Ketiv reads "and they said" (wayyo'meru), whereas the Qere followed here reads the singular "and he said" (wayyo'mer), referring to Saul.
- **15:29** — The unique divine title translated "the Splendor of Israel" (netsach yisra'el) derives from a noun denoting eminence, splendor, enduringness, or everlastingness.
- **15:32** — The adverbial noun translated "confidently" (ma'adannot) is a lexical crux: derived from the root 'dn ("delight, ease"), it suggests walking cheerfully or at ease, though some link it by metathesis to 'nd ("in chains") or follow the Septuagint ("trembling").
