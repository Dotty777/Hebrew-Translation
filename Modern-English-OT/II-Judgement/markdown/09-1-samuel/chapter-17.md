## Text

**17:1** The Philistines gathered their forces for battle, and they assembled at Socoh, which belongs to Judah, and encamped between Socoh and Azekah, at Ephes-dammim.

**17:2** Saul and the men of Israel assembled and encamped in the Valley of Elah, and they drew up for battle to meet the Philistines.

**17:3** The Philistines were standing on the mountain on this side, and Israel was standing on the mountain on that side, and the ravine was between them.

**17:4** A champion stepped out from the camps of the Philistines; his name was Goliath, from Gath, and his height was six cubits and a span.

**17:5** A bronze helmet was on his head, and he was dressed in a coat of scale armor; the weight of the coat of armor was five thousand shekels of bronze.

**17:6** Bronze greaves were on his legs, and a bronze javelin was between his shoulders.

**17:7** The wooden shaft of his spear was like a weavers' beam, and the blade of his spear was six hundred shekels of iron; and the one carrying the large shield walked ahead of him.

**17:8** He stood and called out to the battle lines of Israel and said to them, "Why are you coming out to draw up for battle? Am I not the Philistine, and you are servants of Saul? Select a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me.

**17:9** If he is able to fight with me and strikes me down, then we will be servants to you; but if I prevail over him and strike him down, then you will be servants to us and will serve us."

**17:10** Then the Philistine said, "I taunt the battle lines of Israel this day. Give me a man, and let us fight together."

**17:11** And Saul and all Israel heard these words of the Philistine, and they were dismayed and very afraid.

**17:32** Then David said to Saul, "Let no one's heart sink on his account. Your servant will go and fight with this Philistine."

**17:33** Saul said to David, "You cannot go against this Philistine to fight with him, because you are a youth, while he has been a man of war from his youth."

**17:34** David said to Saul, "Your servant has been tending the sheep for his father, and whenever a lion or a bear would come and carry off a lamb from the flock,

**17:35** I would go out after it, strike it, and rescue it from its mouth; and when it rose up against me, I would seize it by its beard, strike it, and put it to death.

**17:36** Your servant has struck down both the lion and the bear, and this uncircumcised Philistine will be like one of them, because he has taunted the battle lines of the living God."

**17:37** And David said, "The LORD, who rescued me from the paw of the lion and from the paw of the bear, He will rescue me from the hand of this Philistine." Then Saul said to David, "Go, and may the LORD be with you."

**17:38** Saul dressed David in his garments, placed a bronze helmet on his head, and dressed him in a coat of armor.

**17:39** David fastened his sword over his garments and tried to walk, because he had not tested them. Then David said to Saul, "I cannot walk in these, because I have not tested them." So David removed them from himself.

**17:40** Then he took his staff in his hand, selected for himself five smooth stones from the streambed, and placed them in his shepherd's bag, in the pouch; with his sling in his hand, he drew near to the Philistine.

**17:42** The Philistine looked intently and saw David, and he despised him, because he was a youth, ruddy and of handsome appearance.

**17:43** Then the Philistine said to David, "Am I a dog, that you are coming at me with staffs?" And the Philistine cursed David by his gods.

**17:44** And the Philistine said to David, "Come here to me, and I will give your flesh to the birds of the heavens and to the animals of the field."

**17:45** David said to the Philistine, "You are coming to me with a sword, with a spear, and with a javelin, but I am coming to you in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the battle lines of Israel, whom you have taunted.

**17:46** This day the LORD will surrender you into my hand, and I will strike you down and remove your head from you; and this day I will give the corpses of the camp of the Philistines to the birds of the heavens and to the wild beasts of the earth, and all the earth will know that Israel has a God,

**17:47** and this entire assembly will know that the LORD does not save by sword or by spear; for the battle belongs to the LORD, and He will give you into our hand."

**17:48** When the Philistine rose up, came forward, and approached to meet David, David hurried and ran toward the battle line to meet the Philistine.

**17:49** David reached his hand into the bag, took a stone from there, slung it, and struck the Philistine on his forehead; the stone sank into his forehead, and he fell face down to the ground.

**17:51** David ran and stood over the Philistine; he took his sword, pulled it from its sheath, put him to death, and cut off his head with it. When the Philistines saw that their mighty warrior was dead, they fled.

**17:52** Then the men of Israel and Judah rose up, raised a battle shout, and pursued the Philistines as far as the approach to a valley and as far as the gates of Ekron; and the fatally wounded of the Philistines fell along the road to Shaaraim, as far as Gath and as far as Ekron.

**17:53** The sons of Israel turned back from hotly pursuing the Philistines and plundered their camps.

**17:54** And David took the Philistine's head and brought it to Jerusalem, while he placed his weapons in his tent.

## Translator Notes

- **17:4** — The Hebrew phrase rendered 'champion' is literally 'the man of the space between,' referring to a warrior who fights single combat between opposing armies. The Masoretic Text gives his height as six cubits and a span, whereas the Septuagint and 4QSama read four cubits and a span.
- **17:7** — Following the Qere 'we-ets' ('and the wooden shaft of'), matching parallel descriptions in 2 Samuel 21:19 and 1 Chronicles 20:5; the Ketiv reads 'we-chets' ('and the arrow of').
- **17:34** — Hebrew uses singular verbs and habitual weqatal forms across verses 34-35 to describe repeated past encounters. The particle 'et' before 'the bear' is unusual before a subject and functions coordinately here ('or a bear').
- **17:39** — The verb rendered 'tried' is the Hiphil of 'ya'al' ('undertook, attempted'); the Septuagint appears to reflect a transposed root meaning 'grew weary' or 'struggled.'
- **17:52** — Masoretic Hebrew reads 'gay'' ('a valley,' or possibly a place name 'Gai'), whereas the Septuagint reads 'Gath,' matching the end of the verse.
