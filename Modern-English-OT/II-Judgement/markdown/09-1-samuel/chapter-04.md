## Text

**4:1** And the word of Samuel came to all Israel. Israel went out to face the Philistines for battle; they encamped by Ebenezer, while the Philistines encamped at Aphek.

**4:2** The Philistines drew up in formation to meet Israel, and the battle spread out. Israel was defeated before the Philistines, and they struck down about four thousand men in the battle line in the field.

**4:3** The people came into the camp, and the elders of Israel said, "Why has the LORD defeated us before the Philistines today? Let us take the ark of the covenant of the LORD from Shiloh to ourselves, so that it may come into our midst and save us from the grasp of our enemies."

**4:4** The people sent to Shiloh and carried from there the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts, who sits enthroned above the cherubim. And there with the ark of the covenant of God were Eli's two sons, Hophni and Phinehas.

**4:5** It happened when the ark of the covenant of the LORD came into the camp that all Israel shouted a great shout, and the earth reverberated.

**4:6** The Philistines heard the sound of the shout and said, "What is the sound of this great shout in the camp of the Hebrews?" And they learned that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp.

**4:7** So the Philistines were afraid, because they said, "God has come into the camp!" And they said, "Woe to us, for nothing like this has occurred before now.

**4:8** Woe to us! Who will rescue us from the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck Egypt with every plague in the wilderness.

**4:9** Strengthen yourselves and be men, Philistines, or else you will serve the Hebrews just as they served you. Be men and fight!"

**4:10** Then the Philistines fought, and Israel was defeated, and they fled, each man to his tent. The slaughter was very great, and thirty thousand foot soldiers of Israel fell.

**4:11** And the ark of God was captured, and the two sons of Eli died—Hophni and Phinehas.

**4:12** Then a man of Benjamin ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh on that day, with his garments torn and earth on his head.

**4:13** When he came, there was Eli sitting on the seat beside the road, keeping watch, because his heart was trembling for the ark of God. And the man came to report in the city, and the entire city cried out.

**4:14** Eli heard the sound of the outcry and said, "What is the sound of this tumult?" Then the man hurried and came and reported to Eli.

**4:15** Eli was ninety-eight years old, and his eyes were fixed, and he was unable to see.

**4:16** The man said to Eli, "I am the one who came from the battle line, and I myself fled from the battle line today." And he said, "What has happened, my son?"

**4:17** The bearer of the news answered and said, "Israel has fled before the Philistines, and there has also been a great defeat among the people. Also, your two sons have died—Hophni and Phinehas—and the ark of God has been captured."

**4:18** It happened when he mentioned the ark of God that he fell backward off the seat by the side of the gate; his neck was broken and he died, because the man was old and heavy. And he had judged Israel forty years.

**4:19** Now his daughter-in-law, Phinehas's wife, was pregnant and ready to give birth. When she heard the report concerning the capture of the ark of God and that her father-in-law and her husband had died, she crouched down and gave birth, because her labor pains came over her.

**4:20** Around the time of her death, the women standing beside her spoke: "Do not be afraid, because you have given birth to a son." But she did not answer, nor did she take it to heart.

**4:21** And she named the boy Ichabod, saying, "Glory has gone into exile from Israel," because of the capture of the ark of God and because of her father-in-law and her husband.

**4:22** She said, "Glory has gone into exile from Israel, because the ark of God has been captured."

## Translator Notes

- **4:8** — Whereas 4:7 pairs Elohim ("God") with a singular verb ("has come"), the Philistines here combine Elohim with plural modifiers and a plural Hiphil participle ("these mighty gods... who struck"), reflecting their polytheistic view.
- **4:13** — The Ketiv reads yk (an apparent graphic error); the Qere yad (literally "hand," idiomatically "side," as in 4:18) is translated here as "beside."
- **4:19** — Hebrew uses the rare contracted Qal infinitive construct lalat (for standard laledet, "to give birth") and the Niphal of haphak (literally "turned upon her") for the sudden onset of labor pains.
- **4:21** — Ichabod means "Where is glory?" or "No glory." The Qal verb galah means "went into exile" or "departed," and "glory" (kavod) shares the same Hebrew root as Eli being "heavy" (kaved, 4:18) and the LORD's hand being "heavy" (5:6, 11).
