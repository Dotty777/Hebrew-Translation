## Text

**13:1** On that day the book of Moses was read in the people's hearing, and it was found written in it that no Ammonite or Moabite may ever enter the assembly of God,

**13:2** because they did not come out to meet the sons of Israel with bread and water; rather, they hired Balaam against them to curse them, yet our God changed the curse into a blessing.

**13:3** Now when they heard the law, they separated everyone of mixed descent from Israel.

**13:4** Prior to this, Eliashib the priest, having been placed in charge of the chamber of the house of our God, was a relative of Tobiah,

**13:5** and he prepared a large chamber for him, where previously they used to place the grain offering, the frankincense, the vessels, and the tithe of the grain, the new wine, and the fresh oil—the prescribed portion for the Levites, the singers, and the gatekeepers—and the contribution for the priests.

**13:6** During all this I was not in Jerusalem, for in the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes king of Babylon I had gone to the king; then after some days I requested leave from the king.

**13:7** So I came to Jerusalem, and I perceived the evil that Eliashib had done for Tobiah in preparing a chamber for him in the courtyards of the house of God.

**13:8** It displeased me greatly, and I threw all the household furnishings of Tobiah out of the chamber.

**13:9** Then I gave a command, and they purified the chambers; and I returned there the vessels of the house of God, the grain offering, and the frankincense.

**13:10** I also learned that the Levites' portions had not been given, so the Levites and the singers doing the work had fled, each man to his field.

**13:11** So I contended with the officials and said, "Why has the house of God been abandoned?" Then I gathered them and stationed them at their post.

**13:12** Then all of Judah brought the tithe of the grain, the new wine, and the fresh oil to the storehouses.

**13:13** And I appointed as treasurers over the storehouses Shelemiah the priest, Zadok the scribe, and Pedaiah of the Levites, and as their aide, Hanan son of Zaccur, son of Mattaniah; for they were regarded as trustworthy, and the responsibility of distributing to their brothers rested upon them.

**13:14** Remember me for this, my God, and do not erase my faithful deeds that I have done for the house of my God and for its services.

**13:15** In those days I saw in Judah some who were treading winepresses on the Sabbath, bringing in heaps of grain and loading them on donkeys, as well as wine, grapes, figs, and every kind of load, and bringing them into Jerusalem on the Sabbath day. So I solemnly warned them on the day they sold provisions.

**13:16** And Tyrians lived there, bringing in fish and all sorts of wares, and selling them on the Sabbath to the sons of Judah, and in Jerusalem.

**13:17** Then I contended with the nobles of Judah and said to them, "What is this evil thing you are doing, desecrating the Sabbath day?

**13:18** Did your fathers not do like this, so that our God brought all this evil on us and on this city? Yet you are adding fierce wrath against Israel by desecrating the Sabbath."

**13:19** When the gates of Jerusalem fell into shadow before the Sabbath, I gave a command and the doors were shut, and I ordered that they not open them until after the Sabbath. And I stationed some of my attendants at the gates so that no load would enter on the Sabbath day.

**13:20** So the traders and the sellers of every kind of merchandise spent the night outside Jerusalem once or twice.

**13:21** Then I solemnly warned them and said to them, "Why are you spending the night in front of the wall? If you do this a second time, I will lay a hand on you." From that time forward they did not come on the Sabbath.

**13:22** Then I instructed the Levites that they should purify themselves and come to guard the gates in order to sanctify the Sabbath day. Remember me for this also, my God, and spare me according to the abundance of Your faithful love.

**13:23** Also in those days I saw the Jews who had married Ashdodite, Ammonite, and Moabite women.

**13:24** Of their children, half spoke the language of Ashdod, and they did not know how to speak the language of Judah, but spoke according to the language of each people.

**13:25** So I contended with them, cursed them, beat some of the men among them, and pulled out their hair; and I put them under oath by God: "You are not to give your daughters to their sons, nor take any of their daughters for your sons or for yourselves.

**13:26** Did Solomon king of Israel not sin because of these things? Among the many nations there was not a king like him; he was loved by his God, and God appointed him king over all Israel. Still, the foreign women caused even him to sin.

**13:27** And should we listen to you and commit all this great evil, breaking faith with our God by marrying foreign women?"

**13:28** Now one of the sons of Joiada, son of Eliashib the high priest, was a son-in-law to Sanballat the Horonite, and I drove him away from my presence.

**13:29** Remember them, my God, on account of the defiling of the priesthood and of the covenant of the priesthood and the Levites.

**13:30** So I purified them from all that was foreign, and I set up duties for the priests and for the Levites, each man in his work,

**13:31** and for the wood offering at designated times, and for the firstfruits. Remember me for good, my God.

## Translator Notes

- **13:13** — The verb wa'otserah is a rare denominative Hiphil of 'atsar (from 'otsar, storehouse/treasury), meaning appointed as treasurers, forming a wordplay with 'otsarot (storehouses).
- **13:19** — The Qal verb tsalelu (from tsalal, related to tsel, shadow) occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible in the sense of growing dark or falling into shadow, with the gates of Jerusalem as its grammatical subject.
- **13:23** — Ketiv writes the plene forms for Ashdodite and Ammonite while Qere reads the defective forms with no difference in meaning. The Hiphil verb hoshivu (from yashav, literally caused to dwell) is a Late Biblical Hebrew idiom used here and in 13:27 for marrying foreign women.
- **13:25** — Literally "if you give... and if you take," a standard Hebrew elliptical oath formula after the Hiphil verb of swearing (wa'ashbi'em) expressing an emphatic negative prohibition.
- **13:27** — The form hanishma' can be parsed as Qal imperfect 1cp ("should we listen" or "do we hear") or identically vocalized Niphal perfect 3ms ("has it been heard"), yielding either "should we listen to you" or "must we hear concerning you."
