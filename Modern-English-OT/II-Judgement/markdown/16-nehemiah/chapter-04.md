## Text

**4:1** Now when Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabs, the Ammonites, and the Ashdodites heard that repair was going forward on the walls of Jerusalem, that the breaches had begun to be closed up, they became very angry.

**4:2** All of them conspired together to come and fight against Jerusalem and to cause confusion in it.

**4:3** Yet we prayed to our God, and we stationed a guard against them day and night on account of them.

**4:4** Then Judah said, "The strength of the burden-bearer has faltered, and there is much rubble, and we are not able to build on the wall."

**4:5** And our adversaries said, "They will not know and will not see until we come into their midst and kill them and make the work cease."

**4:6** Now when the Jews who lived beside them came, they told us ten times from all the places, "You must return to us."

**4:7** So I stationed the people in the lowest parts of the space behind the wall, in the exposed places—I stationed them by families, with their swords, their spears, and their bows.

**4:8** Then I looked, and I rose and said to the nobles, to the officials, and to the rest of the people, "Do not fear them! Remember the great and awesome Lord, and fight for your brothers, your sons and your daughters, your wives and your houses."

**4:9** Now when our enemies heard that it was known to us and that God had thwarted their plan, all of us returned to the wall, each man to his work.

**4:10** From that day on, half of my attendants were doing the work while half of them held the spears, the shields, the bows, and the coats of mail, and the leaders were behind the entire house of Judah.

**4:11** Those who were building on the wall and those who were carrying burdens were loading up: with one hand each was doing the work, and the other held a weapon.

**4:12** And as for the builders, each man had his sword strapped to his waist as they built; and the one who blew the ram's horn was beside me.

**4:13** And I said to the nobles, to the officials, and to the rest of the people, "The work is great and extensive, and we are separated on the wall, far from one another.

**4:14** In whatever place you hear the sound of the ram's horn, gather to us there. Our God will fight for us."

**4:15** So we were doing the work, while half of them held the spears from the rising of the dawn until the stars came out.

**4:16** At that time I also said to the people, "Let each man and his attendant spend the night inside Jerusalem, so that they may be a guard for us by night and labor by day."

**4:17** So neither I, nor my brothers, nor my attendants, nor the men of the guard who followed me—none of us took off our clothes; each man kept his weapon even at the water.

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — The Hebrew clause beginning with 'from all the places' (mikkol-hammeqomot asher-tashuvu alenu) is notoriously terse. Here asher is taken as introducing speech (as elsewhere in Nehemiah) and al as 'to,' rendering a plea from the outlying Jews for the builders to return home, though others construe it elliptically as a warning of attacks from every direction.
- **4:7** — Translates the Qere batsehihim ('in the exposed places'); the Ketiv btshhyym transposes the medial yod and chet. Hebrew repeats the verb "I stationed" (wa'a'amid) before naming the object "the people" (et-ha'am).
- **4:9** — Translates the Qere wannashov ('and we returned,' standard Qal wayyiqtol 1cp of shuv); the Ketiv wnshwb reflects a fuller spelling of the verb.
- **4:17** — The final three Hebrew words (ish shilho hammayim, literally 'each man his weapon, the water') are famously elliptical, generally understood to mean that each man kept his weapon with him even when going to the water.
