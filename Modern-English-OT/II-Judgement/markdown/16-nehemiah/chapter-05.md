## Text

**5:1** Now there was a great outcry from the people and their wives against their Jewish brothers.

**5:2** There were those who said, "We, with our sons and our daughters, are many; let us get grain so that we may eat and live."

**5:3** And there were those who said, "We are pledging our fields, our vineyards, and our houses so that we may get grain in the famine."

**5:4** And there were those who said, "We have borrowed silver for the king's tax on our fields and our vineyards.

**5:5** And now, our flesh is like the flesh of our brothers, our children are like their children; yet look, we are forcing our sons and our daughters to be slaves, and some of our daughters have been brought into bondage, and there is no power in our hand, since our fields and our vineyards belong to others."

**5:6** I became very angry when I heard their outcry and these words.

**5:7** I took counsel within myself, and I contended with the nobles and the officials and said to them, "You are exacting interest, each man from his brother!" So I held a great assembly against them.

**5:8** And I said to them, "As far as we were able, we have bought back our Jewish brothers who were sold to the nations; and now will you even sell your brothers, so that they must be sold to us?" Then they kept silent and could find no reply.

**5:9** So I said, "The thing that you are doing is not good. Should you not walk in the fear of our God because of the reproach of the nations, our enemies?

**5:10** Moreover, I, my brothers, and my attendants are lending them silver and grain. Please, let us give up this interest.

**5:11** Please, return to them this very day their fields, their vineyards, their olive groves, and their houses, along with the hundredth part of the silver, the grain, the new wine, and the fresh oil that you are exacting from them."

**5:12** Then they said, "We will return it and will demand nothing from them; we will do just as you say." So I summoned the priests and put them under oath to act according to this promise.

**5:13** I also shook out the fold of my garment and said, "In this way may God shake out every man who does not uphold this promise from his house and from his earnings, and in this way may he be shaken out and left empty." Then the entire assembly said, "Amen," and praised the LORD; and the people acted according to this promise.

**5:14** Moreover, from the day that I was appointed to be their governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes—twelve years—neither I nor my brothers ate the food of the governor.

**5:15** But the former governors who were before me laid heavy burdens on the people and took from them food and wine, besides forty shekels of silver; even their attendants lorded it over the people. Yet I did not do so, because of the fear of God.

**5:16** I also persevered in the work on this wall, and we bought no field; and all my attendants were gathered there for the work.

**5:17** Moreover, the Jews and the officials, one hundred and fifty men, as well as those who came to us from the nations that were around us, were at my table.

**5:18** Now what was prepared for each day was one ox and six choice sheep; fowl were also prepared for me, and every ten days all kinds of wine in abundance. Yet for all this, I did not demand the food of the governor, because the service was heavy upon this people.

**5:19** Remember for me, my God, for good, all that I have done for this people.

## Translator Notes

- **5:7** — Translates the Qere noshim (Qal active participle of nashah, 'exacting interest' or 'a loan-claim'); the Ketiv nsh'ym reflects the interchange of nashah with nasha or nasa ('imposing'). The opening verb wayyimmalekh (Niphal of malakh, influenced by Aramaic melakh, 'counsel') literally means 'my heart took counsel within me.'
- **5:9** — Translates the Qere wa'omar ('and I said,' first-person singular matching Nehemiah's memoir); the Ketiv reads third-person wy'mr ('and he said').
- **5:11** — Hebrew ume'at literally means 'and the hundredth part,' widely understood as a monthly one-percent interest rate (twelve percent annually) charged on loans of silver and produce.
- **5:15** — The terse phrase belehem wayayin ahar kesef-sheqalim arba'im uses ahar ('after') in the sense of 'besides' or 'in addition to' forty shekels of silver.
