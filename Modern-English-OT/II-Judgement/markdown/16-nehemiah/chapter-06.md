## Text

**6:1** Now when it was reported to Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem the Arab, and the rest of our enemies that I had built the wall and that no breach remained in it, even though up to that time I had not set up doors in the gates,

**6:2** Sanballat and Geshem sent word to me, saying, "Come, let us meet together at Chephirim in the plain of Ono." But they were planning to do me harm.

**6:3** So I sent messengers to them, saying, "It is a great work that I am doing, and I am not able to go down. Why should the work cease while I let go of it and go down to you?"

**6:4** They sent to me in this same way four times, and I answered them in the same way.

**6:5** Then Sanballat sent his attendant to me in this same way a fifth time, with an open letter in his hand.

**6:6** In it was written, "It is reported among the nations, and Gashmu says, that you and the Jews are planning to rebel; therefore you are building the wall. And you are to become their king, according to these reports.

**6:7** You have also appointed prophets to proclaim concerning you in Jerusalem, saying, 'There is a king in Judah!' Now it will be reported to the king according to these words. So now come, and let us take counsel together."

**6:8** Then I sent word to him, saying, "No such things as you are saying have happened, for you are inventing them out of your own mind."

**6:9** For all of them were trying to intimidate us, saying, "Their hands will grow slack from the work, and it will not be done." But now, strengthen my hands!

**6:10** Now I went to the house of Shemaiah son of Delaiah, son of Mehetabel, who was shut in; and he said, "Let us meet together in the house of God, inside the temple, and let us close the doors of the temple, for they are coming to kill you—at night they are coming to kill you."

**6:11** Yet I answered, "Would a man like me flee? And who like me would enter the temple and live? I will not enter."

**6:12** Then I perceived that indeed God had not sent him; rather, he spoke the prophecy against me because Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him.

**6:13** For this reason he was hired: that I might be afraid, do so, and sin, so that they might have an evil report in order to reproach me.

**6:14** Remember, my God, Tobiah and Sanballat according to these deeds of theirs, and also Noadiah the prophetess and the rest of the prophets who were trying to intimidate me.

**6:15** Thus the wall was finished on the twenty-fifth of Elul, in fifty-two days.

**6:16** Now when all our enemies heard of it, all the nations that were around us were afraid, and they fell very low in their own eyes; for they knew that this work had been done by our God.

**6:17** Also in those days, the nobles of Judah were sending many letters to Tobiah, and letters from Tobiah were coming to them.

**6:18** For many in Judah were bound by oath to him, because he was the son-in-law of Shecaniah son of Arah, and his son Jehohanan had married the daughter of Meshullam son of Berechiah.

**6:19** They were also speaking of his good deeds before me, and they were reporting my words to him. Tobiah sent letters to intimidate me.

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — Hebrew bakkephirim is vocalized as a plural proper place name ('at Chephirim,' compare Chephirah in Neh 7:29), though many interpreters take it as a variant plural of kaphar meaning 'in the villages.'
- **6:9** — The Piel imperative hazzeq ('strengthen') is an abrupt prayer addressed to God without an explicit vocative in the Hebrew text.
- **6:14** — Hebrew kema'asaw elleh literally reads 'according to these deeds of his,' with the third-person masculine singular suffix agreeing with the first-named subject, Tobiah, while encompassing both men.
- **6:16** — Hebrew wayyir'u, written with a single yod and a meteg under the hireq, is translated here as a defective Qal wayyiqtol of yare' ('were afraid,' reversing the intimidation motif of 6:9, 13-14, 19); it can also be read as the Qal of ra'ah ('saw [it]').
