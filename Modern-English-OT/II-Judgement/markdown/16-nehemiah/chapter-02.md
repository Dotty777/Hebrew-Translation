## Text

**2:1** It came about in the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, when wine was before him, that I took up the wine and gave it to the king. Now I had not been sad in his presence.

**2:2** So the king said to me, "Why is your face sad, though you are not sick? This is nothing other than sadness of heart." Then I was very much afraid.

**2:3** I said to the king, "May the king live forever! Why should my face not be sad, when the city, the place of my fathers' tombs, lies desolate, and its gates have been consumed by fire?"

**2:4** Then the king said to me, "What is it that you are requesting?" So I prayed to the God of heaven.

**2:5** And I said to the king, "If it seems good to the king, and if your servant is pleasing before you, send me to Judah, to the city of my fathers' tombs, that I may rebuild it."

**2:6** Then the king said to me—with the queen sitting beside him—"How long will your journey be, and when will you return?" So it pleased the king to send me, and I gave him a set time.

**2:7** And I said to the king, "If it seems good to the king, let letters be given to me for the governors of Beyond the River, that they may let me pass through until I arrive in Judah,

**2:8** and a letter to Asaph, the keeper of the king's forest, that he may give me timber to lay beams for the gates of the citadel belonging to the temple, and for the wall of the city, and for the house that I will enter." And the king granted them to me, according to the good hand of my God upon me.

**2:9** Then I came to the governors of Beyond the River and gave them the king's letters. Now the king had sent army officers and horsemen with me.

**2:10** When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite servant heard of it, it was greatly displeasing to them that a man had come to seek the good of the sons of Israel.

**2:11** So I came to Jerusalem and was there three days.

**2:12** Then I arose in the night, I and a few men with me; and I did not tell anyone what my God was putting into my heart to do for Jerusalem. And there was no animal with me except the animal on which I was riding.

**2:13** So I went out by the Valley Gate at night, toward the front of the Dragon Spring and to the Dung Gate; and I was inspecting the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and its gates had been consumed by fire.

**2:14** Then I passed on to the Fountain Gate and to the King's Pool, but there was no room for the animal under me to pass.

**2:15** So I went up along the valley at night and continued inspecting the wall; then I turned back, entered through the Valley Gate, and returned.

**2:16** And the officials did not know where I had gone or what I was doing, nor had I yet told the Jews, the priests, the nobles, the officials, or the rest of those doing the work.

**2:17** Then I said to them, "You see the distress we are in, how Jerusalem lies desolate and its gates are burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, so that we may no longer be a reproach."

**2:18** And I told them about the hand of my God, which had been good upon me, and also the king's words that he had spoken to me. And they said, "Let us arise and build." So they strengthened their hands for the good cause.

**2:19** But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite servant, and Geshem the Arab heard of it, they mocked us and despised us, and said, "What is this thing that you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

**2:20** So I gave them an answer and said to them, "The God of heaven—He will grant us success, and we, His servants, will arise and build; but as for you, you have no portion, right, or memorial in Jerusalem."

## Translator Notes

- **2:8** — 'the temple' is literally 'the house' (Hebrew habbayit), referring here to the temple in distinction from Nehemiah's own residence ('the house that I will enter') at the end of the clause.
- **2:10** — 'servant' (Hebrew ebed) likely serves here as a Persian administrative title ('official') for Tobiah in Ammon, while preserving the literal term contrasted with 'His servants' in 2:20.
- **2:13** — Ketiv writes hmprwṣym as one word with a medial mem, whereas Qere divides it into two words, hem perusim ('which were broken down').
- **2:20** — 'right' renders Hebrew sedaqa ('righteousness'), used here in its legal sense of a valid claim or standing.
