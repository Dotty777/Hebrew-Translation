## Text

**8:1** Then all the people assembled as one man into the plaza that was in front of the Water Gate, and they told Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses, which the LORD had commanded Israel.

**8:2** So Ezra the priest brought the law before the assembly, both men and women and everyone able to hear with understanding, on the first day of the seventh month.

**8:3** He read from it facing the plaza that was in front of the Water Gate from first light until midday, in the presence of the men, the women, and those who could understand, and the ears of all the people were turned toward the book of the law.

**8:4** Ezra the scribe stood on a wooden platform that they had made for the occasion. Standing beside him on his right were Mattithiah, Shema, Anaiah, Uriah, Hilkiah, and Maaseiah, and on his left were Pedaiah, Mishael, Malchijah, Hashum, Hashbaddanah, Zechariah, and Meshullam.

**8:5** Then Ezra opened the book before the eyes of all the people, for he was above all the people; and as he opened it, all the people stood up.

**8:6** Ezra blessed the LORD, the great God, and all the people answered, "Amen, Amen," lifting up their hands. Then they bowed low and worshiped the LORD with their faces to the ground.

**8:7** Also Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodiah, Maaseiah, Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan, Pelaiah, and the Levites were helping the people understand the law, while the people remained in their place.

**8:8** They read distinctly from the book, from the law of God, and gave the meaning, so that they understood what was read.

**8:9** Then Nehemiah, who was the governor, and Ezra the priest and scribe, and the Levites who were giving the people understanding said to all the people, "This day is holy to the LORD your God; do not mourn and do not weep." For all the people were weeping when they heard the words of the law.

**8:10** Then he said to them, "Go, eat rich foods and drink sweet drinks, and send portions to the one for whom nothing is prepared, for this day is holy to our Lord. And do not be sorrowful, for the joy of the LORD is your stronghold."

**8:11** And the Levites were quieting all the people, saying, "Be quiet, for this day is holy, and do not be sorrowful."

**8:12** Then all the people went away to eat and to drink, to send portions, and to celebrate with great joy, because they understood the words that had been made known to them.

**8:13** On the second day, the heads of the fathers' houses of all the people, the priests, and the Levites gathered to Ezra the scribe in order to gain insight into the words of the law.

**8:14** And they found written in the law, which the LORD had commanded by the hand of Moses, that the sons of Israel should live in booths during the feast in the seventh month,

**8:15** and that they should announce and send out a proclamation in all their cities and in Jerusalem, saying, "Go out to the hill country and bring branches of olive, branches of wild olive, branches of myrtle, branches of palms, and branches of leafy trees to make booths, as it is written."

**8:16** So the people went out and brought them and made booths for themselves, each on his roof, and in their courtyards, and in the courtyards of the house of God, and in the plaza of the Water Gate, and in the plaza of the Gate of Ephraim.

**8:17** The entire assembly of those who had returned from the captivity made booths and lived in the booths. For from the days of Joshua the son of Nun until that day, the sons of Israel had not done so, and there was very great joy.

**8:18** Day by day, from the first day until the last day, he read from the book of the law of God. They observed the feast for seven days, and on the eighth day there was a solemn assembly according to the ordinance.

## Translator Notes

- **8:6** — The noun translated "lifting up" (Hebrew mo'al) is a hapax legomenon derived from the root 'alah ("go up"), referring to raising the hands in prayer and blessing.
- **8:8** — The Pual participle mephorash ("distinctly") can also mean "with translation" or "paragraph by paragraph" (cf. the cognate Aramaic participle in Ezra 4:18). The verb wayyavinu with bammiqra' is taken intransitively as "they understood what was read" (cf. v. 12), though it could also be parsed as a causative Hiphil meaning "they gave understanding of the reading."
- **8:9** — The title "the governor" renders the Persian loanword hattirshata', used of the governor of Judah (cf. Ezra 2:63; Neh 7:65, 70; 10:2).
- **8:10** — The noun ma'oz ("stronghold") denotes a fortified refuge or place of strength.
