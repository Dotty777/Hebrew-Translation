## Text

**3:1** After this, Job opened his mouth and cursed his day.

**3:2** And Job answered and said:

**3:3** "Perish the day on which I was born, and the night that said, 'A male child has been conceived!'

**3:4** That day—let it be darkness! May God not seek it out from above, and may no daylight shine forth upon it.

**3:5** May darkness and the shadow of death lay claim to it; may a thick cloud settle over it; may the blacknesses of day terrify it.

**3:6** That night—let thick gloom seize it; let it not rejoice among the days of the year; into the number of the months let it not enter.

**3:7** Look, may that night be barren; may no shout of rejoicing come into it.

**3:8** May those who place curses on the day curse it, those who stand ready to awaken Leviathan.

**3:9** May the stars of its twilight grow dark; may it wait for light, yet there be none, and may it not look upon the eyelids of the dawn,

**3:10** because it did not close the doors of my mother's womb, nor conceal misery from my eyes.

**3:11** Why did I not die from the womb, and breathe my last when I came forth from the womb?

**3:12** Why did knees meet me, and why breasts, that I should nurse?

**3:13** For by now I would have lain down and stayed quiet; I would have fallen asleep, and then rest would be mine,

**3:14** with kings and earth's counselors, those who built ruins for themselves,

**3:15** or with rulers who possessed gold, those who filled their houses with silver.

**3:16** Or like a hidden miscarriage I would not exist, like infants who never saw light.

**3:17** There the wicked leave off turmoil, and there those worn out in strength rest.

**3:18** Together prisoners are at ease; they do not hear a taskmaster's voice.

**3:19** Both small and great are there, and a slave is free from his master.

**3:20** Why does He give light to one in misery, and life to those bitter of soul,

**3:21** who wait for death, yet it is not there, and dig after it more than for buried treasures,

**3:22** who rejoice even to exultation and are glad when they find a grave—

**3:23** to a man whose path is hidden, and whom God has hedged around?

**3:24** For before my bread my sighing comes, and my groans pour forth like water.

**3:25** For the dread I dreaded has come upon me, and what I feared comes to me.

**3:26** I have not been at ease, nor have I been quiet, nor have I rested—yet turmoil has come."

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — The passive Pual perfect horah ("has been conceived") is the sole Pual form of harah in Biblical Hebrew, paired with gever ("male" / "man," here a male child).
- **3:5** — Qal yig'aluhu comes from ga'al ("reclaim" as kinsman-redeemer, or possibly a homonym meaning "defile"). Masoretic tsalmavet vocalizes "shadow of death" (often analyzed etymologically as "deep gloom"), and kimrire yom ("blacknesses of day") is a hapax legomenon usually linked to a root meaning "grow dark."
- **3:6** — Following the Masoretic pointing, Qal jussive yichad derives from chadah ("rejoice," as in Exod 18:9); some ancient versions and modern translations revocalize to yechad from yachad ("be joined").
- **3:10** — Hebrew literally reads "the doors of my womb" (bitni), referring idiomatically to the womb that conceived and bore him (parallel to "his day" in 3:1).
- **3:20** — Active Qal yitten ("does He give") has no explicit subject here, anticipating "God" (Eloah) in 3:23; some render it impersonally ("is light given"). In 3:23, "hedged around" (wayyasekh ... ba'ado) ironically echoes Satan's words in 1:10.
