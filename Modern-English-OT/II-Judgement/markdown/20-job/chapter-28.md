## Text

**28:1** Indeed, there is a source for silver, and a place for gold where they refine it.

**28:2** Iron is taken out of dust, and stone is smelted into copper.

**28:3** He sets an end to darkness, and to every extremity he searches out the stone of gloom and deep shadow.

**28:4** He breaks open a shaft away from any dweller; forgotten by any foot, they dangle far from mortals, they sway.

**28:5** The earth—bread comes forth from it, yet underneath it is overturned as by fire.

**28:6** Its stones are a place of sapphire, and dust of gold belongs to it.

**28:7** A path—no bird of prey knows it, nor has the eye of the falcon glimpsed it;

**28:8** majestic beasts have not trodden upon it, nor has any lion passed across it.

**28:9** He stretches out his hand against the flint; he overturns mountains from the root.

**28:10** He splits open channels in the rocks, and his eye sees all that is precious.

**28:11** He binds up streams from trickling, and he brings what is hidden forth to light.

**28:12** Yet wisdom—from where can it be found? And where then is the place of understanding?

**28:13** No mortal knows its worth, and it is not to be found in the land of the living.

**28:14** The abyss says, "It is not within me," and the sea says, "It is not with me."

**28:15** Solid gold will not be given in return for it, nor will silver be weighed out for its price.

**28:16** It cannot be appraised against the gold of Ophir, against precious onyx or sapphire.

**28:17** Gold and glass cannot match it, nor can a vessel of refined gold be its exchange.

**28:18** Coral and crystal will not be mentioned, and the possession of wisdom is above pearls.

**28:19** The topaz of Cush cannot match it; against pure gold it cannot be appraised.

**28:20** Yet wisdom—from where does it come? And where then is the place of understanding?

**28:21** It is hidden from the eyes of every living thing, and from the birds of the heavens it is concealed.

**28:22** Abaddon and Death say, "With our ears we heard its rumor."

**28:23** God understands its way, and He Himself knows its place.

**28:24** For He gazes to the ends of the earth and sees beneath all the heavens.

**28:25** When He made a weight for the wind and gauged the waters by measure,

**28:26** when He made a statute for the rain and a way for the flash of thunder,

**28:27** then He saw it and recounted it; He established it and searched it out as well.

**28:28** And He said to mankind, "See, the fear of the Lord—that is wisdom, and turning aside from evil is understanding."

## Translator Notes

- **28:1** — Hebrew has no quotation marks and introduces no new speaker formula between 27:1 and 29:1; interpreters debate whether chapter 28 continues Job's speech or stands as an unmarked wisdom interlude.
- **28:3** — The unexpressed masculine singular subject in vv. 3-4 and 9-11 refers generically to the human miner rather than to God.
- **28:4** — The Masoretic Hebrew of this verse is notoriously obscure, using rare mining imagery: nachal (ordinarily a wadi or stream) here denotes a vertical shaft, meim-gar means literally "away from a sojourner/inhabitant," and dallu refers to miners dangling suspended by ropes.
- **28:8** — Hebrew benei-shachats means literally "sons of pride," a poetic idiom for majestic wild beasts.
- **28:11** — Hebrew mibbekhi means literally "from weeping," a poetic image for underground water seeping or trickling into mine shafts; the Piel verb chibbesh means "binds up" or "dams up."
- **28:18** — Hebrew meshekh (from the root "draw" or "pull") is uncertain here, denoting either the "possession" or "acquisition" of wisdom or possibly a "pouch" full of it.
