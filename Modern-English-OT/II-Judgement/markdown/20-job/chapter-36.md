## Text

**36:1** Then Elihu proceeded and said,

**36:2** "Wait for me a little while, and I will inform you, for there are still words on behalf of God.

**36:3** I will bring my knowledge from afar, and I will attribute righteousness to my Maker.

**36:4** For truly my words are not falsehood; one complete in knowledge is with you.

**36:5** Look, God is mighty and does not despise anyone; He is mighty in strength of heart.

**36:6** He does not preserve the wicked alive, but He grants justice to the afflicted.

**36:7** He does not take His eyes off the righteous; rather, with kings on the throne He seats them forever, and they are lifted high.

**36:8** And if they are bound in shackles, captured in ropes of affliction,

**36:9** then He declares to them their deed and their transgressions, that they have behaved proudly.

**36:10** He opens their ear to discipline and says that they must turn back from iniquity.

**36:11** If they listen and serve Him, they will finish their days in well-being and their years in delights.

**36:12** But if they do not listen, they will pass away by the weapon and expire without knowledge.

**36:13** Yet the godless in heart harbor anger; they do not cry out for help when He binds them.

**36:14** Their soul dies in youth, and their life ends among the shrine prostitutes.

**36:15** He rescues the afflicted in his affliction and opens their ear in oppression.

**36:16** Moreover, He lured you from the mouth of distress—in place of it, a broad space with no confinement—and what rested on your table was full of rich food.

**36:17** Yet you are full of the judgment on the wicked; judgment and justice take hold.

**36:18** For there is wrath—watch that it does not lure you into scoffing, and do not let the magnitude of a ransom turn you aside.

**36:19** Will your cry for help suffice so that you are not in distress, or all the exertions of strength?

**36:20** Do not yearn for the night, when peoples disappear where they stand.

**36:21** Guard yourself—do not turn toward iniquity, for you have chosen this over affliction.

**36:22** Look, God is exalted in His strength; who is an instructor like Him?

**36:23** Who has assigned His way to Him, and who has said, 'You have committed injustice'?

**36:24** Remember to magnify His work, of which people have sung.

**36:25** All mankind has gazed upon it; mortal man looks on from afar.

**36:26** See, God is great, and we do not know Him; the number of His years is beyond searching.

**36:27** For He draws up drops of water; out of His mist they distill as rain,

**36:28** which the skies pour out; they shower upon mankind in abundance.

**36:29** Moreover, can anyone understand the spreadings of the thick clouds, the roarings of His canopy?

**36:30** Look, He spreads out His lightning around Himself, and He covers the roots of the sea.

**36:31** For through them He judges peoples; He supplies food in abundance.

**36:32** He covers His palms with lightning and commands it to hit the target.

**36:33** Its crashing tells of Him; the livestock also tell of what is rising."

## Translator Notes

- **36:16** — The Hebrew syntax is compressed: "lured you" renders the Hiphil perfect of sut (echoed in 36:18), "in place of it" reflects tachteha (lit. "beneath/instead of it"), and "what rested" renders nachat (from nuach, "rest").
- **36:18** — The opening Hebrew clause is elliptic (lit. "for wrath, so that it not lure you"), and the rare noun sepeq (from sapaq, "slap/clap") can mean "scoffing/derision" or alternatively "a blow" or "abundance."
- **36:19** — Hebrew shua can mean either "cry for help" (matching the verb in 36:13) or "wealth/opulence," and the concise phrase lo vetsar (lit. "not in distress") follows the Masoretic vocalization for tsar ("distress").
- **36:30** — Hebrew or literally means "light," used poetically in storm contexts here and in 36:32, 37:3, 37:11, and 37:15 to denote lightning.
- **36:33** — This verse is notoriously difficult in Hebrew; "its crashing" renders reo (from rua, "shout/roar"), while "livestock" (miqneh) and "what is rising" (oleh, Qal participle of alah, "ascend," referring to the rising storm) follow the Masoretic vocalization strictly.
