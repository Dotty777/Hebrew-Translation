## Text

**14:1** Man, born of a woman, is short of days and filled with turmoil.

**14:2** Like a blossom he springs forth and withers; he flees like a shadow and does not endure.

**14:3** Do You open Your eye even on such a one, and bring me into judgment with Yourself?

**14:4** Who can produce what is clean from what is unclean? Not one.

**14:5** Since his days are fixed, the number of his months is with You, and You have set his bounds that he cannot cross,

**14:6** look away from him so that he may rest, until he completes his day like a hired worker.

**14:7** For a tree has hope: if it is cut down, it will sprout again, and its tender shoot will not cease.

**14:8** Even if its root grows old in the ground, and its stump dies in the dust,

**14:9** at the scent of water it will blossom and yield boughs like a sapling.

**14:10** Yet a man dies and lies powerless; a human expires, and where is he?

**14:11** Waters vanish from a sea, and a river grows parched and dries up;

**14:12** so a man lies down and does not rise. Until the heavens are no more, they will not wake up or be roused from their sleep.

**14:13** If only You would conceal me in Sheol, hide me until Your anger turns away, appoint a limit for me, and remember me!

**14:14** If a man dies, will he live? All the days of my hard service I would wait until my relief comes.

**14:15** You would call, and I myself would answer You; You would yearn for the work of Your hands.

**14:16** For then You would count my steps; You would not watch over my sin.

**14:17** My transgression would be sealed in a pouch, and You would plaster over my iniquity.

**14:18** However, a falling mountain crumbles, and a rock shifts from its place;

**14:19** waters grind down stones; its torrents sweep away the dust of the earth; so You destroy the hope of a mortal.

**14:20** You overpower him forever, and he goes away; You alter his face and dismiss him.

**14:21** His sons gain honor, and he does not know; they sink low, and he does not notice them.

**14:22** Only his flesh aches upon him, and his soul mourns within him.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — The Masoretic Text has the singular 'Your eye' and shifts abruptly to the first-person object 'me' rather than third-person 'him'.
- **14:6** — The Qal verb ratzah here can mean 'complete / pay off' a term of service (as in Lev 26:34; Isa 40:2) or 'enjoy / take pleasure in' one's day.
- **14:14** — Hard service (tzava) denotes compulsory or military duty, pairing with 'relief' (chalifah, also 'renewal' or 'replacement', cognate with the Hiphil verb 'sprout again' in v. 7).
- **14:16** — Continuing the wish of vv. 13-15, ki-attah ('for then') and the negative clause depict God ceasing to track Job's sin; alternatively, taking ki-attah as 'for now' and the second clause as an unmarked rhetorical question ('do You not watch over my sin?'), vv. 16-17 describe God preserving Job's sins as evidence.
- **14:19** — Hebrew seficheha ('its torrents', from a root denoting pouring out or scattering) is a rare poetic plural construed with a feminine singular verb, with its suffix referring back to the waters.
