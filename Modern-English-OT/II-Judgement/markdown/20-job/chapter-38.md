## Text

**38:1** Then the LORD answered Job from the storm and said,

**38:2** "Who is this darkening counsel with words lacking knowledge?

**38:3** Now gird your loins like a strong man; I will question you, and inform Me.

**38:4** Where were you when I founded the earth? Declare, if you know understanding.

**38:5** Who set its dimensions, since you know, or who stretched a line across it?

**38:6** Upon what were its pedestals sunk, or who laid its cornerstone,

**38:7** while the stars of morning rang out together and all the sons of God raised a joyful shout?

**38:8** And who hedged in the sea with double doors when it gushed out, emerging from the womb,

**38:9** when I set a cloud as its clothing and dense darkness as its swaddling cloth,

**38:10** and prescribed My boundary for it, and set a bar and double doors,

**38:11** and said, 'Up to here you shall come, and go no further; and here the pride of your waves shall be halted'?

**38:12** Ever since your days began, have you commanded the morning and made the dawn know its place,

**38:13** to grasp the edges of the earth so that the wicked are shaken from it?

**38:14** It is transformed like sealing clay, and they stand out like clothing.

**38:15** And from the wicked their light is held back, and a lifted arm is broken.

**38:16** Have you come as far as the wellsprings of the sea, and walked about in the recesses of the deep?

**38:17** Have the gates of death been uncovered to you, and do you see the gates of deep shadow?

**38:18** Have you discerned the full breadths of the earth? Declare, if you know all of it.

**38:19** Where is the way to where light dwells, and as for darkness, where is its place,

**38:20** so that you might take it to its boundary and understand the paths to its house?

**38:21** You know, since you were born back then, and your days are many in number!

**38:22** Have you come to the treasuries of snow, and have you seen the treasuries of hail,

**38:23** which I have kept in reserve for a time of distress, for a day of battle and war?

**38:24** Where is the way along which light is distributed, and the east wind spreads out over the earth?

**38:25** Who has split open a channel for the downpour, and a way for the bolt of thunder,

**38:26** to send rain on a land with no man, a wilderness with no human in it,

**38:27** to satisfy waste and desolation, and to make shoots of fresh grass sprout?

**38:28** Is there a father for the rain, or who has fathered the drops of dew?

**38:29** From whose womb did the ice emerge, and as for the frost of heaven, who gave birth to it?

**38:30** Waters hide themselves like stone, and the face of the deep locks together.

**38:31** Can you tie the chains of the Pleiades, or untie the cords of Orion?

**38:32** Can you bring out Mazzaroth in its season, and lead the Bear along with her children?

**38:33** Do you know the statutes of heaven, or can you set its rule upon the earth?

**38:34** Can you raise your voice to the cloud so that a deluge of water covers you?

**38:35** Can you send out lightning bolts so that they go and say to you, 'Here we are'?

**38:36** Who placed wisdom in the inner depths, or who gave the mind understanding?

**38:37** Who can count the clouds with wisdom, and who can tip the waterskins of heaven,

**38:38** when dust runs into a hardened mass and clods are stuck together?

**38:39** Can you hunt prey for a lion, and fill the appetite of young lions,

**38:40** when they crouch in the dens, waiting in the thicket in ambush?

**38:41** Who prepares its provision for the raven when its young cry out to God for help, wandering with no food?"

## Translator Notes

- **38:1** — Ketiv writes מנהסערה as a single word; Qere divides it into two words (מִן הַסְּעָרָה, "from the storm"), which is followed in the translation.
- **38:11** — Literally "and here one sets [a limit] against the pride of your waves"; the Qal imperfect יָשִׁית ("sets") is used impersonally to express halting the surging sea.
- **38:12** — Ketiv reads ידעתה שחר (placing the ה on the verb as an archaic 2ms ending); Qere divides it as יִדַּעְתָּ הַשַּׁחַר ("made the dawn know"), using a rare Piel perfect of יָדַע ("cause to know").
- **38:30** — יִתְחַבָּאוּ is a Hithpael of חָבָא ("hide themselves"), depicting water concealed under a stone-hard crust of ice (though some lexicons posit a homonym meaning "harden"), parallel to the reciprocal Hithpael יִתְלַכָּדוּ ("lock together").
- **38:36** — Both טֻחוֹת ("inner depths / inward parts", cf. Ps 51:6 [MT 51:8]) and the hapax legomenon שֶׂכְוִי ("mind", from שׂכה "perceive") are rare and debated; some interpret them instead as meteorological or animal terms ("cloud layers" and "rooster").
