## Text

**10:1** "My soul loathes my life; I will let loose my complaint within me; I will speak in the bitterness of my soul.

**10:2** I will say to God, 'Do not pronounce me guilty; make known to me over what You contend with me.

**10:3** Is it good to You that You oppress, that You reject the toil of Your hands, while You shine forth upon the counsel of the wicked?

**10:4** Do You have eyes of flesh, or do You see as a mortal sees?

**10:5** Are Your days like the days of a mortal, or Your years like the days of a man,

**10:6** that You search for my iniquity and inquire after my sin,

**10:7** even though You know that I am not wicked, and there is no one who rescues from Your hand?

**10:8** Your hands shaped me and made me, yet You swallow me up altogether on every side.

**10:9** Remember, please, that You made me like clay; and will You bring me back to dust?

**10:10** Did You not pour me out as milk and congeal me like cheese?

**10:11** With skin and flesh You clothed me, and with bones and sinews You wove me together.

**10:12** Life and loyal love You have shown to me, and Your watchful care has guarded my spirit.

**10:13** Yet these things You hid away in Your heart; I know that this was with You:

**10:14** If I sin, You keep watch over me, and You will not clear me of my iniquity.

**10:15** If I am wicked, woe to me; and even if I am righteous, I cannot lift my head, filled with disgrace and gazing on my affliction.

**10:16** And if it rises up, like a lion You hunt me, and again You show Yourself wondrous against me.

**10:17** You renew Your witnesses against me and multiply Your indignation toward me; relays and an army are against me.

**10:18** And why did You bring me out from the womb? I should have breathed my last, and no eye would have seen me.

**10:19** I would have been as though I had never existed; from the womb to the grave I would have been carried.

**10:20** Are not my days few? Cease, then, and turn away from me, that I may brighten up a little,

**10:21** before I depart and do not return, to the land of darkness and the shadow of death,

**10:22** a land of gloom like pitch darkness, of the shadow of death with no order, where it shines like pitch darkness.'"

## Translator Notes

- **10:7** — The Hebrew prepositional phrase al-da'tekha (lit., upon Your knowing) functions concessively here, meaning even though You know.
- **10:15** — The form ure'eh can be parsed either as a construct verbal adjective (gazing on, parallel to the construct adjective seva, filled with) or as a Qal imperative (and look upon my affliction).
- **10:20** — Translated from the Masoretic Qere (read forms) wachadal (cease) and weshit (and turn away), both Qal imperatives; the Ketiv (written forms) read third-person imperfects yechdal and yashit (let him cease ... let him turn away).
- **10:22** — The feminine Hiphil verb wattopa (shine forth, echoing v. 3) has land as its subject, expressing the paradox that its very shining is pitch darkness. The noun tsalmaweth is vocalized by the Masoretes as shadow of death, though many linguists derive it from a root meaning deep darkness.
