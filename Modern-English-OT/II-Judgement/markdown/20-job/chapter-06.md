## Text

**6:1** And Job answered and said,

**6:2** "If only my vexation were truly weighed, and my calamity were lifted together in the scales!

**6:3** For then it would weigh more than the sand of the seas; for that reason my words have been reckless.

**6:4** For the Almighty's arrows are within me, whose venom my spirit is drinking; the terrors of God draw up against me.

**6:5** Does a wild donkey bray over fresh grass, or does an ox bellow over its fodder?

**6:6** Can something bland be eaten without salt, or is there any flavor in the juice of purslane?

**6:7** My soul has refused to touch them; they are like sickening food to me.

**6:8** If only my petition would come about, and God would grant my hope,

**6:9** that God would consent and crush me, that He would release His hand and cut me off!

**6:10** Then this would still be my comfort, and I would exult in anguish though He does not spare, for I have not concealed the words of the Holy One.

**6:11** What strength do I have, that I should wait? And what is my end, that I should prolong my life?

**6:12** Is the strength of stones my strength? Or is my flesh made of bronze?

**6:13** Have I truly no help within myself, and has resourcefulness been driven away from me?

**6:14** To the despairing one belongs loyal love from his friend, even if he forsakes the fear of the Almighty.

**6:15** My brothers have acted treacherously like a wadi, like the channel of wadis that pass away,

**6:16** which are murky because of ice, upon which snow conceals itself.

**6:17** At the time they are scorched, they are cut off; when it grows hot, they are extinguished from their place.

**6:18** The paths of their course twist aside; they go up into the wasteland and perish.

**6:19** The caravans of Tema looked out; the traveling companies of Sheba waited in hope for them.

**6:20** They were put to shame because they had been confident; they came there and were humiliated.

**6:21** For now you have become nothing; you see terror and grow afraid.

**6:22** Have I indeed said, 'Give to me, and out of your resources pay a bribe on my behalf,

**6:23** and rescue me from the hand of a foe, and from the hand of ruthless men ransom me'?

**6:24** Instruct me, and I will keep silent; and make me understand where I have erred.

**6:25** How painful are words of uprightness! Yet what does reproving from you prove?

**6:26** Do you plan to reprove words, when the utterances of a despairing man are for the wind?

**6:27** Indeed, you would cast lots over an orphan, and haggle over your friend.

**6:28** And now, consent to look at me, for to your face I will surely not lie.

**6:29** Turn back now; let there be no injustice! And turn back again; my righteousness is in it.

**6:30** Is injustice on my tongue? Or does my palate not discern calamities?"

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — The qere reads wehawwati ("and my calamity," from hawwah), whereas the ketiv reads wehayyati, a morphological by-form from hayyah with the same meaning.
- **6:6** — The Hebrew hapax legomenon challamut (lit. "slime of challamut") is uncertain; modern lexicons generally identify it as a bland mucilaginous plant such as purslane or mallow, while the Targum and rabbinic tradition render it "the white of an egg."
- **6:14** — Following the Masoretic Text lammas ("to the despairing/melting one," from mss), with the second clause taken concessively ("even if he forsakes"); the Vulgate and Peshitta read a form of m's ("one who withholds kindness from his friend forsakes the fear of the Almighty").
- **6:21** — The critical text reads lo' ("not," here "nothing," a substantivized negative); a qere tradition found in some manuscripts reads lo ("to it" or "like it," referring to the dry wadi).
- **6:29** — The qere reads weshuvu ("and turn back," Qal masculine plural imperative of shuv), whereas the ketiv reads weshuvi (either "and my turning/reply" or a feminine singular imperative).
