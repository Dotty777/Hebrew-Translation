## Text

**4:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite responded and said,

**4:2** "If one attempts a word with you, will you grow weary? Yet who is able to hold back words?

**4:3** Look, you instructed many, and you strengthened drooping hands.

**4:4** Your words raised up the one who was stumbling, and you fortified buckling knees.

**4:5** Yet now it comes to you, and you grow weary; it reaches even to you, and you are alarmed.

**4:6** Is not your fear your confidence, and your hope the blamelessness of your ways?

**4:7** Remember now, who has perished while innocent, and where have the upright been wiped out?

**4:8** Just as I have seen, those who plow iniquity and those who sow trouble reap it.

**4:9** At the breath of God they perish, and at the blast of His nostrils they come to an end.

**4:10** The roar of the lion and the sound of the fierce lion—and the teeth of young lions are broken out.

**4:11** The mighty lion perishes from lack of prey, and the cubs of the lioness disperse.

**4:12** Now a word was conveyed to me in stealth, and my ear caught a whisper of it.

**4:13** In troubling thoughts from night visions, when deep sleep falls upon people,

**4:14** dread befell me, and trembling, and it filled all my bones with dread.

**4:15** Then a spirit swept across my face; the hair of my flesh bristled.

**4:16** It stopped, yet I could not recognize its appearance; a form was in front of my eyes; there was stillness, and I heard a voice:

**4:17** 'Can a mortal be more righteous than God? Or can a man be purer than his Maker?

**4:18** See, He does not place trust in His servants, and He charges His angels with folly;

**4:19** how much more the inhabitants of clay houses, whose foundation is in the dust, who are crushed before a moth!

**4:20** From morning to evening they are shattered; with no one paying heed, they perish forever.

**4:21** Is not their tent cord pulled up within them? They die without wisdom.'"

## Translator Notes

- **4:6** — Hebrew yir'atekha is literally "your fear," used absolutely for reverence toward God. The second clause is chiastic with a poetic postponed conjunction ("your hope, and the blamelessness of your ways").
- **4:10** — The opening phrases lack a separate verb in Hebrew, governed by poetic ellipsis or zeugma with the final verb nitta'u, a hapax Niphal perfect of nata' (a by-form of natats, "break out" or "knock out").
- **4:17** — The preposition min on "God" and "his Maker" is rendered in its standard comparative sense ("more righteous than ... purer than"), though it can also be understood relationally ("righteous before ... pure before").
- **4:19** — The phrase lifney-'ash is literally "before a moth," an idiom expressing extreme fragility, as easily crushed as or sooner than a moth.
- **4:21** — Hebrew yitram ("their cord") refers here to a tent cord, matching the Niphal verb nissa' ("pulled up," used of striking camp); some render the homograph "their wealth" or "their remainder."
