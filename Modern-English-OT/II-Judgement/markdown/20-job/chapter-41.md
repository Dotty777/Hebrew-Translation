## Text

**41:1** "Look, hope of him proves false; is one not thrown down even at the sight of him?

**41:2** No one is fierce enough to rouse him; and who is he who can take his stand before Me?

**41:3** Who has given to Me beforehand, so that I should repay? Everything under all the heavens belongs to Me.

**41:4** Concerning him, shall I keep silent about his limbs, and the account of mighty strength, and the grace of his proportion?

**41:5** Who has uncovered the surface of his garment? Who can enter within his double bridle?

**41:6** Who has opened the doors of his face? Surrounding his teeth is terror.

**41:7** His pride is rows of shields, closed up as with a tight seal.

**41:8** One draws close against another, and no air can enter between them.

**41:9** Each is bonded fast to the next; they interlock and cannot come apart.

**41:10** His snorts make light flash, and his eyes are like the eyelids of the dawn.

**41:11** From his mouth torches go forth; sparks of fire shoot out.

**41:12** From his nostrils smoke comes out, like a fanned cauldron and rushes.

**41:13** His breath sets coals ablaze, and a flame comes out from his mouth.

**41:14** In his neck strength resides, and before him dismay leaps.

**41:15** The folds of his flesh adhere together; cast solid upon him, it cannot be shaken.

**41:16** His heart is cast solid like a stone, and cast solid like a lower millstone.

**41:17** At his rising, the mighty are terrified; at the crashings, they lose their bearings.

**41:18** If a sword reaches him, it cannot hold—neither spear, nor dart, nor lance.

**41:19** He considers iron as straw, and bronze as decayed wood.

**41:20** No arrow can put him to flight; for him, stones of a sling are turned into stubble.

**41:21** Clubs are considered like stubble, and he laughs at the whirring of a javelin.

**41:22** Beneath him are sharp potsherds; he spreads a threshing sledge over the mud.

**41:23** He causes the depths to boil like a pot; the sea he makes like an ointment pot.

**41:24** Behind him he lights up a path; one would consider the deep to have white hair.

**41:25** Upon the earth he has no equal, one made without dread.

**41:26** He looks upon everything lofty; he is king over all the children of pride."

## Translator Notes

- **41:4** — Qere reads lo ("concerning him," translated here as an unmarked rhetorical question introducing the description in vv. 5-26); Ketiv reads lo' ("not": "I will not keep silent about his limbs").
- **41:5** — Masoretic Hebrew literally reads "within his double bridle" (be-khefel risno), a poetic figure for Leviathan's jaws; the Septuagint reads "breastplate/armor."
- **41:7** — Masoretic Hebrew reads ga'avah ("pride") with afiqey maginnim ("rows/channels of shields"); some ancient versions presuppose "his back."
- **41:17** — Hebrew elim can mean "the mighty" or "gods/divine beings." The Hithpael verb yitchatta'u (from chata', normally "purify oneself") is taken here in its base root sense of "miss the way / lose one's bearings" amid the crashings (shevarim, likely breaking waves).
- **41:20** — Hebrew literally reads "son of a bow" (ben-qashet), a poetic idiom for an arrow.
