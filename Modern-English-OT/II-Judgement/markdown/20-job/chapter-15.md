## Text

**15:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite replied and said:

**15:2** "Should a wise man reply with knowledge of wind and fill his belly with the east wind,

**15:3** arguing with speech that is of no use, and with words by which he brings no benefit?

**15:4** In fact, you annul reverence and diminish meditation before God.

**15:5** For your iniquity instructs your mouth, and you choose the tongue of the shrewd.

**15:6** Your mouth declares you guilty, and not I; and your lips bear witness against you.

**15:7** Were you the first man born, and before the hills were you brought forth?

**15:8** Do you listen in the inner council of God, and restrict wisdom to yourself?

**15:9** What do you know that we do not know, or understand that is not with us?

**15:10** Both the gray-haired and the elderly are among us, greater in days than your father.

**15:11** Are the comforts of God too slight for you, and a gentle word with you?

**15:12** Why does your heart sweep you away, and why are your eyes flashing,

**15:13** that you turn your spirit against God and send forth words from your mouth?

**15:14** What is mortal man, that he could be pure, or that one born of a woman could be righteous?

**15:15** See, He places no trust in His holy ones, and the heavens are not pure in His eyes;

**15:16** how much less one who is abhorrent and tainted, a man who drinks unrighteousness like water!

**15:17** I will tell you; listen to me, and what I have observed I will recount,

**15:18** what wise men declare, and have not concealed, from their fathers.

**15:19** To them alone the land was given, and no foreigner passed through their midst:

**15:20** All the days of a wicked man he writhes in torment, and numbered years are reserved for the ruthless.

**15:21** The sound of terrors is in his ears; amid peace a plunderer comes upon him.

**15:22** He has no trust that he will return from darkness, and he is spied out for the sword.

**15:23** He roams about for bread, 'Where is it?' He knows that a day of darkness is prepared at his hand.

**15:24** Distress and anguish strike him with terror; they overpower him like a king prepared for the onslaught,

**15:25** because he extended his hand against God and acts defiantly against the Almighty;

**15:26** he charges against Him with outstretched neck, with the thick bosses of his shields,

**15:27** since he covered his face with his fat and formed thick fat on his flanks.

**15:28** And he has settled in ruined cities, houses that no one inhabits, which are set for heaps of rubble.

**15:29** He will not grow rich, and his wealth will not stand, nor will their possessions extend over the earth.

**15:30** He will not depart from darkness; a flame will dry up his young shoot, and he will pass away by the breath of His mouth.

**15:31** Let him not, deluded, trust in futility, for futility will be his recompense.

**15:32** Before his day it will be fulfilled, and his palm frond will not be green.

**15:33** Like the vine he will strip off his unripe grapes, and like the olive tree he will shed his blossoms.

**15:34** For the assembly of the godless is sterile, and fire devours the tents of bribery.

**15:35** They conceive trouble and bear iniquity, and their belly prepares deceit."

## Translator Notes

- **15:12** — The verb yirzemun ("are flashing") is a hapax legomenon, likely related by metathesis to Aramaic rmz ("wink, blink, signal with the eyes") and denoting defiant or agitated flashing of the eyes.
- **15:22** — The Ketiv reads the defective form wspw, while the Qere reads the Qal passive participle wesapuy (from sph, "watch, spy out"), translated here as "spied out" (that is, watched and marked out for the sword).
- **15:24** — The noun kidor ("onslaught") is a hapax legomenon of uncertain etymology, commonly understood from context and Semitic cognates as "battle," "attack," or "onslaught."
- **15:29** — The hapax legomenon minlam ("their possessions") is generally derived from a root meaning "acquire" or "attain," with a third-person plural suffix shifting collectively to the wicked (as in 15:35).
- **15:30** — The suffix on "His mouth" (piw) is generally understood to refer to God's mouth (cf. 4:9), though some take it as the wicked man's own speech.
