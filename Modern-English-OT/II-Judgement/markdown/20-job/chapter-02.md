## Text

**2:1** Now the day arrived when the sons of God came to take their stand before the LORD, and Satan came in their midst as well to take his stand before the LORD.

**2:2** And the LORD said to Satan, "From where are you coming?" Satan answered the LORD and said, "From ranging across the earth and from walking back and forth on it."

**2:3** Then the LORD said to Satan, "Have you turned your thoughts to My servant Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, fearing God and turning aside from evil. He is still holding firmly to his integrity, even though you incited Me against him to swallow him up for nothing."

**2:4** Satan answered the LORD and said, "Skin for skin! Everything that belongs to a man he will give in exchange for his life.

**2:5** However, reach out Your hand now and touch his bone and his flesh—he will surely curse You to Your face."

**2:6** And the LORD said to Satan, "Look, he is in your hand; only preserve his life."

**2:7** Then Satan went out from before the LORD and struck Job with severe boils from the sole of his foot even to the crown of his head.

**2:8** So he took himself a pottery shard to scrape himself with it, while he sat in the midst of the ashes.

**2:9** Then his wife said to him, "Are you still holding firmly to your integrity? Curse God and die!"

**2:10** But he said to her, "You are speaking just as one of the foolish women speaks. Should we accept what is good from God, and not accept what is bad?" Through all of this Job did not sin with his lips.

**2:11** Now Job's three friends heard about all this calamity that had come upon him, and they came, each from his own place—Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite—and they arranged together to come to grieve with him and to comfort him.

**2:12** And they lifted their eyes from a distance and did not recognize him; so they lifted their voices and wept, each one tore his robe, and they tossed dust toward the heavens upon their heads.

**2:13** So they sat with him on the ground for seven days and seven nights, with no one speaking a word to him, because they saw that the pain was exceedingly great.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — Hebrew levalle'o (Piel infinitive construct of bala') literally means "to swallow him up," an idiom for utter ruin; chinnam ("for nothing") directly echoes Satan's question in 1:9.
- **2:7** — Ketiv/Qere: the written ketiv reads 'ad ("to"), while the read qere adds the conjunction we'ad ("and to" / "even to").
- **2:8** — The Hithpael infinitive construct lehithgared ("to scrape himself") is the only occurrence of the root garad in Biblical Hebrew.
