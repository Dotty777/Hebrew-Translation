## Text

**17:1** "My spirit is ruined, my days are snuffed out; tombs await me.

**17:2** Surely mockers are beside me, and my eye lodges on their provocation.

**17:3** Set down a pledge, please; stand surety for me with Yourself! Who is there who will strike hands with mine?

**17:4** For You have hidden their heart from insight; therefore You will not exalt them.

**17:5** One who denounces friends for a share—the eyes of his children will fail.

**17:6** He has set me as a byword to the peoples, and I have become one in whose face people spit.

**17:7** My eye has dimmed from grief, and all my limbs are like a shadow.

**17:8** The upright are horrified at this, and the innocent rouses himself against the godless.

**17:9** Yet the righteous holds fast to his way, and the one with clean hands increases in strength.

**17:10** However, turn back and come, all of you, please; I will not find a wise man among you.

**17:11** My days have gone by; my plans are torn apart, the desires of my heart.

**17:12** They make night into day; light is near in the face of darkness.

**17:13** If I hope for Sheol to be my house, and spread out my couch in the darkness,

**17:14** if I call out to the pit, 'You are my father,' and to the maggot, 'My mother and my sister,'

**17:15** where then is my hope? As for my hope, who will see it?

**17:16** Will it go down to the bars of Sheol, when there is rest together in the dust?"

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — The opening combines two Qal imperatives, literally "put down, please" (simah-na) and "stand surety for me" (arveni), with an implied object ("a pledge"). The Niphal verb yittaqa ("strike hands," lit. "be struck into my hand") refers to the legal gesture of clapping hands to seal a guarantee (cf. Prov 6:1).
- **17:5** — This terse proverb uses yaggid (Hiphil of ngd, "denounce, inform against," cf. Jer 20:10) with leheleq, meaning "for a share" of spoil or reward, though some derive heleq from the homonym meaning "smoothness, flattery."
- **17:6** — Literally "and a spitting to faces (tophet lephanim) I have become," taking the hapax noun tophet as "spitting" (cognate with Aramaic tuph, "to spit"), meaning one spat in the face.
- **17:16** — The feminine plural verb teradnah ("will go down") either serves as an archaic singular form with feminine tiqvati ("my hope," v. 15) or refers to Job's hopes collectively. Masoretic nahat is the pausal noun ("rest" or "descent"), rather than a revocalized 1cp imperfect nehat ("we shall descend").
