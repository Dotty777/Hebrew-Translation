## Text

**9:1** And Job answered and said:

**9:2** "Indeed, I know that it is so, but how can a mortal be righteous before God?

**9:3** If one desires to contend with Him, he cannot answer Him one out of a thousand.

**9:4** He is wise of heart and mighty in strength; who has hardened himself against Him and remained unscathed?

**9:5** He is the one who moves mountains, and they do not know that He has overturned them in His anger;

**9:6** who shakes the earth from its place, and its pillars shudder;

**9:7** who speaks to the sun, and it does not shine forth, and seals off the stars;

**9:8** who stretches out the heavens by Himself and treads upon the heights of the sea;

**9:9** who makes the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades, and the inner chambers of the south;

**9:10** who does great things past searching out, and wonders without number.

**9:11** Look, He passes by me, and I do not see; He glides on, and I do not discern Him.

**9:12** Look, He seizes; who can turn Him back? Who would say to Him, 'What are You doing?'

**9:13** God does not turn back His anger; the helpers of Rahab stooped low beneath Him.

**9:14** How much less can I answer Him, or select my words with Him?

**9:15** Even if I am righteous, I cannot answer; I must plead for favor with my judge.

**9:16** If I called out and He answered me, I would not believe that He was giving ear to my voice,

**9:17** for He crushes me with a storm and multiplies my wounds for no reason.

**9:18** He does not let me recover my breath, but He satiates me with bitterness.

**9:19** If it is a matter of strength, look, He is mighty; and if it is a matter of justice, who will summon me?

**9:20** Even if I am righteous, my own mouth would declare me guilty; I am blameless, yet it would declare me crooked.

**9:21** I am blameless; I have no regard for myself; I despise my life.

**9:22** It is all the same; therefore I say, 'The blameless and the wicked He destroys.'

**9:23** If a scourge brings death suddenly, He scoffs at the despair of the innocent.

**9:24** The earth has been given into the hand of the wicked; He veils the faces of its judges—if not He, then who is it?

**9:25** And my days are faster than a runner; they have fled, they have seen no good.

**9:26** They glide past like boats of reed, like an eagle swooping down upon prey.

**9:27** If I say, 'I will forget my complaint, I will lay aside my gloomy look and brighten up,'

**9:28** I dread all my pains; I know that You will not hold me guiltless.

**9:29** I will be found guilty; why then should I toil in vain?

**9:30** If I washed myself with snow water and cleansed my hands with lye,

**9:31** then You would immerse me in the pit, and my garments would detest me.

**9:32** For He is not a man like me, that I could answer Him, that we could come together into judgment.

**9:33** There is no arbiter between us who could place his hand upon the two of us.

**9:34** May He remove His rod from me, and may His dread not terrify me.

**9:35** Then I would speak, and I would not fear Him, for I am not so with myself."

## Translator Notes

- **9:9** — The Hebrew star names ash, kesil, and kimah are traditionally identified as the Bear (Ursa Major), Orion, and the Pleiades, while "the inner chambers of the south" refers to the constellations of the southern sky.
- **9:15** — The unique Poel participle meshopeti ("my judge") denotes one acting as judge or legal opponent against Job; "plead for favor" (Hithpael of hanan) echoes Bildad's exact verb in 8:5.
- **9:19** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads "summon me" (Hiphil of ya'ad with first-person singular suffix), either asking who can summon Job to a fair hearing or echoing God's unspoken challenge ("Who can summon Me?"); ancient versions such as the Septuagint read a third-person suffix ("summon Him").
- **9:30** — Following the qere (read text) "with snow water" (beme-sheleg); the ketiv (written text) uses the poetic preposition bemo, reading "with snow" (bemo-sheleg).
- **9:35** — The terse Hebrew clause literally reads "for not so am I with myself," meaning either that Job's own conscience does not hold him guilty, or that as matters currently stand with him he cannot speak free of dread.
