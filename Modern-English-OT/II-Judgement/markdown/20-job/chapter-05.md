## Text

**5:1** "Call now—is there anyone who answers you? And to whom among the holy ones will you turn?

**5:2** For resentment kills a fool, and envy puts a naive person to death.

**5:3** I myself saw a fool striking root, and at once I cursed his dwelling.

**5:4** His sons remain far from safety; they are crushed at the gate, and there is no rescuer.

**5:5** The hungry one eats his harvest and takes it even from among the thorns, and a snare pants after their wealth.

**5:6** For iniquity does not come out from the dust, and trouble does not sprout from the soil;

**5:7** rather, mankind is born for trouble, just as sparks soar high.

**5:8** Yet as for me, I would inquire of God, and I would lay my case before God,

**5:9** who does great things past searching out, wonders beyond number.

**5:10** He gives rain upon the face of the earth and sends waters upon the face of the open fields,

**5:11** to set the lowly in a high place, and mourners are raised high to safety.

**5:12** He thwarts the schemes of the crafty, and their hands accomplish no success.

**5:13** He traps the wise in their craftiness, and the counsel of the devious is rushed headlong.

**5:14** In daytime they encounter darkness, and as at night they grope at midday.

**5:15** So He saves the needy one from the sword out of their mouth and from the hand of the strong.

**5:16** So there is hope for the poor, and injustice closes its mouth.

**5:17** Look, blessed is the mortal whom God corrects; so do not reject the discipline of the Almighty.

**5:18** For He causes pain, yet He binds up; He wounds, yet His hands heal.

**5:19** In six distresses He will rescue you, and in seven no harm will touch you.

**5:20** In famine He will redeem you from death, and in battle from the hands of the sword.

**5:21** From the whip of the tongue you will be hidden, and you will not fear devastation when it comes.

**5:22** At devastation and hunger you will laugh, and do not fear the wild animals of the earth.

**5:23** For your covenant will be with the stones of the field, and the wild animals of the field will be at peace with you.

**5:24** And you will know that your tent is at peace, and you will examine your dwelling and find nothing missing.

**5:25** And you will know that your seed will be numerous, and your descendants like the grass of the earth.

**5:26** You will come in mature vigor to the grave, like a grain stack brought up in its season.

**5:27** See, we have searched this out; it is so. Listen to it, and know it for yourself."

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The Masoretic Text reads the first-person Qal verb wa'eqqov ("and I cursed," from naqav), expressing Eliphaz's immediate pronouncement of doom upon the fool's dwelling.
- **5:5** — The Masoretic vocalization tsammim is a masculine singular noun meaning "snare" (as in Job 18:9), governing the singular verb sha'af ("pants after"); a proposed emendation reads tseme'im ("the thirsty").
- **5:6** — Hebrew 'awen ("iniquity") repeats the term from 4:8 alongside 'amal ("trouble") and carries a dual nuance encompassing both wrongdoing and the affliction that accompanies it.
- **5:7** — Hebrew beney-reshef ("sparks") is literally "sons of flame," an idiomatic expression for rising sparks, linked to the first clause by a comparative conjunction ("just as").
- **5:24** — Hebrew lo' tekheta' uses the Qal imperfect of khata' (ordinarily "sin") in its concrete primary sense of "miss the mark" or "find something missing" when inspecting one's property.
