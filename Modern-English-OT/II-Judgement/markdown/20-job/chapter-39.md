# Job 39 — Saints Modern English (Hebrew / Masoretic Text)

## Text

**39:1** "Do you know the time the wild goats of the rock give birth? Do you watch the birthing of the does?

**39:2** Can you count the months they complete, and do you know the time of their giving birth?

**39:3** They crouch down, they deliver their young, and they cast off their birth pangs.

**39:4** Their offspring grow sturdy, they grow large in the open country; they go out and do not come back to them.

**39:5** Who sent the wild donkey out free, and who untied the tethers of the onager,

**39:6** for whom I set the arid plain as its house, and the salt land as its dwellings?

**39:7** It laughs at the uproar of the city; it does not hear the shouts of a driver.

**39:8** The range of the mountains is its pasture, and it seeks after all greenery.

**39:9** Will a wild ox be willing to serve you, or spend the night beside your feeding trough?

**39:10** Can you tie a wild ox in a furrow with its rope, or will it harrow valleys behind you?

**39:11** Will you rely on it because its strength is immense, and leave your toil to it?

**39:12** Will you be confident in it that it will bring back your grain and gather it in at your threshing floor?

**39:13** The ostrich's wing beats with joy, yet are her pinion and plumage caring?

**39:14** For she leaves her eggs to the ground and warms them upon the dust,

**39:15** and she forgets that a foot might crush them, and a beast of the field tread them down.

**39:16** She acts harshly toward her young, as though not hers; her toil is for nothing, without fear,

**39:17** for God has caused her to forget wisdom and has not allotted her a share in understanding.

**39:18** The moment she raises herself up high, she laughs at the horse and its rider.

**39:19** Do you give might to the horse? Do you clothe its neck in a mane?

**39:20** Do you cause it to leap like the locust? The majesty of its snorting is terror.

**39:21** It paws in the valley and revels in strength; it goes out to meet weaponry.

**39:22** It laughs at fear and is not terrified, and it does not turn away before a sword.

**39:23** Against it rattles a quiver, the flash of spear and javelin.

**39:24** With quaking and fury it swallows up the ground, and it will not hold still at the sound of a horn.

**39:25** Whenever the horn sounds, it says, 'Aha!' And from a distance it smells war, the thunder of commanders and the battle shout.

**39:26** Does a hawk take wing by your understanding, spreading its wings toward the south?

**39:27** Or is it at your command that an eagle rises high and sets its nest aloft?

**39:28** It dwells on a rock and lodges overnight, upon a spur of rock and a stronghold.

**39:29** From there it searches out food; its eyes gaze from afar.

**39:30** And its fledglings lap up blood, and wherever the fallen are, there it is."

## Translator Notes

- **39:8** — MT vocalizes יְתוּר as a noun ("range" or "territory", from תּוּר "explore/roam"), translated literally here; some interpreters repoint it as a verb ("ranges").
- **39:12** — Ketiv reads Qal ישוב ("will return", intransitive); Qere reads Hiphil יָשִׁיב ("will bring back", transitive with זַרְעֶךָ, "your grain"), followed in the translation.
- **39:13** — The feminine adjective חֲסִידָה ("caring / kind", modifying absolute אֶבְרָה "pinion") is also the Hebrew name for the stork, creating wordplay contrasting the caring stork with the neglectful ostrich.
- **39:19** — The hapax legomenon רַעְמָה derives from רָעַם ("quiver / thunder") and is generally understood as the horse's quivering mane.
- **39:21** — MT reads the plural verb יַחְפְּרוּ ("they paw", referring generically to horses or to the hooves) before shifting immediately to singular verbs (וְיָשִׂישׂ, יֵצֵא); smoothed to singular "It paws" for English flow.
