## Text

**35:1** Then Elihu responded and said:

**35:2** "Do you consider this to be justice, that you have said, 'My righteousness is greater than that of God'?

**35:3** For you say, 'What does it benefit you? What do I gain more than from my sin?'

**35:4** I will answer you with words, and your companions with you.

**35:5** Look toward the heavens and see, and gaze upon the clouds; they are higher than you.

**35:6** If you sin, what can you effect against Him? And if your transgressions grow many, what can you do to Him?

**35:7** If you are righteous, what can you give Him, or what will He take from your hand?

**35:8** Your wickedness affects a man like you, and your righteousness a son of man.

**35:9** From the multitude of oppressions they raise an outcry; they cry out for help from the arm of the powerful.

**35:10** Yet not one says, 'Where is God, the One who made me, who gives songs during the night,

**35:11** who instructs us more than the animals of the earth, and more than the birds of the heavens makes us wise?'

**35:12** There they cry out, yet He does not respond, because of the arrogance of evil people.

**35:13** Indeed, God will not hear an empty outcry, and the Almighty will not look upon it.

**35:14** How much less when you say you cannot look upon Him! Judgment lies before Him, so wait for Him.

**35:15** And now, because His anger has not punished, and He has not greatly taken note of insolence,

**35:16** Job opens wide his mouth in emptiness; with no knowledge he multiplies words."

## Translator Notes

- **35:2** — The preposition in me'el ("than that of God") is comparative, echoing Elihu's earlier charge that Job justified himself rather than God (32:2). Some read me'el as "before God" or "from God."
- **35:3** — The Hebrew shifts from second-person singular lach ("to you") to first-person singular 'o'il ("I gain") and mechattati ("from my sin"), moving abruptly from indirect or generic speech into Job's direct voice; some take lach as addressed to God ("to You"). The phrase mechattati is a concise comparison meaning "more than if I had sinned."
- **35:15** — Hebrew pash ("insolence") is a hapax legomenon of uncertain meaning, often linked to a root meaning "arrogance" or "excess"; many translations conjecturally emend it to pesha' ("transgression"). Also, 'ayin ("not") rarely negates a finite verb here (paqad 'appo, "His anger has punished").
