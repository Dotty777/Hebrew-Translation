## Text

**20:1** Then Zophar the Naamathite responded and said,

**20:2** "Therefore my restless thoughts bring me a reply, even on account of the urgency within me.

**20:3** I hear a correction that disgraces me, and a spirit from my understanding answers me.

**20:4** Do you know this from ancient times, since mankind was placed upon the earth,

**20:5** that the joyous shouting of the wicked is brief, and the gladness of the godless is only for an instant?

**20:6** Even if his stature ascends to the heavens, and his head reaches to the clouds,

**20:7** like his own dung he will perish forever; those who saw him will say, 'Where is he?'

**20:8** Like a dream he will fly away, and they will not find him; he will be driven away like a vision of the night.

**20:9** The eye that caught sight of him will not do so again, nor will his place look upon him any longer.

**20:10** His sons will seek to appease the poor, and his own hands will restore his wealth.

**20:11** His bones are full of his youth, yet it will lie down with him upon the dust.

**20:12** Though evil tastes sweet in his mouth, and he conceals it beneath his tongue,

**20:13** though he spares it and will not abandon it, holding it back within his palate,

**20:14** yet his food in his stomach turns into the venom of cobras inside him.

**20:15** He has swallowed wealth, yet he will vomit it up; God will drive it out from his belly.

**20:16** The poison of cobras he will suck; a viper's tongue will kill him.

**20:17** He will not gaze at the streams, the rivers, the torrents of honey and curds.

**20:18** He returns what he toiled for and will not swallow it; for all the wealth of his trading, he will not exult.

**20:19** Because he crushed and abandoned the poor, he plundered a house that he did not build.

**20:20** Because he knew no quietness in his belly, he will save nothing of what he treasures.

**20:21** Nothing is left from his devouring; therefore his prosperity will not last.

**20:22** When his plenty is full, he will be hard-pressed; the hand of every afflicted person will come upon him.

**20:23** When he is about to fill his belly, He will unleash against him His burning anger, and will rain it down upon him as his food.

**20:24** He will flee from an iron weapon, yet a bow of bronze will pierce him through.

**20:25** He pulls it out, and it emerges from his back, and the gleaming point comes from his gallbladder; terrors are upon him.

**20:26** Total darkness lies hidden for his treasured stores; an unfanned fire will consume him, and what remains in his tent will come to ruin.

**20:27** The heavens will expose his iniquity, and the earth rises against him.

**20:28** The yield of his house will be carried away, flowing off on the day of His anger.

**20:29** This is a wicked man's allotment from God, and the inheritance appointed for him from God."

## Translator Notes

- **20:10** — The verb yerassu (Piel of rasah) means "seek the favor of, appease," and 'ono here denotes "his wealth" or "his substance" (contrast 21:19).
- **20:18** — Literally, "according to the wealth of his exchange, and he will not exult," meaning he derives no joy from all the proceeds of his trading.
- **20:23** — The noun lahumo ("his food," fitting "fill his belly") can also mean "his flesh" (cf. Zeph 1:17); the poetic suffix on 'alemo here has singular reference ("upon him").
- **20:26** — The verb yera' is the Qal imperfect of ra'a' ("fare badly, come to ruin") with "what remains" as subject; some instead take it from ra'ah ("graze, consume") with "fire" as subject.
- **20:29** — Literally, "the inheritance of his word" (or "decree"), using the suffix objectively for the lot appointed for him by God.
