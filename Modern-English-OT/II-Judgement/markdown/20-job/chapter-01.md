## Text

**1:1** In the land of Uz there was a man—Job was his name—and that man was blameless and upright, fearing God and turning aside from evil.

**1:2** To him were born seven sons and three daughters.

**1:3** His livestock amounted to seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yokes of oxen, and five hundred female donkeys, along with a very large household of servants; so that man was greater than all the sons of the east.

**1:4** Now his sons would go and prepare a banquet at each one's house on his own day, and they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and to drink with them.

**1:5** When the days of the banquet had completed their cycle, Job would send and sanctify them, and he would rise early in the morning and offer up burnt offerings matching the number of them all; for Job said, "Maybe my sons have sinned and cursed God in their hearts." In this way Job would do continually.

**1:6** Now the day arrived when the sons of God came to take their stand before the LORD, and Satan came in their midst as well.

**1:7** And the LORD said to Satan, "Where are you coming from?" Satan answered the LORD and said, "From ranging across the earth and from walking back and forth on it."

**1:8** Then the LORD said to Satan, "Have you turned your thoughts to My servant Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a blameless and upright man, fearing God and turning aside from evil."

**1:9** Satan answered the LORD and said, "Is it for nothing that Job fears God?

**1:10** Have You not hedged around him and around his house and around all that belongs to him on every side? The work of his hands You have blessed, and his livestock have burst forth across the land.

**1:11** Yet reach out Your hand now and touch everything that belongs to him—he will surely curse You to Your face."

**1:12** And the LORD said to Satan, "Look, everything that belongs to him is in your hand; only against him do not reach out your hand." Then Satan went out from before the LORD.

**1:13** Now the day arrived when his sons and his daughters were eating and drinking wine at the house of their firstborn brother.

**1:14** And a messenger came to Job and said, "The oxen were plowing and the female donkeys were grazing alongside them,

**1:15** and the Sabeans fell upon them and took them away; and the young servants they struck down with the edge of the sword, and only I alone have escaped to report to you."

**1:16** While this one was still speaking, another arrived and said, "The fire of God fell from the heavens and burned among the sheep and the young servants and consumed them, and only I alone have escaped to report to you."

**1:17** While this one was still speaking, another arrived and said, "The Chaldeans formed three companies and raided the camels and took them away; and the young servants they struck down with the edge of the sword, and only I alone have escaped to report to you."

**1:18** While this one was still speaking, another arrived and said, "Your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking wine at the house of their firstborn brother,

**1:19** and look, a great wind came from across the wilderness and struck the four corners of the house, and it fell upon the young people and they died; and only I alone have escaped to report to you."

**1:20** Then Job stood up, tore his robe, and shaved his head; and he fell to the ground and bowed in worship.

**1:21** And he said, "Naked I came forth from my mother's womb, and naked I will return there. The LORD has given, and the LORD has taken; may the name of the LORD be blessed."

**1:22** Through all of this Job did not sin, nor did he ascribe wrongdoing to God.

## Translator Notes

- **1:5** — Hebrew uses the Piel of barak ("bless") as a pious euphemism for "curse" when God is the object (likewise in 1:11, 2:5, and 2:9).
- **1:6** — Hebrew ha-satan carries the definite article, designating a role or title ("the adversary" or "the accuser") in the heavenly assembly.
- **1:10** — Ketiv/Qere: the written ketiv has the defective spelling at, while the read qere attah gives the standard masculine singular pronoun "You."
- **1:22** — Literally, "did not give impropriety [or unsavoriness] to God" (Hebrew tiphlah).
