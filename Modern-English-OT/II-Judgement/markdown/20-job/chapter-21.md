## Text

**21:1** Then Job responded and said,

**21:2** "Listen intently to my speech, and let this be your consolation.

**21:3** Bear with me while I myself speak, and once I have spoken, you may mock.

**21:4** As for me, is my complaint aimed at man? Or why should my spirit not grow impatient?

**21:5** Turn to me and be astounded, and place your hand over your mouth.

**21:6** Even when I remember, I am terrified, and trembling grips my flesh.

**21:7** Why do the wicked go on living, grow old, and even become mighty in power?

**21:8** Their offspring stand established in their presence along with them, and their descendants are before their eyes.

**21:9** Their houses are at peace, free from dread, and God's rod is not upon them.

**21:10** His bull breeds and does not fail; his cow calves safely and does not miscarry.

**21:11** They send their little ones out like a flock, and their children leap about.

**21:12** They lift up their voices with tambourine and lyre, and they rejoice at the sound of the flute.

**21:13** They finish their days in prosperity, and in an instant they descend to Sheol.

**21:14** Yet they say to God, 'Turn away from us! We have no desire for the knowledge of Your ways.

**21:15** What is the Almighty, that we should serve Him? And what benefit do we gain if we appeal to Him?'

**21:16** See, their prosperity is not in their own hand; the counsel of the wicked stays far from me.

**21:17** How often does the lamp of the wicked go out, and does their disaster come upon them, and does He apportion pains in His anger?

**21:18** Are they like straw before the wind, and like chaff that a whirlwind steals away?

**21:19** God stores up his iniquity for his sons? Let Him repay him directly, so that he may know it!

**21:20** Let his own eyes see his ruin, and let him drink from the fury of the Almighty.

**21:21** For what interest does he have in his house after him, when the number of his months has been cut off?

**21:22** Will anyone teach knowledge to God, when He Himself judges those on high?

**21:23** One dies in the fullness of his vigor, completely tranquil and at ease;

**21:24** his pails are filled with milk, and the marrow of his bones is moistened.

**21:25** And another dies with a bitter soul, never having eaten of prosperity.

**21:26** Together they lie down upon the dust, and maggots cover over them.

**21:27** Look, I know your thoughts, and the plots with which you do me wrong.

**21:28** For you say, 'Where is the nobleman's house, and where is the tent of the dwellings of the wicked?'

**21:29** Have you not questioned those who pass along the road, and do you not recognize their evidence,

**21:30** that the evil man is spared on the day of disaster, and they are led away on the day of wrath?

**21:31** Who will tell him his way to his face? And for what he has done, who will repay him?

**21:32** And he is carried to the graves, and over the burial mound watch is kept.

**21:33** Sweet to him are the clods of the valley; after him all mankind follows along, and before him they are without number.

**21:34** How then can you console me with emptiness, when in your replies there remains only deceit?"

## Translator Notes

- **21:13** — Ketiv reads yblw ("they wear out/spend," Piel of balah); Qere reads yekallu ("they finish/complete," Piel of kalah). The verb yehattu is Qal imperfect of nahat ("go down, descend").
- **21:19** — The first clause echoes the friends' conventional objection as a rhetorical question, and 'ono here comes from 'awen ("iniquity" or "punishment for iniquity").
- **21:24** — The rare plural noun 'atinayw occurs only here in Biblical Hebrew and is understood as "his pails/vats" (matching the vocalized halab, "milk"), though some connect it with body parts such as "his sides" or "his flanks."
- **21:30** — With the preposition le-, the Niphal yehasek ("be held back, spared") and Hophal yubalu ("be led, carried") can mean either that the evil are spared on the day of disaster and carried safely away on the day of wrath, or that they are reserved for the day of disaster and led forth to the day of wrath.
- **21:32** — The noun gadish (elsewhere "grain stack") here denotes a "burial mound" or "tomb," and the 3ms imperfect yishqod ("keeps watch") is used impersonally ("watch is kept").
