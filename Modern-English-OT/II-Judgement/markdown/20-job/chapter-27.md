## Text

**27:1** And Job continued to take up his discourse and said:

**27:2** "As God lives, who has removed my right, and the Almighty, who has brought bitterness to my soul—

**27:3** for as long as my breath is within me, and God's spirit is in my nostrils—

**27:4** my lips will never speak wrongdoing, and my tongue will never murmur deceit.

**27:5** Far be it from me to pronounce you in the right! Until I breathe my last, I will not remove my integrity from myself.

**27:6** I hold firmly to my righteousness and will not release it; my heart does not blame me for any of my days.

**27:7** Let my enemy be like a wicked man, and the one who rises against me like a wrongdoer.

**27:8** For what hope does a godless man have, though he makes unjust gain, when God draws out his soul?

**27:9** Will God heed his outcry when distress comes over him?

**27:10** Or will he delight in the Almighty? Will he call out to God at all times?

**27:11** I will instruct you about the hand of God; I will not hide what is with the Almighty.

**27:12** Look, all of you yourselves have seen it; and why then do you act so futilely?

**27:13** This is the share of a wicked man with God, and the inheritance that ruthless men receive from the Almighty:

**27:14** Though his sons multiply, they are for the sword, and his offspring will not have their fill of bread.

**27:15** His survivors will be buried by death, and his widows will not weep.

**27:16** If he heaps up silver like the dust and prepares clothing like the clay,

**27:17** he may prepare it, yet a righteous man will wear it, and an innocent man will divide up the silver.

**27:18** He has built his house like the moth, and like a shelter a watchman has made.

**27:19** He lies down rich, yet he will not be gathered; he opens his eyes, and he is no more.

**27:20** Like the waters, terrors overtake him; by night a whirlwind steals him away.

**27:21** The east wind bears him off and he departs, and it sweeps him from his place.

**27:22** And it hurls at him and does not spare; from its hand he flees headlong.

**27:23** It claps its palms over him and whistles at him out of his place."

## Translator Notes

- **27:8** — The Masoretic Qal imperfect yivtsa' ("makes unjust gain") is taken actively with the godless man as subject, though some construe God as delayed subject ("when God cuts him off") or repoint it as a Niphal passive. The rare Qal form yeshel ("draws out") derives from nashal or shalah ("pull off, draw out").
- **27:15** — Hebrew bammawet yiqqaveru (Niphal, "will be buried by death") uses "death" poetically for deadly pestilence or personified Death serving as their sole burial.
- **27:18** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads ka'ash ("like the moth"), referring to a moth's fragile cocoon; the Septuagint and Syriac instead read "spider."
- **27:19** — The Masoretic Text reads the Niphal imperfect lo' ye'asef ("will not be gathered," meaning not gathered to his ancestors in honorable burial); the Septuagint and Syriac instead reflect a reading yosif ("will do so no more").
- **27:23** — Both 'alemo ("over him") and kappemo ("its palms") use the poetic suffix -emo alongside third-person singular verbs continuing the personified east wind from vv. 21-22, though some read the verbs impersonally of mocking onlookers.
