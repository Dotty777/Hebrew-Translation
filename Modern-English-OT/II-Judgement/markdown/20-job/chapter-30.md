## Text

**30:1** "But now those younger than I in days laugh at me, whose fathers I scorned to place with the dogs of my flock.

**30:2** Indeed, what use to me was the strength of their hands? Vigor had perished from them.

**30:3** Gaunt from want and famine, they gnaw the parched earth by night in ruin and wasteland.

**30:4** They pluck saltwort among the bushes, and the root of broom shrubs is their food.

**30:5** They are driven out from the community; people shout at them as at a thief.

**30:6** In the clefts of the ravines they must dwell, in holes of the earth and the rocks.

**30:7** Among the bushes they bray; under the nettles they are huddled together.

**30:8** Sons of a fool, yes, sons without a name, they have been scourged out of the land.

**30:9** And now I have become their song, and to them I am a byword.

**30:10** They detest me, they keep far from me, and they do not hold back from spitting in my face.

**30:11** For He has loosed my cord and afflicted me, and they have cast off the bridle before me.

**30:12** On the right the rabble rises; they push my feet aside, and they cast up against me their paths of destruction.

**30:13** They tear up my path; they advance my downfall, with no one to help them.

**30:14** As through a wide breach they come; amid the ruin they roll onward.

**30:15** Terrors are turned upon me; they chase my dignity like the wind, and like a cloud my welfare has passed away.

**30:16** And now my soul pours itself out within me; days of affliction grip me.

**30:17** Night pierces my bones from me, and my gnawing pains do not rest.

**30:18** By great force my garment is disfigured; it binds me about like the collar of my tunic.

**30:19** He has thrown me into the mud, and I have come to resemble dust and ashes.

**30:20** I cry out to You for help, and You do not answer me; I stand up, and You gaze at me.

**30:21** You turn cruel toward me; with the might of Your hand You assail me.

**30:22** You lift me up to the wind and make me ride on it, and You melt away my substance.

**30:23** For I know that You will bring me back to death, and to the house of assembly for all the living.

**30:24** Yet surely one does not stretch out a hand against a heap of ruins, when in his calamity there is a cry for help.

**30:25** Did I not weep for the one whose day was hard? Did not my soul grieve for the needy?

**30:26** Yet I hoped for good, and evil came; I waited for light, and thick darkness came.

**30:27** My inward parts boil and are not still; days of affliction confront me.

**30:28** I walk about blackened, with no sun; I rise in the assembly and cry out for help.

**30:29** I have become a brother to jackals, and a friend to ostriches.

**30:30** My skin has blackened upon me, and my bones burn with heat.

**30:31** And my lyre has turned to mourning, and my flute to the voice of those who weep."

## Translator Notes

- **30:3** — The Qal participle ha'orqim ("they gnaw") is alternatively derived from an Aramaic cognate meaning "flee" ("who flee to the parched ground"). Hebrew 'emesh literally means "last night" and is taken poetically here as "by night" or "in gloom."
- **30:11** — Ketiv/Qere: the Qere (read form) is yitri ("my cord" or "my bowstring"), whereas the Ketiv (written form) is yitro ("his cord" or "his bowstring").
- **30:17** — Night (laylah) is construed as the subject of the active Piel verb niqqar ("pierces"). The form 'orqay ("my gnawing pains") may alternatively derive from a cognate noun meaning "my sinews" or "my veins."
- **30:22** — Ketiv/Qere: the Qere (read form) is tushiyyah ("substance," "security," or "sound wisdom"), construed with Polel temogegeni as "melt away my substance." The Ketiv (written form) tshwh is commonly read as "roaring storm" or "crash."
- **30:24** — This verse is famously difficult in the Masoretic Text; shalach yad be- ("stretch out a hand against") is taken as an idiom for striking a helpless "heap of ruins" ('i). Many interpreters alternatively read the clause as a rhetorical question describing a falling person reaching out for help in disaster.
