## Text

**42:1** And Job answered the LORD and said,

**42:2** "I know that You are able to do all things, and no plan can be withheld from You.

**42:3** 'Who is this who conceals counsel without knowledge?' Therefore I declared, though I did not understand, things too marvelous for me that I did not know.

**42:4** 'Hear, please, and I will speak; I will question you, and you inform Me.'

**42:5** By the hearing of the ear I had heard of You, but now my eye has seen You.

**42:6** For this reason I recant and repent in dust and ashes."

**42:7** Now it happened, after the LORD had spoken these words to Job, that the LORD said to Eliphaz the Temanite, "My anger burns hot against you and against your two friends, because you have not spoken what is right concerning Me, as My servant Job has.

**42:8** And now, take for yourselves seven bulls and seven rams, and go to My servant Job, and offer up a burnt offering on your behalf; and Job, My servant, will pray for you, for I will surely receive him favorably, so as not to deal with you as your folly deserves, because you have not spoken what is right concerning Me, as My servant Job has."

**42:9** Then Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite went and did just as the LORD had spoken to them, and the LORD received Job favorably.

**42:10** And the LORD restored Job's fortunes as he prayed on behalf of his friends, and the LORD increased everything that belonged to Job to twice as much.

**42:11** Then all his brothers and all his sisters and all who had known him previously came to him, and they ate bread with him in his house. They sympathized with him and comforted him over all the calamity that the LORD had brought on him, and each of them gave him one piece of money, and each one a gold ring.

**42:12** And the LORD blessed Job's latter days more than his beginning, and he came to have fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, one thousand pairs of oxen, and one thousand female donkeys.

**42:13** And he had seven sons and three daughters.

**42:14** He called the name of the first Jemimah, the name of the second Keziah, and the name of the third Keren-happuch.

**42:15** No women as beautiful as the daughters of Job were found in the entire land, and their father gave an inheritance to them among their brothers.

**42:16** And Job lived one hundred and forty years after this, and he saw his sons and his grandsons, four generations.

**42:17** Then Job died, aged and full of days.

## Translator Notes

- **42:2** — Ketiv reads yd't, an archaic or defective first-person spelling without final yod or second-person "You know"; the translated Qere reads yada'ti (Qal perfect 1cs, "I know").
- **42:4** — Job here quotes the LORD's exact challenge from 38:3 and 40:7 ("I will question you, and inform Me"), paralleling his citation of 38:2 in 42:3; some instead read the verse in Job's own voice addressing God ("I will ask You, and inform me").
- **42:6** — The Qal imperfect verb em'as ("reject, despise") has no explicit object in Hebrew and is understood either as withdrawing Job's words ("I recant") or reflexively ("I despise myself"). Niphal nihamti ("repent, relent") can also mean "be comforted," leading some to translate "and I am comforted concerning dust and ashes."
- **42:8** — Hebrew "lift up his face" (repeated in 42:9) is an idiom for receiving someone favorably and granting their intercession. Literally "not to do folly with you," the following phrase means either not to treat the friends as their folly deserves or not to inflict disgrace on them.
- **42:10** — Ketiv reads shevit ("captivity"), while the translated Qere reads shevut ("fortunes/restoration"), a standard Hebrew idiom with shuv for reversing an afflicted state. The noun re'ehu ("his friend") is a collective singular referring to Job's friends.
