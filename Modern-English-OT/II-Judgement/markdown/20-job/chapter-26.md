## Text

**26:1** And Job answered and said:

**26:2** "How you have helped one without strength! You have rescued the arm without might!

**26:3** How you have advised one without wisdom, and made sound insight known in abundance!

**26:4** To whom have you declared words, and whose breath went out from you?

**26:5** The shades writhe beneath the waters and those who dwell in them.

**26:6** Sheol is bare before Him, and there is no covering for Abaddon.

**26:7** He stretches the north over emptiness; He suspends the earth upon nothing.

**26:8** He bundles up the waters in His thick clouds, and the cloud is not split beneath them.

**26:9** He shrouds the face of the throne; He spreads His cloud across it.

**26:10** He has traced a circular boundary over the face of the waters, to the limit of light with darkness.

**26:11** The pillars of the heavens are shaken, and they are stunned at His rebuke.

**26:12** By His strength He stirred up the sea, and by His understanding He crushed Rahab.

**26:13** By His breath the heavens became bright; His hand pierced through the fleeing serpent.

**26:14** Look, these are the edges of His ways, and what a whisper of a word we hear of Him! And the thunder of His mighty deeds, who can comprehend?"

## Translator Notes

- **26:4** — Hebrew 'et-mi can be read either as "To whom" (marking the recipient of the Hiphil verb higgadta, "you have declared") or as the comitative preposition "With whom," meaning with whose assistance, paralleling "whose breath" in the second half.
- **26:5** — Hebrew harepha'im ("the shades") refers to the spirits of the dead in the underworld, and yecholalu ("writhe") is the Polal imperfect of chul/chil ("writhe, tremble in anguish").
- **26:9** — Masoretic kisseh ("throne") is an alternate spelling of kisse' (as in 1 Kings 10:19); the consonants could also be pointed kese' ("full moon"). The verb parshez ("spreads") is a unique quadriliteral form related to paras ("spread out").
- **26:12** — The translation follows the Qere uvitevunato ("and by His understanding") over the transposed Ketiv spelling. The Qal verb raga' ("stirred up") matches the exact phrase "stirs up the sea" in Isaiah 51:15 and Jeremiah 31:35, though it can also be derived from a homonymous root meaning "be still" ("He stilled the sea").
- **26:14** — The verb nishma' ("we hear") is morphologically ambiguous and can be parsed as either the Qal imperfect first-person plural ("we hear") or the Niphal perfect third-person singular ("is heard").
