## Text

**40:1** Then the LORD answered Job and said,

**40:2** "Will a reprover dispute with the Almighty? Let the one who argues with God answer it."

**40:3** Then Job answered the LORD and said,

**40:4** "Look, I am small; what can I reply to You? I have put my hand over my mouth.

**40:5** Once I have spoken, and I will not answer; and twice, and I will not continue."

**40:6** Then the LORD answered Job from a storm and said,

**40:7** "Now gird your waist like a man; I will question you, and you inform Me.

**40:8** Will you indeed invalidate My justice? Will you declare Me guilty so that you may be in the right?

**40:9** Or do you possess an arm like God, and with a voice like His can you thunder?

**40:10** Adorn yourself now with grandeur and loftiness, and clothe yourself in splendor and majesty.

**40:11** Pour forth the outbursts of your anger; look upon every proud one and bring him low.

**40:12** Look upon every proud one, subdue him, and crush the wicked where they stand.

**40:13** Hide them together in the dust; bind up their faces in the hidden place.

**40:14** Then even I will praise you, that your right hand can deliver you.

**40:15** Look now at Behemoth, whom I made alongside you; he eats grass like cattle.

**40:16** Look now: his strength is in his loins, and his vigor is in the muscles of his belly.

**40:17** He stiffens his tail like a cedar; the sinews of his thighs are intertwined.

**40:18** His bones are pipes of bronze; his limbs are like an iron bar.

**40:19** He is the foremost of the ways of God; let the One who made him bring near His sword.

**40:20** For the mountains bear produce for him, and all the wild animals of the field play there.

**40:21** Beneath the lotus trees he lies down, in the hiding place of reeds and marsh.

**40:22** The lotus trees screen him as his shade; the willows of the stream encircle him.

**40:23** If a river surges, he does not panic; he stays confident though the Jordan bursts against his mouth.

**40:24** Can anyone capture him before his eyes, or pierce his nose with snares?

**40:25** Can you haul out Leviathan with a fishhook, or force down his tongue with a rope?

**40:26** Can you place a reed-cord in his nose, or pierce his jaw with a barb?

**40:27** Will he multiply pleas for mercy to you, or will he speak gentle words to you?

**40:28** Will he cut a covenant with you? Will you take him as a slave forever?

**40:29** Will you play with him like a bird, or tie him up for your girls?

**40:30** Will partners barter over him? Will they portion him out among merchants?

**40:31** Can you fill his hide with barbed spears, or his head with a fishing harpoon?

**40:32** Place your hand upon him; remember the battle—do not do it again!"

## Translator Notes

- **40:2** — Hebrew harov combines the interrogative particle with the Qal infinitive absolute of riv ("dispute/contend") functioning as a finite verb, paired with the rare noun yissor ("reprover/faultfinder," from yasar).
- **40:6** — Ketiv writes mns'rh as one word without a space; Qere divides it into two words, min se'arah ("from a storm"), followed here.
- **40:17** — The Qal verb yahpots (from a rare root chaphats II, distinct from "delight in") is lexically uncertain, meaning either "stiffens" or "bends."
- **40:19** — The second clause (literally "the One who made him brings near His sword") may mean either that only God can approach Behemoth with His sword, or that God supplied Behemoth with his own sword-like weapon.
