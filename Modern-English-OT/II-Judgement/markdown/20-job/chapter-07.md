## Text

**7:1** "Is there not hard service for mortal man upon the earth, and are his days not like the days of a hired worker?

**7:2** Like a slave panting for the shade, and like a hired worker waiting for his wages,

**7:3** so I have been made to inherit months of futility, and nights of misery have been assigned to me.

**7:4** When I lie down, I say, 'When will I rise?' Yet the evening stretches out, and I have my fill of tossing until dawn.

**7:5** My flesh is clothed with maggots and a crust of dust; my skin hardens and oozes.

**7:6** My days are faster than a weaver's shuttle, and they end without hope.

**7:7** Remember that my life is wind; my eye will not return to see good.

**7:8** The eye of the one who sees me will not look on me; Your eyes will be on me, and I will be no more.

**7:9** A cloud fades and goes away; so the one who descends to Sheol does not come up.

**7:10** He does not return to his house again, nor does his place recognize him anymore.

**7:11** I too will not hold back my mouth; I will speak out of the distress of my spirit; I will complain out of the bitterness of my soul.

**7:12** Am I a sea, or a sea monster, that You place a watch over me?

**7:13** When I say, 'My couch will comfort me; my bed will lighten my complaint,'

**7:14** then You dismay me with dreams, and through visions You terrify me,

**7:15** so that my soul chooses strangling, death rather than my bones.

**7:16** I despise it; I will not live forever. Leave me alone, for my days are a vapor.

**7:17** What is mortal man, that You magnify him, and that You place Your heart upon him,

**7:18** and You inspect him morning by morning, and moment by moment You test him?

**7:19** For how long will You not look away from me, nor release me until I swallow my saliva?

**7:20** If I have sinned, what do I do to You, Watcher of mankind? Why have You made me a target for Yourself, so that I have become a burden to myself?

**7:21** And why do You not forgive my transgression and make my iniquity pass away? For soon I will lie down in the dust, and You will search diligently for me, but I will be no more."

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The qere reads the poetic preposition aley ("upon") for the ketiv al (same meaning); tsava ("hard service") denotes conscripted military or forced labor.
- **7:4** — The phrase umiddad-erev takes middad as a Piel perfect of mdd ("the evening stretches out"); some connect the form to ndd ("flee") as continuing the question, "and when will the evening pass away?"
- **7:5** — The Qere reads wegush ("and a clod/crust," a biblical hapax legomenon, singular) for the Ketiv wegish (same meaning); raga means either "hardens/crusts over" or "cracks," followed by Niphal wayyimma'es (from m's II, "dissolves/oozes").
- **7:20** — The Masoretic Text reads alay ("to myself"), which rabbinic tradition lists among the scribal corrections (tiqqune soferim) for an original aleykha ("to You"), reflected in the Septuagint.
