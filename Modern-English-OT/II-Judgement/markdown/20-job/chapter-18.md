## Text

**18:1** Then Bildad the Shuhite replied and said:

**18:2** "How long until you put an end to words? Show discernment, and afterward we will speak.

**18:3** Why are we reckoned as beasts? Why are we dull in your eyes?

**18:4** You who tear yourself in your rage—shall the earth be abandoned for your sake, or a rock be shifted from its place?

**18:5** Surely the light of the wicked dies out, and the flame of his fire will not glow.

**18:6** The light grows dark in his tent, and his lamp above him dies out.

**18:7** The strides of his vigor are cramped, and his own counsel casts him down.

**18:8** For he is driven into a net by his own feet, and he walks about over netting.

**18:9** A trap catches him by the heel; a snare holds fast upon him.

**18:10** Concealed in the ground is a noose for him, and a trap for him upon the path.

**18:11** On all sides terrors horrify him, and drive him at his heels.

**18:12** His vigor grows famished, and disaster stands ready for his fall.

**18:13** It devours the parts of his skin; the firstborn of death devours his limbs.

**18:14** He is torn away from his tent, his security, and marched to the king of terrors.

**18:15** What does not belong to him dwells in his tent; sulfur is strewn over his dwelling.

**18:16** Below, his roots dry out, and above, his bough withers.

**18:17** His remembrance perishes from the earth, and he has no name in the open country.

**18:18** They thrust him out of light into darkness, and banish him from the world.

**18:19** He has neither offspring nor grandchild among his people, and no survivor in his dwelling places.

**18:20** People of the west are stunned at his day, and those of the east are gripped with horror.

**18:21** Indeed, these are the dwellings of the wrongdoer, and this is the place of the one who does not know God."

## Translator Notes

- **18:2** — The hapax qintsei is an Aramaizing plural construct of qets ("end") with nasalization, matching the Aramaic plural millin ("words"), though some connect it to an Arabic root meaning "hunt" or "snare." Bildad addresses Job using 2mp forms in vv. 2-3.
- **18:3** — The Niphal perfect nitminu derives from tmh ("be stopped up, dull, senseless"), though it may also be a by-form of tma ("be regarded as unclean").
- **18:12** — Hebrew letsalo can mean either "for his fall/stumbling" (tsela II, as in Ps 38:18, which pairs the same noun with nakon) or "at his side" (tsela I).
- **18:15** — Masoretic mibbelilo is written as one word fused from mibbeli-lo ("what does not belong to him," lit. "of not-to-him"), with the 3fs verb tishkon ("dwells") taking an indefinite neuter subject or anticipating feminine gophrit ("sulfur").
