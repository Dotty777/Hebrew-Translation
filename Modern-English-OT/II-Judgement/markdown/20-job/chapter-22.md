## Text

**22:1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite replied and said,

**22:2** "Can a strong man be of benefit to God? Rather, a discerning man benefits himself.

**22:3** Is it a delight to the Almighty when you are righteous, or a gain when you make your ways blameless?

**22:4** Is it on account of your reverence that He rebukes you and comes with you into judgment?

**22:5** Is your wickedness not great, and is there no end to your iniquities?

**22:6** For you took pledges from your brothers for nothing, and stripped off the garments of the naked.

**22:7** You gave no water for the weary to drink, and from the hungry you kept back bread.

**22:8** As for the man of power, the land was his, and the honored man lived in it.

**22:9** Widows you sent away empty-handed, and the arms of the fatherless were crushed.

**22:10** Therefore snares are on every side of you, and sudden dread terrifies you,

**22:11** or darkness, so you cannot see, and a flood of waters covers you.

**22:12** Is God not in the height of the heavens? And look at the topmost stars, how high they are!

**22:13** Yet you say, 'What does God know? Does He judge through dense darkness?

**22:14** Thick clouds are a covering for Him, so He does not see, and He walks about on the circle of the heavens.'

**22:15** Will you keep the path of old that men of iniquity have trodden,

**22:16** who were snatched away before their time, whose foundation was washed away by a river,

**22:17** who said to God, 'Turn away from us!' and, 'What can the Almighty do to them?'

**22:18** Yet He Himself filled their houses with good things; still, the counsel of the wicked is far from me.

**22:19** The righteous see and rejoice, and the innocent person scorns them:

**22:20** 'Indeed, our adversaries have been wiped out, and fire has consumed their abundance.'

**22:21** Now become reconciled with Him and be at peace; through these things good will come to you.

**22:22** Accept instruction now from His mouth, and place His words in your heart.

**22:23** If you return to the Almighty, you will be built up; put injustice far from your tent,

**22:24** and set gold ore in the dust, and the gold of Ophir as stones of the streambeds,

**22:25** then the Almighty will be your gold ore, and the finest silver for you.

**22:26** For then you will take delight in the Almighty, and lift your face to God.

**22:27** You will pray to Him and He will listen to you, and you will fulfill your vows.

**22:28** You will decree a matter, and it will stand for you, and upon your ways light will shine.

**22:29** When people are brought low, you will say, 'There is lifting up!' and He will save the one with downcast eyes.

**22:30** He will rescue the one who is not innocent, and he will be rescued by the purity of your hands."

## Translator Notes

- **22:2** — The poetic suffix -מוֹ on עָלֵימוֹ here refers to a singular antecedent ("himself," i.e., the discerning man), though some take it as "to Him" (God).
- **22:17** — MT reads third-person לָמוֹ ("to them" or "for them"), shifting abruptly from the first-person מִמֶּנּוּ ("from us"); some ancient versions harmonize this to "to us."
- **22:24** — בֶּצֶר (also in v. 25) is a rare term for gold ore; MT reads כְּצוּר ("as stones/rock") rather than בְּצוּר ("among the stones").
- **22:29** — The Hebrew כִּי־הִשְׁפִּילוּ וַתֹּאמֶר גֵּוָה is terse: Hiphil הִשְׁפִּילוּ can be read impersonally ("when people are brought low"), and גֵּוָה means either "lifting up" or "pride." שַׁח עֵינַיִם is literally "bowed of eyes" (humble or downcast).
- **22:30** — MT reads אִי־נָקִי ("one who is not innocent"), using the rare negative particle אִי (cf. 1 Sam 4:21); יְמַלֵּט (Piel) and נִמְלַט (Niphal) pair active and passive forms of מלט ("rescue").
