## Text

**11:1** Then Zophar the Naamathite answered and said,

**11:2** "Should an abundance of words go unanswered, or a man of lips be vindicated?

**11:3** Should your empty boasts silence men, and should you scoff with no one to put you to shame?

**11:4** For you said, 'My teaching is pure, and I have been clean in Your eyes.'

**11:5** Yet if only God would speak and open His lips toward you,

**11:6** and declare to you the hidden things of wisdom—for sound wisdom is twofold! Know then that God overlooks for you some of your iniquity.

**11:7** Can you find the depths of God? Or can you attain to the boundary of the Almighty?

**11:8** The heights of heaven—what can you do? Deeper than Sheol—what can you know?

**11:9** Longer than the earth is its measure, and wider than the sea.

**11:10** If He sweeps past and imprisons, and convenes an assembly, then who can turn Him back?

**11:11** For He knows men of falsehood, and He sees wrongdoing—will He not take notice?

**11:12** But a hollow man will gain understanding when the colt of a wild donkey is born a human.

**11:13** If you direct your heart and spread out your palms to Him,

**11:14** if wrongdoing is in your hand, send it far away, and do not allow injustice to dwell in your tents;

**11:15** then indeed you will lift up your face without blemish, and you will be firmly established and will not fear.

**11:16** For you will forget trouble; you will recall it like waters that have passed by.

**11:17** And life will arise brighter than noonday; even if it grows dark, it will be like the morning.

**11:18** And you will be confident, for there is hope; you will search around and lie down in safety.

**11:19** And you will rest with no one to frighten you, and many will seek your favor.

**11:20** But the eyes of the wicked will fail, escape will perish from them, and their hope is the breathing out of life."

## Translator Notes

- **11:6** — The dual noun kiflayim (twofold) depicts sound wisdom as multifaceted and deep. The Hiphil verb yasheh (from nshh, cause to forget) with partitive min indicates that God overlooks or remits part of Job's iniquity.
- **11:11** — The clause welo yithbonan (Hithpolel of byn, consider or take notice) is taken as an unmarked rhetorical question (will He not take notice?), though some construe it circumstantially (without needing to investigate).
- **11:12** — The Hebrew plays on navuv (Qal passive participle, hollow) and the denominative Niphal yillavev (from lev, heart or mind, meaning gain understanding). The conjunction we- introduces a proverbial impossibility (when the colt of a wild donkey is born a human), though some take it concessively.
- **11:17** — The Masoretic vocalization ta'ufah reflects a Qal imperfect with paragogic -ah from 'wf (be dark), yielding even if it grows dark; some interpreters revocalize it as a noun meaning gloom.
