## Text

**34:1** Then Elihu responded and said:

**34:2** "Listen to my words, you wise men, and you who have knowledge, give ear to me.

**34:3** For the ear tests words, just as the palate tastes when eating.

**34:4** Let us choose justice for ourselves; let us discern among us what is good.

**34:5** For Job has said, 'I am righteous, yet God has set aside my right.

**34:6** Am I to lie against my right? My arrow wound is incurable, without transgression.'

**34:7** What man is there like Job, who drinks mockery like water,

**34:8** and journeys in fellowship with workers of iniquity, and walks with men of wickedness?

**34:9** For he said, 'A man does not benefit when he takes delight with God.'

**34:10** Therefore, men of discernment, listen to me: far be wickedness from God, and injustice from the Almighty.

**34:11** For He repays a person his deed, and according to a man's path He causes it to find him.

**34:12** Indeed, truly God will not act wickedly, and the Almighty will not distort justice.

**34:13** Who entrusted the earth to Him, and who placed the entire world upon Him?

**34:14** If He should set His heart toward Himself, and gather His spirit and His breath to Himself,

**34:15** all flesh would expire together, and humanity would return to dust.

**34:16** And if you have understanding, listen to this; give ear to the sound of my words.

**34:17** Can one who hates justice truly govern? Or will you condemn the righteous, mighty One—

**34:18** should one say to a king, 'Worthless!' or 'Wicked!' to nobles?—

**34:19** who shows no favoritism to princes and does not regard the wealthy ahead of the poor, since all of them are the work of His hands?

**34:20** In an instant they die, and at midnight the people are convulsed and pass away, and the mighty one is removed by no hand.

**34:21** For His eyes are over the ways of a man, and He sees all his footsteps.

**34:22** There is no darkness and there is no deep gloom where workers of iniquity can hide themselves.

**34:23** For He does not set His attention further upon a man, for him to go to God in judgment.

**34:24** He shatters mighty men without inquiry, and stations others in their place.

**34:25** Therefore He recognizes their deeds, and He overturns them by night so they are crushed.

**34:26** As wicked men He strikes them in the place of onlookers,

**34:27** because they turned away from following Him and paid no heed to any of His ways,

**34:28** so as to bring to Him the outcry of the poor, and He hears the outcry of the afflicted.

**34:29** Yet when He remains silent, who can condemn? And when He hides His face, who can look upon Him, whether toward a nation or toward a person alike?—

**34:30** so that a godless person may not reign, nor those who are snares to the people.

**34:31** For has anyone said to God, 'I have endured; I will not act corruptly.

**34:32** Beyond what I see, You teach me; if I have committed injustice, I will not repeat it'?

**34:33** Is He to repay it on your terms, since you have rejected it? For it is you who will choose, and not I; so speak what you know.

**34:34** Men of discernment will say to me, and a wise man who listens to me:

**34:35** 'Job does not speak with knowledge, and his words have no insight.'

**34:36** If only Job were tested to the very end, because of his answers like those of men of iniquity;

**34:37** for he adds transgression to his sin; among us he claps his hands, and he multiplies his sayings against God."

## Translator Notes

- **34:6** — The Piel imperfect 'akhazzev ("I lie") is rendered as an unmarked rhetorical question ("Am I to lie?"); some interpret it passively as "am I treated as a liar?" Also, chitsi literally means "my arrow," a metonymy for an arrow-inflicted wound (cf. 6:4).
- **34:18** — The Masoretic Text points ha'emor as the interrogative particle plus Qal infinitive construct ("should one say?"), treated here as a parenthetical question. Ancient versions and many translations revocalize it as a participle (ha'omer, "who says") referring to God.
- **34:23** — The Qal imperfect yasim ("sets") is used elliptically for "sets the heart/attention" (cf. 23:6; 34:14), and the Masoretic Text reads 'od ("further"), indicating God requires no further investigation. Some emend 'od to mo'ed ("appointed time").
- **34:29** — Verses 29-30 have abrupt, elliptical syntax in Hebrew, literally "and over a nation and over a person alike, from the reigning of a godless person, from snares of the people." The Hiphil yashqit ("remains silent") can also be taken transitively as "gives quietness."
- **34:36** — The opening word 'avi is an obscure optative particle probably derived from the root 'avah ("desire, wish"), hence "If only" or "My wish is."
