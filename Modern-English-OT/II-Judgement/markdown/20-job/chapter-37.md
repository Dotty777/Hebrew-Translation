## Text

**37:1** "At this too my heart quakes and springs out of its place.

**37:2** Listen intently to the raging of His voice and the rumble that goes out from His mouth.

**37:3** Beneath all the heavens He releases it, and His lightning to the edges of the earth.

**37:4** After it a voice roars; He thunders with the voice of His majesty, and He does not hold them back when His voice is heard.

**37:5** God thunders marvelously with His voice, performing great things that we do not know.

**37:6** For He says to the snow, 'Fall upon the earth,' and also to the downpour of rain, even the downpour of His mighty rains.

**37:7** He puts a seal on the hand of every person, so that all the people of His making may know.

**37:8** Then the wild animals enter a lair and dwell in their dens.

**37:9** Out of the chamber comes the storm wind, and out of the scattering winds comes the cold.

**37:10** From the breath of God ice is produced, and the breadth of the waters is frozen solid.

**37:11** Indeed, He weighs down the thick cloud with moisture; He scatters the cloud of His lightning.

**37:12** It turns about in circuits by His direction, so that they carry out everything He commands them across the face of the inhabited earth.

**37:13** Whether for a rod, or for His land, or for loyal love, He brings it about.

**37:14** Give ear to this, Job; stand still and ponder the marvels of God.

**37:15** Do you know how God lays His charge upon them and makes the lightning of His cloud flash forth?

**37:16** Do you know about the balancings of the thick clouds, the marvels of the One complete in knowledge,

**37:17** you whose clothes are hot when the earth lies still from the south?

**37:18** Can you beat out the skies with Him, firm as a cast mirror?

**37:19** Make known to us what we should say to Him; we cannot frame an argument because of darkness.

**37:20** Is it to be reported to Him that I would speak? Did a man ever say that he should be swallowed up?

**37:21** And now people cannot look at the light, brilliant as it is in the skies, after a wind has passed over and cleared them.

**37:22** From the north a golden radiance comes; upon God is awesome majesty.

**37:23** As for the Almighty, we cannot find Him; He is great in strength, and He does not violate justice and abundance of righteousness.

**37:24** Therefore people fear Him; He has no regard for any who are wise in heart."

## Translator Notes

- **37:6** — The imperative heve ("fall" or "be") uses an Aramaic-influenced form of the root hawah, and the doubled phrase "downpour of rain, even the downpour of His mighty rains" follows the Masoretic vocalization of uzzo ("of His might") as a noun with suffix rather than a verb.
- **37:7** — Following the Masoretic construct plural anshei, kol-anshei maasehu literally reads "all the people of His making" (those He has created), though some interpret it as "that all people may know His work."
- **37:9** — "The chamber" (hacheder) likely alludes to the southern storehouse of tempests (cf. Job 9:9), and mezarim ("scattering winds," a hapax Piel participle of zarah, "scatter") traditionally denotes the cold north winds.
- **37:16** — The hapax noun miflesei ("balancings," from palas, "weigh/balance") creates deliberate wordplay with mifleot ("marvels"), and "the One complete in knowledge" echoes Elihu's own phrase in 36:4.
- **37:22** — "A golden radiance" translates Hebrew zahav (lit. "gold"), used metaphorically for the bright golden glow of the northern sky or divine splendor.
