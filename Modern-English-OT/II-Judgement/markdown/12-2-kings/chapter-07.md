## Text

**7:1** Then Elisha said, "Hear the word of the LORD! This is what the LORD says: 'About this time tomorrow, a seah of fine flour will go for a shekel, and two seahs of barley for a shekel, at the gate of Samaria.'"

**7:2** Then the officer on whose hand the king leaned answered the man of God and said, "Look, if the LORD should make windows in the heavens, could this thing happen?" And he said, "Look, you will see it with your own eyes, yet you will not eat any of it."

**7:3** Now four leprous men were at the entrance of the gate, and they said to one another, "Why are we sitting here until we die?

**7:4** If we say, 'We will enter the city,' the famine is in the city, and we will die there; and if we sit here, we will die. So now, come, let us surrender to the camp of Aram. If they let us live, we will live; and if they put us to death, then we will die."

**7:5** So they rose at twilight to go to the camp of Aram; and when they came to the edge of the camp of Aram, look, no one was there.

**7:6** For the Lord had caused the camp of Aram to hear the sound of chariots, the sound of horses, the sound of a great army. So they said to one another, "Look, the king of Israel has hired the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Egypt against us to come upon us!"

**7:7** Therefore they rose and fled at twilight, and they abandoned their tents, their horses, and their donkeys, the camp just as it was, and fled for their lives.

**7:8** When these leprous men came to the edge of the camp, they entered one tent and ate and drank, and they carried away silver, gold, and garments from there, and went and hid them. Then they returned and entered another tent, carried things away from there, and went and hid them.

**7:9** Then they said to one another, "We are not doing right. This day is a day of good news, yet we are keeping silent. If we wait until the light of morning, punishment will overtake us. So now, come, let us go and report at the king's palace."

**7:10** So they came and called out to the gatekeepers of the city, and they told them, saying, "We went to the camp of Aram, and look, no one was there and no human voice, only the horses tied up, the donkeys tied up, and the tents just as they were."

**7:11** Then the gatekeepers called out, and they reported it inside the king's palace.

**7:12** The king rose in the night and said to his servants, "Let me tell you now what the Arameans have done to us. They know that we are hungry, so they have left the camp to hide themselves in the field, saying, 'When they come out of the city, we will capture them alive and enter the city.'"

**7:13** Then one of his servants answered and said, "Please let them take five of the remaining horses that are left in it—look, they are like all the multitude of Israel who are left in it; look, they are like all the multitude of Israel who have perished—and let us send and see."

**7:14** So they took two chariots with horses, and the king sent them after the army of Aram, saying, "Go and see."

**7:15** They went after them as far as the Jordan, and look, the entire road was full of garments and equipment that the Arameans had thrown away in their haste. Then the messengers returned and told the king.

**7:16** So the people went out and plundered the camp of Aram. Then a seah of fine flour went for a shekel, and two seahs of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the LORD.

**7:17** Now the king had appointed the officer on whose hand he leaned to be in charge of the gate, but the people trampled him in the gate, and he died, just as the man of God had spoken, who spoke when the king came down to him.

**7:18** For when the man of God had spoken to the king, saying, "Two seahs of barley will go for a shekel, and a seah of fine flour for a shekel, about this time tomorrow at the gate of Samaria,"

**7:19** the officer had answered the man of God and said, "Now look, if the LORD should make windows in the heavens, could anything like this happen?" And he had said, "Look, you will see it with your own eyes, yet you will not eat any of it."

**7:20** So it happened to him; the people trampled him in the gate, and he died.

## Translator Notes

- **7:4** — Hebrew literally says 'let us fall to the camp of Aram,' a standard military idiom for deserting or surrendering to an enemy.
- **7:12** — Following the qere bassadeh ('in the field') rather than the ketiv bahassadeh, which retains an unelided definite article; both carry the same meaning.
- **7:13** — Following the qere hamon ('multitude,' standard construct state) rather than the ketiv hahamon, which retains an anomalous definite article in construct; the Masoretic Text preserves both parallel clauses comparing the horses' fate to that of all Israel.
- **7:15** — Following the qere bechofzam (Qal infinitive construct of chafaz, 'in their haste') rather than the ketiv behechafzam (Niphal infinitive construct, 'in their panicked flight').
- **7:18** — Hebrew wayehi kedabber uses a temporal Piel infinitive construct ('when the man of God had spoken') whose main clause follows in verse 19; the order of barley and fine flour is reversed here from verse 1.
