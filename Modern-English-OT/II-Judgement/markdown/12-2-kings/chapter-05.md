## Text

**5:1** Now Naaman, commander of the army of the king of Aram, was a great man before his master and highly esteemed, because through him the LORD had given deliverance to Aram. The man was a mighty warrior, yet he was leprous.

**5:2** Now the Arameans had gone out in raiding bands and had taken captive a young girl from the land of Israel, and she attended Naaman's wife.

**5:3** She said to her mistress, "If only my master were before the prophet who is in Samaria! Then he would cure him of his leprosy."

**5:4** So he went in and told his master, saying, "Thus and thus spoke the girl who is from the land of Israel."

**5:5** Then the king of Aram said, "Go ahead, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel." So he departed and took with him ten talents of silver, six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of garments.

**5:6** And he brought the letter to the king of Israel, saying, "Now when this letter comes to you, see, I have sent Naaman my servant to you, so that you may cure him of his leprosy."

**5:7** When the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his garments and said, "Am I God, to put to death and to make alive, that this one sends to me to cure a man of his leprosy? Indeed, only consider now and see that he is seeking a quarrel against me!"

**5:8** When Elisha the man of God heard that the king of Israel had torn his garments, he sent word to the king, saying, "Why have you torn your garments? Please let him come to me, and he will know that there is a prophet in Israel."

**5:9** So Naaman came with his horses and chariot and stood at the doorway of Elisha's house.

**5:10** And Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, "Go and wash seven times in the Jordan, so that your flesh may be restored to you, and be clean."

**5:11** But Naaman became furious and walked away, and he said, "Look, I thought, 'He will surely come out to me, and stand and call on the name of the LORD his God, and wave his hand over the place and cure the leper.'

**5:12** Are not Amanah and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not wash in them and become clean?" So he turned and went away in rage.

**5:13** Then his servants drew near and spoke to him and said, "My father, had the prophet spoken a great thing to you, would you not have done it? How much more, then, when he has said to you, 'Wash and be clean'?"

**5:14** So he went down and immersed himself in the Jordan seven times, according to the word of the man of God; and his flesh was restored like the flesh of a young boy, and he became clean.

**5:15** Then he returned to the man of God, he and all his company, and he came and stood before him and said, "Look now, I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. And now, please take a gift from your servant."

**5:16** But he said, "As the LORD lives, before whom I stand, I will take nothing." And he urged him to take it, but he refused.

**5:17** Then Naaman said, "If not, please let a load of earth for a pair of mules be given to your servant; for your servant will no longer offer burnt offering or sacrifice to other gods, but only to the LORD.

**5:18** In this matter may the LORD pardon your servant: when my master goes into the house of Rimmon to bow down there, and he leans on my hand, and I bow down in the house of Rimmon—when I bow down in the house of Rimmon, may the LORD pardon your servant in this matter."

**5:19** And he said to him, "Go in peace." So he departed from him a short distance.

**5:20** Then Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said, "Look, my master has spared this Aramean Naaman by not taking from his hand what he brought. As the LORD lives, I will surely run after him and take something from him."

**5:21** So Gehazi pursued after Naaman. When Naaman saw someone running after him, he stepped down from the chariot to meet him and said, "Is all well?"

**5:22** And he said, "All is well. My master has sent me, saying, 'Look, just now two young men from the hill country of Ephraim, from the sons of the prophets, have come to me. Please give them a talent of silver and two changes of garments.'"

**5:23** Naaman said, "Be pleased to take two talents." And he pressed him, and tied up two talents of silver in two bags, with two changes of garments, and handed them to two of his servants; and they carried them before him.

**5:24** When he came to the hill, he took them from their hand and stored them away in the house; then he sent the men away, and they departed.

**5:25** Then he went in and stood before his master. And Elisha said to him, "Where have you come from, Gehazi?" And he said, "Your servant has not gone anywhere at all."

**5:26** Then he said to him, "Did not my heart go along when the man turned back from his chariot to meet you? Is it a time to take the silver and to take garments, and olive groves and vineyards, and flocks and herds, and male servants and female servants?

**5:27** Therefore the leprosy of Naaman shall cling to you and to your offspring forever." So he went out from his presence leprous, like snow.

## Translator Notes

- **5:1** — Hebrew tsara'at ('leprosy,' with the Pual participle metsora', 'leprous') designates a range of serious, ritually defiling skin afflictions rather than solely modern Hansen's disease.
- **5:3** — The verb translated 'cure' here and in vv. 6, 7, and 11 is Qal asaph ('gather in, remove'), used idiomatically for removing leprosy and restoring the afflicted person to the community.
- **5:12** — The translation follows the qere reading 'Amanah' rather than the ketiv 'Abana,' reflecting a known interchange of b and m in the river's ancient names.
- **5:18** — In the second petition, the particle na ('please') is written in the ketiv but omitted in the qere (a ketiv velo qere); the translation follows the qere.
- **5:25** — For 'Where have you come from,' the ketiv writes a contracted spelling while the qere reads the standard interrogative me'ayin ('from where'), with identical meaning.
