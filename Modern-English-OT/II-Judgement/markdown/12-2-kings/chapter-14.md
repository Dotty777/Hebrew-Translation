## Text

**14:1** In the second year of Joash son of Joahaz, king of Israel, Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, began to reign.

**14:2** He was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jehoaddan from Jerusalem.

**14:3** He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, though not like his father David; he did according to everything his father Joash had done.

**14:4** Only the high places were not removed; the people were still sacrificing and burning incense on the high places.

**14:5** Now when the kingdom grew strong in his hand, he struck down his servants who had struck down his father the king.

**14:6** Yet he did not put to death the sons of those who had struck him down, in accordance with what is written in the book of the law of Moses, in which the LORD commanded, saying, "Fathers shall not be put to death on account of sons, and sons shall not be put to death on account of fathers; instead, each man shall be put to death for his own sin."

**14:7** He struck down ten thousand of Edom in the Valley of Salt, captured Sela in battle, and called its name Joktheel, to this day.

**14:8** Then Amaziah sent messengers to Jehoash son of Jehoahaz, son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, "Come, let us look each other in the face."

**14:9** Then Jehoash king of Israel sent word to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, "The thornbush that was in Lebanon sent word to the cedar that was in Lebanon, saying, 'Give your daughter to my son for a wife.' Then a wild animal of the field that was in Lebanon passed by and trampled the thornbush.

**14:10** You have indeed struck down Edom, and your heart has lifted you up. Relish your honor and remain in your house; for why should you stir up disaster so that you fall, you and Judah with you?"

**14:11** Amaziah, however, did not listen. So Jehoash king of Israel went up, and they looked each other in the face, he and Amaziah king of Judah, at Beth-shemesh, which belongs to Judah.

**14:12** And Judah was routed before Israel, and they fled, each man to his tent.

**14:13** Jehoash king of Israel captured Amaziah king of Judah, son of Jehoash, son of Ahaziah, at Beth-shemesh. Then he came to Jerusalem and breached the wall of Jerusalem from the Gate of Ephraim to the Corner Gate, four hundred cubits.

**14:14** He took all the gold and silver and all the vessels that were found in the house of the LORD and in the treasuries of the king's house, as well as the hostages, and returned to Samaria.

**14:15** As for the rest of the deeds of Jehoash that he did, and his might, and how he fought with Amaziah king of Judah, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

**14:16** Then Jehoash lay down with his fathers and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel, and his son Jeroboam reigned in his place.

**14:17** Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, lived fifteen years after the death of Jehoash son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel.

**14:18** As for the rest of the deeds of Amaziah, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**14:19** They formed a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish; yet they sent after him to Lachish and put him to death there.

**14:20** Then they carried him on horses, and he was buried in Jerusalem with his fathers in the City of David.

**14:21** Then all the people of Judah took Azariah, when he was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah.

**14:22** He built Elath and restored it to Judah after the king lay down with his fathers.

**14:23** In the fifteenth year of Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, Jeroboam son of Joash, king of Israel, became king in Samaria for forty-one years.

**14:24** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD; he did not turn aside from all the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, with which he caused Israel to sin.

**14:25** He restored the border of Israel from Lebo-hamath to the Sea of the Arabah, in accordance with the word of the LORD, the God of Israel, which He spoke by the hand of His servant Jonah son of Amittai, the prophet who was from Gath-hepher.

**14:26** For the LORD saw that the affliction of Israel was very bitter; there was no one bond and no one free, and there was no helper for Israel.

**14:27** Yet the LORD had not spoken of blotting out the name of Israel from under heaven, so He saved them by the hand of Jeroboam son of Joash.

**14:28** As for the rest of the deeds of Jeroboam and all that he did, and his might, how he fought, and how he restored Damascus and Hamath for Judah in Israel, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

**14:29** Then Jeroboam lay down with his fathers, with the kings of Israel, and his son Zechariah reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **14:2** — The Qere (read form) is Jehoaddan, whereas the Ketiv (written form) reads Jehoaddin.
- **14:6** — The Qere reads the Hophal imperfect "shall be put to death" (matching Deut 24:16), whereas the Ketiv reads the Qal imperfect "shall die."
- **14:13** — The Qere reads the singular "he came," whereas the Ketiv reads the plural "they came."
- **14:26** — Hebrew moreh ("bitter") resembles the participle of marah ("rebellious") but is widely understood in context as a variant for "bitter." The paired passive participles "bond" and "free" (literally "confined" and "released") form a merism for all classes of people.
- **14:28** — The Masoretic Hebrew literally reads "for Judah in Israel," a difficult phrase often understood as territories formerly belonging to Judah now restored to Israel.
