## Text

**6:1** The sons of the prophets said to Elisha, "Look now, the place where we dwell before you is too cramped for us.

**6:2** Please let us go as far as the Jordan, and let every man take one beam from there, and let us make for ourselves a place there to dwell." So he said, "Go."

**6:3** Then one of them said, "Please consent and go with your servants." He replied, "I will go."

**6:4** He went with them, and they arrived at the Jordan and cut down the trees.

**6:5** Now as one of them was felling a beam, the iron fell into the water. He cried out and said, "Alas, my master, for it was borrowed!"

**6:6** The man of God asked, "Where did it fall?" He showed him the place, and he cut off a piece of wood, cast it there, and caused the iron to float.

**6:7** And he said, "Lift it up for yourself." So he stretched out his hand and took it.

**6:8** Now the king of Aram was waging war against Israel, and he took counsel with his servants, saying, "In such and such a place my encampment will be."

**6:9** Then the man of God sent word to the king of Israel, saying, "Be on your guard against passing this place, for the Arameans are coming down there."

**6:10** The king of Israel sent to the place about which the man of God had told him and warned him, so he kept on his guard there, not once and not twice.

**6:11** The heart of the king of Aram was greatly agitated over this matter, so he summoned his servants and said to them, "Will you not tell me which one of ours is for the king of Israel?"

**6:12** One of his servants said, "No, my lord the king; rather, Elisha, the prophet who is in Israel, tells the king of Israel the words that you speak in your bedroom."

**6:13** So he said, "Go and see where he is, so that I may send and take him." And it was told to him, "Look, he is in Dothan."

**6:14** He sent horses and chariots and a massive army there, and they came by night and surrounded the city.

**6:15** Now the attendant of the man of God got up early and went out, and look, an army with horses and chariots was encircling the city. His young servant said to him, "Alas, my master! What are we to do?"

**6:16** He answered, "Do not be afraid, for those with us are more numerous than those with them."

**6:17** Then Elisha prayed and said, "LORD, please open his eyes so that he may see." The LORD opened the young man's eyes, and he saw, and look, the mountain was filled with horses and chariots of fire encircling Elisha.

**6:18** When they came down to him, Elisha prayed to the LORD and said, "Please strike this nation with blindness." So He struck them with blindness according to the word of Elisha.

**6:19** Then Elisha said to them, "This is not the road, and this is not the city. Follow after me, and I will lead you to the man whom you are seeking." So he led them to Samaria.

**6:20** When they entered Samaria, Elisha said, "LORD, open the eyes of these men so that they may see." The LORD opened their eyes, and they saw, and look, they were in the middle of Samaria.

**6:21** When the king of Israel saw them, he said to Elisha, "Should I strike them down? Should I strike them down, my father?"

**6:22** He replied, "Do not strike them down. Would you strike down those you captured with your sword and bow? Place bread and water in front of them, and let them eat and drink and go to their master."

**6:23** So he prepared a great banquet for them, and they ate and drank; then he sent them away, and they went to their master. And the raiding bands of Aram did not come again into the land of Israel.

**6:24** After this, Ben-hadad king of Aram assembled his entire army, went up, and laid siege to Samaria.

**6:25** There was a great famine in Samaria, and look, they besieged it until a donkey's head went for eighty pieces of silver, and a quarter of a kab of dove's droppings for five pieces of silver.

**6:26** While the king of Israel was passing along the wall, a woman cried out to him, "Help, my lord the king!"

**6:27** He replied, "If the LORD does not help you, from where could I help you? From the threshing floor or from the winepress?"

**6:28** Then the king asked her, "What troubles you?" She replied, "This woman said to me, 'Give your son so that we may eat him today, and we will eat my son tomorrow.'

**6:29** So we boiled my son and ate him. Then on the next day I said to her, 'Give your son so that we may eat him,' but she has hidden her son."

**6:30** When the king heard the woman's words, he tore his garments while he was passing along the wall; and the people looked, and look, sackcloth was underneath on his flesh.

**6:31** Then he said, "May God do so to me, and so may He add, if the head of Elisha son of Shaphat remains on him today!"

**6:32** Now Elisha was sitting in his house, and the elders were sitting with him. The king had sent a man from his presence, but before the messenger reached him, Elisha said to the elders, "Do you see that this son of a murderer has sent to remove my head? Look, when the messenger arrives, shut the door and press him back with the door. Is not the sound of his master's feet behind him?"

**6:33** While he was still speaking with them, look, the messenger came down to him. And he said, "Look, this calamity is from the LORD; why should I keep waiting for the LORD?"

## Translator Notes

- **6:5** — Hebrew literally reads 'the iron,' referring to the iron ax head, and places the particle et before the subject of the intransitive verb nafal, a syntactic usage attested occasionally in Kings.
- **6:6** — The verb wayyatsef is the Hiphil of tsuf ('to float'), translated causatively here ('caused the iron to float'), though it can also be taken intransitively ('the iron floated').
- **6:22** — The Masoretic Text frames a rhetorical 'how much less' question — you would not strike down even captives taken with your own weapons — without the negative particle supplied by some ancient versions.
- **6:25** — Following the euphemistic qere div-yonim ('droppings/discharge of doves') rather than the cruder ketiv chirei-yonim ('dung of doves'); some identify the expression as a colloquial name for a wild plant or seed pods.
- **6:27** — Hebrew al-yoshi'ekh is literally a negated jussive ('May the LORD not help you'), functioning idiomatically here as a negative conditional protasis ('If the LORD does not help you').
