## Text

**25:1** It happened in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, came, he and all his army, against Jerusalem. He camped against it, and they built a siege wall against it all around.

**25:2** Thus the city came under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah.

**25:3** On the ninth of the month, the famine grew severe in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

**25:4** Then the city was breached, and all the men of war went out at night by way of the gate between the two walls, which was by the king's garden, while the Chaldeans were against the city all around; and he went along the road to the Arabah.

**25:5** The army of the Chaldeans pursued after the king, and they overtook him in the plains of Jericho, and all his army dispersed away from him.

**25:6** They seized the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon, to Riblah, and they pronounced judgment on him.

**25:7** They slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes; then he blinded the eyes of Zedekiah, bound him in bronze shackles, and brought him to Babylon.

**25:8** Now in the fifth month, on the seventh of the month—this was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon—Nebuzaradan, chief of the bodyguard, servant of the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.

**25:9** He burned the house of the LORD and the house of the king, and all the houses of Jerusalem and every great house he burned with fire.

**25:11** And Nebuzaradan, chief of the bodyguard, took into exile the rest of the people who were left in the city, the defectors who had defected to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the multitude.

**25:12** But of the poorest of the land, the chief of the bodyguard left some as vinedressers and farmers.

**25:13** And the Chaldeans smashed to pieces the bronze pillars that were in the house of the LORD, and the stands and the bronze sea that were in the house of the LORD, and they carried their bronze to Babylon.

**25:14** They took the pots, the shovels, the snuffers, the ladles, and all the bronze articles with which they used to minister.

**25:15** And the chief of the bodyguard took the firepans and the sprinkling bowls—whatever was gold as gold, and whatever was silver as silver.

**25:16** As for the two pillars, the one sea, and the stands that Solomon had made for the house of the LORD, the bronze of all these articles could not be weighed.

**25:17** Eighteen cubits was the height of the one pillar, and a bronze capital was on top of it; the height of the capital was three cubits, and latticework and pomegranates were on the capital all around, all of it bronze. And things like these were on the second pillar, along with the latticework.

**25:18** Then the chief of the bodyguard took Seraiah, the chief priest, and Zephaniah, the priest of second rank, and the three threshold keepers.

**25:19** And from the city he took one official who was an overseer over the men of war; five men from those who saw the king's face, who were found in the city; the scribe, the commander of the army, who mustered the people of the land; and sixty men from the people of the land who were found in the city.

**25:20** Nebuzaradan, chief of the bodyguard, took them and led them to the king of Babylon, to Riblah.

**25:21** Then the king of Babylon struck them down and killed them in Riblah, in the land of Hamath. Thus Judah went into exile from its soil.

**25:22** And over the people who were left in the land of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, had left, he appointed Gedaliah, son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan.

**25:23** When all the commanders of the forces, they and the men, heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah, they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah—Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, and Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite, they and their men.

**25:24** And Gedaliah swore to them and to their men, and he said to them, "Do not fear the servants of the Chaldeans; reside in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will be good for you."

**25:25** And it happened in the seventh month that Ishmael, son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, of the royal seed, came with ten men, and they struck down Gedaliah—and he died—as well as the Judeans and the Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah.

**25:26** Then all the people, from small to great, along with the commanders of the forces, rose up and went into Egypt, for they feared the Chaldeans.

**25:27** And it happened in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin, king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-seventh of the month, that Evil-merodach, king of Babylon, in the year he became king, lifted up the head of Jehoiachin, king of Judah, out of prison.

**25:28** He spoke kind words with him and placed his throne above the throne of the kings who were with him in Babylon.

**25:29** He changed his prison garments, and he ate bread continually in his presence all the days of his life.

**25:30** And his provision—a continual provision—was given to him from the king, each day's portion on its day, all the days of his life.

## Additional Verses (Not in Saints Numbering)

**25:10** *(after `25:9`)* And the whole army of the Chaldeans who were with the commander of the guard tore down the walls of Jerusalem all around.

## Translator Notes

- **25:3** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads simply 'on the ninth of the month,' without naming the month; compare the parallel account in Jeremiah 52:6, which specifies the fourth month.
- **25:4** — The Hebrew omits a verb of motion after 'all the men of war' (supplied here as 'went out' for readability; compare Jeremiah 52:7) and uses a singular Qal verb 'and he went' at the end, referring to King Zedekiah.
- **25:15** — The terse Hebrew phrasing literally reads 'what was gold, gold, and what was silver, silver,' indicating either items taken for their bullion value or items made of pure gold and pure silver.
- **25:17** — The written ketiv has the singular ammah ('cubit'), whereas the read qere translated here has the plural ammot ('cubits'), agreeing with the numeral 'three.'
- **25:27** — 'Lifted up the head of' is a Hebrew idiom meaning to show royal favor, restore to honor, and grant release from prison.
- **25:10 *(additional)*** — The Masoretic Text omits a preposition before "commander of the guard," reading literally "who the commander of the guard."
