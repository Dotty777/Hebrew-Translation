## Text

**8:1** Now Elisha spoke to the woman whose son he had brought back to life, saying, "Get up and go, you and your household, and sojourn wherever you can sojourn, for the LORD has summoned a famine, and it is indeed coming upon the land for seven years."

**8:2** So the woman got up and acted according to the word of the man of God; she and her household went and sojourned in the land of the Philistines for seven years.

**8:3** At the end of seven years, the woman returned from the land of the Philistines, and she went out to cry out to the king concerning her house and concerning her field.

**8:4** Now the king was speaking to Gehazi, the attendant of the man of God, saying, "Please recount to me all the great things that Elisha has done."

**8:5** While he was recounting to the king how he had brought the dead one back to life, look, the woman whose son he had brought back to life was crying out to the king concerning her house and concerning her field. Then Gehazi said, "My lord the king, this is the woman, and this is her son whom Elisha brought back to life."

**8:6** When the king questioned the woman, she recounted it to him. So the king assigned an official to her, saying, "Restore all that belongs to her and all the produce of the field, from the day she left the land until now."

**8:7** Then Elisha came to Damascus. Now Ben-hadad king of Aram was sick, and it was reported to him, saying, "The man of God has come as far as here."

**8:8** The king said to Hazael, "Take a gift in your hand and go to meet the man of God, and inquire of the LORD through him, saying, 'Will I recover from this sickness?'"

**8:9** So Hazael went to meet him and took a gift in his hand, even every good thing of Damascus, a load of forty camels. He came and stood before him and said, "Your son Ben-hadad king of Aram has sent me to you, saying, 'Will I recover from this sickness?'"

**8:10** Elisha said to him, "Go, say to him, 'You will surely recover,' yet the LORD has shown me that he will surely die."

**8:11** He fixed his gaze and kept it set to the point of embarrassment, and the man of God wept.

**8:12** Hazael said, "Why is my lord weeping?" And he said, "Because I know the evil that you will do to the sons of Israel: their strongholds you will set on fire, their young men you will kill with the sword, their little ones you will dash to pieces, and their pregnant women you will rip open."

**8:13** Hazael said, "But what is your servant, a dog, that he should do this great thing?" Elisha answered, "The LORD has shown me that you will be king over Aram."

**8:14** So he departed from Elisha and came to his master, who said to him, "What did Elisha say to you?" He answered, "He said to me, 'You will surely recover.'"

**8:15** On the next day, he took the bedcover, dipped it in water, and spread it over his face, and he died. Then Hazael became king in his place.

**8:16** Now in the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab, king of Israel, while Jehoshaphat was king of Judah, Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat, king of Judah, became king.

**8:17** He was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.

**8:18** He walked in the way of the kings of Israel, just as the house of Ahab had done, for a daughter of Ahab was his wife; and he did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**8:19** Yet the LORD was not willing to destroy Judah, for the sake of His servant David, just as He had promised him to give him a lamp for his sons always.

**8:20** In his days Edom revolted from under the hand of Judah and set a king over themselves.

**8:21** So Joram crossed over to Zair, and all the chariots were with him. He arose by night and struck down Edom, who had surrounded him, and the chariot commanders, but the troops fled to their tents.

**8:22** Thus Edom has been in revolt from under the hand of Judah to this day. Then Libnah revolted at that time.

**8:23** Now the rest of the deeds of Joram, and all that he did, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah?

**8:24** So Joram lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of David, and his son Ahaziah became king in his place.

**8:25** In the twelfth year of Joram son of Ahab, king of Israel, Ahaziah son of Jehoram, king of Judah, became king.

**8:26** Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Athaliah, daughter of Omri king of Israel.

**8:27** He walked in the way of the house of Ahab and did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, just like the house of Ahab, for he was related by marriage to the house of Ahab.

**8:28** He went with Joram son of Ahab to battle against Hazael king of Aram at Ramoth-Gilead, and the Arameans wounded Joram.

**8:29** So King Joram returned to be healed in Jezreel from the wounds that the Arameans had inflicted on him at Ramah when he fought against Hazael king of Aram. And Ahaziah son of Jehoram, king of Judah, went down to see Joram son of Ahab in Jezreel, because he was sick.

## Translator Notes

- **8:10** — The Qere reads 'to him' (lo), matching Hazael's report in v. 14; the Ketiv reads 'not' (lo), which would yield 'Go, say, "You will surely not recover."' Both 'surely recover' and 'surely die' translate emphatic infinitive absolutes.
- **8:11** — Literally 'he made his face stand and set [it] until being ashamed,' an idiom for staring fixedly to an awkward or embarrassing extent; Hebrew leaves unspecified whether Hazael or Elisha felt the discomfort.
- **8:15** — The Hebrew noun makber ('bedcover') occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and denotes a thick woven cloth or netting.
- **8:16** — The Masoretic text retains the circumstantial clause 'while Jehoshaphat was king of Judah,' indicating a coregency between Jehoshaphat and his son Jehoram.
