## Text

**22:1** Josiah was eight years old when he began reigning, and he reigned for thirty-one years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Jedidah, the daughter of Adaiah, from Bozkath.

**22:2** He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, and he walked in the entire way of his father David, and did not turn aside to the right or left.

**22:3** Then in the eighteenth year of King Josiah, the king sent Shaphan son of Azaliah, son of Meshullam, the scribe, to the house of the LORD, saying,

**22:4** "Go up to Hilkiah the high priest so that he may sum up the silver brought into the house of the LORD, which the threshold keepers have gathered from the people.

**22:5** And let them give it into the hand of the workers who are appointed over the house of the LORD, and let them give it to the workers who are in the house of the LORD to repair the breaches of the house,

**22:6** to the craftsmen, the builders, and the masons, and for purchasing timber and quarried stone to repair the house.

**22:7** However, no reckoning shall be held with them for the silver given into their hand, because they act faithfully."

**22:8** Hilkiah the high priest said to Shaphan the scribe, "I have found the scroll of the law in the house of the LORD." Then Hilkiah gave the scroll to Shaphan, and he read it.

**22:9** Shaphan the scribe came to the king and brought word back to the king, and he said, "Your servants have poured out the silver found in the house and have given it into the hand of the workers who are appointed over the house of the LORD."

**22:10** Then Shaphan the scribe informed the king, saying, "Hilkiah the priest has given me a scroll." And Shaphan read it in the king's presence.

**22:11** When the king heard the words of the scroll of the law, he tore his garments.

**22:12** Then the king commanded Hilkiah the priest, and Ahikam son of Shaphan, and Achbor son of Micaiah, and Shaphan the scribe, and Asaiah, the servant of the king, saying,

**22:13** "Go, inquire of the LORD on my behalf, on behalf of the people, and on behalf of all Judah, regarding the words of this scroll that has been found; for great is the LORD's wrath that has been kindled against us, because our fathers did not listen to the words of this scroll, to do according to everything written concerning us."

**22:14** So Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan, and Asaiah went to Huldah the prophetess, wife of Shallum son of Tikvah, son of Harhas, keeper of the garments—she was living in Jerusalem in the Second Quarter—and they spoke to her.

**22:15** She said to them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Say to the man who sent you to Me,

**22:16** 'This is what the LORD says: Look, I am bringing calamity upon this place and upon its inhabitants—all the words of the scroll that the king of Judah has read.

**22:17** Because they have forsaken Me and burned incense to other gods, so as to provoke Me to anger by all the work of their hands, My wrath will be kindled against this place, and it will not be extinguished.'

**22:18** But to the king of Judah, who sent you to inquire of the LORD, say this to him: 'This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: As for the words that you have heard,

**22:19** because your heart softened and you humbled yourself before the LORD when you heard what I spoke against this place and against its inhabitants—that they would become a desolation and a curse—and you tore your garments and wept before Me, I too have heard, declares the LORD.

**22:20** Therefore, look, I am gathering you to your fathers, and you will be gathered to your burial place in peace; and your eyes will not look upon all the calamity that I am bringing on this place.'" Then they brought word back to the king.

## Translator Notes

- **22:4** — The verb 'sum up' renders the Hiphil jussive of tamam ('make complete,' here to finish tallying or total the collected silver).
- **22:5** — Two ketiv/qere variants occur here: first, the qere reads weyittenuhu ('and let them give it,' third-person plural), while the ketiv writes w-y-t-n-h (singular or defective); second, the qere omits the preposition before 'the house of the LORD' (matching v. 9), whereas the ketiv reads 'in the house of the LORD.'
- **22:9** — The verb 'poured out' is the Hiphil perfect of natak ('pour out' or 'melt down'), referring to emptying the accumulated silver from the temple chest (or melting it for weighing) to pay the workers.
- **22:14** — 'The Second Quarter' translates ha-mishneh (literally 'the Second'), designating the newer expanded district of Jerusalem (cf. Zephaniah 1:10).
- **22:15** — Syntactically, 'to Me' stands inside the LORD's speech introduced by 'Thus says the LORD,' paralleling 'to inquire of the LORD' in v. 18, though some interpreters take it as Huldah speaking in her own voice ('to me').
