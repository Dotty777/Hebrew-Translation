## Text

**19:1** Now when King Hezekiah heard it, he tore his garments, covered himself in sackcloth, and went into the house of the LORD.

**19:2** He sent Eliakim, who was over the household, Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests, covered in sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz.

**19:3** They said to him, "This is what Hezekiah says: 'This day is a day of distress, rebuke, and contempt, for children have come to the opening of the womb, and there is no strength to give birth.

**19:4** Perhaps the LORD your God will hear all the words of Rabshakeh, whom the king of Assyria, his master, sent to taunt the living God, and will rebuke the words that the LORD your God has heard. So lift up a prayer on behalf of the remnant that remains.'"

**19:5** Then the servants of King Hezekiah came to Isaiah.

**19:6** Isaiah said to them, "This is what you shall say to your master: 'This is what the LORD says: Do not be afraid on account of the words you have heard, with which the attendants of the king of Assyria have reviled Me.

**19:7** Look, I am putting a spirit in him, so that he will hear a report and return to his own land, and I will cause him to fall by the sword in his own land.'"

**19:8** Then Rabshakeh turned back and found the king of Assyria waging war against Libnah, for he had heard that the king had broken camp from Lachish.

**19:9** When he heard concerning Tirhakah king of Cush, "Look, he has marched out to fight with you," he again sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,

**19:10** "Thus you shall speak to Hezekiah king of Judah, saying, 'Do not let your God in whom you trust deceive you by saying, "Jerusalem will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria."

**19:11** Look, you yourself have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, devoting them to destruction. And shall you be delivered?

**19:12** Did the gods of the nations that my fathers destroyed deliver them—Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the sons of Eden who were in Telassar?

**19:13** Where is the king of Hamath, the king of Arpad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, of Hena, and of Ivvah?'"

**19:14** Hezekiah took the letters from the hand of the messengers and read them; then he went up to the house of the LORD, and Hezekiah spread it out before the LORD.

**19:15** And Hezekiah prayed before the LORD and said, "LORD, God of Israel, enthroned upon the cherubim, You alone are God over all the kingdoms of the earth; You made the heavens and the earth.

**19:16** Incline Your ear, LORD, and hear; open Your eyes, LORD, and see. Hear the words of Sennacherib that he has sent to taunt the living God.

**19:17** Truly, LORD, the kings of Assyria have laid waste the nations and their land,

**19:18** and have cast their gods into the fire; for they were not gods, only the work of human hands, wood and stone, and so they destroyed them.

**19:19** Now therefore, LORD our God, please save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that You, LORD, are God alone."

**19:20** Then Isaiah son of Amoz sent word to Hezekiah, saying, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'I have heard what you prayed to Me concerning Sennacherib king of Assyria.'

**19:21** This is the word that the LORD has spoken concerning him: 'She has despised you, she has mocked you, the virgin daughter of Zion; she has shaken her head behind you, the daughter of Jerusalem.

**19:22** Whom have you taunted and reviled? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes on high? Against the Holy One of Israel!

**19:23** Through your messengers you have taunted the Lord, and you have said, "With my multitude of chariots I have gone up to the height of the mountains, to the farthest recesses of Lebanon; I will cut down its tallest cedars and the choicest of its cypresses, and I will enter its remotest lodging place, its lush forest.

**19:24** I dug and drank foreign waters, and with the sole of my feet I will dry up all the streams of Egypt."

**19:25** Have you not heard? Long ago I made it; from days of old I formed it. Now I have brought it to pass, that you should reduce fortified cities to desolate heaps of ruins,

**19:26** while their inhabitants, short of strength, were shattered and put to shame; they became plants of the field and green shoots of grass, grass on the rooftops, blighted before it reaches standing grain.

**19:27** Your sitting down, your going out, and your coming in I know, as well as your raging against Me.

**19:28** Because of your raging against Me, and because your insolence has risen to My ears, I will place My hook in your nose and My bit between your lips, and I will turn you back along the road by which you came.

**19:29** And this shall be the sign for you: eat self-sown growth this year, and in the second year what sprouts from that; then in the third year, sow and harvest, plant vineyards, and eat their fruit.

**19:30** And the escaped survivors of the house of Judah who remain shall again strike root downward and yield fruit upward.

**19:31** For from Jerusalem a remnant will go out, and survivors from Mount Zion. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will accomplish this.'

**19:32** Therefore thus says the LORD concerning the king of Assyria: 'He will not enter this city or shoot an arrow there; he will not confront it with a shield or pile up a siege ramp against it.

**19:33** By the road on which he came, he will return; and this city he will not enter, declares the LORD.

**19:34** For I will shield this city to save it, for My own sake and for the sake of My servant David.'"

**19:35** That night the angel of the LORD went out and struck down one hundred eighty-five thousand in the camp of Assyria. When people rose early in the morning, look, all of them were dead bodies.

**19:36** Then Sennacherib king of Assyria broke camp, departed, and returned, and he lived in Nineveh.

**19:37** And it happened, as he was bowing down in the house of Nisroch his god, that Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons struck him down with the sword, and they escaped to the land of Ararat. Then Esarhaddon his son became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **19:14** — Hebrew uses the plural noun hassepharim ("letters/documents") and plural object suffix in wayyiqra'em ("read them") for a single official dispatch, then shifts to the singular suffix in wayyiphresehu ("spread it out").
- **19:23** — Ketiv brkb ("with the chariotry of") vs. Qere berob ("with the multitude of"), which is followed here and matches Isaiah 37:24.
- **19:24** — Hebrew Masor is a poetic designation for Egypt (cognate with Akkadian Musur; cf. Isaiah 19:6; 37:25; Micah 7:12), paired with ye'ore ("Nile streams/channels"), though homonymous with the noun meaning "siege" or "fortress."
- **19:31** — In the Masoretic Text, seba'ot ("of hosts") is a Qere without Ketiv, read aloud in the Masoretic tradition though absent from the consonantal text (cf. Isaiah 37:32).
- **19:37** — In the Masoretic Text, banaw ("his sons") is a Qere without Ketiv, read according to the Masoretic tradition though absent from the consonantal text (cf. Isaiah 37:38).
