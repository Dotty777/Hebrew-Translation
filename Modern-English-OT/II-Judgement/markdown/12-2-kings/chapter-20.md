## Text

**20:1** In those days Hezekiah fell sick to the point of death. Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet came to him and said to him, "This is what the LORD says: 'Give instructions for your household, for you are about to die and will not live.'"

**20:2** Then he turned his face toward the wall and prayed to the LORD, saying,

**20:3** "Please, LORD, remember how I have walked before You in truth and with a whole heart, and have done what is good in Your eyes." And Hezekiah wept with great weeping.

**20:4** Before Isaiah had left the middle courtyard, the word of the LORD came to him, saying,

**20:5** "Turn back and say to Hezekiah, ruler of My people, 'This is what the LORD, the God of your father David, says: I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. Look, I am going to heal you; on the third day you shall go up to the house of the LORD.

**20:6** I will add fifteen years to your days, and I will deliver you and this city from the grasp of the king of Assyria; and I will shield this city for My own sake and for the sake of My servant David.'"

**20:7** Then Isaiah said, "Take a cake of pressed figs." So they took one and laid it on the boil, and he recovered.

**20:8** Now Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "What is the sign that the LORD will heal me, and that I shall go up to the house of the LORD on the third day?"

**20:9** Isaiah said, "This is the sign for you from the LORD, that the LORD will carry out the word He has spoken: shall the shadow go forward ten steps, or shall it turn back ten steps?"

**20:10** Hezekiah said, "It is a simple thing for the shadow to lengthen ten steps; no, rather let the shadow turn backward ten steps."

**20:11** Then Isaiah the prophet called out to the LORD, and He caused the shadow to return backward ten steps along the steps by which it had descended on the steps of Ahaz.

**20:12** At that time Berodach-baladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, for he had heard that Hezekiah had been sick.

**20:13** Hezekiah listened to them and showed them his entire treasure house—the silver, the gold, the spices, and the fine oil, along with his armory and all that was found in his storehouses. There was not a thing that Hezekiah did not show them in his house and in all his dominion.

**20:14** Then Isaiah the prophet came to King Hezekiah and said to him, "What did these men say, and from where have they come to you?" And Hezekiah said, "From a distant land they have come, from Babylon."

**20:15** He said, "What have they seen in your house?" And Hezekiah said, "All that is in my house they have seen; there was not a thing in my storehouses that I did not show them."

**20:16** Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the word of the LORD:

**20:17** 'Look, days are coming when everything in your house, and all that your fathers have stored up until this day, will be carried away to Babylon; not a thing will be left,' says the LORD.

**20:18** 'And some of your sons who will come forth from you, whom you will father, will be taken away, and they will become eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon.'"

**20:19** Then Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "Good is the word of the LORD that you have spoken." And he said, "Is it not so, if there will be peace and truth in my days?"

**20:20** As for the rest of the deeds of Hezekiah, all his might, and how he made the pool and the conduit and brought the water into the city, are they not written in the Book of the Annals of the Kings of Judah?

**20:21** Then Hezekiah lay down with his fathers, and Manasseh his son became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **20:4** — Ketiv h'yr ("the city") vs. Qere haser ("courtyard"), which is followed here ("the middle courtyard").
- **20:11** — The verb yaredah ("had descended") is feminine singular despite the masculine noun hassel ("the shadow"), likely pointing implicitly to the sun (hassemes, feminine; cf. Isaiah 38:8).
- **20:13** — The Masoretic Text reads wayyisma' ("listened," Qal of sm'), whereas Isaiah 39:2 has wayyismah ("rejoiced"); bet nekotoh ("his treasure house") reflects an Akkadian loanword (bit nakamti) with an archaic 3ms suffix.
- **20:18** — Ketiv yqh (3ms singular) vs. Qere yiqqahu (Qal imperfect 3mp in pause of lqh, with indefinite plural subject rendered passively as "will be taken away," cf. Isaiah 39:7).
- **20:19** — Hebrew halo' 'im ("is it not, if...") is elliptical, resuming tob ("good") from the preceding clause ("Is it not so/good, if...").
