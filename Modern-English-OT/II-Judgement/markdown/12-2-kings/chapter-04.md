## Text

**4:1** Now a woman from among the wives of the sons of the prophets cried out to Elisha, saying, "Your servant, my husband, has died, and you know that your servant was one who feared the LORD; yet the creditor has come to take my two children for himself as slaves."

**4:2** Elisha said to her, "What should I do for you? Tell me, what do you have in the house?" And she said, "Your maidservant has nothing at all in the house except a flask of oil."

**4:3** Then he said, "Go, borrow vessels for yourself from outside, from all your neighbors—empty vessels; do not gather only a few.

**4:4** Then go inside and shut the door behind you and behind your sons, and pour into all these vessels; and set aside each one that is full."

**4:5** So she departed from him and shut the door behind her and behind her sons; they were bringing them to her, and she kept pouring.

**4:6** When the vessels were filled, she said to her son, "Bring me another vessel." And he said to her, "There is not another vessel." Then the oil stopped.

**4:7** Then she came and told the man of God. And he said, "Go, sell the oil and repay your debt, and you and your sons may live on what is left over."

**4:8** Now one day it happened that Elisha crossed over to Shunem, where there was a prominent woman, and she pressed him to eat bread. So it came about that whenever he passed by, he would turn aside there to eat bread.

**4:9** She said to her husband, "Look now, I know that he is a holy man of God who passes by us continually.

**4:10** Please let us make a small walled upper room and place there for him a bed, a table, a chair, and a lampstand; then when he comes to us, he can turn aside there."

**4:11** One day he came there, and he turned aside into the upper room and lay down there.

**4:12** Then he said to Gehazi his servant, "Call this Shunammite woman." So he called her, and she stood before him.

**4:13** And he said to him, "Please say to her, 'Look, you have cared for us with all this anxious care; what is to be done for you? Is there anything to be spoken on your behalf to the king or to the commander of the army?'" And she said, "I live in the midst of my own people."

**4:14** So he said, "Then what is to be done for her?" And Gehazi said, "Indeed, she has no son, and her husband is old."

**4:15** He said, "Call her." So he called her, and she stood in the doorway.

**4:16** Then he said, "At this appointed time, when the season comes round again, you will be embracing a son." And she said, "No, my lord, man of God, do not deceive your maidservant!"

**4:17** And the woman conceived and gave birth to a son at that appointed time, when the season came round again, as Elisha had spoken to her.

**4:18** The child grew, and one day he went out to his father, to the reapers.

**4:19** He said to his father, "My head, my head!" And he said to the servant, "Carry him to his mother."

**4:20** So he carried him and brought him to his mother, and he sat on her knees until noon, and then died.

**4:21** She went up and laid him on the bed of the man of God, shut the door behind him, and went out.

**4:22** Then she called to her husband and said, "Please send me one of the servants and one of the female donkeys, so that I may run to the man of God and return."

**4:23** He said, "Why are you going to him today? It is neither new moon nor Sabbath." And she said, "All is well."

**4:24** Then she saddled the female donkey and said to her servant, "Drive on and go; do not hold back my riding unless I tell you."

**4:25** So she went and came to the man of God at Mount Carmel. When the man of God saw her at a distance, he said to Gehazi his servant, "Look, there is that Shunammite woman!

**4:26** Now please run to meet her and say to her, 'Is all well with you? Is all well with your husband? Is all well with the child?'" And she said, "All is well."

**4:27** When she came to the man of God at the mountain, she grasped his feet. Gehazi came near to push her away, but the man of God said, "Leave her alone, for her soul is bitter within her, and the LORD has concealed it from me and has not told me."

**4:28** Then she said, "Did I ask for a son from my lord? Did I not say, 'Do not mislead me'?"

**4:29** Then he said to Gehazi, "Belt your waist, take my staff in your hand, and go. If you meet anyone, do not greet him, and if anyone greets you, do not answer him; and lay my staff on the boy's face."

**4:30** But the boy's mother said, "As the LORD lives and as your soul lives, I will not leave you!" So he rose and went after her.

**4:31** Now Gehazi had gone on ahead of them and laid the staff on the boy's face, but there was no sound and no response. So he returned to meet him and told him, saying, "The boy has not awakened."

**4:32** When Elisha came into the house, there was the boy, dead, laid upon his bed.

**4:33** So he went in, shut the door behind the two of them, and prayed to the LORD.

**4:34** Then he climbed up and lay upon the child, and put his mouth on his mouth, his eyes on his eyes, and his palms on his palms; and he bent over him, and the flesh of the child became warm.

**4:35** Then he turned back and walked in the house, once this way and once that way, and climbed up and bent over him. And the boy sneezed seven times, and the boy opened his eyes.

**4:36** Then he called to Gehazi and said, "Call this Shunammite woman." So he called her, and she came to him. And he said, "Pick up your son."

**4:37** She came in, fell at his feet, and bowed down to the ground; then she picked up her son and went out.

**4:38** Now Elisha returned to Gilgal, and there was a famine in the land. While the sons of the prophets were sitting before him, he said to his servant, "Put on the large pot and boil stew for the sons of the prophets."

**4:39** Then one of them went out into the field to gather herbs, and found a wild vine and gathered from it wild gourds, a garment full; and he came and sliced them into the pot of stew, because they did not know them.

**4:40** Then they poured it out for the men to eat. And as they were eating from the stew, they cried out and said, "There is death in the pot, man of God!" And they could not eat it.

**4:41** But he said, "Then bring flour." And he threw it into the pot and said, "Pour it out for the people that they may eat." And there was nothing harmful in the pot.

**4:42** Now a man came from Baal-shalishah and brought to the man of God bread of the firstfruits, twenty loaves of barley and fresh ears of grain in his sack. And he said, "Give it to the people that they may eat."

**4:43** But his attendant said, "How can I set this before a hundred men?" Yet he said, "Give it to the people that they may eat, for this is what the LORD says, 'They shall eat and have some left over.'"

**4:44** So he set it before them, and they ate and had some left over, according to the word of the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — The ketiv preserves the archaic second-person feminine singular pronominal suffix -ki (also in vv. 3 and 7, along with archaic feminine singular pronoun and verb forms in vv. 16 and 23), while the qere reads the standard Masoretic form; the translation follows the qere. The rare noun translated 'flask' (asuk) is a hapax legomenon derived from the root 'to anoint.'
- **4:5** — For 'kept pouring,' the ketiv writes a I-yod Hiphil feminine participle while the qere reads the pausal I-waw Hiphil feminine participle (motsaqet); both have the same active meaning.
- **4:13** — Literally 'you have trembled toward us with all this trembling,' a Qal verb with its cognate noun expressing attentive, anxious care.
- **4:16** — The Hebrew idiom literally means 'according to a living time,' referring to the return of the same season in the following year (cf. Gen 18:10, 14).
- **4:42** — The noun translated 'sack' (tsiqelon) is a hapax legomenon, traditionally understood as a bag or pouch, though some modern scholars interpret it as a husk or sheath.
