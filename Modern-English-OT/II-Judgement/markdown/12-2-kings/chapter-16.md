## Text

**16:1** In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham, king of Judah, began his reign.

**16:2** Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem; he did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD his God, like David his father.

**16:3** Instead, he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, and he even caused his son to pass through the fire, following the abominations of the nations whom the LORD had dispossessed from before the sons of Israel.

**16:4** He sacrificed and burned incense at the high places, on the hills, and under every lush tree.

**16:5** Then Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, came up to Jerusalem for battle. They laid siege to Ahaz, yet they were not able to do battle.

**16:6** At that time Rezin king of Aram restored Elath to Aram and cleared the Judeans out of Eloth; and Edomites came to Elath and have lived there to this day.

**16:7** Then Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, saying, "I am your servant and your son. Come up and save me from the grasp of the king of Aram and from the grasp of the king of Israel, who are rising up against me."

**16:8** Ahaz took the silver and the gold found in the house of the LORD and in the treasuries of the king's house, and he sent a bribe to the king of Assyria.

**16:9** So the king of Assyria listened to him; the king of Assyria went up against Damascus, seized it, carried it into exile to Kir, and put Rezin to death.

**16:10** Then King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, and he saw the altar that was in Damascus. King Ahaz sent to Uriah the priest the likeness of the altar and its design, for all its workmanship.

**16:11** So Uriah the priest built the altar; according to everything King Ahaz had sent from Damascus, thus Uriah the priest did before King Ahaz came from Damascus.

**16:12** When the king came from Damascus, the king saw the altar; then the king approached the altar and went up onto it.

**16:13** He offered up in smoke his burnt offering and his grain offering, poured out his drink offering, and dashed the blood of the peace offerings that were his upon the altar.

**16:14** As for the bronze altar that was before the LORD, he moved it from the front of the house, from between the altar and the house of the LORD, and placed it on the north side of the altar.

**16:15** Then King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, saying, "On the great altar offer up in smoke the morning burnt offering and the evening grain offering, the king's burnt offering and his grain offering, and the burnt offering of all the people of the land, and their grain offering and their drink offerings; and on it you are to dash all the blood of a burnt offering and all the blood of a sacrifice. But the bronze altar will be mine for inquiry."

**16:16** So Uriah the priest did in accordance with all that King Ahaz had commanded.

**16:17** King Ahaz cut off the side panels of the stands and removed the basin from them; he also took down the sea from the bronze oxen that were under it and set it on a stone pavement.

**16:18** And the covered structure of the Sabbath that they had built in the house, and the king's outer entrance, he shifted at the house of the LORD because of the king of Assyria.

**16:19** As for the rest of the deeds of Ahaz that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**16:20** Then Ahaz lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of David; and his son Hezekiah became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **16:6** — Ketiv reads "and Arameans" (w'rmym), while Qere reads "and Edomites" (wa'edomim), which is followed here. Earlier in the verse the Masoretic Text reads "to Aram," reflecting the graphical similarity of dalet and resh in Hebrew.
- **16:14** — The verb rendered "moved" (wayyaqrev) is the Hiphil of q-r-v, literally "brought near" or "brought forward," resuming the fronted direct object ("the bronze altar") to describe relocating it from the temple front to the north side of the new altar.
- **16:15** — Qere reads the Piel wayyiqtol waytsawweh ("commanded") without an object suffix, whereas Ketiv adds a proleptic third-person masculine singular suffix ("commanded him"). The Piel infinitive levaqqer ("for inquiry") denotes examining, deliberating, or seeking an oracle.
- **16:18** — Qere reads musakh (Ketiv mysk), a hapax legomenon generally derived from s-k-kh ("cover") meaning a covered structure or canopy. The Hiphil verb hesev (from s-v-v, "turn aside, shift, divert") with "house of the LORD" as an adverbial accusative indicates that Ahaz relocated or altered these structures at the temple.
