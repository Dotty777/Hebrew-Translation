## Text

**15:1** The lot for the tribe of the sons of Judah, by their clans, extended to the boundary of Edom, to the wilderness of Zin southward, at the far end of the south.

**15:2** Their southern boundary was from the end of the Salt Sea, from the inlet that faces southward.

**15:3** It went out to the south of the Ascent of Akrabbim, passed on to Zin, and went up south of Kadesh-barnea; then it passed on to Hezron, went up to Addar, and turned around to Karka.

**15:4** It passed on to Azmon and went out to the Brook of Egypt, and the endpoints of the boundary were at the sea. This will be your southern boundary.

**15:5** The eastern boundary was the Salt Sea as far as the end of the Jordan. And the boundary for the northern side was from the inlet of the sea, at the end of the Jordan.

**15:6** Then the boundary went up to Beth-hoglah and passed north of Beth-arabah, and the boundary went up to the stone of Bohan son of Reuben.

**15:7** The boundary went up to Debir from the Valley of Achor, turning northward toward Gilgal, which is opposite the Ascent of Adummim, which lies south of the brook. Then the boundary passed on to the waters of En-shemesh, and its endpoints were at En-rogel.

**15:8** Then the boundary went up the Valley of the son of Hinnom to the southern flank of the Jebusite, that is, Jerusalem. And the boundary went up to the summit of the mountain that faces the Valley of Hinnom on the west, which is at the northern end of the Valley of Rephaim.

**15:9** Then the boundary curved from the summit of the mountain to the spring of the waters of Nephtoah and went out to the cities of Mount Ephron; and the boundary curved to Baalah, that is, Kiriath-jearim.

**15:10** Then the boundary turned around from Baalah westward to Mount Seir, passed on to the northern flank of Mount Jearim, that is, Chesalon, went down to Beth-shemesh, and passed on to Timnah.

**15:11** The boundary went out to the northern flank of Ekron, and the boundary curved to Shikkeron, passed on to Mount Baalah, and went out to Jabneel; and the endpoints of the boundary were at the sea.

**15:12** The western boundary was the Great Sea and its coastline. This is the boundary of the sons of Judah all around, by their clans.

**15:13** To Caleb son of Jephunneh he gave a portion among the sons of Judah, at the command of the LORD to Joshua: Kiriath-arba—Arba was the father of Anak—that is, Hebron.

**15:14** From there Caleb dispossessed the three sons of Anak: Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, the offspring of Anak.

**15:15** From there he went up against the residents of Debir; previously the name of Debir was Kiriath-sepher.

**15:16** Caleb said, "Anyone who strikes Kiriath-sepher and captures it, I will give him my daughter Achsah as a wife."

**15:17** Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's brother, captured it, and he gave him his daughter Achsah as a wife.

**15:18** Now when she came, she urged him to ask her father for a field. Then she dismounted from the donkey, and Caleb said to her, "What do you want?"

**15:19** She said, "Give me a blessing: for you have given me the land of the Negev, so give me springs of water." And he gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.

**15:20** This is the inheritance of the tribe of the sons of Judah, by their clans.

## Translator Notes

- **15:4** — The Qere reads plural 'were' (agreeing with plural 'endpoints', as in verses 7 and 11), whereas the Ketiv has singular 'was'. The Hebrew also shifts unexpectedly to second-person plural ('your') at the close of the verse, echoing the border formula in Numbers 34.
- **15:12** — Literally 'the Great Sea and border', an established Hebrew geographical phrase (cf. Numbers 34:6) referring to the Mediterranean Sea together with its shoreline as the western boundary.
- **15:18** — The rare Qal verb translated 'dismounted' occurs only here, in the parallel account in Judges 1:14, and in Judges 4:21 (of a tent peg sinking into the ground); with 'from upon the donkey' it is traditionally understood as sliding down or dismounting.
- **15:32** — The Masoretic summary gives a total of twenty-nine cities, whereas thirty-six city names appear in verses 21-32 (treating Hazor-hadattah and Kerioth-hezron as compound names). Similar numerical discrepancies occur in other town lists in Joshua, possibly reflecting later administrative additions or towns subsequently allotted to Simeon.
- **15:45** — Hebrew literally reads 'its daughters', a geographical idiom for surrounding satellite towns dependent on a major city, as distinct from unwalled 'villages' (also in verse 47).
- **15:47** — The Qere reads 'the Great' ('the Great Sea and its coastline', matching verse 12), whereas the Ketiv reads 'the border', likely an accidental anticipation of the following word.
- **15:53** — The Qere reads the place name 'Janum', whereas the Ketiv reads 'Janim'.
- **15:63** — The Qere reads the Qal perfect of the verb 'be able', whereas the Ketiv reads the Qal imperfect form; with the negation both convey 'were unable' in this past-tense clause.
