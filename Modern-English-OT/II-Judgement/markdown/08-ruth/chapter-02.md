## Text

**2:1** Now Naomi had a relative of her husband, a prominent man of worth from the clan of Elimelech, and his name was Boaz.

**2:2** Ruth the Moabite said to Naomi, "Please let me go to the field and glean among the heads of grain behind one in whose eyes I find favor." And she said to her, "Go, my daughter."

**2:3** So she went and arrived and gleaned in the field behind the reapers; and by chance she happened upon the portion of the field belonging to Boaz, who was from the clan of Elimelech.

**2:4** Now look, Boaz arrived from Bethlehem, and he said to the reapers, "The LORD be with you." They replied to him, "May the LORD bless you."

**2:5** Then Boaz said to his young man who was set over the reapers, "To whom does this young woman belong?"

**2:6** The young man set over the reapers answered and said, "She is a Moabite young woman, the one who returned with Naomi from the fields of Moab.

**2:7** She said, 'Please let me glean and gather among the sheaves behind the reapers.' So she came and has remained standing from the morning until now; this sitting of hers in the shelter has been only brief."

**2:8** Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Have you not heard, my daughter? Do not go to glean in another field, and do not leave from here either, but stay closely here with my young women.

**2:9** Keep your eyes on the field that they are reaping, and walk behind them. Have I not commanded the young men not to touch you? And whenever you are thirsty, go to the vessels and drink from what the young men draw."

**2:10** Then she fell on her face and bowed down to the ground, and she said to him, "Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should take notice of me, when I am a foreigner?"

**2:11** Boaz answered and said to her, "It has been fully reported to me everything that you have done for your mother-in-law after the death of your husband, and how you left your father and your mother and the land of your birth and came to a people whom you had not known previously.

**2:12** May the LORD repay your deed, and may your reward be complete from the LORD, the God of Israel, beneath whose wings you have come to take refuge."

**2:13** She said, "May I find favor in your eyes, my lord, because you have comforted me and spoken to the heart of your servant girl, even though I am not like one of your servant girls."

**2:14** At mealtime Boaz said to her, "Come over here, eat from the bread, and dip your morsel in the vinegar." So she sat beside the reapers, and he passed her roasted grain; she ate, was satisfied, and had some left over.

**2:15** When she rose to glean, Boaz commanded his young men, saying, "Let her glean even among the sheaves, and do not humiliate her.

**2:16** Furthermore, be sure to pull out some for her from the bundles and leave it so that she may glean it, and do not rebuke her."

**2:17** So she gleaned in the field until evening. Then she beat out what she had gleaned, and it came to about an ephah of barley.

**2:18** She lifted it up and went into the city, and her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned. She also brought out and gave to her what she had left over after being satisfied.

**2:19** Her mother-in-law asked her, "Where did you glean today, and where did you work? May the one who took notice of you be blessed." So she told her mother-in-law with whom she had worked, and she said, "The name of the man with whom I worked today is Boaz."

**2:20** Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, "May he be blessed by the LORD, who has not forsaken His loyal kindness toward the living and toward the dead." Then Naomi said to her, "The man is a close relative of ours; he is one of our redeemers."

**2:21** Then Ruth the Moabite said, "Furthermore, he even told me, 'Stay closely with my young men until they have finished all of my harvest.'"

**2:22** Naomi said to Ruth, her daughter-in-law, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women, so that no one accosts you in another field."

**2:23** So she stayed closely with Boaz's young women to glean until the completion of the barley harvest and the wheat harvest, and she lived with her mother-in-law.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The ketiv reads meyuda' or mida' (an acquaintance), while the qere reads moda' ("a relative"); the phrase "man of worth" uses chayil, the same term Boaz later applies to Ruth ("woman of worth," 3:11).
- **2:7** — The final clause of the Hebrew text literally reads "this is her sitting in the house a little," a concise expression understood as referring to a brief rest in the field shelter.
- **2:10** — Ruth creates a wordplay between the Hiphil infinitive lehakkireni ("take notice of me") and nokhriyyah ("foreigner"), both derived from the Hebrew root nkr.
- **2:20** — The pronoun "His" follows the nearest grammatical antecedent, the LORD (compare Gen 24:27), though the Hebrew clause could also refer to Boaz; "redeemer" translates Hebrew go'el, a close kinsman responsible for protecting a family's property and line.
