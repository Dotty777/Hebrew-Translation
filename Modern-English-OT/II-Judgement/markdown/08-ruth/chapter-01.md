## Text

**1:1** Now it happened in the days when the judges judged that there was a famine in the land, and a man went from Bethlehem of Judah to reside as a foreigner in the fields of Moab—he, his wife, and his two sons.

**1:2** The man's name was Elimelech, his wife's name was Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion—Ephrathites from Bethlehem of Judah. They came to the fields of Moab and stayed there.

**1:3** Then Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left—she and her two sons.

**1:4** They took Moabite wives for themselves; the name of one was Orpah, and the name of the second was Ruth. They lived there for about ten years.

**1:5** Then both of them also died, Mahlon and Chilion, and the woman was left without her two children and without her husband.

**1:6** Then she rose, she and her daughters-in-law, and turned back from the fields of Moab, because she had heard in the countryside of Moab that the LORD had attended to His people by giving them bread.

**1:7** So she went out from the place where she had been, and her two daughters-in-law were with her, and they walked on the road to return to the land of Judah.

**1:8** Then Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go, return, each woman to her mother's house. May the LORD deal in loyal kindness with you, just as you have dealt with the dead and with me.

**1:9** May the LORD grant you that you find a place of rest, each woman in the house of her husband." Then she kissed them, and they raised their voices and wept.

**1:10** And they said to her, "No, with you we will return to your people."

**1:11** But Naomi said, "Return, my daughters. Why should you go with me? Do I still have sons in my womb who could become husbands for you?

**1:12** Return, my daughters, go, for I am too old to belong to a husband. Even if I said, 'I have hope'—even if I belonged to a husband tonight and even bore sons—

**1:13** would you wait expectantly for them until they grew up? Would you hold yourselves back for them from belonging to a husband? No, my daughters, for it is far more bitter for me than for you, because the hand of the LORD has gone out against me."

**1:14** Then they raised their voices and wept again. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her.

**1:15** She said, "Look, your sister-in-law has returned to her people and to her gods. Return after your sister-in-law."

**1:16** But Ruth replied, "Do not press me to abandon you, to turn back from following you. For to wherever you go, I will go, and wherever you spend the night, I will spend the night. Your people are my people, and your God is my God.

**1:17** Wherever you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD do thus to me, and thus add to it, if anything other than death separates me from you."

**1:18** When she saw that she was firmly resolved to go with her, she stopped speaking to her.

**1:19** So the two of them walked on until they reached Bethlehem. And it happened when they arrived in Bethlehem that the entire city was stirred up over them, and the women asked, "Is this Naomi?"

**1:20** She replied to them, "Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, because the Almighty has made it exceedingly bitter for me.

**1:21** I left full, yet the LORD has brought me back empty-handed. Why do you call me Naomi, while the LORD has testified against me and the Almighty has brought harm upon me?"

**1:22** So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, with her—the one who returned from the fields of Moab. And they arrived in Bethlehem at the start of the barley harvest.

## Translator Notes

- **1:8** — The ketiv writes the full imperfect form ya'aseh, while the qere reads the shortened jussive form ya'as ("May the LORD deal").
- **1:13** — The Niphal verb te'agenah ("hold yourselves back") occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible; the phrase mar-li me'od mikkem can mean either "far more bitter for me than for you" (comparative min) or "exceedingly bitter for me on your account" (causal min).
- **1:17** — The oath clause literally reads "if death separates between me and you," an elliptical Hebrew oath idiom standardly understood as "if anything other than death" (though some render "if even death").
- **1:20** — Naomi plays on the meanings of the names: Naomi (from a root meaning "pleasant") versus Mara ("bitter"), echoed by the Hiphil verb hemar ("has made it bitter").
