## Text

**3:1** Then Naomi, her mother-in-law, said to her, "My daughter, should I not seek a place of rest for you, that it may go well for you?

**3:2** Now, is not Boaz our relative, whose young women you have been with? Look, he is winnowing barley at the threshing floor tonight.

**3:3** Therefore wash yourself, anoint yourself, put on your garments, and go down to the threshing floor. Do not let yourself be known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking.

**3:4** And when he lies down, note the place where he lies; then go in, uncover his feet, and lie down. He himself will tell you what you should do."

**3:5** She answered her, "Everything that you say to me, I will do."

**3:6** So she went down to the threshing floor and did according to all that her mother-in-law had commanded her.

**3:7** When Boaz had eaten and drunk and his heart was glad, he went to lie down at the edge of the grain heap. Then she came quietly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.

**3:8** And it happened in the middle of the night that the man was startled and turned over, and look, a woman was lying at his feet.

**3:9** He asked, "Who are you?" She replied, "I am Ruth, your maidservant. Spread your wing over your maidservant, for you are a redeemer."

**3:10** Then he said, "May you be blessed by the LORD, my daughter. You have made your later loyal love even better than the first, by not going after the young men, whether poor or rich.

**3:11** And now, my daughter, do not fear. Everything that you say I will do for you, for the entire gate of my people knows that you are a woman of worth.

**3:12** Now, it is indeed true that I am a redeemer, yet there is also a redeemer who is closer than I.

**3:13** Stay the night, and in the morning, if he will redeem you, good—let him redeem you. But if he does not desire to redeem you, then I myself will redeem you, as the LORD lives! Lie down until the morning."

**3:14** So she lay at his feet until the morning, yet she rose before one person could recognize another. And he said, "Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."

**3:15** Then he said, "Bring the shawl that is on you and hold it." So she held it, and he measured out six measures of barley and placed it upon her; then he went into the city.

**3:16** When she came to her mother-in-law, she asked, "Who are you, my daughter?" Then she told her everything that the man had done for her.

**3:17** And she said, "He gave me these six measures of barley, for he said to me, 'Do not go empty-handed to your mother-in-law.'"

**3:18** Then she said, "Sit still, my daughter, until you know how the matter falls out, for the man will not be at rest unless he finishes the matter today."

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — Two ketiv/qere variants occur here: ketiv singular "your garment" versus qere plural "your garments" (simlotayikh), and ketiv weyaradti (preserving an archaic second-person feminine ending -ti) versus qere weyaradt ("and go down"); the same archaic ending appears in 3:4 (ketiv weshakhavti, qere weshakhavt, "and lie down").
- **3:5** — The words "to me" (elay) are qere wela ketiv, read in the Masoretic vocalization tradition though absent from the consonantal text (the same occurs in 3:17).
- **3:9** — Hebrew kanaf ("wing") also denotes the corner or edge of a garment; Ruth's request echoes Boaz's blessing in 2:12 ("beneath whose wings") while invoking a symbolic gesture of protective marriage.
- **3:12** — The particle im ("if") is ketiv wela qere, written in the consonantal text but omitted in the Masoretic reading tradition.
- **3:15** — Hebrew literally reads "six of barley," leaving the unit of measurement unstated, and the final verb wayyavo ("and he went") is masculine singular in the Masoretic Text, referring to Boaz.
- **3:17** — The words "to me" (elay) are Qere without Ketiv, read in the Masoretic vocalization tradition though absent from the written consonants (as in 3:5).
