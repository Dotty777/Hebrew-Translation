## Text

**14:1** Then Samson went down to Timnah, and he saw a woman in Timnah from the daughters of the Philistines.

**14:2** So he went up and told his father and his mother, saying, "I saw a woman in Timnah from the daughters of the Philistines; so now, get her for me as a wife."

**14:3** Then his father and his mother said to him, "Is there no woman among the daughters of your relatives, or among all my people, that you are going to take a wife from the uncircumcised Philistines?" But Samson said to his father, "Get her for me, because she is right in my eyes."

**14:4** Yet his father and his mother did not know that this was from the LORD, because He was seeking a pretext against the Philistines; and at that time the Philistines were ruling over Israel.

**14:5** Then Samson and his father and his mother went down to Timnah, and they came as far as the vineyards of Timnah; and look, a young lion came roaring toward him.

**14:6** And the Spirit of the LORD surged upon him, and he tore it apart as one tears apart a young goat, though there was nothing in his hand. Yet he did not tell his father or his mother what he had done.

**14:7** Then he went down and spoke to the woman, and she was right in the eyes of Samson.

**14:8** After some days he returned to take her, and he turned aside to look at the carcass of the lion; and look, a swarm of bees was in the body of the lion, along with honey.

**14:9** So he scraped it out into his palms and went along, eating as he walked. Then he went to his father and his mother and gave some to them, and they ate; yet he did not tell them that he had scraped the honey out from the body of the lion.

**14:10** Then his father went down to the woman, and Samson made a feast there, because that is what the young men used to do.

**14:11** And it happened that when they saw him, they brought thirty companions, and they were with him.

**14:12** Then Samson said to them, "Let me now pose a riddle to you: if you surely tell it to me within the seven days of the feast and find it out, then I will give you thirty linen garments and thirty changes of clothing.

**14:13** But if you are unable to tell me, then you yourselves shall give me thirty linen garments and thirty changes of clothing." And they said to him, "Pose your riddle, that we may hear it."

**14:14** So he said to them, "From the eater came forth food, and from the strong came forth what is sweet." And for three days they were unable to tell the riddle.

**14:15** And it happened on the seventh day that they said to Samson's wife, "Entice your husband so that he tells us the riddle, or else we will burn you and your father's house with fire. Was it to impoverish us that you invited us? Is it not so?"

**14:16** Then Samson's wife wept before him and said, "You only hate me and do not love me! You have posed a riddle to the sons of my people, yet you have not told it to me." And he said to her, "Look, I have not told my father or my mother, so should I tell you?"

**14:17** Yet she wept before him during the seven days that their feast lasted. And it happened on the seventh day that he told her, because she pressed him hard; then she told the riddle to the sons of her people.

**14:18** So on the seventh day, before the sun went down, the men of the city said to him, "What is sweeter than honey? And what is stronger than a lion?" And he said to them, "Had you not plowed with my heifer, you would not have found my riddle."

**14:19** Then the Spirit of the LORD surged upon him, and he went down to Ashkelon and struck down thirty men from among them, and took their spoil and gave the changes of clothing to those who had told the riddle. And his anger burned, and he went up to his father's house.

**14:20** And Samson's wife came to belong to his companion who had acted as his friend.

## Translator Notes

- **14:4** — The noun to'anah ('pretext' or 'occasion' for confrontation) occurs only here in Biblical Hebrew, and the pronoun 'He' refers back to the LORD.
- **14:9** — The verb radah ('scraped out') is a homonym distinct from the verb 'to rule' and appears only in this verse in Biblical Hebrew.
- **14:15** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads 'on the seventh day' (unlike the Septuagint and Syriac, which read 'on the fourth day'); the final interrogative particle halo ('Is it not so?') is likewise translated as preserved in the Masoretic text.
- **14:18** — Hebrew hacharsah ('the sun') uses the rare poetic noun cheres ('sun') with a poetic or archaic -ah ending.
- **14:20** — The verb re'ah ('acted as his friend') is a rare Piel form cognate with mere'ehu ('his companion'), denoting the groom's special attendant or best man.
