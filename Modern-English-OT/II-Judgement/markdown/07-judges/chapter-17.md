## Text

**17:1** Now there was a man from the hill country of Ephraim, and his name was Micah.

**17:2** He said to his mother, "The eleven hundred pieces of silver that were taken from you, over which you uttered a curse and also spoke in my ears—look, the silver is with me; I myself took it." And his mother said, "Blessed be my son by the LORD."

**17:3** So he returned the eleven hundred pieces of silver to his mother, and his mother said, "I wholly dedicate the silver to the LORD from my hand for my son, to make a carved image and a cast image; so now I will return it to you."

**17:4** So when he returned the silver to his mother, his mother took two hundred pieces of silver and gave them to the silversmith, and he made them into a carved image and a cast image, and it was in the house of Micah.

**17:5** Now the man Micah had a house of gods, and he made an ephod and teraphim, and he installed one of his sons, and he became his priest.

**17:6** In those days there was no king in Israel; each man would do what was right in his own eyes.

**17:7** Now there was a young man from Bethlehem in Judah, of the clan of Judah, who was a Levite and was sojourning there.

**17:8** Then the man departed from the city, from Bethlehem in Judah, to sojourn wherever he could find a place; and he came to the hill country of Ephraim, to the house of Micah, as he made his way.

**17:9** Micah asked him, "Where do you come from?" He answered him, "I am a Levite from Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going to sojourn wherever I can find a place."

**17:10** Then Micah said to him, "Stay with me and be a father and a priest to me, and I will give you ten pieces of silver a year, a set of garments, and your sustenance." So the Levite went in.

**17:11** The Levite agreed to stay with the man, and the young man became to him like one of his sons.

**17:12** So Micah installed the Levite, and the young man became his priest and remained in the house of Micah.

**17:13** Then Micah said, "Now I know that the LORD will do good to me, because the Levite has become my priest."

## Translator Notes

- **17:1** — Verses 1 and 4 spell the name in its fuller Masoretic form Mikayehu ("Who is like the LORD?"), while vv. 5-13 use the shortened form Mikah ("Micah").
- **17:2** — Qere reads the standard second-person feminine singular pronoun at ("you"), whereas Ketiv preserves the archaic spelling atti.
- **17:4** — The paired terms "a carved image and a cast image" (pesel u-massekhah) take a singular verb ("it was"), pointing either to a single composite metal-plated wooden idol or to grammatical agreement with the collective "silver."
- **17:5** — Hebrew beit elohim ("a house of gods," or "a house of God") denotes a private shrine, and "installed" translates the priestly ordination idiom mille et-yad, literally "filled the hand of" (also in v. 12).
