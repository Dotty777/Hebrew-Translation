## Text

**3:1** These are the nations that the LORD left behind to test Israel through them, all those who had not known any of the wars of Canaan,

**3:2** only so that the generations of the sons of Israel might know, to instruct them in war—at least those who previously had not known them:

**3:3** the five rulers of the Philistines, and all the Canaanites, and the Sidonians, and the Hivites who inhabited Mount Lebanon, from Mount Baal-hermon as far as Lebo-hamath.

**3:4** They were there to test Israel through them, to know whether they would obey the commands of the LORD that He had commanded their fathers by the hand of Moses.

**3:5** And the sons of Israel lived in the midst of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites.

**3:6** They took their daughters as wives for themselves, their own daughters they gave to their sons, and they served their gods.

**3:7** So the sons of Israel did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD; they forgot the LORD their God, and they served the Baals and the Asherahs.

**3:8** Then the LORD's anger burned against Israel, and He sold them into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram-naharaim; and the sons of Israel served Cushan-rishathaim for eight years.

**3:9** Then the sons of Israel cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up a deliverer for the sons of Israel, and he delivered them: Othniel son of Kenaz, the younger brother of Caleb.

**3:10** The Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he judged Israel. He went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram into his hand, and his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim.

**3:11** So the land was quiet for forty years, and Othniel son of Kenaz died.

**3:12** Then the sons of Israel again did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, so the LORD strengthened Eglon king of Moab against Israel because they had done what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**3:13** He gathered the sons of Ammon and Amalek to himself, went and struck Israel, and they took possession of the City of Palms.

**3:14** So the sons of Israel served Eglon king of Moab for eighteen years.

**3:15** Then the sons of Israel cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up a deliverer for them: Ehud son of Gera, the Benjaminite, a left-handed man. And the sons of Israel sent tribute by his hand to Eglon king of Moab.

**3:16** Ehud made for himself a sword having two edges, a short cubit long, and he strapped it beneath his garments on his right thigh.

**3:17** He presented the tribute to Eglon king of Moab. Now Eglon was a very fat man.

**3:18** When he finished presenting the tribute, he dismissed the people who had carried the tribute.

**3:19** But he himself turned back from the carved images near Gilgal and said, "I have a secret word for you, King." He said, "Silence!" And all those standing in attendance on him went out from him.

**3:20** Then Ehud came to him as he was sitting in the cool upper chamber that was his alone. And Ehud said, "I have a word from God for you." So he rose from the seat.

**3:21** Then Ehud extended his left hand, took the sword from his right thigh, and drove it into his belly.

**3:22** Even the hilt went in after the blade, and the fat closed over the blade, for he did not pull the sword out of his belly; and the excrement came out.

**3:23** Then Ehud went out into the vestibule, shut the doors of the upper chamber behind him, and locked them.

**3:24** After he had gone out, his servants came and looked, and see, the doors of the upper chamber were locked. So they said, "Surely he is only relieving himself in the inner room of the cool chamber."

**3:25** They waited to the point of embarrassment, and look, he was not opening the doors of the upper chamber. So they took the key and opened them, and there was their master, fallen on the ground, dead.

**3:26** Meanwhile, Ehud had escaped while they lingered; he passed beyond the carved images and escaped to Seirah.

**3:27** When he arrived, he blew the ram's horn in the hill country of Ephraim; and the sons of Israel went down with him from the hill country, and he was in front of them.

**3:28** He said to them, "Pursue after me, for the LORD has given your enemies, Moab, into your hand." So they went down after him and captured the fords of the Jordan toward Moab, and they did not allow anyone to cross over.

**3:29** They struck down Moab at that time, about ten thousand men, every one stout and every one a man of valor, and not a man escaped.

**3:30** So Moab was subdued on that day under the hand of Israel, and the land was quiet for eighty years.

**3:31** After him came Shamgar son of Anath, and he struck down six hundred men of the Philistines with an oxgoad; and he also delivered Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — The Hebrew syntax is compact: the Qal infinitive construct da'at ('knowing') is clarified by the Piel infinitive construct lelammedam ('to instruct them'). The masculine plural suffix on yeda'um ('had known them') refers back to the wars of Canaan in verse 1.
- **3:15** — Literally 'a man restricted in his right hand' (itter yad-yemino), an idiom for being left-handed that creates irony with his tribal designation 'Benjaminite' (ben-hayemini, literally 'son of the right-hander').
- **3:16** — Hebrew gomed occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and refers to a short cubit, roughly the length of a forearm to the knuckles.
- **3:22** — Hebrew happarshedonah occurs only here and its meaning is uncertain; traditionally linked to peresh ('excrement/dung'), though its penultimate stress resembles a directional -ah ending (as in v. 23), which could indicate 'out at the rear' or a part of the chamber.
- **3:24** — Literally 'covering his feet' (mesik et-raglaw), a Hebrew euphemism for relieving oneself (cf. 1 Samuel 24:3).
