## Text

**19:1** Now it happened in those days, when there was no king in Israel, that there was a Levite man sojourning in the remote parts of the hill country of Ephraim, who took a concubine for himself from Bethlehem in Judah.

**19:2** But his concubine acted unfaithfully against him, and went away from him to her father's house, to Bethlehem in Judah; and she was there for a period of four months.

**19:3** Then her husband arose and went after her to speak to her heart, to bring her back; his attendant was with him, along with a pair of donkeys. She brought him into her father's house, and when the father of the young woman saw him, he rejoiced to meet him.

**19:4** His father-in-law, the father of the young woman, detained him, so he stayed with him three days; they ate and drank and spent the night there.

**19:5** On the fourth day they got up early in the morning, and he arose to go; but the father of the young woman said to his son-in-law, "Sustain your heart with a piece of bread, and afterward you may go."

**19:6** So they sat down, and the two of them ate and drank together; and the father of the young woman said to the man, "Please agree and spend the night, and let your heart be glad."

**19:7** When the man arose to go, his father-in-law urged him insistently, so he turned back and spent the night there.

**19:8** On the fifth day he got up early in the morning to go, and the father of the young woman said, "Please sustain your heart, and linger until the day declines." So the two of them ate.

**19:9** Then the man arose to go, he and his concubine and his attendant; but his father-in-law, the father of the young woman, said to him, "Look now, the day has waned toward evening; please spend the night. See, the day is drawing to a close; spend the night here and let your heart be glad. Tomorrow you may rise early for your way and go to your tent."

**19:10** But the man was not willing to spend the night, so he arose and went, and came opposite Jebus, that is, Jerusalem. With him was a pair of saddled donkeys, and his concubine was with him.

**19:11** When they were near Jebus, the day had gone far down; and the attendant said to his master, "Please come, let us turn aside into this city of the Jebusites and spend the night in it."

**19:12** His master said to him, "We will not turn aside into a city of foreigners, who are not of the sons of Israel; we will pass on as far as Gibeah."

**19:13** And he said to his attendant, "Come, let us draw near to one of the places, and spend the night in Gibeah or in Ramah."

**19:14** So they passed on and went, and the sun went down on them beside Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin.

**19:15** They turned aside there to go in to spend the night in Gibeah. He went in and sat down in the open square of the city, and there was no one taking them into a house to spend the night.

**19:16** Then look, an old man was coming from his work out of the field in the evening. Now the man was from the hill country of Ephraim, and he was sojourning in Gibeah; but the men of the place were Benjaminites.

**19:17** He lifted up his eyes and saw the traveling man in the open square of the city; and the old man said, "Where are you going, and where are you coming from?"

**19:18** He said to him, "We are passing through from Bethlehem in Judah to the remote parts of the hill country of Ephraim. I am from there, and I went to Bethlehem in Judah; now I am going to the house of the LORD, yet there is no one taking me into a house.

**19:19** Yet there is both straw and fodder for our donkeys, and also bread and wine for me, for your maidservant, and for the attendant who is with your servants; there is no lack of anything."

**19:20** The old man said, "Peace to you! Only let all that you lack be upon me; only do not spend the night in the open square."

**19:21** So he brought him into his house and gave fodder to the donkeys; and they washed their feet, and ate and drank.

**19:22** While they were making their hearts glad, look, the men of the city, men who were sons of worthlessness, surrounded the house, pounding on the door; and they spoke to the old man, the owner of the house, saying, "Bring out the man who came into your house, so that we may know him."

**19:23** Then the man, the owner of the house, went out to them and said to them, "No, my brothers, please do not act wickedly! Since this man has come into my house, do not commit this outrage.

**19:24** Here are my virgin daughter and his concubine; please let me bring them out, and violate them and do to them what is good in your eyes, but to this man you must not do this outrageous thing."

**19:25** But the men were not willing to listen to him. So the man seized his concubine and brought her outside to them; and they knew her and abused her all night until the morning, and let her go as the dawn was rising.

**19:26** And the woman came as morning approached, and fell at the entrance of the house of the man where her master was, until daylight.

**19:27** When her master arose in the morning, opened the doors of the house, and went out to go on his way, look, the woman, his concubine, was lying fallen at the entrance of the house, with her hands on the threshold.

**19:28** He said to her, "Get up, and let us go." But there was no answer. So he took her up onto the donkey, and the man arose and went to his place.

**19:29** When he came into his house, he took the knife, seized his concubine, and cut her up by her bones into twelve pieces, and sent her throughout all the territory of Israel.

**19:30** And it came about that everyone who saw it said, "Nothing like this has happened or been seen from the day the sons of Israel came up from the land of Egypt until this day. Set your minds to it, take counsel, and speak!"

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — The Masoretic verb (Qal of zanah with al) literally means "committed sexual unfaithfulness against him"; some ancient versions render instead "became angry with him."
- **19:3** — Translating the Qere (read form), "to bring her back" (Hiphil infinitive construct of shuv with 3fs suffix); the Ketiv (written form) has a masculine suffix ("to bring him/it back").
- **19:18** — The Masoretic Text reads "to the house of the LORD" (using et idiomatically to mark destination with halakh); the Septuagint reads "to my house."
- **19:21** — Translating the Qere, "and gave fodder" (Qal wayyiqtol of balal, meaning to mix or provide provender); the Ketiv preserves a plene spelling of the same verb form.
- **19:25** — Translating the Qere, "as the dawn was rising" (preposition ke- with Qal infinitive construct of alah); the Ketiv reads the preposition be- ("at the rising of the dawn").
