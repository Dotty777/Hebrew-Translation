## Text

**15:1** Now it came about after some days, during the days of the wheat harvest, that Samson visited his wife with a young goat and said, "Let me go in to my wife in the inner room." But her father did not permit him to go in.

**15:2** Her father said, "I surely thought that you utterly hated her, so I gave her to your companion. Is not her younger sister better than she? Please let her be yours in her place."

**15:3** Samson said to them, "I am clear of guilt toward the Philistines this time when I do harm to them."

**15:4** Samson went and caught three hundred foxes. He took torches, turned them tail to tail, and placed one torch in the middle between every two tails.

**15:5** He kindled fire in the torches and sent them into the standing grain of the Philistines, and he burned up stacked grain and standing grain alike, as well as olive groves.

**15:6** Then the Philistines asked, "Who did this?" And they answered, "Samson, the son-in-law of the Timnite, because he took his wife and gave her to his companion." So the Philistines went up and burned her and her father with fire.

**15:7** Samson said to them, "If this is how you act, I will certainly take vengeance on you, and after that I will stop."

**15:8** He struck them leg upon thigh with a great blow; then he went down and settled in the cleft of the rock of Etam.

**15:9** Then the Philistines went up, encamped in Judah, and spread out in Lehi.

**15:10** The men of Judah asked, "Why have you come up against us?" They answered, "We have come up to bind Samson, to do to him just as he did to us."

**15:11** So three thousand men from Judah went down to the cleft of the rock of Etam and said to Samson, "Do you not know that the Philistines rule over us? And what is this you have done to us?" He said to them, "Just as they did to me, so I have done to them."

**15:12** They said to him, "We have come down to bind you, to give you into the hand of the Philistines." Samson said to them, "Swear to me that you yourselves will not fall upon me."

**15:13** They spoke to him, saying, "No, rather we will surely bind you and give you into their hand, yet we will certainly not put you to death." So they bound him with two new ropes and brought him up from the rock.

**15:14** When he came to Lehi, the Philistines shouted to meet him. Then the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him, and the ropes that were on his arms became like flax that has burned in fire, and his bonds melted off his hands.

**15:15** He found a fresh jawbone of a donkey, reached out his hand and took it, and struck down a thousand men with it.

**15:16** Then Samson said, "With the jawbone of a donkey, one heap, two heaps; with the jawbone of a donkey, I have struck down a thousand men."

**15:17** When he had finished speaking, he threw the jawbone from his hand, and he called that place Ramath-lehi.

**15:18** Then he became very thirsty, and he called to the LORD and said, "You have given this great deliverance by the hand of Your servant; and now shall I die of thirst and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised?"

**15:19** So God split open the hollow basin that is in Lehi, and water came out of it. He drank, and his spirit returned and he revived. Therefore he called its name En-hakkore, which is in Lehi to this day.

**15:20** And he judged Israel in the days of the Philistines for twenty years.

## Translator Notes

- **15:8** — The phrase "leg upon thigh" renders the Hebrew idiom shoq al-yarekh, expressing a thorough, crushing defeat.
- **15:16** — Samson's saying puns on the Hebrew homonym chamor, meaning both "donkey" and "heap," paired with the dual chamoratayim ("two heaps").
- **15:19** — Hebrew maktesh denotes a mortar-shaped hollow or basin, and the spring name En-hakkore means "Spring of the One Who Calls," echoing Samson's calling out in v. 18; "in Lehi" (ba-lechi) also plays on "in the jawbone."
