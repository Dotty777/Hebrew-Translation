## Text

**8:1** Then the men of Ephraim said to him, "What is this thing you have done to us, not calling us when you went to fight against Midian?" And they contended fiercely with him.

**8:2** He said to them, "What have I done now compared to you? Are not the gleanings of Ephraim better than the grape harvest of Abiezer?

**8:3** Into your hand God has given the commanders of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb; and what was I able to do compared to you?" Then their anger against him subsided as he spoke this word.

**8:4** Then Gideon reached the Jordan, crossing it, he and the three hundred men who were with him, weary, yet still pursuing.

**8:5** He said to the men of Succoth, "Please give loaves of bread to the people who are following me, for they are weary, and I am pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian."

**8:6** The officials of Succoth said, "Are the palms of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your hand, that we should give bread to your troops?"

**8:7** Gideon said, "Therefore, when the LORD gives Zebah and Zalmunna into my hand, I will thresh your flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with the briers."

**8:8** From there he went up to Penuel and spoke to them in the same way; and the men of Penuel answered him just as the men of Succoth had answered.

**8:9** So he also said to the men of Penuel, "When I return in peace, I will demolish this tower."

**8:10** Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor, and their armies were with them, about fifteen thousand, all who were left from the entire army of the sons of the east; for those who had fallen were one hundred and twenty thousand men who drew the sword.

**8:11** Gideon went up by the way of those who dwell in tents, east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and struck the camp while the camp felt secure.

**8:12** Zebah and Zalmunna fled, but he pursued them and captured the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna, and threw the entire camp into panic.

**8:13** Then Gideon son of Joash returned from the battle by the ascent of Heres.

**8:14** He captured a young man from the men of Succoth and questioned him, and he wrote down for him the officials of Succoth and its elders, seventy-seven men.

**8:15** Then he came to the men of Succoth and said, "Here are Zebah and Zalmunna, about whom you taunted me, saying, 'Are the palms of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your hand, that we should give bread to your weary men?'"

**8:16** He took the elders of the city, together with the thorns of the wilderness and the briers, and taught the men of Succoth a lesson with them.

**8:17** He also demolished the tower of Penuel and killed the men of the city.

**8:18** Then he said to Zebah and Zalmunna, "What were the men like whom you killed at Tabor?" And they said, "As you are, so were they; each one had the appearance of the sons of a king."

**8:19** He said, "They were my brothers, the sons of my mother. As the LORD lives, if you had kept them alive, I would not kill you."

**8:20** He told Jether his firstborn, "Rise up and kill them!" Yet the boy did not draw his sword, for he was afraid, since he was still a boy.

**8:21** Then Zebah and Zalmunna said, "Arise yourself and strike us down, for as a man is, so is his might." So Gideon arose and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, and he took the crescent ornaments that were on the necks of their camels.

**8:22** Then the men of Israel said to Gideon, "Rule over us, you and your son and your son's son as well, for you have delivered us from the hand of Midian."

**8:23** Gideon replied to them, "I myself will not rule over you, nor will my son rule over you; the LORD will rule over you."

**8:24** Then Gideon said to them, "Let me ask a request of you: let each man give me an earring from his plunder." For they had gold earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.

**8:25** They replied, "We will surely give them." So they spread out a cloak, and each man threw an earring from his plunder onto it.

**8:26** The weight of the gold earrings that he had requested was one thousand seven hundred shekels of gold, apart from the crescent ornaments, the pendants, and the purple garments that were on the kings of Midian, and apart from the necklaces that were on the necks of their camels.

**8:27** Gideon made it into an ephod and set it up in his city, in Ophrah; and all Israel prostituted themselves after it there, so that it became a snare to Gideon and to his household.

**8:28** Thus Midian was subdued before the sons of Israel, and they never lifted up their heads again; and the land remained quiet for forty years during the days of Gideon.

**8:29** So Jerubbaal son of Joash went away and lived in his house.

**8:30** Now Gideon had seventy sons who came from his own loins, because he had many wives.

**8:31** His concubine who was in Shechem also bore him a son, and he gave him the name Abimelech.

**8:32** Then Gideon son of Joash died at a good old age, and he was buried in the tomb of his father Joash, in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

**8:33** As soon as Gideon died, the sons of Israel turned back and prostituted themselves after the Baals, and made Baal-berith their god.

**8:34** The sons of Israel did not remember the LORD their God, who had rescued them from the hand of all their enemies on every side;

**8:35** nor did they show loyal love to the household of Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon, in accordance with all the good that he had done for Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **8:13** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads "by the ascent of Heres," taking ha-cheres (literally "the sun") as a place name; some ancient versions interpret the phrase temporally as "before sunrise."
- **8:16** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads a defective Hiphil form of y-d-' ("to know"), literally "he made [them] know," idiomatically "taught a lesson" (or possibly a homonymous root meaning "subdue/humiliate"); some ancient versions read "threshed" as in verse 7.
- **8:18** — The interrogative ephoh normally means "where?" but uniquely asks here about quality or appearance ("what were they like?"), as confirmed by the kings' comparison.
- **8:26** — Literally "one thousand seven hundred gold"; Biblical Hebrew regularly omits the standard unit of weight ("shekels") before a metal.
