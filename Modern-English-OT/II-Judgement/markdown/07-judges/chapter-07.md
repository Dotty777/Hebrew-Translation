## Text

**7:1** Then Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon, and all the people who were with him rose early and camped beside the spring of Harod. The camp of Midian was north of him, near the hill of Moreh in the valley.

**7:2** The LORD said to Gideon, "The people who are with you are too numerous for Me to give Midian into their hand, otherwise Israel might boast against Me, saying, 'My hand has delivered me.'

**7:3** Now therefore, proclaim in the ears of the people, saying, 'Whoever is afraid and trembling, let him turn back and depart from Mount Gilead.'" So twenty-two thousand of the people turned back, and ten thousand remained.

**7:4** Then the LORD said to Gideon, "The people are still too numerous. Lead them down to the water, and I will test them for you there. It will be that the one about whom I say to you, 'This one will go with you,' he will go with you; and everyone about whom I say to you, 'This one will not go with you,' he will not go."

**7:5** So he led the people down to the water. And the LORD said to Gideon, "Everyone who laps from the water with his tongue as a dog laps, you will set him apart by himself, and likewise everyone who kneels on his knees to drink."

**7:6** The number of those who lapped with their hand to their mouth was three hundred men, while all the rest of the people knelt on their knees to drink water.

**7:7** The LORD said to Gideon, "With the three hundred men who lapped I will deliver you, and I will give Midian into your hand. Let all the people go, each man to his place."

**7:8** So they took the provisions of the people in their hand and their trumpets; and he sent all the men of Israel away, each man to his tents, but he retained the three hundred men. Now the camp of Midian was below him in the valley.

**7:9** Now on that night the LORD said to him, "Arise, go down against the camp, for I have given it into your hand.

**7:10** But if you are afraid to go down, go down, you and Purah your attendant, to the camp,

**7:11** and you will hear what they say; afterward your hands will be strengthened, and you will go down against the camp." So he went down, he and Purah his attendant, to the edge of the armed men who were in the camp.

**7:12** Now Midian, Amalek, and all the sons of the east were spread out in the valley like locusts in abundance, and their camels were beyond number, like the sand on the shore of the sea in abundance.

**7:13** When Gideon arrived, there was a man recounting a dream to his companion. And he said, "Look, I dreamed a dream, and see, a loaf of barley bread was tumbling into the camp of Midian. It came up to the tent and struck it so that it fell, and it turned it upside down, and the tent lay fallen."

**7:14** His companion answered and said, "This is nothing other than the sword of Gideon son of Joash, a man of Israel! God has given Midian and all the camp into his hand."

**7:15** When Gideon heard the account of the dream and its interpretation, he bowed down in worship. Then he returned to the camp of Israel and said, "Arise, for the LORD has given the camp of Midian into your hand."

**7:16** He divided the three hundred men into three companies, and he put trumpets into the hand of every one of them, along with empty jars, and torches inside the jars.

**7:17** He said to them, "Watch me and do the same. Now look, I am coming to the edge of the camp, and just as I do, so you will do.

**7:18** When I blow the trumpet, I and all who are with me, then you also are to blow the trumpets all around the entire camp and say, 'For the LORD and for Gideon!'"

**7:19** So Gideon and the hundred men who were with him came to the edge of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch, just after they had posted the watchmen; and they blew the trumpets and smashed the jars that were in their hands.

**7:20** Then the three companies blew the trumpets and broke the jars. They grasped the torches in their left hands and the trumpets to blow in their right hands, and they shouted, "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!"

**7:21** Each man stood in his place around the camp, while the whole camp ran, cried out, and fled.

**7:22** When they blew the three hundred trumpets, the LORD set each man's sword against his companion and throughout the entire camp; and the camp fled as far as Beth-shittah toward Zererah, as far as the border of Abel-meholah, by Tabbath.

**7:23** Then the men of Israel were called out from Naphtali, from Asher, and from all Manasseh, and they pursued Midian.

**7:24** Gideon also sent messengers throughout all the hill country of Ephraim, saying, "Come down to meet Midian and capture the waters ahead of them as far as Beth-barah, as well as the Jordan." So all the men of Ephraim were called out, and they captured the waters as far as Beth-barah, as well as the Jordan.

**7:25** They captured the two commanders of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb; they killed Oreb at the rock of Oreb, and they killed Zeeb at the winepress of Zeeb. Then they pursued Midian, and they brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon across the Jordan.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The verb rendered "depart" (Qal jussive of ts-p-r) is a Biblical Hebrew hapax legomenon whose exact nuance is uncertain, possibly "depart early" or "hurry away." The Masoretic Text explicitly reads "Mount Gilead," though Gilead normally refers to the highlands east of the Jordan.
- **7:5** — The Masoretic Hebrew ends the verse elliptically after "to drink," leaving the instruction to set this second group apart as well implied from the preceding clause.
- **7:8** — The phrase "the provisions of the people" follows the definite object marker (et-) before an uninflected feminine noun (tsedah) joined to "the people," with the three hundred men as the plural subject; alternatively, "the people" may be read as the subject ("the people took provisions").
- **7:15** — The noun rendered "interpretation" literally means "breaking" (breaking open the meaning of a dream), and "account" (mispar, usually "number") echoes the Piel verb "recounting" in verse 13.
- **7:21** — Following the Qere (Qal of n-w-s, "and fled"); the Ketiv reflects a Hiphil form ("and put to flight" or "carried off in flight").
