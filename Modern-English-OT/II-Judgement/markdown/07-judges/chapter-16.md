## Text

**16:1** Samson went to Gaza, and there he saw a prostitute, and he went in to her.

**16:2** It was reported to the Gazites, saying, "Samson has come here." So they encircled him and lay in ambush for him all night at the gate of the city. They kept silent all night, saying, "Until the light of morning—then we will kill him."

**16:3** Samson lay until midnight, and at midnight he got up, grasped the doors of the city gate and the two posts, pulled them up along with the bar, put them on his shoulders, and carried them up to the top of the mountain that faces Hebron.

**16:4** Afterward it happened that he loved a woman in the Valley of Sorek, and her name was Delilah.

**16:5** The lords of the Philistines went up to her and said to her, "Entice him and see where his great strength lies and how we may overpower him, so that we may bind him to subdue him; and each of us will give you eleven hundred pieces of silver."

**16:6** So Delilah said to Samson, "Please tell me where your great strength lies, and with what you could be bound to subdue you."

**16:7** Samson told her, "If they bind me with seven fresh bowstrings that have not been dried, then I will become weak and be like any other man."

**16:8** Then the lords of the Philistines brought up to her seven fresh bowstrings that had not been dried, and she bound him with them.

**16:9** Now men in ambush were waiting for her in the inner room. She said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" But he snapped the bowstrings just as a strand of tow snaps when it smells fire. So his strength was not known.

**16:10** Then Delilah said to Samson, "Look, you have mocked me and spoken lies to me. Now please tell me with what you can be bound."

**16:11** He said to her, "If they bind me fast with new ropes with which no work has been done, then I will become weak and be like any other man."

**16:12** So Delilah took new ropes and bound him with them, and she said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" Meanwhile, the men in ambush were waiting in the inner room. But he snapped them off his arms like a thread.

**16:13** Then Delilah said to Samson, "Up to now you have mocked me and spoken lies to me. Tell me with what you can be bound." And he said to her, "If you weave the seven braids of my head with the web."

**16:14** So she fastened them with the pin and said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" He awoke from his sleep and pulled up the pin of the loom and the web.

**16:15** Then she said to him, "How can you say, 'I love you,' when your heart is not with me? These three times you have mocked me and have not told me where your great strength lies."

**16:16** And it came about, when she pressed him with her words every day and urged him, that his soul grew weary to death.

**16:17** So he told her all his heart and said to her, "A razor has never come upon my head, for I have been a Nazirite to God from my mother's womb. If I am shaved, my strength will depart from me, and I will become weak and be like all other men."

**16:18** When Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart, she sent and called for the lords of the Philistines, saying, "Come up this one time, for he has told me all his heart." Then the lords of the Philistines came up to her and brought up the silver in their hands.

**16:19** She made him sleep on her knees, and she called for a man and shaved off the seven braids of his head. Then she began to subdue him, and his strength departed from him.

**16:20** She said, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" He awoke from his sleep and said, "I will go out as at other times and shake myself free." But he did not know that the LORD had departed from him.

**16:21** Then the Philistines seized him and gouged out his eyes. They brought him down to Gaza and bound him with bronze shackles, and he was grinding in the prison house.

**16:22** Yet the hair of his head began to grow back after he was shaved.

**16:23** Now the lords of the Philistines assembled to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god and to rejoice, and they said, "Our god has given Samson our enemy into our hand."

**16:24** When the people saw him, they praised their god, for they said, "Our god has given into our hand our enemy, the devastator of our land, and the one who multiplied our dead."

**16:25** When their hearts were merry, they said, "Call for Samson, so that he may entertain us." So they called Samson out of the prison house, and he entertained before them. Then they stationed him between the pillars.

**16:26** Then Samson said to the young man who held him by the hand, "Let go of me, and let me feel the pillars on which the house rests, so that I may lean against them."

**16:27** Now the house was full of men and women, and all the lords of the Philistines were there. And on the roof were about three thousand men and women watching Samson entertain.

**16:28** Then Samson called to the LORD and said, "Lord GOD, please remember me and please strengthen me just this once, God, so that I may be avenged on the Philistines for one of my two eyes."

**16:29** And Samson grasped the two middle pillars on which the house rested, and he braced himself against them, one with his right hand and one with his left.

**16:30** And Samson said, "Let my soul die with the Philistines!" Then he bent forward with strength, and the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people who were in it. So the dead whom he put to death in his death were more than those he had put to death in his life.

**16:31** Then his brothers and all his father's household went down and carried him, and they went up and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of Manoah his father. He had judged Israel for twenty years.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — The Masoretic Text is elliptical at the opening of the verse, reading literally "To the Gazites, saying," with the introductory verb "it was reported" implied.
- **16:13** — The Masoretic Text omits Samson's expected consequence at the end of v. 13 and Delilah's act of weaving at the start of v. 14, leaving both implicit from the preceding episodes.
- **16:18** — Ketiv reads "to her" (indirect speech within the messenger's report), whereas Qere reads "to me" (direct speech), followed here.
- **16:21** — Qere reads "the house of those bound" (Qal passive participle asurim, echoed in v. 25), whereas Ketiv reads the noun form "the house of the prisoners" (asirim).
- **16:25** — Qere reads ke-tov (preposition plus Qal infinitive construct, "when [their hearts] were merry"), while Ketiv reads ki tov (conjunction plus Qal perfect); the same ketiv/qere on "prison house" recurs from v. 21.
- **16:26** — Qere reads the Hiphil imperative of mush ("let me feel"), whereas Ketiv reflects a by-form from the root ymsh with the same meaning.
- **16:28** — Hebrew neqam-achat mi-shetei einai ("vengeance for one of my two eyes," with feminine achat matching feminine ayin, "eye") can alternatively be construed adverbially as "vengeance at one stroke for my two eyes."
