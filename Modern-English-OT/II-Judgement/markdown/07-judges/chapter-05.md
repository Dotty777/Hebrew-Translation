## Text

**5:1** Then Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang on that day, saying:

**5:2** "When locks hung loose in Israel, when the people offered themselves freely, bless the LORD!

**5:3** Listen, kings; give ear, rulers! I, yes I, will sing to the LORD; I will make music to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**5:4** LORD, when You came out from Seir, when You strode from the country of Edom, the earth shook, the skies too dripped, and the clouds dripped water.

**5:5** The mountains streamed before the LORD, the One of Sinai, before the LORD, the God of Israel.

**5:6** In the days of Shamgar son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the roads stood deserted, and those who traveled the paths walked along winding roads.

**5:7** The village dwellers ceased in Israel, they ceased, until I arose, Deborah, until I arose, a mother in Israel.

**5:8** New gods were chosen; then fighting was at the gates. Was a shield seen, or a spear, among forty thousand in Israel?

**5:9** My heart is with the commanders of Israel, those who offered themselves freely among the people. Bless the LORD!

**5:10** You who ride white donkeys, who sit on carpets, and who walk along the road, recount it!

**5:11** Away from the sound of archers among the watering troughs, there they recount the righteous deeds of the LORD, the righteous deeds of His village dwellers in Israel. Then the people of the LORD went down to the gates.

**5:12** Wake up, wake up, Deborah! Wake up, wake up, utter a song! Rise, Barak, and take captive your captives, son of Abinoam!

**5:13** Then a remnant went down to the nobles, the people; the LORD went down for me among the mighty warriors.

**5:14** From Ephraim came those whose root is in Amalek; after you, Benjamin, among your peoples; from Machir commanders went down, and from Zebulun those who wield the scribe's staff.

**5:15** And my princes in Issachar were with Deborah, and Issachar was likewise with Barak; into the valley they were sent forth at his heels. Among the divisions of Reuben, great were the resolutions of heart.

**5:16** Why did you sit among the sheepfolds, to hear the whistling for the flocks? For the divisions of Reuben, great were the searchings of heart.

**5:17** Gilead remained across the Jordan; and Dan, why did he linger by the ships? Asher sat at the coast of the seas and remained by his inlets.

**5:18** Zebulun is a people that scorned its life to the point of death, and Naphtali, on the heights of the field.

**5:19** Kings came, they fought; then the kings of Canaan fought at Taanach, beside the waters of Megiddo; no gain of silver did they take.

**5:20** From the heavens they fought; the stars from their courses fought against Sisera.

**5:21** The Kishon torrent swept them away, the ancient torrent, the Kishon torrent. Tread on, my soul, in strength!

**5:22** Then the horses' hooves pounded from the galloping, the galloping of his stallions.

**5:23** 'Curse Meroz,' said the angel of the LORD, 'curse its inhabitants bitterly, for they did not come to the aid of the LORD, to the aid of the LORD among the mighty warriors.'

**5:24** Blessed above women be Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite; above women in the tent may she be blessed.

**5:25** Water he asked for, milk she gave; in a nobles' bowl she presented curds.

**5:26** She put her hand to the tent peg and her right hand to the laborers' hammer; she hammered Sisera, smashed his head, and shattered and pierced through his temple.

**5:27** Between her feet he collapsed, he fell, he lay; between her feet he collapsed, he fell; where he collapsed, there he fell, destroyed.

**5:28** Through the window Sisera's mother looked out and wailed through the lattice, 'Why is his chariot so slow to come? Why do the treads of his chariots lag behind?'

**5:29** The wisest of her noblewomen answer her; indeed, she repeats her own words to herself:

**5:30** 'Are they not finding, dividing plunder—a womb, two wombs for each warrior; plunder of dyed cloths for Sisera, plunder of dyed cloths, embroidery, a dyed cloth of double embroidery for the necks of the plunder?'

**5:31** So may all Your enemies perish, LORD, but may those who love Him be like the sun coming out in its strength." And the land was quiet for forty years.

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — Hebrew bipheroa pera'ot (Qal infinitive construct plus cognate plural noun from para') literally means "when locks hang loose," likely referring to consecrated warriors unbinding their hair for battle (cf. Num 6:5; Deut 32:42); alternatively, from an Arabic cognate, "when leaders took the lead."
- **5:5** — The verb nazelu is vocalized in the MT as Qal perfect of nazal ("flowed/streamed"), though some link it to zalal ("quaked"). The archaic phrase zeh Sinai ("the One of Sinai") uses zeh as a relative epithet for God, or alternatively means "even Sinai."
- **5:11** — The hapax Piel participle mehatsetsim is uncertain: traditionally taken as a denominative from hets ("arrow") meaning "archers" (with privative min in miqqol, "away from the sound of"), or alternatively "musicians" or "those who divide" ("to the sound of").
- **5:13** — The unusual MT verb form yerad (occurring twice) is generally taken as a poetic form of yarad ("went down," matching vv. 11 and 14), though traditionally parsed from radah ("had dominion"). The clause break follows the Masoretic athnach after 'am ("the people").
- **5:30** — The MT literally reads letsavvere shalal, "for the necks of the plunder" (likely referring to garments draped around necks as spoil, or to captive spoil); some ancient versions read "for my neck" or "for the necks of the spoilers."
