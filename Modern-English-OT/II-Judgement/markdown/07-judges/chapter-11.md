## Text

**11:1** Now Jephthah the Gileadite was a mighty fighting man, and he was the son of a prostitute; Gilead fathered Jephthah.

**11:2** Gilead's wife also bore him sons; when the wife's sons grew up, they drove Jephthah out and said to him, "You will not inherit in our father's house, because you are the son of another woman."

**11:3** So Jephthah fled from his brothers and settled in the land of Tob; worthless men gathered around Jephthah and went out with him.

**11:4** Some time later, the Ammonites went to war against Israel.

**11:5** And when the Ammonites fought against Israel, the elders of Gilead went to bring Jephthah from the land of Tob.

**11:6** They said to Jephthah, "Come and be our commander, so that we may fight against the Ammonites."

**11:7** Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "Was it not you who hated me and drove me away from my father's house? Why then have you come to me now that you are in distress?"

**11:8** The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "For that very reason we have turned back to you now: come with us and fight against the Ammonites, and you will become head for us, over all the residents of Gilead."

**11:9** Jephthah said to the elders of Gilead, "If you are bringing me back to fight against the Ammonites and the LORD gives them over before me, I myself will become your head."

**11:10** The elders of Gilead said to Jephthah, "The LORD will be listening between us: we will surely act just according to your word."

**11:11** So Jephthah went with the elders of Gilead, and the people set him over themselves as head and commander. Then Jephthah spoke all his words before the LORD at Mizpah.

**11:12** Then Jephthah sent messengers to the king of the Ammonites, saying, "What is between me and you, that you have come against me to fight in my land?"

**11:13** The king of the Ammonites answered Jephthah's messengers, "Because Israel took my land when it came up from Egypt, from the Arnon as far as the Jabbok and to the Jordan. Now then, restore them in peace."

**11:14** Once again Jephthah sent messengers to the king of the Ammonites.

**11:15** He said to him, "This is what Jephthah says: Israel did not take the land of Moab or the land of the Ammonites.

**11:16** Instead, when they came up from Egypt, Israel marched through the wilderness as far as the Sea of Reeds and arrived at Kadesh.

**11:17** Then Israel sent messengers to the king of Edom, saying, 'Please allow me to cross through your land,' but the king of Edom did not listen. They also sent to the king of Moab, and he was unwilling; so Israel stayed at Kadesh.

**11:18** Next they traveled through the wilderness, circled around the land of Edom and the land of Moab, arrived on the east side of the land of Moab, and encamped across the Arnon; they did not enter the territory of Moab, because the Arnon was the border of Moab.

**11:19** Israel then sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites, king of Heshbon, and Israel said to him, 'Please let us cross through your land to my place.'

**11:20** Sihon, however, did not trust Israel to cross through his territory; so Sihon gathered all his people, they encamped at Jahaz, and he fought against Israel.

**11:21** Then the LORD, the God of Israel, gave Sihon and all his people into Israel's hand, and they struck them down; thus Israel took possession of the entire land of the Amorites who lived in that land.

**11:22** They took possession of all the territory of the Amorites, from the Arnon as far as the Jabbok, and from the wilderness as far as the Jordan.

**11:23** So now the LORD, the God of Israel, has dispossessed the Amorites before His people Israel, and should you possess it?

**11:24** What Chemosh your god causes you to possess, do you not possess that? And everything the LORD our God has dispossessed from before us, that we will possess.

**11:25** Now then, are you really any better than Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab? Did he ever contend with Israel, or did he ever fight against them?

**11:26** While Israel inhabited Heshbon and its villages, Aroer and its villages, and all the towns along the sides of the Arnon for three hundred years, why did you not reclaim them during that time?

**11:27** I myself have not sinned against you, yet you are doing me wrong by fighting against me. May the LORD, the Judge, judge today between the sons of Israel and the Ammonites."

**11:28** Yet the king of the Ammonites did not listen to the words of Jephthah that he had sent to him.

**11:29** Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jephthah, and he passed through Gilead and Manasseh; he also passed through Mizpeh of Gilead, and from Mizpeh of Gilead he crossed over to the Ammonites.

**11:30** Jephthah made a vow to the LORD and said, "If You indeed give the Ammonites into my hand,

**11:31** then the one who comes out from the doors of my house to meet me when I come back in peace from the Ammonites will belong to the LORD, and I will offer that one up as a burnt offering."

**11:32** So Jephthah crossed over to the Ammonites to fight against them, and the LORD gave them into his hand.

**11:33** He struck them down from Aroer until you reach Minnith—twenty towns—and as far as Abel-keramim, with a very severe blow. Thus the Ammonites were subdued before the sons of Israel.

**11:34** When Jephthah arrived at Mizpah, at his house, look—his daughter was coming out to meet him with tambourines and with dancing! Now she alone was his only child; he had no other son or daughter of his own.

**11:35** The moment he saw her, he tore his clothes and said, "Alas, my daughter! You have brought me completely to my knees, and you have become among those who trouble me; for I have opened my mouth to the LORD, and I cannot turn back."

**11:36** She said to him, "My father, you have opened your mouth to the LORD; do to me according to what has come out from your mouth, now that the LORD has carried out vengeance for you upon your enemies, the Ammonites."

**11:37** Then she said to her father, "Let this thing be done for me: leave me alone for two months, so that I may depart and go down onto the mountains and weep over my virginity, I and my companions."

**11:38** He said, "Go." So he sent her off for two months; she and her companions went, and she wept over her virginity on the mountains.

**11:39** At the end of two months she returned to her father, and he carried out on her his vow that he had vowed. She had never known a man. And it became a custom in Israel.

**11:40** Year by year the daughters of Israel would go to commemorate the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite for four days each year.

## Translator Notes

- **11:13** — The Masoretic Text uses a feminine plural pronoun ('them') following singular 'my land,' referring collectively to the cities or tracts within that territory.
- **11:31** — The masculine singular participle ('the one who comes out') and object suffix ('that one') are grammatically generic in Hebrew; the conjunction 'and' literally connects belonging to the LORD with offering as a burnt offering.
- **11:34** — The Masoretic Text literally reads 'from him' (mimmennu), understood either as having no other child 'of his own' or as a rare masculine-for-feminine suffix meaning 'besides her.'
- **11:37** — The qere (read text) is plural 'and my companions' (matching verse 38), whereas the ketiv (written text) appears to reflect a singular form ('and my companion') or an older plural spelling. The verb 'go down' (yarad) literally describes descending onto the mountain slopes and is often understood as wandering over the hills.
- **11:40** — The rare Piel verb tanah ('recount, commemorate,' as in Judges 5:11) is also commonly understood in this context as 'lament.'
