## Text

**24:1** The LORD showed me, and look, two baskets of figs were set before the temple of the LORD, after Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had exiled Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, along with the officials of Judah and the craftsmen and the smiths, from Jerusalem and brought them to Babylon.

**24:2** One basket had very good figs, like first-ripe figs, and the other basket had very bad figs, which could not be eaten because of their badness.

**24:3** Then the LORD said to me, "What do you see, Jeremiah?" And I said, "Figs: the good figs are very good, and the bad ones are very bad, which cannot be eaten because of their badness."

**24:4** Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying:

**24:5** "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Like these good figs, so I will regard for good the exiles of Judah, whom I have sent away from this place to the land of the Chaldeans.

**24:6** I will set My eye upon them for good, and I will bring them back to this land; I will build them up and not tear down, and I will plant them and not uproot.

**24:7** And I will give them a heart to know Me, that I am the LORD; and they will be a people for Me, and I Myself will be God for them, because they will turn back to Me with all their heart.

**24:8** But like the bad figs, which cannot be eaten because of their badness—for this is what the LORD says—so I will treat Zedekiah king of Judah, and his officials, and the remnant of Jerusalem who remain in this land, as well as those who dwell in the land of Egypt.

**24:9** I will make them an object of terror, a calamity to all the kingdoms of the earth—a reproach and a byword, a taunt and a curse in all the places where I will drive them.

**24:10** And I will send against them the sword, the famine, and the pestilence, until they are completely gone from the soil that I gave to them and to their fathers."

## Translator Notes

- **24:8** — Hebrew etten (Qal of natan, literally "I will give/make") introduces the comparison elliptically ("treat/render like"), which is then expanded by the same verb in v. 9 ("I will make them").
- **24:9** — Translates the qere lezaavah ("an object of terror/horror"); the ketiv reflects a metathesized form of the same noun with identical meaning.
