## Text

**12:1** Righteous are You, LORD, even if I contend with You; yet I will speak with You regarding judgments. Why does the way of the wicked succeed? Why are all who act in treachery untroubled?

**12:2** You have planted them, and they have taken root; they keep growing, and they even yield fruit. You are near in their mouth, yet far from their innermost being.

**12:3** Yet You, LORD, know me; You see me, and You have examined my heart toward You. Drag them off like sheep for slaughter, and set them apart for a day of killing.

**12:4** How long will the land mourn and the vegetation of every field wither? Because of the wickedness of those who dwell in it, animals and birds have been swept away; for they have said, "He will not see our end."

**12:5** If you have raced with men on foot and they have exhausted you, then how will you compete with horses? And though you feel safe in a land of peace, how will you fare in the thicket of the Jordan?

**12:6** For even your brothers and your father's house, even they have acted treacherously against you; even they have cried out in full voice after you. Do not trust them, even when they speak pleasant words to you.

**12:7** I have forsaken My house; I have abandoned My inheritance. I have given the beloved of My soul into the grasp of her enemies.

**12:8** My inheritance has become to Me like a lion in the forest; she has lifted her voice against Me, therefore I hate her.

**12:9** Is My inheritance a speckled bird of prey to Me? Are the birds of prey all around against her? Go, gather all the wild beasts of the field; bring them to devour!

**12:10** Many shepherds have ruined My vineyard; they have trampled down My plot of land. They have turned My cherished plot into a wilderness of desolation.

**12:11** They have made it a desolation; desolate, it mourns to Me. The entire land has been laid desolate, because no man lays it to heart.

**12:12** Over all the bare heights in the wilderness devastators have come, for a sword of the LORD devours from one end of the land to the other end of the land; there is no peace for any flesh.

**12:13** They have sown wheat, yet thorns they have reaped; they have exhausted themselves without profit. Be ashamed over your yields because of the burning anger of the LORD.

**12:14** This is what the LORD says: "Concerning all My evil neighbors who touch the inheritance that I caused My people Israel to inherit, look, I am about to uproot them from their soil, and I will uproot the house of Judah from their midst.

**12:15** And it will happen that after I have uprooted them, I will once again have compassion on them, and I will bring them back, each one to his inheritance and each one to his land.

**12:16** And it will be that if they truly learn the ways of My people, to swear by My name, 'As the LORD lives,' just as they taught My people to swear by Baal, then they will be built up in the midst of My people.

**12:17** But if they will not listen, then I will uproot that nation, uprooting and destroying it," declares the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **12:2** — Hebrew "kilyoteihem" literally means "their kidneys," the biblical idiom for a person's innermost thoughts, conscience, and affections.
- **12:4** — The third-person subject "He" most likely refers to God (echoing "You see me" in v. 3), though some take it to refer to the prophet Jeremiah; Hebrew "aharitenu" means "our end" or "our final outcome."
- **12:5** — Hebrew "tetahareh" is a rare Tiphel form of "harah" meaning "compete with" or "contend against" (also in Jer 22:15). "The thicket of the Jordan" renders "ge'on hayyarden," literally "the pride/swelling of the Jordan," referring to the dense, lion-infested riverbank vegetation.
- **12:9** — Hebrew "ayit tsavua" literally means "a speckled/colored bird of prey," though the exact lexical sense of "tsavua" is debated.
