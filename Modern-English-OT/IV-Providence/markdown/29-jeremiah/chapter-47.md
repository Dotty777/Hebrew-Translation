## Text

**47:1** The word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD after Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard had let him go from Ramah, when he had taken him while he was bound in chains among all the exiles of Jerusalem and Judah who were being exiled to Babylon.

**47:2** The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him, "The LORD your God pronounced this disaster against this place,

**47:3** and the LORD has brought it about and done just as He spoke. Because you sinned against the LORD and did not obey His voice, this thing has happened to you.

**47:4** And now, look, I am freeing you today from the chains that are on your hands. If it is good in your eyes to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will watch over you; but if it is bad in your eyes to come with me to Babylon, refrain. See, the whole land is before you; wherever it is good and right in your eyes to go, go there."

**47:5** While he had not yet turned back, he said, "Go back to Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, whom the king of Babylon has appointed over the cities of Judah, and stay with him among the people; or go wherever it is right in your eyes to go." Then the captain of the guard gave him provisions and a gift, and let him go.

**47:6** So Jeremiah went to Gedaliah son of Ahikam at Mizpah, and stayed with him among the people who remained in the land.

**47:7** Now all the commanders of the forces who were in the field, they and their men, heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam over the land, and that he had entrusted to him men, women, and children, along with some of the poorest of the land, from those who had not been exiled to Babylon.

**47:8** So they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah: Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan and Jonathan the sons of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth, the sons of Ephai the Netophathite, and Jezaniah son of the Maacathite, they and their men.

**47:9** Then Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, swore to them and to their men, saying, "Do not be afraid of serving the Chaldeans. Stay in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will go well with you.

**47:10** As for me, look, I am staying at Mizpah to stand before the Chaldeans who come to us; but as for you, gather wine, summer fruit, and oil, and put them in your vessels, and live in your cities that you have taken."

**47:11** Likewise all the Judeans who were in Moab and among the sons of Ammon and in Edom, and who were in all the countries, heard that the king of Babylon had left a remnant for Judah and that he had appointed over them Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan.

**47:12** Then all the Judeans returned from all the places where they had been driven, and came to the land of Judah, to Gedaliah at Mizpah, and they gathered wine and summer fruit in very great abundance.

**47:13** Now Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the forces who were in the field came to Gedaliah at Mizpah.

**47:14** And they said to him, "Do you indeed know that Baalis king of the sons of Ammon has sent Ishmael son of Nethaniah to strike you dead?" But Gedaliah son of Ahikam did not believe them.

**47:15** Then Johanan son of Kareah spoke to Gedaliah in secret at Mizpah, saying, "Please let me go and strike down Ishmael son of Nethaniah, and no one will know. Why should he strike you dead, so that all Judah who are gathered to you would be scattered, and the remnant of Judah would perish?"

**47:16** But Gedaliah son of Ahikam said to Johanan son of Kareah, "Do not do this thing, for you are speaking a lie about Ishmael."

## Translator Notes

- **47:3** — Ketiv reads indefinite דבר, whereas Qere supplies the definite article הַדָּבָר ("this thing") in agreement with the demonstrative הַזֶּה.
- **47:5** — The clause וְעוֹדֶנּוּ לֹא־יָשׁוּב וְשֻׁבָה (lit. "And while he had not yet turned back, and go back...") shifts abruptly from a 3ms narrative aside into Nebuzaradan's direct imperative with the verb of speaking implied.
- **47:8** — Ketiv reads עופי ("Ophai"), whereas Qere reads עֵיפַי ("Ephai").
- **47:14** — "To strike you dead" renders the Hebrew idiom לְהַכֹּתְךָ נָפֶשׁ (Hiphil infinitive construct of נָכָה with adverbial accusative נֶפֶשׁ, lit. "to strike you as to life"; likewise יַכֶּכָּה נֶּפֶשׁ in 47:15).
- **47:16** — Ketiv writes the short jussive form תעש, whereas Qere reads the full form תַּעֲשֵׂה (both Qal 2ms of עָשָׂה, "do," with the same sense after אַל).
