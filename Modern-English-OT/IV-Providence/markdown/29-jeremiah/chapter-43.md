## Text

**43:1** Now it happened in the fourth year of Jehoiakim, son of Josiah, king of Judah, that this word came to Jeremiah from the LORD, saying,

**43:2** "Take for yourself a book scroll, and write on it all the words that I have spoken to you about Israel, about Judah, and about all the nations, from the day I spoke to you, from the days of Josiah up to this day.

**43:3** Perhaps the house of Judah will hear all the calamity that I am planning to do to them, so that they may turn back, each one from his evil way, and I will forgive their iniquity and their sin."

**43:4** Then Jeremiah called Baruch, son of Neriah, and Baruch wrote on a book scroll, from the mouth of Jeremiah, all the words of the LORD that He had spoken to him.

**43:5** Jeremiah commanded Baruch, saying, "I am restrained; I cannot enter the house of the LORD.

**43:6** So you yourself go and read the words of the LORD from the scroll that you wrote from my mouth, in the hearing of the people at the house of the LORD on a fast day; and you shall also read them in the hearing of all Judah who come from their cities.

**43:7** Perhaps their supplication will be laid before the LORD, and they will turn back, each one from his evil way; for great are the anger and the fury that the LORD has spoken against this people."

**43:8** So Baruch, son of Neriah, did according to everything Jeremiah the prophet had commanded him, reading from the book the words of the LORD at the house of the LORD.

**43:9** Now it happened in the fifth year of Jehoiakim, son of Josiah, king of Judah, in the ninth month, that all the people in Jerusalem and all the people who had come from the cities of Judah into Jerusalem proclaimed a fast before the LORD.

**43:10** Then Baruch read the words of Jeremiah from the book at the house of the LORD, in the chamber of Gemariah, son of Shaphan the scribe, in the upper court, at the entrance of the New Gate of the house of the LORD, in the hearing of all the people.

**43:11** Now Micaiah, son of Gemariah, son of Shaphan, heard all the words of the LORD from the book.

**43:12** He went down to the king's house, to the scribe's chamber, and look, all the officials were sitting there: Elishama the scribe, Delaiah son of Shemaiah, Elnathan son of Achbor, Gemariah son of Shaphan, Zedekiah son of Hananiah, and all the officials.

**43:13** Micaiah reported to them all the words that he had heard when Baruch read from the book in the hearing of the people.

**43:14** Then all the officials sent Jehudi, son of Nethaniah, son of Shelemiah, son of Cushi, to Baruch, saying, "The scroll from which you read in the hearing of the people—take it in your hand and come." So Baruch, son of Neriah, took the scroll in his hand and came to them.

**43:15** They said to him, "Please sit down and read it in our hearing." So Baruch read in their hearing.

**43:16** When they heard all the words, they turned in dread, each toward his companion, and said to Baruch, "We must certainly report all these words to the king."

**43:17** Then they questioned Baruch, saying, "Please tell us, how did you write all these words from his mouth?"

**43:18** And Baruch said to them, "From his mouth he would recite all these words to me, while I was writing them in the book with ink."

**43:19** So the officials said to Baruch, "Go, conceal yourself, you and Jeremiah, and let no one know where you are."

**43:20** Then they went to the king in the courtyard—having deposited the scroll in the chamber of Elishama the scribe—and they reported all the words in the hearing of the king.

**43:21** The king sent Jehudi to fetch the scroll, and he took it from the chamber of Elishama the scribe. Then Jehudi read it in the hearing of the king and in the hearing of all the officials who stood beside the king.

**43:22** Now the king was seated in the winter house in the ninth month, and the brazier was burning before him.

**43:23** And whenever Jehudi had read three or four columns, the king would cut it with a scribe's knife and throw it into the fire that was in the brazier, until the entire scroll was consumed in the fire that was on the brazier.

**43:24** Yet they felt no dread and did not tear their garments—neither the king nor any of his servants who heard all these words.

**43:25** Indeed, Elnathan, Delaiah, and Gemariah appealed to the king not to burn the scroll, yet he did not listen to them.

**43:26** Then the king commanded Jerahmeel the king's son, Seraiah son of Azriel, and Shelemiah son of Abdeel to seize Baruch the scribe and Jeremiah the prophet, but the LORD concealed them.

**43:27** Now the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah after the king had burned the scroll and the words that Baruch had written from the mouth of Jeremiah, saying,

**43:28** "Once more, take for yourself another scroll and write on it all the earlier words that were on the first scroll that Jehoiakim king of Judah burned.

**43:29** And about Jehoiakim king of Judah you shall say, 'This is what the LORD says: You yourself burned this scroll, saying, "Why did you write on it, saying that the king of Babylon will surely come and destroy this land, and eliminate human and animal from it?"

**43:30** Therefore this is what the LORD says regarding Jehoiakim king of Judah: He shall have no one sitting on the throne of David, and his corpse shall be thrown out to the heat by day and to the frost by night.

**43:31** And I will call him, his offspring, and his servants to account for their iniquity, and I will bring on them, on the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and on the men of Judah all the calamity that I spoke against them, yet they did not listen.'"

**43:32** So Jeremiah took another scroll and gave it to Baruch, son of Neriah, the scribe, and he wrote on it from the mouth of Jeremiah all the words of the book that Jehoiakim king of Judah had burned in the fire; and even more—many words like them—were added to them.

## Translator Notes

- **43:2** — Hebrew megillat-sepher literally means "scroll of a book" (or document); this chapter distinguishes megillah ("scroll") from sepher ("book").
- **43:7** — Hebrew tippol techinnatam liphne literally reads "their supplication will fall before," an idiom for humbly presenting a plea for favorable reception (also in 44:20).
- **43:16** — Hebrew pachadu 'ish 'el-re'ehu literally means "they dreaded, each toward his companion," a pregnant construction expressing that they turned to one another in dread.
- **43:22** — Hebrew we'et-ha'ach lephanaw mebo'aret places the marker 'et before the grammatical subject ha'ach ("the brazier") with the feminine Pual participle mebo'aret ("burning, kept alight").
- **43:23** — Hebrew delatot literally means "doors," used as a scribal term for the rectangular columns of text on a scroll; wehashlekh is a Hiphil infinitive absolute continuing the iterative action of the preceding verb.
