## Text

**52:1** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. And his mother's name was Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

**52:4** Now it happened in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he and all his army, against Jerusalem, and they encamped against it and built a siege wall against it all around.

**52:5** So the city came under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah.

**52:6** In the fourth month, on the ninth day of the month, the famine grew severe in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

**52:7** Then the city was breached, and all the men of war fled and went out from the city by night by way of the gate between the two walls that was beside the king's garden, while the Chaldeans were all around the city, and they went by the way of the Arabah.

**52:8** But the army of the Chaldeans pursued after the king and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho, and all his army was scattered from him.

**52:9** Then they seized the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah in the land of Hamath, and he pronounced judgment on him.

**52:10** The king of Babylon slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes, and he also slaughtered all the officials of Judah at Riblah.

**52:11** Then he blinded the eyes of Zedekiah and bound him with bronze fetters, and the king of Babylon brought him to Babylon and put him in the house of custody until the day of his death.

**52:12** Now in the fifth month, on the tenth day of the month, which was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard, who stood before the king of Babylon, came into Jerusalem.

**52:13** And he burned the house of the LORD, the house of the king, and all the houses of Jerusalem, and every great house he burned with fire.

**52:14** And all the army of the Chaldeans who were with the captain of the guard tore down all the walls of Jerusalem all around.

**52:16** But some of the poorest of the land Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard left behind as vinedressers and as plowmen.

**52:17** Now the Chaldeans broke in pieces the bronze pillars that belonged to the house of the LORD, and the stands, and the bronze sea that was in the house of the LORD, and they carried all their bronze to Babylon.

**52:18** And they took the pots, the shovels, the wick trimmers, the basins, the ladles, and all the bronze vessels with which they used to minister.

**52:19** And the captain of the guard took the bowls, the firepans, the basins, the pots, the lampstands, the ladles, and the pitchers, what was of gold as gold, and what was of silver as silver.

**52:20** As for the two pillars, the one sea, and the twelve bronze oxen that were under the stands, which King Solomon had made for the house of the LORD, there was no weighing the bronze of all these vessels.

**52:21** Now as for the pillars, the height of the one pillar was eighteen cubits, and a cord of twelve cubits encircled it, and its thickness was four fingers, and it was hollow.

**52:22** And a bronze capital was upon it, and the height of the one capital was five cubits, with latticework and pomegranates upon the capital all around, all of bronze; and the second pillar had the same, with pomegranates.

**52:23** There were ninety-six pomegranates on the outside; all the pomegranates upon the latticework all around were one hundred.

**52:24** And the captain of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, and Zephaniah the second priest, and the three keepers of the threshold.

**52:25** And from the city he took one officer who was overseer over the men of war, and seven men from those who saw the king's face who were found in the city, and the scribe of the commander of the army, who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men from the people of the land who were found in the midst of the city.

**52:26** And Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard took them and led them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.

**52:27** Then the king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. Thus Judah went into exile from its land.

**52:31** Now it happened in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-fifth day of the month, that Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the year he became king, lifted up the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah and brought him out of the prison house.

**52:32** And he spoke kindly with him, and set his throne above the throne of the kings who were with him in Babylon.

**52:33** And he changed his prison garments, and he ate bread before him continually all the days of his life.

**52:34** And as for his allowance, a continual allowance was given to him from the king of Babylon, each day's portion, until the day of his death, all the days of his life.

## Translator Notes

- **52:1** — Translated from the qere (read form) Hamutal; the ketiv (written form) spells the name Hamital.
- **52:11** — Translated from the qere, which omits the preposition 'in' before 'house' (using an adverbial accusative of place), whereas the ketiv writes 'in the house of'; Hebrew beit-happequddot ('house of custody') is a unique term for a place of detention.
- **52:20** — The Masoretic Hebrew literally reads 'that were under the stands,' whereas 1 Kings 7:25 locates the twelve bronze oxen under the sea (and the Septuagint adjusts accordingly).
- **52:21** — Translated from the qere construct form 'the height of the one pillar'; the ketiv reads the absolute noun 'in height.'
- **52:23** — Hebrew ruchah (literally 'toward the wind') refers to the pomegranates exposed on the outer sides of the capital's latticework.
- **52:31** — Translated from the qere 'the house of confinement' (Qal passive participle of kl' 'confine'); the ketiv reads an orthographic variant of the same noun for prison.
- **52:32** — Translated from the qere 'the kings' (with the definite article); the ketiv omits the definite article ('kings').
- **52:15 *(additional)*** — The Hebrew term rendered "craftsmen" refers to skilled artisans, though some understand it as a variant spelling for "populace" or "multitude" as in 2 Kings 25:11.
- **52:28 *(additional)*** — Hebrew spells the king's name Nebuchadrezzar here and in the following two verses, a variant of Nebuchadnezzar.
