## Text

**48:1** Now it happened in the seventh month that Ishmael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, from the royal offspring and the chief officers of the king, and ten men with him, came to Gedaliah son of Ahikam at Mizpah, and they ate bread together there in Mizpah.

**48:2** Then Ishmael son of Nethaniah and the ten men who were with him rose up and struck Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, with the sword, and he put him to death, the one whom the king of Babylon had appointed over the land.

**48:3** Ishmael also struck down all the Jews who were with him, with Gedaliah, in Mizpah, and the Chaldeans who were found there, the men of war.

**48:4** Now it happened on the second day after Gedaliah was put to death, when no one knew,

**48:5** that men came from Shechem, from Shiloh, and from Samaria—eighty men—with their beards shaved and their garments torn, and having gashed themselves, with grain offering and frankincense in their hands to bring to the house of the LORD.

**48:6** And Ishmael son of Nethaniah went out from Mizpah to meet them, weeping continuously as he walked. When he encountered them, he said to them, "Come to Gedaliah son of Ahikam."

**48:7** And when they came into the middle of the city, Ishmael son of Nethaniah slaughtered them into the middle of the cistern, he and the men who were with him.

**48:8** But ten men were found among them, and they said to Ishmael, "Do not put us to death, for we have hidden stores in the field—wheat and barley and oil and honey." So he held back and did not put them to death among their brothers.

**48:9** Now the cistern into which Ishmael threw all the corpses of the men whom he had struck down on account of Gedaliah was the one that King Asa had made because of Baasha king of Israel; Ishmael son of Nethaniah filled it with those who were killed.

**48:10** Then Ishmael took captive all the remnant of the people who were in Mizpah, the king's daughters and all the people remaining in Mizpah, whom Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard had entrusted to Gedaliah son of Ahikam. Ishmael son of Nethaniah took them captive and set out to cross over to the sons of Ammon.

**48:11** Now Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the forces who were with him heard about all the evil that Ishmael son of Nethaniah had done.

**48:12** So they took all the men and went to fight against Ishmael son of Nethaniah, and they found him at the great waters that are in Gibeon.

**48:13** And when all the people who were with Ishmael saw Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the forces who were with him, they rejoiced.

**48:14** So all the people whom Ishmael had taken captive from Mizpah turned around, and they turned back and went to Johanan son of Kareah.

**48:15** But Ishmael son of Nethaniah escaped from Johanan with eight men and went to the sons of Ammon.

**48:16** Then Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the forces who were with him took all the remnant of the people whom he had brought back from Ishmael son of Nethaniah, from Mizpah, after he had struck down Gedaliah son of Ahikam—men, men of war, women, children, and eunuchs whom he had brought back from Gibeon.

**48:17** And they set out and stayed at Geruth Chimham, which is beside Bethlehem, intending to go on to enter Egypt,

**48:18** because of the Chaldeans; for they were afraid of them, because Ishmael son of Nethaniah had struck down Gedaliah son of Ahikam, whom the king of Babylon had appointed over the land.

## Translator Notes

- **48:1** — The phrase "and the chief officers of the king" (werabbe hammelekh) is syntactically ambiguous in the MT, either governed by the preceding preposition "from" as part of Ishmael's own status or listing additional companions who came with him.
- **48:7** — The clause "slaughtered them into the middle of the cistern" (wayyishhatem el-tokh habbor) uses a pregnant construction, compressing the acts of slaughtering the men and casting their bodies into the cistern.
- **48:9** — The MT phrase "on account of Gedaliah" (beyad-Gedalyahu) reads literally "by the hand of Gedaliah," an unusual idiom understood here as "on account of" or "alongside" Gedaliah.
- **48:17** — The Ketiv reads kmwhm while the Qere reads kimham ("Chimham"). Geruth (gerut) is a hapax legomenon from the root gur ("sojourn"), meaning "lodging place," functioning here with Chimham as a place name.
