## Text

**11:1** The word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD, saying:

**11:2** "Hear the words of this covenant, and speak to the men of Judah and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

**11:3** And you shall say to them, 'This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Cursed is the man who does not listen to the words of this covenant,

**11:4** which I commanded your fathers on the day I brought them out of the land of Egypt, from the iron furnace, saying: Listen to My voice, and do them according to all that I command you; then you will be My people, and I will be your God,

**11:5** in order to uphold the oath that I swore to your fathers, to give them a land flowing with milk and honey, as at this day.'" And I answered and said, "Amen, LORD."

**11:6** And the LORD said to me, "Proclaim all these words in the cities of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem, saying, 'Hear the words of this covenant and do them.'

**11:8** Yet they did not listen and did not incline their ear, but walked, each one in the stubbornness of their evil heart. Therefore I brought upon them all the words of this covenant, which I commanded them to do, yet they did not."

**11:9** Then the LORD said to me, "A conspiracy has been found among the men of Judah and among the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

**11:10** They have turned back to the iniquities of their earlier forefathers, who refused to listen to My words; and they themselves have gone after other gods to serve them. The house of Israel and the house of Judah have broken My covenant which I made with their fathers."

**11:11** Therefore, this is what the LORD says: "Look, I am bringing disaster upon them from which they will not be able to escape. And they will cry out to Me, yet I will not listen to them.

**11:12** Then the cities of Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem will go and cry out to the gods to whom they burn incense, but they will not save them at all in the time of their disaster.

**11:13** For your gods have become as numerous as your cities, Judah; and as many as the streets of Jerusalem, you have set up altars to that shameful thing, altars to burn incense to Baal.

**11:14** As for you, do not pray for this people, and do not lift up a cry or a prayer on their behalf; for I will not listen at the time when they call to Me because of their disaster.

**11:15** What right has My beloved in My house, since she has carried out vile schemes with many, and consecrated flesh passes away from you? When you do your evil, then you exult!

**11:16** 'A flourishing olive tree, beautiful in fruit and form,' the LORD called your name; with the roar of a great tumult He has kindled fire upon it, and its branches are broken.

**11:17** And the LORD of hosts, who planted you, has pronounced disaster against you because of the evil of the house of Israel and the house of Judah, which they have done for themselves to provoke Me to anger by burning incense to Baal."

**11:18** Now the LORD made it known to me, and I knew; then You showed me their deeds.

**11:19** Yet I was like a tame lamb brought to the slaughter, and I did not know that they had plotted schemes against me: "Let us destroy the tree with its fruit, and let us cut him off from the land of the living, so that his name will be remembered no longer."

**11:20** Yet, LORD of hosts, who judges righteously, who tests the kidneys and the heart, let me see Your vengeance upon them, for to You I have revealed my cause.

**11:21** Therefore, this is what the LORD says concerning the men of Anathoth, who seek your life, saying, "Do not prophesy in the name of the LORD, so that you will not die by our hand."

**11:22** Therefore, this is what the LORD of hosts says: "Look, I am about to punish them: the young men will die by the sword; their sons and their daughters will die by famine.

**11:23** And there will be no remnant for them, for I will bring disaster upon the men of Anathoth in the year of their punishment."

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — The Hebrew uses second-person plural verbs ("Hear... and speak") in v. 2 before shifting to second-person singular ("And you shall say") in v. 3.
- **11:15** — The Masoretic Hebrew is notoriously compressed: a poetic feminine singular noun ("vile scheme") stands next to masculine plural "many," "consecrated flesh" takes a Qal plural verb ("passes away"), and "your evil" preserves an archaic second-person feminine singular suffix.
- **11:19** — Literally "the tree with its bread," an idiom for a tree together with its fruit.
- **11:20** — In Hebrew idiom, the "kidneys" and "heart" denote the deepest seat of human emotions, conscience, and inner motives.
