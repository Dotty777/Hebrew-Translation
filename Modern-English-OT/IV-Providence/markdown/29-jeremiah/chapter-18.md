## Text

**18:1** The word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD, saying,

**18:2** "Get up and go down to the potter's house, and there I will cause you to hear My words."

**18:3** So I went down to the potter's house, and look, he was doing work on the wheel.

**18:4** And the vessel that he was making of clay was ruined in the potter's hand; so he made it again into another vessel, just as it was right in the potter's eyes to do.

**18:5** Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying,

**18:6** "Am I not able to do with you like this potter, house of Israel?" declares the LORD. "Look, like the clay in the potter's hand, so are you in My hand, house of Israel.

**18:7** At one moment I may speak concerning a nation and concerning a kingdom, to uproot and to tear down and to destroy;

**18:8** if that nation against which I have spoken turns from its evil, then I will relent concerning the calamity that I planned to do to it.

**18:9** And at another moment I may speak concerning a nation and concerning a kingdom, to build and to plant;

**18:10** if it does what is evil in My eyes, by not listening to My voice, then I will relent concerning the good that I said I would do to benefit it.

**18:11** And now, speak to the men of Judah and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying, 'This is what the LORD says: Look, I am forming calamity against you and devising a plan against you. Turn back now, each man from his evil way, and make your ways and your deeds good.'

**18:12** But they will say, 'It is hopeless! For we will walk after our own plans, and each of us will carry out the stubbornness of his evil heart.'"

**18:13** Therefore this is what the LORD says: "Ask now among the nations: Who has heard things like these? The virgin Israel has done a most horrifying thing.

**18:14** Does the snow of Lebanon depart from the rock of the open country? Or do the cold, flowing waters from afar fail?

**18:15** Yet My people have forgotten Me; they burn incense to what is worthless, and they have caused them to stumble in their ways, the ancient paths, to walk in trails, on a road not built up,

**18:16** to make their land a desolation, an object of perpetual hissing; everyone who passes by it will be appalled and will shake his head.

**18:17** Like an east wind I will scatter them before an enemy; I will see their back and not their face in the day of their disaster."

**18:18** Then they said, "Come, let us devise plans against Jeremiah, for instruction will not perish from a priest, nor counsel from a wise man, nor a word from a prophet. Come, let us strike him with the tongue, and let us not pay attention to any of his words."

**18:19** Pay attention to me, LORD, and listen to the voice of my adversaries.

**18:20** Should evil be repaid for good? Yet they have dug a pit for my life. Remember how I stood before You to speak good on their behalf, to turn Your wrath away from them.

**18:21** Therefore give their children over to famine, and pour them out to the power of the sword; let their wives become childless and widowed. Let their men be killed by death, and their young men struck down by the sword in battle.

**18:22** Let an outcry be heard from their houses when You bring a raiding band upon them suddenly! For they have dug a pit to capture me and have hidden snares for my feet.

**18:23** Yet You, LORD, know all their counsel against me to put me to death. Do not forgive their iniquity, and do not blot out their sin from before You. Let them be made to stumble before You; deal with them in the time of Your anger.

## Translator Notes

- **18:10** — Ketiv reads the feminine ha-ra'ah, while Qere reads the masculine ha-ra' ("what is evil"), matching the standard Hebrew idiom for doing evil in God's eyes.
- **18:14** — Tsur saday uses the poetic singular noun saday ("open country / field"), and zarim ("foreign / distant") refers to cold streams flowing from distant mountain sources. Yinnateshu is Niphal imperfect of natash (elsewhere "be uprooted," cf. v. 7), used here of waters failing or drying up.
- **18:16** — Ketiv reads a variant singular spelling shrwqt, while Qere reads the plural construct sheriqot ("hissings of eternity," i.e., perpetual hissing).
- **18:17** — The Masoretic Text vocalizes er'em as Qal ("I will see them [with respect to] back and not face," i.e., seeing their back as they flee); revocalizing as Hiphil (ar'em) would yield "I will show them My back and not My face."
- **18:23** — Ketiv reads weqatal whyw ("and they will be"), while Qere reads jussive/imperfect weyihyu ("let them be"), continuing the preceding petitions. Mukhshalim is Hophal participle of kashal ("made to stumble"), echoing the Hiphil in v. 15.
