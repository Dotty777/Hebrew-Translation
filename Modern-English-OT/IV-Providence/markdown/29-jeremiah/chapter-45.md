## Text

**45:1** Now Shephatiah son of Mattan, Gedaliah son of Pashhur, Jucal son of Shelemiah, and Pashhur son of Malchijah heard the words that Jeremiah was speaking to all the people, saying,

**45:2** "This is what the LORD says: 'Whoever remains in this city will die by the sword, by famine, and by pestilence, but whoever goes out to the Chaldeans will live; his own life will be his as plunder, and he will live.'

**45:3** This is what the LORD says: 'This city will certainly be given into the hand of the army of the king of Babylon, and he will capture it.'"

**45:4** Then the officials said to the king, "Please let this man be put to death, because he is weakening the hands of the men of war who remain in this city and the hands of all the people by speaking words like these to them; for this man is not seeking welfare for this people, but rather harm."

**45:5** And King Zedekiah said, "Look, he is in your hand; for the king cannot do anything against you."

**45:6** So they took Jeremiah and threw him into the cistern of Malchijah the king's son, which was in the courtyard of the guard, and they let Jeremiah down with ropes. Now in the cistern there was no water, only mud, and Jeremiah sank into the mud.

**45:7** Now Ebed-melech the Cushite, a eunuch who was in the king's house, heard that they had put Jeremiah into the cistern. The king was sitting at the Benjamin Gate.

**45:8** So Ebed-melech went out from the king's house and spoke to the king, saying,

**45:9** "My lord the king, these men have done evil in all that they have done to Jeremiah the prophet, whom they have thrown into the cistern, and he will die right where he is because of the famine, for there is no more bread in the city."

**45:10** Then the king commanded Ebed-melech the Cushite, saying, "Take thirty men from here with you, and bring Jeremiah the prophet up from the cistern before he dies."

**45:11** So Ebed-melech took the men with him and went into the king's house beneath the treasury, and took from there worn-out rags and worn-out garments, and let them down with ropes to Jeremiah into the cistern.

**45:12** Then Ebed-melech the Cushite said to Jeremiah, "Please put the worn-out rags and garments under your armpits beneath the ropes." And Jeremiah did so.

**45:13** So they pulled Jeremiah with the ropes and brought him up from the cistern. And Jeremiah remained in the courtyard of the guard.

**45:14** Then King Zedekiah sent and brought Jeremiah the prophet to himself at the third entrance that is in the house of the LORD. And the king said to Jeremiah, "I am asking you something; do not hide anything from me."

**45:15** Jeremiah said to Zedekiah, "If I tell you, will you not certainly put me to death? And if I advise you, you will not listen to me."

**45:16** So King Zedekiah swore to Jeremiah in secret, saying, "As the LORD lives, who made this life for us, I will not put you to death, nor will I give you into the hand of these men who are seeking your life."

**45:17** Then Jeremiah said to Zedekiah, "This is what the LORD, the God of hosts, the God of Israel, says: 'If you will indeed go out to the officials of the king of Babylon, then your soul will live, and this city will not be burned with fire, and you and your household will live.

**45:18** But if you do not go out to the officials of the king of Babylon, then this city will be given into the hand of the Chaldeans, and they will burn it with fire, and you will not escape from their hand.'"

**45:19** Then King Zedekiah said to Jeremiah, "I am afraid of the Judeans who have defected to the Chaldeans, that they might give me into their hand and abuse me."

**45:20** But Jeremiah said, "They will not give you over. Please obey the voice of the LORD in what I am speaking to you, so that it may go well with you and your soul may live.

**45:21** But if you refuse to go out, this is the word that the LORD has shown me:

**45:22** Look, all the women who remain in the house of the king of Judah are being brought out to the officials of the king of Babylon, and they are saying, 'Your trusted friends have misled you and prevailed over you; your feet have been sunk in the mire; they have turned back.'

**45:23** And they are bringing out all your wives and your sons to the Chaldeans, and you will not escape from their hand, for you will be seized by the hand of the king of Babylon, and you will burn this city with fire."

**45:24** Then Zedekiah said to Jeremiah, "Let no one know about these words, and you will not die.

**45:25** And if the officials hear that I have spoken with you, and they come to you and say to you, 'Please tell us what you spoke to the king—do not hide it from us, and we will not put you to death—and what the king spoke to you,'

**45:26** then you shall say to them, 'I was presenting my plea before the king, that he would not send me back to the house of Jonathan to die there.'"

**45:27** Then all the officials came to Jeremiah and questioned him, and he told them according to all these words that the king had commanded. So they stopped speaking with him, for the matter had not been overheard.

**45:28** And Jeremiah remained in the courtyard of the guard until the day that Jerusalem was captured. And so it was when Jerusalem was captured.

## Translator Notes

- **45:2** — Ketiv reads the Qal imperfect יחיה ("will live"), whereas Qere reads the waw-consecutive Qal perfect וְחָיָה ("will live"), marking the apodosis after the participial subject.
- **45:11** — Ketiv includes the definite article on the second noun of the construct chain (הסחבות), whereas Qere omits the article (סְחָבוֹת, "worn-out rags"), matching the indefinite בְּלוֹיֵי מְלָחִים that follows.
- **45:16** — Ketiv writes the object marker את before אֲשֶׁר; this is a ketiv velo qere (written but not read) and is omitted in the Qere.
- **45:23** — The Masoretic vocalization תִּשְׂרֹף is Qal 2ms active ("you will burn"), holding Zedekiah directly responsible for the city's burning (contrast the Niphal passive תִּשָּׂרֵף in 45:17).
- **45:28** — The closing clause וְהָיָה כַּאֲשֶׁר נִלְכְּדָה יְרוּשָׁלִָם (lit. "And it came about when Jerusalem was captured") stands in the MT as an abrupt transitional clause linking to the capture account in chapter 46.
