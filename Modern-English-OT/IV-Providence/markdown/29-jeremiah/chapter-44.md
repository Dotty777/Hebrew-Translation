## Text

**44:1** Now Zedekiah, son of Josiah—whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had made king in the land of Judah—reigned as king in place of Coniah, son of Jehoiakim.

**44:2** Yet neither he nor his servants nor the people of the land listened to the words of the LORD that He spoke by the hand of Jeremiah the prophet.

**44:3** Now King Zedekiah sent Jehucal, son of Shelemiah, and Zephaniah, son of Maaseiah, the priest, to Jeremiah the prophet, saying, "Please pray on our behalf to the LORD our God."

**44:4** Meanwhile, Jeremiah was coming in and going out among the people, for they had not placed him in the prison.

**44:5** And Pharaoh's army had marched out from Egypt; when the Chaldeans who were besieging Jerusalem heard the report of them, they withdrew from Jerusalem.

**44:6** Then the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah the prophet, saying,

**44:7** "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Thus you shall say to the king of Judah, who sent you to Me to inquire of Me, 'Look, Pharaoh's army, which has marched out to aid you, is returning to its own land, Egypt.

**44:8** And the Chaldeans will return and fight against this city; they will capture it and burn it with fire.'

**44:9** This is what the LORD says: Do not deceive yourselves, saying, 'The Chaldeans will surely depart from us,' for they will not depart.

**44:10** For even if you struck down the entire army of Chaldeans who are fighting against you, and only wounded men were left among them, they would still rise up, each man in his tent, and burn this city with fire."

**44:11** Now it happened, when the army of the Chaldeans had withdrawn from Jerusalem because of Pharaoh's army,

**44:12** that Jeremiah went out from Jerusalem to go to the land of Benjamin, to obtain a share from there among the people.

**44:13** When he was at the Benjamin Gate, an officer of the guard was there whose name was Irijah, son of Shelemiah, son of Hananiah; and he seized Jeremiah the prophet, saying, "You are defecting to the Chaldeans!"

**44:14** Jeremiah said, "A lie! I am not defecting to the Chaldeans." Yet he would not listen to him; so Irijah seized Jeremiah and brought him to the officials.

**44:15** The officials were enraged with Jeremiah, and they beat him and placed him in the house of imprisonment, the house of Jonathan the scribe, for they had made it into the prison.

**44:16** Thus Jeremiah entered the dungeon and the vaulted cells, and Jeremiah remained there many days.

**44:17** Then King Zedekiah sent and had him brought, and the king questioned him in secret in his house and said, "Is there a word from the LORD?" Jeremiah said, "There is." And he said, "Into the hand of the king of Babylon you will be given."

**44:18** And Jeremiah said to King Zedekiah, "In what way have I sinned against you, against your servants, or against this people, that you have placed me in the prison?

**44:19** And where are your prophets who prophesied to you, saying, 'The king of Babylon will not come against you or against this land'?

**44:20** And now, please listen, my lord the king; please let my supplication be laid before you, and do not send me back to the house of Jonathan the scribe, so that I do not die there."

**44:21** Then King Zedekiah commanded, and they consigned Jeremiah to the courtyard of the guard, giving him a loaf of bread each day from the street of the bakers until all the bread was exhausted from the city. Thus Jeremiah remained in the courtyard of the guard.

## Translator Notes

- **44:4** — Translated according to the Qere hakkelu' ("the prison," from kala', "restrain, shut up"); the Ketiv is an orthographic variant spelling h-k-l-y-'.
- **44:10** — Hebrew meduqqarim is the Pual participle of daqar ("pierce through"), referring to soldiers critically wounded in battle.
- **44:12** — Hebrew lachaliq is a difficult form, usually taken as a defective Hiphil infinitive construct of chalaq ("divide, apportion") meaning to obtain a share of property or produce; some derive it from a homonymous root meaning "to slip away."
- **44:16** — Hebrew bet habbor literally means "house of the cistern" (a subterranean dungeon), and hachanuyot is a hapax legomenon denoting vaulted subterranean cells or chambers.
- **44:19** — Translated according to the Qere we'ayyeh ("and where [are]...?"), agreeing with plural "your prophets"; the Ketiv reads w-'-y-w (we'ayyo, "and where is he?"), bearing a third-person masculine singular suffix.
