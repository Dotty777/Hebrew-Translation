## Text

**10:1** Hear the word that the LORD has spoken to you, house of Israel.

**10:2** This is what the LORD says: "Do not learn the way of the nations, and do not be terrified by the signs of the heavens, even though the nations are terrified by them.

**10:3** For the customs of the peoples are futility; for someone cuts down a tree from the forest, the work of the hands of a craftsman with an axe.

**10:4** With silver and gold one adorns it; with nails and hammers they fasten them so that it does not totter.

**10:5** They are like a scarecrow in a cucumber patch, and they cannot speak; they must indeed be carried, because they cannot take a step. Do not be afraid of them, for they cannot cause harm, nor is doing good within their power.

**10:9** Beaten silver is brought from Tarshish, and gold from Uphaz, the work of a craftsman and the hands of a goldsmith; blue and purple are their clothing; all of them are the work of skilled artisans.

**10:11** In this way you shall say to them: 'The gods who did not make the heavens and the earth will perish from the earth and from beneath these heavens.'"

**10:12** He is the One who made the earth by His strength, who firmly established the world by His wisdom, and by His understanding He stretched out the heavens.

**10:13** When He utters His voice, there is a tumult of waters in the heavens, and He makes clouds ascend from the ends of the earth; He makes flashes of lightning for the rain, and brings forth the wind from His storehouses.

**10:14** Every human being is senseless, without knowledge; every goldsmith is put to shame by a carved image, for his cast metal image is falsehood, and there is no breath in them.

**10:15** They are futility, a work of mockery; at the time of their punishment they will perish.

**10:16** Not like these is the portion of Jacob, for He is the Fashioner of everything, and Israel is the tribe of His inheritance; the LORD of hosts is His name.

**10:17** Gather up your bundle from the ground, you who dwell under siege.

**10:18** For this is what the LORD says: "Look, I am slinging out the inhabitants of the land at this time, and I will bring distress upon them so that they may feel it."

**10:19** Woe to me because of my shattering! My wound is grievous. Yet I said, "Surely this is a sickness, and I must bear it."

**10:20** My tent is devastated, and all my cords are snapped. My children have gone out from me and are no more; there is no one anymore to stretch out my tent and set up my curtains.

**10:21** For the shepherds have become senseless and have not sought the LORD; therefore they have not prospered, and their entire flock is scattered.

**10:22** The sound of a report! Look, it comes, and a great commotion from the land of the north, to make the cities of Judah a desolation, a lair of jackals.

**10:23** I know, LORD, that a human being's way is not his own; it does not belong to the person who walks to direct his step.

**10:24** Discipline me, LORD, yet in justice; not in Your anger, or else You will reduce me to nothing.

**10:25** Pour out Your wrath upon the nations that do not know You, and upon the families that do not call on Your name; for they have devoured Jacob; they have devoured him and consumed him, and have made his habitation desolate.

**10:7** *(after `10:5`)* Who would not fear You, King of the nations? For to You it is due, because among all the wise of the nations and in all their kingdoms there is no one like You.

**10:8** *(after `10:5`)* And altogether they are senseless and foolish; the instruction of worthless things—it is wood.

**10:10** *(after `10:9`)* Yet the LORD is the true God; He is the living God and the eternal King. At His wrath the earth trembles, and nations cannot withstand His indignation.

## Translator Notes

- **10:5** — Literally "like a post/palm-pillar in a cucumber patch," widely understood as a scarecrow (though the noun is also a homonym for "turned/hammered work").
- **10:11** — This verse is the only passage in Jeremiah written in Biblical Aramaic; it preserves both the archaic spelling arqa and the standard Aramaic spelling ar'a for "the earth."
- **10:13** — Qere adds the definite article ("the earth"), whereas Ketiv reads "earth" without the article. The opening phrase literally reads "at the sound of His giving," an idiomatic expression for uttering His voice in thunder.
- **10:17** — Qere reads the standard Qal feminine singular participle ("you who dwell"), replacing the archaic Ketiv feminine ending with hireq compaginis. The noun rendered "bundle" is a hapax legomenon denoting packed belongings or wares.
- **10:18** — The Masoretic Qal imperfect literally reads "so that they may find," without an explicit object, idiomatically meaning "so that they may feel/experience it."
- **10:8 *(additional)*** — Hebrew habalim, literally "vanities" or "empty breaths," refers here to worthless idols.
