## Text

**47:1** Come down and sit on the dust, virgin daughter of Babylon; sit on the ground without a throne, daughter of the Chaldeans! For no longer will they call you "Tender and Pampered."

**47:2** Take millstones and grind flour; uncover your veil, strip off the skirt, uncover the leg, cross over rivers.

**47:3** Your nakedness will be uncovered, and your disgrace will also be seen; I will take vengeance, and I will spare no human.

**47:4** Our Redeemer—the LORD of hosts is His name—is the Holy One of Israel.

**47:5** Sit in silence and go into the darkness, daughter of the Chaldeans! For no longer will they call you "Mistress of Kingdoms."

**47:6** I was angry with My people; I profaned My inheritance and gave them into your hand. You showed them no compassion; on the aged you made your yoke exceedingly heavy.

**47:7** And you said, "Forever I will be a mistress," so that you did not lay these things to your heart; you did not remember its outcome.

**47:8** And now, listen to this, you lover of luxury, who sits securely, who says in her heart, "I am, and there is no one besides me; I will not sit as a widow, and I will not know the loss of children."

**47:9** Both of these things will come to you in a moment, in a single day: loss of children and widowhood. In full measure they will come upon you, in spite of your many sorceries, in spite of the exceeding power of your spells.

**47:10** You trusted in your wickedness; you said, "No one sees me." Your wisdom and your knowledge—it is they that led you astray; and you said in your heart, "I am, and there is no one besides me."

**47:11** So disaster will come upon you that you will not know how to charm away; and ruin will fall upon you that you will not be able to buy off; and devastation will come upon you suddenly that you do not know.

**47:12** Stand fast now with your spells and with your many sorceries, in which you have labored from your youth; perhaps you will be able to profit, perhaps you will strike terror.

**47:13** You have grown weary with your many counsels; let them stand up now and save you—those who divide the heavens, who gaze at the stars, who make known at the new moons what will come upon you.

**47:14** Look, they have become like stubble; fire has burned them up. They cannot deliver themselves from the grip of the flame; it is no coal for warming oneself, no fire to sit before!

**47:15** Such have they become to you with whom you have labored, your traders from your youth: each one has wandered off in his own direction; there is no one to save you.

## Translator Notes

- **47:3** — Hebrew וְלֹא אֶפְגַּע אָדָם literally means "and I will not meet a human," a difficult idiom understood here as "I will spare no human" (or alternatively "I will not accept any human intercession").
- **47:9** — The preposition בְּ before both "your many sorceries" and "the exceeding power of your spells" carries a concessive force ("in spite of"), anticipating the ironic challenge in 47:12.
- **47:11** — Hebrew שַׁחְרָהּ is taken as a Piel infinitive construct ("charm it away"), matching the parallel Piel infinitive כַּפְּרָהּ ("buy it off"); alternatively, it may be derived from the noun שַׁחַר ("its dawn"). Hebrew רָעָה ("disaster") also plays on רָעָתֵךְ ("your wickedness") in 47:10.
- **47:13** — Qere הֹבְרֵי ("those who divide," Qal active participle construct of the hapax root הבר, referring to astrologers partitioning the sky) is translated; Ketiv הברו reads a plural perfect verb ("they divided") or an archaic spelling.
