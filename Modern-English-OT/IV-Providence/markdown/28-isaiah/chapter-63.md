## Text

**63:1** Who is this coming from Edom, with crimson garments from Bozrah, this One stately in His attire, striding in the abundance of His strength? "It is I, speaking in righteousness, powerful to save."

**63:2** Why is there red on Your attire, and Your garments like those of one treading in a winepress?

**63:3** "I have trodden the wine trough by Myself, and from the peoples no man was with Me; I trod them in My anger and trampled them in My fury, and their lifeblood spattered onto My garments, and I stained all My attire.

**63:4** For a day of vengeance was in My heart, and the year of My redemption had come.

**63:5** I looked, and there was no helper; I was astounded, and there was no one to sustain; so My own arm won salvation for Me, and My fury itself sustained Me.

**63:6** And I trampled peoples in My anger, made them drunk in My fury, and brought their lifeblood down to the ground."

**63:7** I will bring to remembrance the LORD's acts of loyal love, the praises of the LORD, in keeping with everything the LORD has bestowed on us, and the abundant goodness toward the house of Israel that He bestowed on them in accordance with His compassions and in accordance with the multitude of His acts of loyal love.

**63:8** And He said, "Truly they are My people, children who will not prove false." So He became a Savior to them.

**63:9** In all their distress He was distressed, and the angel of His presence saved them; in His love and in His mercy He Himself redeemed them, and He raised them up and bore them all the days of old.

**63:10** Yet they rebelled and grieved His Holy Spirit; so He turned into an enemy toward them, and He Himself fought against them.

**63:11** Then His people recalled the days of old, of Moses: "Where is the One who brought them up from the sea along with the shepherds of His flock? Where is the One who placed His Holy Spirit within them,

**63:12** who made the arm of His splendor move at the right hand of Moses, who split the waters before them to make an everlasting name for Himself,

**63:13** who guided them through the depths?" As a horse in the wilderness, they did not stumble.

**63:14** Like livestock descending into the valley, the Spirit of the LORD granted them rest; so You led Your people, to make a name of splendor for Yourself.

**63:15** Look down from heaven and see, from the habitation of Your holiness and Your splendor. Where are Your zeal and Your mighty acts? The yearning of Your inner being and Your compassions are held back toward me.

**63:16** For You are our Father, even though Abraham does not know us and Israel does not recognize us; You, LORD, are our Father; "Our Redeemer from Everlasting" is Your name.

**63:17** Why, LORD, do You cause us to wander from Your ways and harden our heart away from fearing You? Turn back for the sake of Your servants, the tribes of Your inheritance.

**63:18** For a short while Your holy people held possession; our adversaries have trampled Your sanctuary.

**63:19** We have become like those over whom You have not ruled from long ago, over whom Your name has not been called. If only You would tear open the heavens and come down, so that mountains would quake before Your presence!

## Translator Notes

- **63:3** — The verb eg'alti ('I stained') is an Aramaizing Hiphil perfect form of ga'al II ('to stain, defile'), creating wordplay with ge'ulay ('My redemption,' from ga'al I) in v. 4. The noun netsach ('lifeblood') denotes spurting juice or blood.
- **63:9** — Following the qere lo ('to Him'), forming the Hebrew idiom 'He was distressed' (literally 'it was distressing to Him'); the written ketiv reads lo' ('not').
- **63:11** — The Masoretic text has the compressed clause 'remembered the days of old, Moses, His people,' here smoothed with 'His people' as subject, and reads the plural ro'ey ('shepherds of His flock,' e.g., Moses and Aaron).
- **63:14** — The Masoretic verb tenichennu is the Hiphil of nuach ('granted them rest,' literally 'gave it rest,' referring collectively to the people), rather than nachah ('led').
- **63:19** — In the Masoretic verse division the plea 'If only You would tear open the heavens...' belongs to 63:19, and chapter 64 begins with the next verse. The verb nazollu is the Niphal of zalal ('to quake').
