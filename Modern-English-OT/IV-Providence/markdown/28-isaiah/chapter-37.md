## Text

**37:1** Now when King Hezekiah heard this, he tore his garments, covered himself with sackcloth, and entered the house of the LORD.

**37:2** And he sent Eliakim, who was over the household, Shebna the scribe, and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah son of Amoz, the prophet.

**37:3** And they said to him, "This is what Hezekiah says: This day is a day of distress, rebuke, and contempt; for children have come to the point of birth, and there is no strength to give birth.

**37:4** Perhaps the LORD your God will hear the words of Rabshakeh, whom his master, the king of Assyria, has sent to revile the living God, and will rebuke the words that the LORD your God has heard. So lift up a prayer for the remnant that remains."

**37:5** So the servants of King Hezekiah came to Isaiah.

**37:6** And Isaiah said to them, "This is what you shall say to your master: 'This is what the LORD says: Do not be afraid because of the words that you have heard, with which the attendants of the king of Assyria have blasphemed Me.

**37:7** Look, I am putting a spirit in him, and he will hear a report and return to his own land; and I will make him fall by the sword in his own land.'"

**37:8** Then Rabshakeh returned and found the king of Assyria fighting against Libnah, for he had heard that he had departed from Lachish.

**37:9** Now he heard concerning Tirhakah, king of Cush, "He has come out to fight against you." When he heard it, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,

**37:10** "This is what you shall say to Hezekiah, king of Judah: 'Do not let your God, in whom you trust, deceive you by saying, "Jerusalem will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria."

**37:11** Look, you yourself have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, destroying them completely. And will you be rescued?

**37:12** Did the gods of the nations that my fathers destroyed rescue them—Gozan, Haran, Rezeph, and the sons of Eden who were in Telassar?

**37:13** Where is the king of Hamath, the king of Arpad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, of Hena, and of Ivvah?'"

**37:14** Then Hezekiah took the letter from the hand of the messengers and read it; and he went up to the house of the LORD, and Hezekiah spread it out before the LORD.

**37:15** And Hezekiah prayed to the LORD, saying,

**37:16** "LORD of hosts, God of Israel, enthroned above the cherubim, You alone are God over all the kingdoms of the earth; You made the heavens and the earth.

**37:17** Incline Your ear, LORD, and hear; open Your eyes, LORD, and see; and hear all the words of Sennacherib, which he has sent to revile the living God.

**37:18** Truly, LORD, the kings of Assyria have laid waste all the countries and their land,

**37:19** and have cast their gods into the fire, for they were not gods, but the work of human hands, wood and stone; so they destroyed them.

**37:20** And now, LORD our God, save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that You alone are the LORD."

**37:21** Then Isaiah son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Because you have prayed to Me concerning Sennacherib, king of Assyria,

**37:22** this is the word that the LORD has spoken against him: The virgin daughter of Zion despises you, she mocks you; the daughter of Jerusalem shakes her head behind you.

**37:23** Whom have you reviled and blasphemed? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes on high? Against the Holy One of Israel!

**37:24** By the hand of your servants you have reviled the Lord, and you have said, 'With my many chariots I have gone up to the height of the mountains, to the far recesses of Lebanon; I will cut down its tallest cedars, the choicest of its cypresses, and I will come to its farthest height, its lush forest.

**37:25** I dug wells and drank water, and with the sole of my feet I will dry up all the streams of Egypt.'

**37:26** Have you not heard? Long ago I did it; from days of old I formed it. Now I have brought it about, that you should turn fortified cities into heaps of ruins.

**37:27** And their inhabitants, of little strength, were dismayed and put to shame; they became plants of the field and tender green shoots, grass on the rooftops and a field before the standing grain.

**37:28** But I know your sitting down, your going out, and your coming in, and your raging against Me.

**37:29** Because of your raging against Me, and because your arrogance has come up into My ears, I will put My hook in your nose and My bridle in your lips, and I will turn you back along the way by which you came.

**37:30** And this shall be the sign for you: this year you shall eat what grows of itself, and in the second year what springs from that; and in the third year sow and reap, plant vineyards and eat their fruit.

**37:31** And the survivors of the house of Judah who remain will again take root downward and bear fruit upward.

**37:32** For out of Jerusalem a remnant will go forth, and survivors from Mount Zion. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will accomplish this.

**37:33** Therefore this is what the LORD says concerning the king of Assyria: He will not enter this city, nor shoot an arrow there, nor confront it with a shield, nor heap up a siege ramp against it.

**37:34** By the way that he came, he will return, and he will not enter this city, declares the LORD.

**37:35** For I will defend this city to save it, for My own sake and for the sake of My servant David."

**37:36** Then the angel of the LORD went out and struck down one hundred eighty-five thousand in the camp of Assyria; and when they rose early in the morning, look, all of them were dead bodies.

**37:37** So Sennacherib, king of Assyria, departed, went away, and returned, and he lived in Nineveh.

**37:38** And it happened as he was worshiping in the house of Nisroch his god that Adrammelech and Sharezer, his sons, struck him down with the sword, and they escaped to the land of Ararat. And Esarhaddon his son became king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **37:18** — Masoretic Isaiah literally reads "all the lands/countries (ha'aratsot) and their land (artsam)," whereas the parallel in 2 Kings 19:17 reads "the nations (haggoyim) and their land."
- **37:21** — Masoretic Isaiah omits the verb "I have heard" found at the end of 2 Kings 19:20, linking the causal clause ("Because you have prayed to Me...") directly into v. 22.
- **37:24** — "Its farthest height" translates Masoretic Isaiah's merom qitso, whereas the parallel in 2 Kings 19:23 reads "its farthest lodging place" (melon qitso).
- **37:27** — "Of little strength" renders the idiom qitsre-yad (lit. "short of hand"). At the end of the verse, Masoretic Isaiah reads "a field (shedemah) before the standing grain," whereas 2 Kings 19:26 reads "blighted grain" (shedephah).
- **37:30** — Ketiv/Qere: the written ketiv reads a Qal infinitive absolute ("and eating"), while the read qere conforms to the preceding imperatives with the second-person masculine plural Qal imperative "and eat" (we'ikhlu).
