## Text

**31:1** Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help and lean upon horses, trusting in chariots because they are numerous and in horsemen because they are exceedingly mighty, while they do not look to the Holy One of Israel and do not seek the LORD.

**31:2** Yet He too is wise and brings disaster, and He does not retract His words; He will rise up against the house of evildoers and against the help of those who practice iniquity.

**31:3** And the Egyptians are human and not God, and their horses are flesh and not spirit. When the LORD stretches out His hand, the one who helps will stumble and the one who is helped will fall, and all of them together will perish.

**31:4** For this is what the LORD has said to me: "Just as the lion, even the young lion, growls over its prey when a full band of shepherds is summoned against it—it is not dismayed at their voice nor humbled by their uproar—so the LORD of hosts will come down to wage war upon Mount Zion and upon its hill.

**31:5** Like hovering birds, so the LORD of hosts will shield Jerusalem; shielding it, He will also deliver it; passing over it, He will also rescue it."

**31:6** Return to Him from whom the sons of Israel have deeply revolted.

**31:7** For in that day each one will reject his idols of silver and his idols of gold, which your own hands have made for you as a sin.

**31:8** And Assyria will fall by a sword not of a man, and a sword not of a mortal will devour him; he will flee from the sword, and his young men will become forced laborers.

**31:9** And his rock will pass away in terror, and his officials will be terrified at the standard, declares the LORD, whose fire is in Zion and whose furnace is in Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **31:5** — Hebrew pairs two Qal infinitives absolute with Hiphil verbs (ganon wehitsil pasoah wehimlit), using pasoah—the Passover verb—for "passing over" or "sparing."
- **31:6** — The 2mp imperative shubu ("Return") shifts to the 3mp Hiphil perfect he'miqu ("have deeply revolted"), taking "the sons of Israel" as its subject (alternatively a vocative, "from whom they have deeply revolted, sons of Israel").
- **31:9** — "His rock" (sal'o) metaphorically denotes Assyria's ruler or stronghold, and "at the standard" (minnes) plays on "will flee" (wenas) in v. 8.
