## Text

**35:1** The wilderness and the parched ground will be glad, and the arid plain will exult and bloom like a crocus.

**35:2** It will bloom richly and exult, yes, with exultation and ringing shouts. The glory of Lebanon is given to it, the splendor of Carmel and Sharon. They themselves will see the glory of the LORD, the splendor of our God.

**35:3** Strengthen drooping hands, and make stumbling knees firm.

**35:4** Say to those of agitated heart, "Be strong; do not be afraid! Look, your God will come with vengeance, with God's repayment; He Himself will come and save you."

**35:5** Then the eyes of the blind will open wide, and the ears of the deaf will be opened.

**35:6** Then the lame will bound like a deer, and the tongue of the mute will ring out with joy; for waters will burst out in the wilderness, and streams in the arid plain.

**35:7** And the mirage will turn into a pool, and the thirsty land into springs of water; in the lair of jackals, its resting place, grass will become reeds and papyrus.

**35:8** And a highway and a road will be there, and it will be called the Way of Holiness. No unclean person will pass over it, but it will be for them; whoever walks the way, even fools, will not stray.

**35:9** There will be no lion there, and no predatory beast will climb onto it; none will be found there, but the redeemed will walk on it.

**35:10** And those ransomed by the LORD will return and enter Zion with ringing shouts, with eternal joy upon their heads; gladness and joy they will attain, and grief and groaning will flee.

## Translator Notes

- **35:1** — The MT verb yesusum ('will be glad') displays an unusual final -m ending on a third-person plural Qal imperfect of sws, often explained as assimilation of a paragogic nun before the initial m of midbar ('wilderness').
- **35:7** — The noun sharab can mean either 'mirage' or 'scorching heat/ground' (cf. Isa 49:10); in the second half, chatsir ('grass') may also be taken as its homonym meaning 'abode/enclosure' (echoing 34:13).
- **35:8** — The clause wehu'-lamo ('but it will be for them') is syntactically terse; taking lamo as plural ('for them,' following the Masoretic punctuation) distinguishes the redeemed travelers from the unclean, though some construe lamo as singular ('for him who walks the way').
