## Text

**17:1** The pronouncement concerning Damascus: Look, Damascus is being removed from being a city, and it will become a fallen heap of ruins.

**17:2** Abandoned are the cities of Aroer; they will belong to flocks, and they will lie down with no one to alarm them.

**17:3** The fortress will cease from Ephraim, and kingship from Damascus, and the remnant of Aram—they will be like the glory of the sons of Israel, declares the LORD of hosts.

**17:4** And it will come about on that day that the glory of Jacob will dwindle, and the fatness of his flesh will waste away.

**17:5** And it will be as when a harvester gathers standing grain and his arm harvests the heads of grain; and it will be like someone gleaning heads of grain in the Valley of Rephaim.

**17:6** Yet gleanings will remain in it, as at the beating of an olive tree: two or three berries at the tip of the topmost bough, four or five on the branches of a fruit-bearing tree, declares the LORD, the God of Israel.

**17:7** On that day man will look toward his Maker, and his eyes will gaze upon the Holy One of Israel.

**17:8** And he will not look toward the altars, the work of his hands, nor will he gaze upon what his fingers have made—the Asherah poles and the incense altars.

**17:9** On that day the cities of his stronghold will be like the abandoned places of the woodland and the summit, which they abandoned before the sons of Israel; and it will become a desolation.

**17:10** For you have forgotten the God of your salvation and have not remembered the Rock of your stronghold. Therefore you plant plantings of delight and sow alien vine shoots.

**17:11** On the day of your planting you make it grow, and in the morning you bring your seed to blossom; yet gone is the harvest on a day of sickness and incurable pain.

**17:12** Woe! The roar of many peoples—like the roaring of seas they roar! And the thunder of nations—like the thunder of mighty waters they thunder!

**17:13** Nations thunder like the thunder of many waters, yet He will rebuke them, and they will flee far away, and be pursued like chaff of the mountains before the wind and like tumbleweed before a whirlwind.

**17:14** At evening time, look—terror! Before morning, they are gone. This is the share of those who despoil us, and the lot for those who loot us.

## Translator Notes

- **17:6** — Hebrew bis'ifeha poriyyah ("on the branches of a fruit-bearing tree") uses an anticipatory feminine singular suffix ("its branches") explained by the appositional Qal feminine active participle poriyyah ("fruit-bearing").
- **17:9** — The Masoretic Text reads hahoresh weha'amir ("the woodland and the summit/topmost bough," echoing 'amir in v. 6); the Septuagint reflects a different consonantal reading ("the Hivites and the Amorites").
- **17:11** — Hebrew tesagsegi ("you make it grow") is a hapax legomenon (Pilpel imperfect), alternatively derived from sug ("hedge/fence in"). Hebrew ned ("gone") is taken as a verbal form from nud ("flee/vanish"), though as pointed it also resembles the noun ned ("a heap"); nahalah ("sickness," from halah) puns on nahalah ("inheritance").
- **17:13** — Hebrew shifts to collective masculine singular forms in vv. 13-14 (bo, "it/him"; nas, "flees"; ruddaf, Pual "is pursued"; 'einennu, "it is no more") referring to the assembled nations; these are smoothed to plural "them/they" in English.
