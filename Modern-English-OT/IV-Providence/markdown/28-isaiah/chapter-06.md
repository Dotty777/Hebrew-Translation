## Text

**6:1** In the year of the death of King Uzziah, I saw the Lord seated on a throne, lofty and lifted up, and the hems of His robe were filling the temple.

**6:2** Seraphim were standing above Him; each one had six wings: with two he would cover his face, and with two he would cover his feet, and with two he would fly.

**6:3** And one would call to another and say, "Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the fullness of all the earth is His glory."

**6:4** And the bases of the thresholds trembled at the voice of the one calling, and the house was filling with smoke.

**6:5** Then I said, "Woe to me, for I am destroyed, because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips; for my eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts."

**6:6** And one of the seraphim flew to me, and in his hand was a glowing coal that he had taken with tongs from the altar.

**6:7** He touched it to my mouth and said, "Look, this has touched your lips; your iniquity has departed, and your sin is atoned for."

**6:8** And I heard the Lord's voice saying, "Whom shall I send, and who will go for Us?" So I said, "Here I am; send me."

**6:9** And He said, "Go and tell this people, 'Keep hearing, yet do not understand; and keep seeing, yet do not know.'

**6:10** Make the heart of this people fat, make its ears heavy, and smear its eyes shut, otherwise it might see with its eyes, and hear with its ears, and understand with its heart, and turn back and be healed."

**6:11** Then I said, "How long, Lord?" And He said, "Until cities fall into ruins without an inhabitant, and houses without a human being, and the ground is ruined into a desolation,

**6:12** and the LORD has sent humanity far away, and forsakenness grows vast in the midst of the land.

**6:13** And even though a tenth still remains in it, it will again be for burning—like a terebinth and like an oak which, when felled, still have a stump in them; a holy seed is its stump."

## Translator Notes

- **6:3** — The clause "the fullness of all the earth is His glory" follows the Hebrew construct noun melo ("fullness") literally, meaning that His glory is what fills the entire earth.
- **6:4** — Hebrew ammot hassippim ("bases of the thresholds") uses a rare architectural sense of ammah referring to the structural foundations or door-pivots supporting the temple thresholds.
- **6:10** — The three Hiphil imperatives literally command making the people's heart "fat" (an idiom for dull and unresponsive), its ears "heavy," and its eyes "smeared shut."
- **6:13** — Hebrew lebaer (Piel infinitive construct of b'r) means "for burning" or "for consuming," and beshalleket denotes either the felling of trees or the shedding of leaves, leaving behind a stump (matstsebet).
