## Text

**61:1** The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on me, because the LORD has anointed me to announce good news to the humble; He has sent me to bandage those broken in heart, to proclaim release to the captives and freedom to those who are bound,

**61:2** to proclaim a year of the LORD's favor and a day of our God's vengeance, to console all mourners,

**61:3** to provide for the mourners of Zion, to give them a headdress in place of ashes, oil of joy in place of mourning, a cloak of praise in place of a failing spirit. And they will be called terebinths of righteousness, a planting of the LORD, so that He may glorify Himself.

**61:4** They will build up the ruins of old; they will raise up the desolations of former times, and they will renew the cities of ruin, the desolations of generation after generation.

**61:5** Strangers will stand and shepherd your flock, and foreigners will be your plowmen and your vineyard workers.

**61:6** But you will be called "Priests of the LORD," and it will be said of you, "Ministers of our God." You will consume the wealth of nations, and you will boast in their glory.

**61:7** In place of your shame there will be a double share, and in place of disgrace they will shout for joy over their portion; therefore in their land they will inherit a double share, and everlasting gladness will belong to them.

**61:8** For I, the LORD, love justice; I hate robbery in a burnt offering. And I will give their recompense in faithfulness, and I will make an everlasting covenant with them.

**61:9** Their seed will become known among the nations, and their posterity in the midst of the peoples; everyone who sees them will recognize them, that they are a seed whom the LORD has blessed.

**61:10** I rejoice exceedingly in the LORD; my soul exults in my God, for He has dressed me in garments of salvation, and He has wrapped me in a robe of righteousness, like a bridegroom who puts on a headdress like a priest, and like a bride who arrays herself with her ornaments.

**61:11** For just as the earth brings out its sprouts, and as a garden makes its sown seeds sprout, so the Lord GOD will make righteousness and praise sprout in the sight of all the nations.

## Translator Notes

- **61:1** — The rare reduplicated noun peqach-qoach, translated 'freedom,' derives from the root paqach ('to open,' usually of eyes) and literally means a 'wide opening,' pointing to the opening of the prison or release from dungeon darkness into light.
- **61:3** — The Hebrew employs wordplay between pe'er ('headdress') and epher ('ashes'), and concludes with the Hithpael infinitive lehithpa'er ('to glorify Himself' or 'display His splendor') from the same root as 'headdress.'
- **61:6** — The rare Hithpael verb tithyammaru ('you will boast') is usually derived from a by-form of amar ('to boast, exalt oneself'), though some derive it from mur ('to exchange,' i.e., succeed to their glory).
- **61:8** — The Masoretic pointing be'olah literally reads 'in a burnt offering' (rejecting stolen goods offered to God), though the same consonants can represent a contracted spelling of 'awlah, 'wrongdoing' or 'injustice.'
- **61:10** — The verb yekahen ('puts on... like a priest') is the Piel of kahan, normally 'to serve as a priest,' here paired with pe'er ('headdress') to evoke a priest's ceremonial turban (cf. 61:3, 6).
