## Text

**7:1** Now it happened in the days of Ahaz, son of Jotham, son of Uzziah, king of Judah, that Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem for war against it, yet he was not able to fight against it.

**7:2** When it was reported to the house of David, "Aram has settled upon Ephraim," his heart and the heart of his people trembled as the trees of a forest tremble before the wind.

**7:3** Then the LORD said to Isaiah, "Go out now to meet Ahaz, you and your son Shear-jashub, at the end of the water channel of the upper pool, by the highway of the launderer's field,

**7:4** and say to him, 'Take care and keep calm; do not be afraid, and do not let your heart grow faint because of these two tail ends of smoking firebrands, at the burning anger of Rezin and Aram and the son of Remaliah.

**7:5** Because Aram has devised evil against you, along with Ephraim and the son of Remaliah, saying,

**7:6** "Let us go up against Judah and fill it with dread, and breach it for ourselves, and install a king in its midst, the son of Tabeel,"

**7:7** this is what the Lord GOD says: "It will not stand, and it will not happen.

**7:8** For the head of Aram is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is Rezin; and within another sixty-five years Ephraim will be shattered from being a people.

**7:9** And the head of Ephraim is Samaria, and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah. If you do not trust, surely you will not remain firm."'"

**7:10** And the LORD spoke again to Ahaz, saying,

**7:11** "Ask a sign for yourself from the LORD your God; go deep in asking, or go high above."

**7:12** Then Ahaz said, "I will not ask, and I will not test the LORD."

**7:13** Then he said, "Listen now, house of David: is it too small a thing for you to weary men, that you weary my God as well?

**7:14** Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: see, the young woman is pregnant and will give birth to a son, and she will call his name Immanuel.

**7:15** He will eat curds and honey by the time he knows how to reject the evil and choose the good.

**7:16** For before the boy knows how to reject the evil and choose the good, the land that you dread because of its two kings will be forsaken.

**7:17** The LORD will bring on you, and on your people, and on your father's house, days that have not come since the day Ephraim departed from Judah—the king of Assyria.

**7:18** And it will happen on that day that the LORD will whistle for the fly that is at the end of the streams of Egypt, and for the bee that is in the land of Assyria.

**7:19** And all of them will come and settle in the precipitous ravines, and in the crevices of the crags, and on all the thornbushes, and at all the watering places.

**7:20** On that day, with a razor hired from the regions beyond the River—with the king of Assyria—the Lord will shave the head and the hair of the feet, and it will sweep away the beard as well.

**7:21** And it will happen on that day that a man will keep alive a young cow of the herd and two sheep,

**7:22** and it will happen that because of the abundance of milk they produce, he will eat curds; for everyone remaining in the midst of the land will eat curds and honey.

**7:23** And it will happen on that day that every spot where there are a thousand vines, valued at a thousand silver pieces, will belong to briers and thorns.

**7:24** With arrows and a bow one will go there, for the whole land will be briers and thorns.

**7:25** And as for all the hills that used to be hoed with a hoe, you will not go there out of fear of briers and thorns; instead, it will become a place for turning an ox loose and a trampling ground for sheep."

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The singular verbs "went up" and "was not able" agree grammatically with the first-named subject, Rezin; "not able to fight against it" idiomatically expresses inability to capture the city in battle.
- **7:3** — The Hebrew name Shear-jashub means "A remnant will return," echoing the verb "turn back" in 6:10.
- **7:9** — The concluding warning plays on two stems of the same Hebrew root 'mn: Hiphil ta'aminu ("trust" or "believe") and Niphal te'amenu ("remain firm" or "be established").
- **7:11** — The Masoretic pointing she'alah represents the lengthened pausal Qal imperative of sh'l ("ask"), giving "go deep in asking"; an alternative vocalization (she'olah, reflected in several ancient versions) reads "as deep as Sheol."
- **7:14** — Hebrew ha'almah denotes a young woman of marriageable age, normally unmarried (rendered parthenos, "virgin," in the Greek Septuagint); Immanuel means "God is with us," and weqara't is an archaic third-person feminine singular form ("and she will call").
