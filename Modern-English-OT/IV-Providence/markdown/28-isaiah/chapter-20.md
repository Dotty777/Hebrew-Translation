## Text

**20:1** In the year that the chief commander came to Ashdod, when Sargon king of Assyria sent him, he fought against Ashdod and captured it.

**20:2** At that time the LORD spoke through Isaiah son of Amoz, saying, "Go and untie the sackcloth from your waist, and remove your sandals from your feet." And he did so, walking naked and barefoot.

**20:3** Then the LORD said, "Just as My servant Isaiah has walked naked and barefoot for three years as a sign and a portent against Egypt and against Cush,

**20:4** so the king of Assyria will drive away the captives of Egypt and the exiles of Cush, young and old, naked and barefoot, with bared buttocks, Egypt's nakedness.

**20:5** And they will be dismayed and put to shame because of Cush, their hope, and because of Egypt, their pride.

**20:6** And the inhabitants of this coastland will say in that day, 'Look, so it is with our hope, to which we fled for help to be rescued from the king of Assyria! And how can we ourselves escape?'"

## Translator Notes

- **20:1** — "The chief commander" translates Hebrew tartan, a loanword from the Assyrian military title turtanu ("commander-in-chief").
- **20:2** — "Your sandals from your feet" translates collective singular forms in the Masoretic Text (literally "your sandal from your foot").
- **20:4** — "With bared buttocks" translates a rare Qal passive participle masculine plural construct (chasufay) with the noun shet ("buttocks," cf. 2 Sam 10:4).
