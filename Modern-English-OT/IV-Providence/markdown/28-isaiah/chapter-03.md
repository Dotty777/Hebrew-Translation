## Text

**3:1** For look, the Lord, the LORD of hosts, is removing from Jerusalem and from Judah support and mainstay, every support of bread and every support of water;

**3:2** hero and man of war, judge and prophet, diviner and elder,

**3:3** commander of fifty and honored man, counselor, skilled craftsman, and expert in charms.

**3:4** And I will make youths their officials, and willful children will rule over them.

**3:5** And the people will oppress one another, one against another and each against his neighbor; the youth will act arrogantly toward the elder, and the disdained toward the honored.

**3:6** When a man takes hold of his brother in his father's house, saying, "You have a cloak; be our leader, and let this ruin be under your hand,"

**3:7** on that day he will speak up, saying, "I will not be a binder of wounds, and in my house there is no bread and no cloak; you must not make me leader of the people."

**3:8** For Jerusalem has stumbled and Judah has fallen, because their tongue and their deeds are against the LORD, to defy the eyes of His glory.

**3:9** The appearance of their faces testifies against them, and they proclaim their sin like Sodom; they do not conceal it. Woe to their souls, for they have dealt evil to themselves!

**3:10** Say of the righteous that it will be well, for they will eat the fruit of their deeds.

**3:11** Woe to the wicked! It will go ill, for the repayment of his hands will be done to him.

**3:12** My people—a child is their taskmaster, and women rule over them. My people, those who direct you are leading you astray, and they confound the way of your paths.

**3:13** The LORD stations Himself to contend, and He stands to judge peoples.

**3:14** The LORD comes into judgment with the elders of His people and its officials: "You yourselves have consumed the vineyard; what was robbed from the poor is in your houses.

**3:15** What right do you have to crush My people and grind the faces of the poor?" declares the Lord GOD of hosts.

**3:16** And the LORD said, "Because the daughters of Zion are haughty and walk with necks stretched out, flirting with their eyes, tripping along as they walk and jingling with their feet,

**3:17** therefore the Lord will cover the scalps of the daughters of Zion with scabs, and the LORD will expose their private parts."

**3:18** On that day the Lord will remove the splendor of the anklets, the sun-disks, and the crescents,

**3:19** the ear-drops, the bracelets, and the veils,

**3:20** the headdresses, the ankle chains, the sashes, the scent bottles, and the charms,

**3:21** the rings and the nose rings,

**3:22** the festive robes, the mantles, the shawls, and the purses,

**3:23** and the hand mirrors, the fine linens, the turbans, and the wraps.

**3:24** And it will be that instead of perfume there will be decay; instead of a belt, a rope; instead of braided hair, baldness; instead of a fine robe, a wrapping of sackcloth; and a brand mark instead of beauty.

**3:25** Your men will fall by the sword, and your might in war.

**3:26** And her gates will grieve and mourn, and emptied out, she will sit on the ground.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The masculine and feminine pairing מַשְׁעֵן וּמַשְׁעֵנָה (lit. "support and prop", both from the root שׁען) is a Hebrew merism expressing every kind of support.
- **3:15** — Ketiv reads מלכם (a contracted one-word spelling), while Qere separates it into two words, מַה־לָּכֶם ("what is it to you?"), which is translated here.
- **3:16** — Ketiv reads נטוות while Qere reads נְטוּיוֹת (Qal passive participle feminine plural of נטה, "stretched out"); both convey the same sense with an orthographic/morphological variation.
- **3:17** — The hapax Piel verb שִׂפַּח is traditionally linked to סַפַּחַת ("scab"), though some take it as "make bare/bald"; פׇּתְהֵן ("their private parts") is a euphemism paired with Piel יְעָרֶה ("expose/lay bare").
- **3:24** — The closing phrase כִּי־תַחַת יֹפִי uses the rare noun כִּי ("brand/burn mark", from the root כוה) rather than the conjunction "for", reversing the preceding "instead of X, Y" sequence into a chiastic climax.
