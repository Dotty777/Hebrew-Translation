## Text

**48:1** Hear this, house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel and came forth from the waters of Judah, who swear by the name of the LORD and make mention of the God of Israel, not in truth and not in righteousness.

**48:2** For they are called after the holy city and rely on the God of Israel; the LORD of hosts is His name.

**48:3** The former things I declared from long ago; out of My mouth they went forth, and I made them heard. Suddenly I acted, and they came about.

**48:4** Because I knew that you are stubborn, and your neck is a sinew of iron and your forehead is bronze,

**48:5** I declared it to you from long ago; before it came about I made you hear it, so that you would not say, "My idol made them, and my carved image and my cast image commanded them."

**48:6** You have heard; see all of it. And as for you, will you not declare it? From now on I cause you to hear new things, secret things that you have not known.

**48:7** Now they are created, and not from long ago; and before today you did not hear them, so that you would not say, "Look, I knew them."

**48:8** You have neither heard nor known, nor from long ago has your ear opened; for I knew that you would indeed act treacherously, and you were called a transgressor from the womb.

**48:9** For My name's sake I delay My anger, and for My praise I curb it for you, so as not to cut you off.

**48:10** Look, I have refined you, though not as silver; I have tested you in the smelting furnace of affliction.

**48:11** For My own sake, for My own sake, I will act; for how could it be profaned? And I will not give My glory to another.

**48:12** Listen to Me, Jacob, and Israel, My called one: I am He; I am the first, and also I am the last.

**48:13** Indeed, My hand laid the foundation of the earth, and My right hand spread out the heavens; I call to them, and they stand up together.

**48:14** Gather together, all of you, and listen! Who among them has declared these things? The LORD loves him; he will do His pleasure on Babylon, and His arm will be against the Chaldeans.

**48:15** I, I Myself have spoken; yes, I have called him. I have brought him, and his way will succeed.

**48:16** Come close to Me, hear this: from the start I did not speak in secret; from the time it came to be, there I was. And now the Lord GOD has sent me, and His Spirit.

**48:17** This is what the LORD, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel, says: "I am the LORD your God, who teaches you for your benefit, who guides you in the way you should walk.

**48:18** If only you had given heed to My commandments, then your peace would have been like a river and your righteousness like the waves of the sea;

**48:19** and your offspring would have been like the sand, and the descendants from your body like its grains; its name would not be cut off or destroyed from before Me."

**48:20** Go out from Babylon, flee from the Chaldeans! With a voice of joyful shouting declare it, make this heard, send it forth to the end of the earth; say, "The LORD has redeemed His servant Jacob."

**48:21** They did not become thirsty when He led them through the waste places; He caused water to stream from the rock for them; He split open the rock, and water gushed out.

**48:22** "There is no peace," the LORD says, "for the wicked."

## Translator Notes

- **48:1** — The Hebrew literally reads "from the waters of Judah," using "waters" as a poetic idiom for physical descent or lineage.
- **48:10** — The Hebrew Qal verb normally means "I have chosen you," though in parallel with "refined" and "smelting furnace" it is widely understood as a cognate or byform meaning "test, examine."
- **48:11** — The Niphal imperfect verb "could be profaned" has no expressed subject in the Masoretic Text; it refers back to "My name" in verse 9.
- **48:16** — The final clause shifts from the LORD's direct speech to the messenger or Servant; the syntax of "and His Spirit" allows it to be read either as a second subject alongside the Lord GOD or as a second object sent with the speaker.
- **48:19** — The Hebrew forms a wordplay between "your body" and the rare noun rendered "its grains," referring to the grains of sand.
