## Text

**18:1** Ah, land of buzzing wings, which lies beyond the rivers of Cush,

**18:2** sending envoys by the sea and in papyrus vessels upon the surface of the waters. Go, swift messengers, to a nation tall and smooth, to a people dreaded far and wide, a nation of might and trampling, whose land rivers cut through.

**18:3** All inhabitants of the world and dwellers on the earth, when a banner is lifted on the mountains, look, and when a horn is blown, listen.

**18:4** For this is what the LORD has said to me: "I will stay quiet and watch from My dwelling place, like glowing heat in sunlight, like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest."

**18:5** For before the harvest, when the blossoming is over and the flower becomes a ripening grape, He will cut off the shoots with pruning knives, and the spreading branches He will remove and lop off.

**18:6** They will be left together for the birds of prey of the mountains and for the beasts of the earth; the birds of prey will pass the summer on them, and all the beasts of the earth will pass the winter on them.

**18:7** At that time tribute will be brought to the LORD of hosts from a people tall and smooth, and from a people dreaded far and wide, a nation of might and trampling, whose land rivers cut through, to the place of the name of the LORD of hosts, Mount Zion.

## Translator Notes

- **18:1** — "Buzzing wings" translates a rare construct phrase (tsiltsal kenafayim) from an onomatopoeic root meaning "buzz" or "whir" (cf. Deut 28:42), though some connect it to a homonymous root meaning "shadow."
- **18:2** — Several rare expressions appear here: "tall and smooth" is literally "drawn out and burnished" (Pual participles of mashakh and marat); "far and wide" is literally "from it and onward"; "might and trampling" renders an obscure reduplicated form (qav-qav) paired with "trampling"; and "cut through" translates a rare verb (baza) attested only here and in 18:7.
- **18:4** — The ketiv spells the verb plene (with a vav) while the qere spells it defectively (without a vav); both represent the Qal 1st-person singular cohortative of shaqat ("stay quiet"). The Masoretic Text also reads "in the heat of harvest."
- **18:7** — The Masoretic Text has the preposition "from" (min) only before the second phrase ("and from a people"), either serving poetic double duty for the first phrase as well or leaving "a people tall and smooth" in apposition to "tribute."
