## Text

**15:1** The pronouncement concerning Moab: Truly, in a night Ar of Moab is ravaged, it is cut off; truly, in a night Kir of Moab is ravaged, it is cut off.

**15:2** Bayith and Dibon have gone up to the high places to weep; over Nebo and over Medeba Moab wails. On all its heads is baldness; every beard is shaved off.

**15:3** In its streets they have wrapped themselves in sackcloth; on its rooftops and in its public squares everyone wails, going down in weeping.

**15:4** Heshbon and Elealeh cry out; as far as Jahaz their voice is heard. Therefore the armed warriors of Moab shout aloud; its soul quivers within it.

**15:5** My heart cries out for Moab; its fugitives flee as far as Zoar, to Eglath-shelishiyah. For on the ascent of Luhith one goes up with weeping; for along the road to Horonaim they stir up a cry of ruin.

**15:6** For the waters of Nimrim will become desolations; indeed, the grass has dried up, the tender shoots have vanished, no greenery remains.

**15:7** Therefore the surplus it has acquired and their stored possessions they carry across the Wadi of the Willows.

**15:8** For the outcry has encircled the border of Moab; as far as Eglaim reaches its wailing, and to Beer-elim its wailing.

**15:9** For the waters of Dimon are full of blood; indeed, I will lay still more upon Dimon: a lion for those of Moab who escape, and for the remnant of the land.

## Translator Notes

- **15:2** — Hebrew habbayit literally means "the house" and is taken here as a Moabite town name (Bayith) paired with Dibon following the Masoretic disjunctive accent on Dibon; alternatively, "he [Moab] has gone up to the temple, and Dibon to the high places."
- **15:3** — The phrase "going down in weeping" renders the Hebrew Qal participle yored babbeki, a vivid idiom for collapsing or dissolving in tears.
- **15:5** — Hebrew beriheha is a defective plural of bariah ("fugitives"; or from beriah, "bars"). "Eglath-shelishiyah" is taken as a Moabite place name near Zoar (literally "third Eglath" or "a three-year-old heifer"); the rare verb ye'o'eru ("they stir up") is a reduplicated stem form of 'ur ("rouse").
- **15:7** — Hebrew yitrah 'asah is an asyndetic relative clause with a singular verb for collective Moab ("the surplus it has acquired"), shifting immediately to plural suffixes and verb ("their stored possessions they carry"). "Wadi of the Willows" (nahal ha'aravim) may also refer to Euphrates poplars or to the Arabah.
