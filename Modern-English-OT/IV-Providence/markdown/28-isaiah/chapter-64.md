## Text

**64:1** As fire ignites brushwood and fire sets water boiling, to make Your name known to Your foes, nations tremble at Your presence.

**64:2** When You performed awesome deeds we did not anticipate, You came down; mountains quaked at Your presence.

**64:3** And from ancient times they have not heard nor given ear, nor has an eye seen a God apart from You who acts for the one who waits for Him.

**64:4** You meet the one who rejoices and practices righteousness, those who remember You in Your ways. Look, You were angry, and we sinned; in them we have been a long time, and shall we be saved?

**64:5** And we have all become like someone unclean, and all our righteous deeds are like a menstrual cloth; we all wither like a leaf, and our iniquities, like the wind, carry us away.

**64:6** And there is no one who calls on Your name, who stirs himself to lay hold of You, because You have hidden Your face from us and melted us in the hand of our iniquity.

**64:7** Yet now, LORD, You are our Father; we are the clay and You are our potter; all of us are the work of Your hand.

**64:8** Do not be exceedingly angry, LORD, and do not remember iniquity forever. Now, please look: all of us are Your people.

**64:9** Your holy cities have turned into a wilderness; Zion has turned into a wilderness, Jerusalem a wasteland.

**64:10** Our holy and splendid house, where our fathers praised You, has been burned up with fire, and all our treasured things have become a ruin.

**64:11** Will You hold Yourself back over these things, LORD? Will You remain silent and afflict us exceedingly?

## Translator Notes

- **64:1** — MT 64:1-11 corresponds to 64:2-12 in most English Bibles (where MT 63:19b is 64:1), and this verse continues the theophany plea of 63:19. The noun hamasim ("brushwood") is a hapax legomenon denoting dry twigs or kindling.
- **64:2** — The verb nazollu ("quaked") is pointed as a Niphal perfect of zll II ("shake, quake"), matching 63:19, though its sound also evokes nzl ("flow, melt").
- **64:4** — The closing clause bahem olam weniwwashea ("in them we have been a long time, and shall we be saved?") is sharply elliptic in the Masoretic Text; "in them" is usually taken to refer to the sins just confessed, with a verb of continuance understood before the Niphal niwwashea.
- **64:6** — The Masoretic form wattemugenu is a transitive Qal wayyiqtol of mwg ("melt, dissolve"), whereas ancient versions such as the Septuagint and Syriac reflect the root mgn ("deliver up").
