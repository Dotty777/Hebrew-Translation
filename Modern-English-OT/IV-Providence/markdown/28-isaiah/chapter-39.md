## Text

**39:1** At that time Merodach-baladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, for he heard that he had fallen sick and had grown strong again.

**39:2** Hezekiah rejoiced over them and showed them his treasure house, the silver, the gold, the spices, and the fine oil, as well as his entire armory and everything found in his treasuries. There was not a thing in his house or in all his realm that Hezekiah did not show them.

**39:3** Then the prophet Isaiah came to King Hezekiah and said to him, "What did these men say, and where do they come from to you?" And Hezekiah said, "From a distant land they have come to me, from Babylon."

**39:4** He said, "What did they see in your house?" Hezekiah said, "Everything that is in my house they saw; there was not a thing in my treasuries that I did not show them."

**39:5** Isaiah then said to Hezekiah, "Listen to the word of the LORD of hosts:

**39:6** Look, days are coming when everything in your house and what your fathers have stored up until this day will be carried off to Babylon; nothing will remain, says the LORD.

**39:7** And some of your sons who come forth from you, whom you will father, will be taken, and they will become eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon."

**39:8** Then Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "Good is the word of the LORD that you have spoken." And he said, "For there will be peace and truth in my days."

## Translator Notes

- **39:1** — The verb vayyechezaq ("and had grown strong again," Qal of chazaq) creates a Hebrew wordplay on the name Hezekiah (Chizqiyyahu).
- **39:2** — Hebrew bet nekotoh ("his treasure house") uses an archaic third-person masculine singular suffix and a loanword related to Akkadian bit nakamti ("storehouse").
- **39:7** — Hebrew yiqqachu is a pausal Qal imperfect third-person masculine plural of laqach with an indefinite subject ("they will take"), rendered passively here as "will be taken."
