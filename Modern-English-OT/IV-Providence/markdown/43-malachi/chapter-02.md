## Text

**2:1** "And now, this command is for you, priests.

**2:2** If you will not listen, and if you will not take it to heart to give honor to My name," says the LORD of armies, "then I will send the curse among you and I will curse your blessings; indeed, I have already cursed them, because you are not taking it to heart.

**2:3** Look, I am about to rebuke your offspring, and I will spread dung on your faces, the dung of your festivals; and you will be carried off with it.

**2:4** Then you will know that I have sent this command to you, so that My covenant with Levi may remain," says the LORD of armies.

**2:5** "My covenant with him was one of life and peace, and I gave them to him for reverence; so he feared Me and before My name he was filled with awe.

**2:6** Instruction of truth was in his mouth, and wrongdoing was not found on his lips; in peace and uprightness he walked with Me, and many he turned back from iniquity.

**2:7** For a priest's lips should keep knowledge, and people should seek instruction from his mouth; for he is the messenger of the LORD of armies.

**2:8** But you yourselves have turned aside from the way; you have caused many to stumble in the instruction; you have corrupted the covenant of Levi," says the LORD of armies.

**2:9** "So I also have made you despised and brought low before all the people, just as you are not keeping My ways, but are showing partiality in the instruction."

**2:10** Is there not one Father for all of us? Did not one God create us? Why do we break faith, each man with his brother, so as to profane the covenant of our fathers?

**2:11** Judah has broken faith, and an abomination has been done in Israel and in Jerusalem; for Judah has profaned what is holy to the LORD, which He loves, and has married the daughter of a foreign god.

**2:12** As for the man who does this, may the LORD cut off from the tents of Jacob both the one who wakes and the one who answers, and the one who presents an offering to the LORD of armies.

**2:13** And this second thing you do: you cover the altar of the LORD with tears, with weeping and groaning, because He no longer turns toward the offering nor receives it favorably from your hand.

**2:14** Yet you say, "For what cause?" Because the LORD has borne witness between you and the wife of your youth, with whom you yourself have broken faith, even though she is your partner and the wife of your covenant.

**2:15** Did He not make them one, though a remnant of spirit belonged to Him? And why the one? He was seeking godly offspring. Therefore guard yourselves in your spirit, and let none of you break faith with the wife of his youth.

**2:16** For He hates divorce, says the LORD, the God of Israel, and violence covers his garment, says the LORD of armies. Therefore guard yourselves in your spirit, and do not break faith.

**2:17** You have wearied the LORD with your words. Yet you say, "In what way have we wearied Him?" In that you say, "Everyone who does evil is good in the eyes of the LORD, and in them He delights," or, "Where is the God of justice?"

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — Masoretic Hebrew reads 'rebuking for you the seed/offspring' (et-hazzera), which can denote either priestly descendants or sown grain. The final verb is an impersonal third-person singular Qal (wenasa, literally 'and one will carry you away to/with it').
- **2:12** — Hebrew er weoneh, literally 'one who wakes and one who answers,' is an idiomatic merism meaning every living person or descendant without exception.
- **2:15** — The Hebrew of the first half is highly compressed and syntactically difficult; an alternative reading takes 'the One' as the subject throughout ('Did not the One make them, and a remnant of spirit belongs to Him? And what does the One seek?'). At the end of the verse, Hebrew shifts from second-person singular ('your youth') to a third-person singular jussive ('let him not break faith').
- **2:16** — Masoretic Hebrew has the third-person singular Qal perfect sane ('He hates') with the Piel infinitive construct shallach ('sending away/divorce'), followed by Piel perfect wekhissah ('and violence covers his garment' or 'and he covers his garment with violence'). Other translations render sane as first-person ('I hate divorce') or take the husband as subject ('the one who hates and divorces covers his garment with violence').
