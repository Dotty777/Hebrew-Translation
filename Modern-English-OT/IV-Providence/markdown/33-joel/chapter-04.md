## Text

**4:1** For look, during those days and at that time, when I bring back the fortunes of Judah and Jerusalem,

**4:2** I will assemble all the nations and bring them down into the Valley of Jehoshaphat, and I will contend in judgment with them there on account of My people and My heritage Israel, whom they dispersed among the nations; and they apportioned My land.

**4:3** And over My people they cast lots; they gave a boy for a prostitute, and they sold a girl for wine and drank.

**4:4** And furthermore, what are you to Me, Tyre and Sidon, and all the districts of Philistia? Is it retaliation that you are paying back to Me? And if you are retaliating against Me, quickly, speedily I will bring back your retaliation upon your head.

**4:5** For you took My silver and My gold, and you brought My choice treasures into your temples,

**4:6** and you sold the children of Judah and the children of Jerusalem to the Greeks, so as to put them far away from their border.

**4:7** Look, I am rousing them from the place to which you sold them, and I will bring back your retaliation upon your head.

**4:8** And I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hand of the children of Judah, and they will sell them to the Sabeans, to a far-off nation, for the LORD has spoken.

**4:9** Announce this among the nations: Consecrate war! Rouse the warriors! Let all the men of war approach; let them come up!

**4:10** Hammer your plowshares into swords, and your pruning knives into lances; let the weak one say, "I am a warrior."

**4:11** Hurry and come, all you nations from all around, and assemble together! Bring down Your warriors there, LORD!

**4:12** Let the nations be roused and come up into the Valley of Jehoshaphat; for I will sit there to judge all the nations from all around.

**4:13** Thrust in the sickle, for the harvest has ripened! Come, tread, for the winepress is filled; the vats are overflowing, for abundant is their wickedness.

**4:14** Throngs, throngs in the Valley of Decision! For near is the day of the LORD in the Valley of Decision.

**4:15** Sun and moon darken, and stars withdraw their radiance.

**4:16** And from Zion the LORD roars, and from Jerusalem He raises His voice, and heaven and earth quake; yet the LORD is a shelter for His people and a fortress for the children of Israel.

**4:17** And you will know that I, the LORD your God, am dwelling in Zion, My holy mountain; and Jerusalem will be holy, and foreigners will not pass through her again.

**4:18** And it will be on that day that the mountains will drip fresh wine, and the hills will run with milk, and all the channels of Judah will run with water; and a spring will come out from the house of the LORD and water the Wadi of Shittim.

**4:19** Egypt will become a desolation, and Edom will become a wilderness of desolation, on account of the violence against the children of Judah, because they shed innocent blood in their land.

**4:20** Yet Judah will remain inhabited forever, and Jerusalem for generation after generation.

**4:21** And I will clear their blood that I have not cleared, and the LORD dwells in Zion.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — The written ketiv reads the Qal imperfect form (אשוב, "I return"), while the read qere (translated here) has the Hiphil imperfect (אָשִׁיב, "I bring back/restore"). The idiom "bring back the fortunes" (lit. "bring back the turning/captivity") denotes a full reversal of Judah and Jerusalem's distressed state.
- **4:2** — The name Jehoshaphat means "the LORD judges," creating a wordplay with the following Niphal verb "I will contend in judgment" (וְנִשְׁפַּטְתִּי, from שׁפט), echoed again in 4:12.
- **4:11** — The imperative "Hurry" translates the hapax legomenon עוּשׁוּ, understood in Hebrew tradition as "hasten" (or alternatively "assemble/come to aid"). Following the Masoretic atnah accent after "assemble together," "there" (שָׁמָּה) is taken with the Hiphil imperative הַנְחַת ("bring down") in the appeal to the LORD.
- **4:13** — The imperative "tread" (רְדוּ) is taken from רדה ("tread" grapes in the winepress), though the form can equally be parsed as the Qal imperative of ירד ("go down" into the winepress).
- **4:21** — The Masoretic Text repeats the Piel of נקה ("clear, acquit, leave unpunished")—weqatal "I will clear" followed by negated qatal "I have not cleared"—indicating either the final pardon of Judah's bloodguilt or an unmarked rhetorical question ("Will I leave their blood unpunished? I have not"). Ancient versions such as the Septuagint instead read "avenge" (reflecting the root נקם).
