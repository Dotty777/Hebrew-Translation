## Text

**1:1** The vision of Obadiah. Thus the Lord GOD has said concerning Edom: We have heard a report from the LORD, and an envoy has been dispatched among the nations, "Rise, and let us rise against her for battle!"

**1:2** "Look, I have made you small among the nations; you are deeply despised.

**1:3** The insolence of your heart has deceived you, you who dwell in the crevices of the rock, whose home is on high, who says in his heart, 'Who will bring me down to the ground?'

**1:4** Even if you rise high like an eagle, and even if your nest is placed among the stars, from there I will bring you down," declares the LORD.

**1:5** "If thieves came to you, if plunderers of the night—how you have been ruined!—would they not steal what is enough for them? If grape harvesters came to you, would they not leave gleanings?

**1:6** How Esau has been searched through, his hidden stores sought out!

**1:7** All the men of your covenant have sent you away to the border; the men of your peace have deceived you and prevailed against you; those who share your bread lay a snare beneath you—there is no discernment in him.

**1:8** On that day," declares the LORD, "will I not destroy wise men from Edom and discernment from Mount Esau?

**1:9** And your warriors will be terrified, Teman, so that every man will be cut off from Mount Esau by slaughter.

**1:10** On account of the violence against your brother Jacob, shame will cover you, and you will be cut off forever.

**1:11** On the day you stood at a distance, on the day strangers carried off his wealth, and foreigners entered his gates and cast lots over Jerusalem, even you were like one of them.

**1:12** And do not gloat over the day of your brother, on the day of his disaster; and do not rejoice over the sons of Judah on the day of their destruction; and do not boast with your mouth on the day of distress.

**1:13** Do not enter the gate of My people on the day of their calamity; do not gloat, even you, over his misery on the day of his calamity; and do not lay hands on his wealth on the day of his calamity.

**1:14** And do not stand at the crossroads to cut off his escapees, and do not turn over his survivors on the day of distress.

**1:15** For the day of the LORD is near against all the nations. Just as you have done, it will be done to you; your repayment will return upon your head.

**1:16** For just as you have drunk on My holy mountain, all the nations will drink continually; they will drink and gulp down, and they will be as if they had never been.

**1:17** But on Mount Zion there will be escape, and it will be holy; and the house of Jacob will take possession of their possessions.

**1:18** And the house of Jacob will be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, but the house of Esau will be stubble; they will blaze among them and devour them, and there will be no survivor for the house of Esau," for the LORD has spoken.

**1:19** And those of the Negev will possess Mount Esau, and those of the lowland the Philistines; and they will possess the country of Ephraim and the country of Samaria, and Benjamin will possess Gilead.

**1:20** And the exiles of this company of the sons of Israel, who are among the Canaanites as far as Zarephath, and the exiles of Jerusalem who are in Sepharad, will possess the cities of the Negev.

**1:21** And deliverers will go up onto Mount Zion to judge Mount Esau, and the kingdom will belong to the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — Hebrew shokheni ("you who dwell") is a Qal active participle with an archaic poetic connecting ending (-i), not a first-person suffix. The following third-person suffixes ("whose home... in his heart") preserve the Hebrew shift from second to third person after the vocative participle.
- **1:7** — Hebrew lachmekha is literally "your bread," understood elliptically from the preceding "men of your peace" as those who share your bread. The rare noun mazor is rendered "snare" in this context, though a homonym elsewhere means "wound."
- **1:12** — Verses 12-14 employ the negative particle al with jussive forms (direct prohibitions, "do not..."), vividly addressing Edom as if Jerusalem's fall were unfolding. In context they reproach Edom's past actions, carrying the force of "you should not have..."
- **1:13** — Hebrew tishlachnah exhibits an unusual -nah ending on a Qal verb with a second-person masculine singular subject, likely an archaic emphatic form. The verb shalach followed by the preposition be- idiomatically implies extending one's hand against something ("lay hands on").
- **1:20** — Hebrew hachel ("this company") reflects a variant form of chayil ("force/company"), though the spelling chel elsewhere means "rampart." The clause asher-kenaanim lacks a preposition; following the Masoretic accentuation, both exiled groups serve as the subject of "will possess the cities of the Negev," though others read the first group as possessing the territory of the Canaanites as far as Zarephath.
