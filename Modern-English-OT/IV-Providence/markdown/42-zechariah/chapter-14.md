## Text

**14:1** Look, a day is coming for the LORD, when your plunder will be divided within you.

**14:2** And I will gather all the nations against Jerusalem for war, and the city will be captured, the houses will be ransacked, and the women will be violated; and half of the city will go out into exile, but the remainder of the people will not be cut off from the city.

**14:3** Then the LORD will go out and fight against those nations, as on the day of His fighting on a day of battle.

**14:4** And His feet will stand on that day on the Mount of Olives, which faces Jerusalem on the east; and the Mount of Olives will be split down its center eastward and westward into a very great valley, and half of the mountain will move northward and half of it southward.

**14:5** And you will flee by the valley of My mountains, for the valley of the mountains will reach to Azal; and you will flee just as you fled from the earthquake in the days of Uzziah king of Judah. Then the LORD my God will come, and all the holy ones with You.

**14:6** And it will happen on that day that there will be no light, bright splendors, or freezing.

**14:7** And it will be a single day, which is known to the LORD, not day and not night; and it will happen that at evening time there will be light.

**14:8** And it will happen on that day that living water will flow out from Jerusalem, half of it to the eastern sea and half of it to the western sea; in summer and in winter it will happen.

**14:9** And the LORD will become king over the whole earth; on that day the LORD will be one, and His name will be one.

**14:10** The entire land will be turned into a plain, from Geba to Rimmon, south of Jerusalem; and Jerusalem will rise high and remain in its place, from the Gate of Benjamin to the site of the First Gate, to the Corner Gate, and from the Tower of Hananel to the winepresses of the king.

**14:11** And people will dwell in it, and there will never again be a ban of destruction, and Jerusalem will dwell in safety.

**14:12** And this will be the plague by which the LORD will strike all the peoples who marched to war against Jerusalem: their flesh will decay while they stand on their feet, their eyes will decay in their sockets, and their tongues will decay in their mouths.

**14:13** And it will happen on that day that a great panic from the LORD will be among them, and each man will seize the hand of his companion, and his hand will rise up against the hand of his companion.

**14:14** And Judah too will fight at Jerusalem; and the wealth of all the nations all around will be gathered up, gold and silver and clothing in very great abundance.

**14:15** And likewise will be the plague on the horse, the mule, the camel, and the donkey, and all the livestock that will be in those camps, just like this plague.

**14:16** And it will happen that everyone left from all the nations that came against Jerusalem will go up year after year to bow down to the King, the LORD of hosts, and to celebrate the Feast of Booths.

**14:17** And it will happen that whichever of the families of the earth does not go up to Jerusalem to bow down to the King, the LORD of hosts, on them there will be no rain.

**14:18** And if the family of Egypt does not go up and does not come, then on them there will be none; there will be the plague by which the LORD will strike the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Feast of Booths.

**14:19** This will be the penalty of Egypt, and the penalty of all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Feast of Booths.

**14:20** On that day there will be on the bells of the horses, "Holy to the LORD." And the pots in the house of the LORD will be like the sprinkling bowls before the altar.

**14:21** And every pot in Jerusalem and in Judah will be holy to the LORD of hosts, and all who sacrifice will come and take from them and boil in them. And there will never again be a merchant in the house of the LORD of hosts on that day.

## Translator Notes

- **14:2** — Ketiv reads tishshagalnah (Niphal imperfect of shagal, a coarser verb for sexual assault); Qere substitutes the euphemistic Niphal imperfect tishshakhavnah (from shakhav, "be lain with," i.e., "be violated").
- **14:5** — MT reads wenastem ("and you will flee," Qal perfect of nus) twice, matching nastem ("you fled"); ancient versions such as the Septuagint read a Niphal of satam ("will be blocked"). Note also the abrupt person shifts in MT from "My mountains" (the LORD speaking) to "my God" and "with You" (the prophet addressing the LORD).
- **14:6** — A difficult Hebrew text: MT reads yeqarot ("bright/precious things," likely celestial lights, cf. Job 31:26) followed by Qere weqippa'on ("and/or freezing/congealing," a noun from qafa'); Ketiv reads the Qal imperfect verb yiqpa'un ("they will congeal/dwindle").
- **14:18** — MT places the major disjunctive accent on welo alehem ("and not on them"), elliptically echoing "no rain" from v. 17 since Egypt relied on the Nile rather than rain; some read welo as a rhetorical question ("will not the plague be on them?").
- **14:21** — Hebrew kena'ani literally means "Canaanite," but is also used idiomatically in Biblical Hebrew for "merchant" or "trader" (cf. Prov 31:24), fitting the temple-vessel context of vv. 20-21.
