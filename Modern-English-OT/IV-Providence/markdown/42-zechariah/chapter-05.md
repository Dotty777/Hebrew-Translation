## Text

**5:1** Again I lifted up my eyes and looked, and there was a flying scroll.

**5:2** He said to me, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a flying scroll; its length is twenty cubits, and its width is ten cubits."

**5:3** Then he said to me, "This is the curse that is going out over the face of the whole land; for everyone who steals will be purged on one side according to it, and everyone who swears will be purged on the other side according to it.

**5:4** 'I have sent it out,' declares the LORD of hosts, 'and it will enter the house of the thief and the house of the one who swears falsely by My name; and it will lodge inside his house and consume it, along with its timbers and its stones.'"

**5:5** Then the angel who spoke with me came forward and said to me, "Now lift up your eyes and see what this is that is coming out."

**5:6** And I said, "What is it?" And he said, "This is the ephah that is coming out." And he said, "This is their appearance across the whole land."

**5:7** And look, a disc of lead was lifted up, and there was a woman sitting inside the ephah.

**5:8** He said, "This is Wickedness." And he threw her into the middle of the ephah, and threw the lead weight onto its mouth.

**5:9** Then I lifted up my eyes and looked, and there were two women coming out, and wind was in their wings; they had wings like the wings of a stork, and they lifted up the ephah between the earth and the sky.

**5:10** Then I said to the angel who spoke with me, "Where are they taking the ephah?"

**5:11** He said to me, "To build a house for it in the land of Shinar; and when that is prepared, it will be set down there on its base."

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — מִזֶּה ... מִזֶּה idiomatically means "on one side ... on the other side" (the two sides of the scroll), כָּמוֹהָ ("according to it") refers to the curse, and Niphal נִקָּה (from נקה) carries the sense "be purged/cleaned out" (cf. Isa 3:26) rather than "be acquitted."
- **5:6** — הָאֵיפָה ("the ephah") is a dry measuring vessel of roughly 22 liters. Masoretic עֵינָם (lit. "their eye") is used idiomatically for "their appearance/aspect" (cf. Lev 13:55; Num 11:7), whereas the Septuagint and Syriac reflect a variant Hebrew reading עֲוֹנָם ("their iniquity").
- **5:11** — וְהוּכַן (Hophal of כּוּן) is masculine singular, referring to the בַיִת ("house"), whereas וְהֻנִּיחָה (Hophal of נוּחַ) and מְכֻנָתָהּ ("its base/pedestal," also from כּוּן) are feminine singular, referring to the ephah.
