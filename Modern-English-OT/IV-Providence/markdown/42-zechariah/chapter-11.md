## Text

**11:1** Open your doors, Lebanon, so that fire may consume your cedars.

**11:2** Wail, cypress, because the cedar has fallen, because the majestic ones have been laid waste. Wail, oaks of Bashan, because the dense forest has come down.

**11:3** The sound of the wailing of the shepherds, for their majesty has been laid waste! The sound of the roaring of young lions, for the pride of the Jordan has been laid waste!

**11:4** This is what the LORD my God says: "Shepherd the flock of slaughter,

**11:5** whose buyers slaughter them and incur no guilt, and each of their sellers says, 'Blessed be the LORD, for I have grown rich!' And their shepherds have no pity on them.

**11:6** For I will no longer have pity on the inhabitants of the land," declares the LORD. "Look, I am about to hand people over, each one into the hand of his neighbor and into the hand of his king; they will shatter the land, and I will not rescue them from their hand."

**11:7** So I shepherded the flock of slaughter—indeed, the afflicted of the flock. I took for myself two staffs: the one I named Favor, and the other I named Bonds; and I shepherded the flock.

**11:8** And I cut off the three shepherds in one month; my soul grew impatient with them, and their soul also detested me.

**11:9** Then I said, "I will not shepherd you. What is dying, let it die; what is being cut off, let it be cut off; and as for those that remain, let each one eat the flesh of another."

**11:10** And I took my staff Favor and snapped it in two, to annul my covenant that I had made with all the peoples.

**11:11** It was annulled on that day, and thus the afflicted of the flock who were observing me knew that it was the word of the LORD.

**11:12** Then I said to them, "If it is good in your eyes, give me my wages; and if not, refrain." So they weighed out my wages, thirty pieces of silver.

**11:13** And the LORD said to me, "Throw it to the potter—the majestic price at which I was prized by them!" So I took the thirty pieces of silver and threw them into the house of the LORD, to the potter.

**11:14** Then I snapped my second staff, Bonds, in two, to annul the brotherhood between Judah and Israel.

**11:15** And the LORD said to me, "Once more, take for yourself the equipment of a foolish shepherd.

**11:16** For look, I am about to raise up a shepherd in the land: he will not attend to those being cut off, he will not search for the young, he will not heal the injured, and he will not sustain the one standing firm; rather, he will eat the flesh of the fat ones and tear off their hooves.

**11:17** Woe to the worthless shepherd who deserts the flock! A sword against his arm and against his right eye! His arm will completely wither, and his right eye will grow utterly dim!"

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — Ketiv reads habbatsur (Qal passive participle of batsar, "fortified" or "inaccessible"), while Qere reads the adjective habbatsir ("impenetrable" or "dense"); both describe a thick, impenetrable forest.
- **11:7** — Following the Masoretic Text laken aniyye hattson ("indeed, the afflicted of the flock"; cf. v. 11). The Septuagint redivides the consonants to read "for the sheep merchants."
- **11:13** — The Masoretic Text clearly reads hayyotser (Qal participle of yatsar, "the potter"). Some ancient versions and modern translations emend to ha'otsar ("the treasury"), which departs from the Hebrew text.
- **11:16** — Hebrew hanna'ar normally means "the young" (collectively, the young of the flock). Some interpreters instead connect the word to the verb na'ar ("shake out") and render it "the scattered" or "straying."
- **11:17** — The final -i on ro'i ("shepherd") and ozevi ("who deserts") is the archaic poetic linking vowel (hireq compaginis) rather than the first-person possessive suffix "my."
