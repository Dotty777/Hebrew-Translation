## Text

**3:1** Then He showed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD, with the Accuser standing at his right hand to accuse him.

**3:2** And the LORD said to the Accuser, "May the LORD rebuke you, Accuser, and may the LORD who chooses Jerusalem rebuke you! Is this not a firebrand rescued from the fire?"

**3:3** Now Joshua was dressed in filthy garments and was standing before the angel.

**3:4** And he answered and spoke to those standing before him, saying, "Take the filthy garments off him." And he said to him, "See, I have caused your iniquity to pass away from you, and I will dress you in festive robes."

**3:5** Then I said, "Let them place a clean turban on his head." So they placed the clean turban on his head and dressed him in garments, while the angel of the LORD stood by.

**3:6** And the angel of the LORD solemnly charged Joshua, saying:

**3:7** "This is what the LORD of hosts says: If you walk in My ways and if you keep My charge, then you yourself will both govern My house and also guard My courts, and I will give you the right to walk among these who are standing here.

**3:8** Listen now, Joshua the high priest, you and your companions who sit before you—for they are men of a sign—for look, I am bringing My servant, Branch.

**3:9** For look at the stone that I have placed before Joshua: on a single stone are seven eyes. Look, I am engraving its inscription, declares the LORD of hosts, and I will take away the iniquity of that land in a single day.

**3:10** On that day, declares the LORD of hosts, each of you will invite his neighbor to come under a vine and under a fig tree."

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Hebrew ha-satan ("the Accuser" or "the Adversary," traditionally rendered "Satan") carries the definite article as a courtroom title and forms a cognate wordplay with the Qal infinitive construct lesitno ("to accuse him").
- **3:4** — Hebrew wehalbesh is a Hiphil infinitive absolute of labash ("to clothe/dress") continuing the preceding finite verb he'evarti (Hiphil perfect of avar, literally "I have caused to pass away"); machalatsot denotes costly or festive robes (elsewhere only in Isa 3:22).
- **3:7** — The rare form mahlekim is variously parsed as the plural of mahalak ("walkways/rights of access") or an Aramaizing Hiphil participle of halak ("ones who walk"), granting Joshua access among the heavenly attendants.
- **3:8** — Hebrew tsemach ("Branch" or "Sprout") lacks the definite article and functions here as a title or proper name (cf. 6:12).
