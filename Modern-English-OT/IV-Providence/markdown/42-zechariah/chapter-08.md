## Text

**8:1** Then the word of the LORD of hosts came, saying:

**8:2** This is what the LORD of hosts says: "I am jealous for Zion with great jealousy, and with great fury I am jealous for her."

**8:3** This is what the LORD says: "I have returned to Zion and will reside within Jerusalem; and Jerusalem will be called the City of Truth, and the mountain of the LORD of hosts, the Mountain of Holiness."

**8:4** This is what the LORD of hosts says: "Old men and old women will once again sit in the squares of Jerusalem, each with his staff in his hand because of his many days.

**8:5** And the squares of the city will be filled with boys and girls playing in its squares."

**8:6** This is what the LORD of hosts says: "If it seems too extraordinary in the eyes of the remnant of this people in those days, should it also be too extraordinary in My eyes?" declares the LORD of hosts.

**8:7** This is what the LORD of hosts says: "Look, I am saving My people from the land of the east and from the land of the setting of the sun,

**8:8** and I will bring them, and they will dwell in the midst of Jerusalem. They will be a people to Me, and I will be God to them, in truth and in righteousness."

**8:9** This is what the LORD of hosts says: "Let your hands be strong, you who in these days are hearing these words from the mouth of the prophets who were there on the day the house of the LORD of hosts had its foundation laid, for the temple to be built.

**8:10** For before those days there was no wage for man and no wage for animal, and for the one going out or the one coming in there was no peace from the adversary, and I set all people loose, each man against his neighbor.

**8:11** But now I am not toward the remnant of this people as in the former days," declares the LORD of hosts.

**8:12** "For it will be a sowing of peace: the vine will yield its fruit, the land will yield its produce, and the heavens will yield their dew, and I will cause the remnant of this people to inherit all these things.

**8:13** And it will come about that just as you have been a curse among the nations, house of Judah and house of Israel, so I will save you, and you will become a blessing. Do not be afraid; let your hands be strong."

**8:14** For this is what the LORD of hosts says: "Just as I purposed to do harm to you when your fathers provoked Me to wrath," says the LORD of hosts, "and I did not relent,

**8:15** so I have purposed again in these days to do good to Jerusalem and to the house of Judah. Do not be afraid.

**8:16** These are the things that you are to do: speak truth, each man with his neighbor; judge with truth and the justice of peace in your gates.

**8:17** And do not devise evil in your hearts, each man against his neighbor, and do not love a false oath; for all these are things that I hate," declares the LORD.

**8:18** Then the word of the LORD of hosts came to me, saying:

**8:19** This is what the LORD of hosts says: "The fast of the fourth month, the fast of the fifth, the fast of the seventh, and the fast of the tenth will become for the house of Judah joy and gladness and happy festivals; therefore love truth and peace."

**8:20** This is what the LORD of hosts says: "It will yet be that peoples and the inhabitants of many cities will come.

**8:21** The inhabitants of one city will go to another, saying, 'Let us surely go to implore the favor of the LORD and to seek the LORD of hosts; I too will go.'

**8:22** So many peoples and powerful nations will come to seek the LORD of hosts in Jerusalem and to implore the favor of the LORD."

**8:23** This is what the LORD of hosts says: "In those days ten men from every language of the nations will take hold, yes, they will grasp the edge of a Jewish man's garment, saying, 'Let us go with you, for we have heard that God is with you.'"

## Translator Notes

- **8:6** — The Niphal verb (yippale, from pala) denotes what is extraordinary, marvelous, or beyond human ability, and the second clause functions as an unmarked rhetorical question.
- **8:12** — The opening clause literally reads "for a sowing/seed of peace" without an expressed verb, introducing the threefold promise of agricultural abundance.
- **8:17** — The Hebrew combines the direct-object marker (et) with the relative pronoun (asher) before "I hate" (ki et-kol-elleh asher saneti), an emphatic Late Biblical Hebrew construction.
