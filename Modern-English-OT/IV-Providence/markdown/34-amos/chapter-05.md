## Text

**5:1** Listen to this word that I raise over you—a lamentation, house of Israel:

**5:2** "She has fallen—never again will she rise, virgin Israel! She is abandoned on her soil; there is no one to raise her."

**5:3** For this is what the Lord GOD says: "The city that marches out with a thousand will be left with a hundred, and the one that marches out with a hundred will be left with ten, for the house of Israel."

**5:4** For this is what the LORD says to the house of Israel: "Seek Me and live!

**5:5** But do not seek Bethel, and do not enter Gilgal, and do not cross over to Beersheba; for Gilgal will surely go into exile, and Bethel will come to nothing."

**5:6** Seek the LORD and live, or else He will rush like fire against the house of Joseph, and it will devour, with no one to quench it for Bethel.

**5:7** You who turn justice into wormwood and throw righteousness down to the ground!

**5:8** He who makes the Pleiades and Orion, who turns deep darkness into morning and darkens day into night, who summons the waters of the sea and pours them out across the face of the earth—the LORD is His name.

**5:9** It is He who sends devastation flashing against the strong, and devastation comes upon the fortress.

**5:10** They hate the one who reproves in the gate, and the one who speaks with integrity they detest.

**5:11** Therefore, because you trample on the poor and take a levy of grain from him, you have built houses of cut stone, but you will not live in them; you have planted delightful vineyards, but you will not drink their wine.

**5:12** For I know that your transgressions are many and your sins are mighty—harassing the righteous, taking a bribe, and thrusting the needy aside in the gate.

**5:13** Therefore the discerning one will stay silent at that time, for it is an evil time.

**5:14** Seek good and not evil so that you may live, and in this way the LORD, God of hosts, will be with you, just as you have said.

**5:15** Hate evil and love good, and set up justice in the gate; perhaps the LORD, God of hosts, will show favor to the remnant of Joseph.

**5:16** Therefore this is what the LORD, God of hosts, the Lord, says: "In all public squares there will be wailing, and in all streets they will say, 'Alas! Alas!' And they will call the farmer to mourning, and proclaim wailing to those who know lamentation;

**5:17** and in all vineyards there will be wailing, because I will pass through your midst," says the LORD.

**5:18** Woe to those who long for the day of the LORD! What use to you is the day of the LORD? It is darkness, and not light—

**5:19** just as when a man flees from a lion, and a bear encounters him, and he enters the house and rests his hand on the wall, and a snake bites him.

**5:20** Is the day of the LORD not darkness and not light, and gloom with no radiance in it?

**5:21** "I hate, I spurn your festivals, and I will not delight in your solemn assemblies.

**5:22** Even if you offer up burnt offerings to Me and your grain offerings, I will not accept them; and at the peace offering of your fattened animals I will not look.

**5:23** Remove from Me the clamor of your songs, and I will not listen to the melody of your harps.

**5:24** But let justice roll forth like waters, and righteousness like a perennial stream.

**5:25** Was it sacrifices and grain offering that you presented to Me in the wilderness for forty years, house of Israel?

**5:26** And you will carry Sikkuth your king and Kiyyun, your images, the star of your god that you made for yourselves,

**5:27** and I will exile you beyond Damascus," says the LORD—God of hosts is His name.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — The Hebrew plays on both sanctuary names: Gilgal will surely go into exile (galōh yigleh, Qal infinitive absolute plus imperfect of galah), and Bethel ('House of God') will become 'nothing/trouble' (aven, echoing the derogatory name Beth-aven).
- **5:9** — The rare Hiphil participle ham-mablig (from balag, 'gleam/flash forth') vividly portrays God making sudden devastation flash against the strong.
- **5:11** — The verb 'you trample' renders the hapax Poel infinitive construct bōshaskem, traditionally linked to bus ('trample'), though some compare an Akkadian cognate meaning 'exact a tax.'
- **5:21** — The clause 'I will not delight in' literally reads 'I will not smell' (Hiphil imperfect of ruach/riach), a Hebrew idiom drawn from sacrificial aroma expressing divine acceptance or pleasure.
- **5:26** — The weqatal verb 'you will carry' (u-nesatem) naturally reads as future leading into exile in v. 27, though some take it as past ('you carried'). Sikkuth and Kiyyun reflect Mesopotamian astral deity titles associated with Saturn, vocalized in the MT with the vowels of shiqquts ('detestable idol'), or alternatively nouns meaning 'shrine' and 'pedestal.'
