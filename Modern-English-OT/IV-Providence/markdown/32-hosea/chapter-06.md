## Text

**6:1** "Come, and let us return to the LORD, for He is the one who has torn us apart, and He will heal us; He has struck us, and He will bind us up.

**6:2** He will restore us to life after two days; on the third day He will raise us up, and we will live in His presence.

**6:3** And let us know, let us pursue knowing the LORD: as certain as the dawn is His coming forth, and He will come to us like the rain, like the spring rain that soaks the earth."

**6:4** What am I to do with you, Ephraim? What am I to do with you, Judah? For your loyal love is like a morning cloud, and like the dew that departs early.

**6:5** Therefore I have hewn them down by the prophets; I have killed them by the words of My mouth, and your judgments are like light going forth.

**6:6** For I take pleasure in loyal love and not sacrifice, and in the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.

**6:7** But they, like Adam, have violated the covenant; there they have betrayed Me.

**6:8** Gilead is a city of those who work wickedness, marked with bloody footprints.

**6:9** And as bands of raiders lie in wait for a man, a company of priests murders along the road to Shechem, for they have practiced depravity.

**6:10** In the house of Israel I have seen an appalling thing: Ephraim's prostitution is there; Israel is defiled.

**6:11** For you too, Judah, a harvest has been set, when I restore the fortunes of My people.

## Translator Notes

- **6:3** — Hebrew yoreh is rendered as a Qal participle ("that soaks/waters," from yarah), though it can also be read as the noun for the autumn (early) rain paired with the spring rain (malqosh).
- **6:5** — The Masoretic Text reads umishpatekha or yetse ("and your judgments are like light going forth," referring to the judgments pronounced against you), whereas the Septuagint divides the consonants differently to read "and My judgment goes forth as light."
- **6:7** — Hebrew ke'adam can be translated either as "like Adam" (referring to the first man) or generically as "like mankind/humans."
- **6:10** — The written Ketiv reads sh'ryryh (with yod instead of waw); the translation follows the read Qere sha'aruriyyah ("an appalling thing" or "a horrible thing").
- **6:11** — The idiom shuv shevut ("restore the fortunes," literally "turn a turning" or "bring back the captivity") closes verse 11 in the Masoretic Text, though some modern versions attach this clause to 7:1.
