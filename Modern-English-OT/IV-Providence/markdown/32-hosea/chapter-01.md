## Text

**1:1** The word of the LORD that came to Hosea son of Beeri in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, the kings of Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash, the king of Israel.

**1:2** At the beginning, when the LORD spoke through Hosea, the LORD said to Hosea, "Go, take for yourself a wife of prostitution and children of prostitution, for the land utterly prostitutes itself, departing from the LORD."

**1:3** He went and took Gomer daughter of Diblaim, and she conceived and gave birth to a son for him.

**1:4** Then the LORD said to him, "Call his name Jezreel, for in just a little while I will call the house of Jehu to account for the bloodshed of Jezreel, and I will bring the kingdom of the house of Israel to an end.

**1:5** And it will happen on that day that I will break the bow of Israel in the Valley of Jezreel."

**1:6** Then she conceived again and gave birth to a daughter. And He said to him, "Call her name Lo-ruhamah, for I will no longer show compassion to the house of Israel, that I should ever pardon them.

**1:7** Yet to the house of Judah I will show compassion, and I will save them by the LORD their God; I will not save them by bow, sword, or war, nor by horses or horsemen."

**1:8** Then she weaned Lo-ruhamah, and she conceived and gave birth to a son.

**1:9** And He said, "Call his name Lo-ammi, for you are not My people, and I will not be yours."

## Translator Notes

- **1:2** — The opening clause places a construct noun directly before a finite Piel verb (tehillat dibber-YHWH, literally "the beginning of the LORD spoke"); the preposition be- ("through") can also mean "with" or "to."
- **1:6** — Lo-ruhamah means "She has not been shown compassion" (Pual perfect of raham, echoing the Piel verb "show compassion"). The closing clause (ki naso essa lahem, literally "that forgiving I should forgive them") carries forward the preceding negation ("I will no longer"), though some take ki adversatively as "but I will surely take them away."
- **1:9** — Lo-ammi means "Not My people." The closing Hebrew clause (we-anoki lo-ehyeh lakem, literally "and I will not be to you") omits any predicate noun such as "God" and echoes the covenantal name Ehyeh ("I AM") of Exodus 3:14.
