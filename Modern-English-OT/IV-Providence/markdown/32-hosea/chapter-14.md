## Text

**14:1** Samaria will bear guilt, because she has rebelled against her God; they will fall by the sword, their young children will be dashed to pieces, and its pregnant women will be ripped open.

**14:2** Return, Israel, to the LORD your God, for you have stumbled through your iniquity.

**14:3** Take words with you and return to the LORD. Say to Him, "Forgive all iniquity and accept what is good, so that we may offer our lips as bulls.

**14:4** Assyria will not save us; we will not ride on horses, and we will no longer say 'Our God' to the work of our hands, for in You the orphan receives compassion."

**14:5** I will heal their turning away; I will love them freely, for My anger has turned away from him.

**14:6** I will be to Israel like the dew; he will blossom like the lily and strike his roots like Lebanon.

**14:7** His shoots will reach out, and his splendor will be like the olive tree, and he will have fragrance like Lebanon.

**14:8** Those who dwell in his shadow will return; they will grow grain and blossom like the vine; his renown will be like the wine of Lebanon.

**14:9** Ephraim, what more have I to do with idols? I Myself have answered and will watch over him. I am like a flourishing cypress; from Me your fruit is found.

**14:10** Who is wise? Let him understand these things, and let the discerning one know them. For the ways of the LORD are straight, and the righteous walk in them, yet transgressors stumble in them.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — The Masoretic Text reads "bulls" with "our lips" in apposition, portraying spoken repentance and praise offered in place of bull sacrifices. Ancient versions divide the Hebrew consonants differently to read "the fruit of our lips."
- **14:8** — The Piel verb literally means "keep alive" or "make grow," taking "grain" directly as its object without a comparative "like." The singular suffix in "his shadow" continues the metaphor of Israel as the flourishing tree of vv. 6-7, though some take it to refer to the LORD.
- **14:9** — Many interpreters read the opening question as spoken by repentant Ephraim, to which the LORD replies in the following clauses. The Hebrew shifts from third person ("watch over him") to second person ("your fruit"), a common poetic device in Hosea.
