## Text

**3:1** Then the LORD said to me, "Go again, love a woman who is loved by a companion and is an adulteress, just as the LORD loves the sons of Israel, even though they turn to other gods and are lovers of raisin cakes."

**3:2** So I bought her for myself for fifteen shekels of silver, a homer of barley, and a lethech of barley.

**3:3** Then I said to her, "For many days you must remain for me; you must not prostitute yourself, and you must not belong to any man; and I too toward you."

**3:4** For the sons of Israel will remain many days without king and without prince, without sacrifice and without pillar, and without ephod or teraphim.

**3:5** Afterward, the sons of Israel will turn back and seek the LORD their God and David their king, and they will come in trembling to the LORD and to His goodness in the latter days.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — The Masoretic accents group od ("again") with "Go" rather than with "said to me." The noun rea ("companion") can refer either to an illicit lover or to a woman's own husband.
- **3:2** — The Masoretic text literally reads "fifteen of silver" (with "shekels" implied) and repeats "barley" after lethech, a rare dry measure occurring only here in the Hebrew Bible and traditionally reckoned as half a homer.
- **3:3** — The closing clause (we-gam-ani elayik, literally "and also I toward you") is verbless and elliptical, leaving open whether Hosea vows equal fidelity or mutual abstention during the waiting period.
- **3:4** — Teraphim were household or divinatory cult images (cf. Judges 17:5; Zechariah 10:2) paired here with the priestly ephod.
