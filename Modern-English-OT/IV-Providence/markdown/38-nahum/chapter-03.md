## Text

**3:1** Woe to the city of bloodshed! All of her is full of deceit and plunder; prey does not depart.

**3:2** The sound of a whip, and the sound of a wheel's rattle, and a galloping horse, and a leaping chariot!

**3:3** A charging horseman, and the flash of a sword, and the gleam of a spear, and a multitude of the fallen, and a heavy mass of corpses; and there is no end to the dead bodies, and they stumble over their dead bodies—

**3:4** because of the abundant prostitution of a prostitute lovely in charm, mistress of sorceries, who sells nations through her prostitution and clans through her sorceries.

**3:5** "Look, I am against you," declares the LORD of hosts, "and I will uncover your skirts over your face, and I will show nations your nakedness and kingdoms your shame.

**3:6** And I will hurl filth upon you and treat you as contemptible, and I will set you like a spectacle.

**3:7** And it will happen that everyone who sees you will flee from you and say, 'Nineveh has been ruined! Who will mourn for her?' From where shall I seek comforters for you?"

**3:8** Are you any better than No-amon, who sat among the Nile canals, with waters all around her, whose rampart was the sea, and from the sea was her wall?

**3:9** Cush was her might, and Egypt, and there was no end; Put and the Libyans were your help.

**3:10** Even she went into exile, into captivity; even her young children were smashed to pieces at the head of all the streets. For her dignitaries they cast a lot, and all her great men were fastened in chains.

**3:11** Even you will grow drunk; you will be concealed. Even you will seek a stronghold from an enemy.

**3:12** All your fortresses are fig trees with early-ripe fruit; if they are shaken, they fall into the mouth of an eater.

**3:13** Look, your people are women in your midst! To your enemies the gates of your land have been opened wide; fire has devoured your gate bars.

**3:14** Draw siege water for yourself; strengthen your fortresses! Go into the mud and trample in the mortar; grasp the brick mold!

**3:15** There fire will devour you, a sword will cut you off; it will devour you like the young locust. Make yourself numerous like the young locust; make yourself numerous like the locust!

**3:16** You have multiplied your merchants beyond the stars of the sky; the young locust raids and flies away.

**3:17** Your crowned officials are like the locust, and your marshals are like a swarm of grasshoppers, camping in the stone walls on a day of cold. The sun shines forth, and it flees away, and its place is not known—where they are.

**3:18** Your shepherds slumber, king of Assyria; your nobles lie at rest. Your people are dispersed upon the mountains, and there is no one gathering them.

**3:19** There is no relief for your injury; grievous is your wound. All who hear the report of you clap hands over you, for upon whom has your evil not passed continually?

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — Qere reads the weqatal wekhashelu ("and they stumble"), whereas Ketiv reads the imperfect ykshlw ("they stumble").
- **3:8** — No-amon is the Hebrew name for Egyptian Thebes (city of the god Amon). The plural ye'orim denotes the Nile and its channels, and MT miyyam ("from the sea") poetically depicts the broad river itself as the city's wall.
- **3:9** — The Masoretic text reads the second-person feminine singular be'ezratekh ("your help"), shifting into direct apostrophe to No-amon; 'otsmah lacks a mappiq in the final he, reading either as "her might" (with omitted mappiq) or as the abstract noun "might."
- **3:16** — The Qal perfect pashat ("raids") can mean either "makes a raid / spreads out to plunder" or "strips / sheds [its husk]" as the wingless young locust (yeleq) matures and flies away.
- **3:17** — Minnezarayikh ("your crowned officials") and taphserayikh ("your marshals," from Akkadian tupsharru) designate high Assyrian officials. The Masoretic text shifts from plural participle (hachonim, "camping") to collective singular (wenodad, "it flees away"; meqomo, "its place") and back to plural ('ayyam, "where they are").
