## Text

**6:1** And Darius the Mede received the kingdom when he was about sixty-two years old.

**6:2** It pleased Darius to set one hundred twenty satraps over the kingdom, who were to be throughout the entire kingdom,

**6:3** and over them three chief officials, one of whom was Daniel, so that these satraps would give an account to them and the king would suffer no loss.

**6:4** Then this Daniel kept excelling above the chief officials and the satraps, because an extraordinary spirit was in him, and the king intended to set him over the whole kingdom.

**6:5** Then the chief officials and the satraps kept seeking to find a charge against Daniel regarding the kingdom, yet they could not find any charge or corruption, because he was trustworthy, and no negligence or corruption was found against him.

**6:6** Then those men said, "We will not find any charge against this Daniel unless we find it against him in the law of his God."

**6:7** Then these chief officials and satraps came thronging to the king and said to him, "King Darius, live forever!

**6:8** All the chief officials of the kingdom, the prefects and the satraps, the counselors and the governors, have taken counsel together to establish a royal statute and enforce an injunction that anyone who makes a petition to any god or man for thirty days, except to you, King, shall be thrown into the den of lions.

**6:9** Now, King, establish the injunction and sign the document so that it may not be changed, according to the law of Media and Persia, which cannot be revoked."

**6:10** Because of this, King Darius signed the document and the injunction.

**6:11** Now when Daniel knew that the document had been signed, he entered his house, where he had open windows in his upper room facing Jerusalem, and three times a day he knelt on his knees, praying and giving thanks before his God, just as he had been doing before this.

**6:12** Then those men came thronging and found Daniel making petition and pleading for favor before his God.

**6:13** Then they came near and spoke before the king concerning the king's injunction, "Did you not sign an injunction that any man who petitions any god or man for thirty days, except to you, King, shall be thrown into the den of lions?" The king answered and said, "The matter stands firm, according to the law of Media and Persia, which cannot be revoked."

**6:14** Then they answered and said before the king, "Daniel, who is one of the exiles of Judah, pays no regard to you, King, or to the injunction that you signed, but three times a day he makes his petition."

**6:15** Then when the king heard this report, he was deeply distressed, and he set his mind on Daniel to rescue him; until sunset he kept exerting himself to deliver him.

**6:17** Then the king gave the order, and they brought Daniel and threw him into the den of lions. The king spoke and said to Daniel, "Your God, whom you serve continually, may He rescue you!"

**6:18** And a stone was brought and laid over the mouth of the den, and the king sealed it with his own signet ring and with the signet rings of his nobles, so that nothing regarding Daniel could be changed.

**6:19** Then the king went to his palace and spent the night fasting; he had no entertainment brought before him, and his sleep fled from him.

**6:20** Then the king arose at daybreak, at the first light of dawn, and went in haste to the den of lions.

**6:21** When he came near the den, he cried out to Daniel with an anguished voice. The king spoke and said to Daniel, "Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you serve continually, been able to rescue you from the lions?"

**6:22** Then Daniel spoke with the king, "King, live forever!

**6:23** My God sent His angel and shut the mouths of the lions, and they have not harmed me, because innocence was found in me before Him; and before you as well, King, I have done no wrong."

**6:24** Then the king was overjoyed, and he ordered that Daniel be lifted up out of the den. So Daniel was lifted up out of the den, and no harm at all was found on him, because he had trusted in his God.

**6:25** Then the king gave the order, and they brought those men who had maliciously accused Daniel and threw them, their children, and their wives into the den of lions; and they had not yet reached the bottom of the den before the lions overpowered them and crushed all their bones.

**6:26** Then King Darius wrote to all the peoples, nations, and languages who dwell in all the earth: "May your peace abound!

**6:27** A decree is issued by me that in every dominion of my kingdom people are to tremble and fear before the God of Daniel; for He is the living God, enduring forever, His kingdom is one that will not be destroyed, and His dominion is to the end.

**6:28** He rescues and delivers, and He works signs and wonders in heaven and on earth, for He has rescued Daniel from the paw of the lions."

**6:29** So this Daniel prospered during the reign of Darius and during the reign of Cyrus the Persian.

## Translator Notes

- **6:7** — Aramaic hargishu (Haphel of rgsh, also in 6:12) literally means "came thronging" or "crowded in noisily," though ancient versions often understood it as "came by agreement."
- **6:17** — The Aramaic imperfect yeshezivinnakh (Shaphel of shyzb) can express either a wish ("may He rescue you") or a confident statement ("He will rescue you").
- **6:19** — The meaning of the rare Aramaic noun dachawan is uncertain; it is most commonly rendered "entertainment" or "diversions," though proposals include "musical instruments," "concubines," or "food."
- **6:25** — Literally "who had eaten the pieces of Daniel," a standard Aramaic and Akkadian legal idiom meaning "maliciously accused" or "slandered."
