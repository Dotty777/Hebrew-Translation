## Text

**9:1** In the first year of Darius son of Ahasuerus, from the seed of Media, who was made king over the kingdom of the Chaldeans,

**9:2** in the first year of his reign, I, Daniel, perceived in the books the number of the years concerning which the word of the LORD had come to Jeremiah the prophet, that seventy years must be fulfilled for the ruins of Jerusalem.

**9:3** So I set my face toward the Lord God to seek by prayer and supplications, with fasting, sackcloth, and ashes.

**9:4** I prayed to the LORD my God and made confession, saying, "Please, Lord, the great and awesome God, who keeps the covenant and loyal love for those who love Him and keep His commandments,

**9:5** we have sinned and committed iniquity, acted wickedly and rebelled, and turned aside from Your commandments and from Your ordinances.

**9:6** We have not listened to Your servants the prophets, who spoke in Your name to our kings, our princes, and our fathers, and to all the people of the land.

**9:7** To You, Lord, belongs righteousness, yet to us belongs shame of face, as on this day—to the men of Judah, to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and to all Israel, those who are near and those who are far off in all the lands where You have driven them, because of their unfaithfulness with which they acted unfaithfully against You.

**9:8** LORD, to us belongs shame of face, to our kings, to our princes, and to our fathers, because we have sinned against You.

**9:9** To the Lord our God belong mercies and forgiveness, for we have rebelled against Him

**9:10** and have not obeyed the voice of the LORD our God, to walk in His laws which He set before us by the hand of His servants the prophets.

**9:11** All Israel has transgressed Your law and turned aside so as not to obey Your voice; therefore the curse and the oath written in the law of Moses, the servant of God, have been poured out upon us, because we have sinned against Him.

**9:12** He has confirmed His word that He spoke against us and against our judges who judged us, by bringing upon us a great calamity; for under all heaven nothing has been done like what has been done in Jerusalem.

**9:13** Just as it is written in the law of Moses, all this calamity has come upon us; yet we have not sought the favor of the LORD our God by turning from our iniquity and gaining insight into Your truth.

**9:14** Therefore the LORD kept watch over the calamity and brought it upon us; for the LORD our God is righteous in all His works that He has done, yet we have not obeyed His voice.

**9:15** And now, Lord our God, who brought Your people out from the land of Egypt with a strong hand and made a name for Yourself, as on this day—we have sinned, we have done wickedly.

**9:16** Lord, in accordance with all Your righteous acts, please let Your anger and Your wrath turn away from Your city Jerusalem, Your holy mountain; for because of our sins and the iniquities of our fathers, Jerusalem and Your people have become a reproach to all who surround us.

**9:17** And now, our God, listen to the prayer of Your servant and to his supplications, and cause Your face to shine upon Your desolate sanctuary, for the Lord's sake.

**9:18** Incline Your ear, my God, and hear; open Your eyes and see our desolations and the city over which Your name is called. For we are not laying our supplications before You on the basis of our righteous acts, but on the basis of Your abundant mercies.

**9:19** Lord, hear! Lord, forgive! Lord, pay heed and act! Do not delay, for Your own sake, my God, because Your name is called over Your city and over Your people."

**9:20** Now while I was still speaking and praying, confessing my sin and the sin of my people Israel, and laying my supplication before the LORD my God on behalf of the holy mountain of my God—

**9:21** while I was still speaking in prayer, the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision at the beginning, reached me in swift flight about the time of the evening offering.

**9:22** He gave instruction and spoke with me, saying, "Daniel, now I have come forth to instruct you in understanding.

**9:23** At the beginning of your supplications a word went forth, and I have come to declare it, for you are greatly treasured. So ponder the word and discern the vision.

**9:24** Seventy weeks have been decreed upon your people and upon your holy city, to restrain the transgression, to bring sin to an end, to atone for iniquity, to bring in eternal righteousness, to seal up vision and prophet, and to anoint a holy of holies.

**9:25** So know and gain insight: from the going forth of a word to restore and to build Jerusalem until an anointed one, a prince, there will be seven weeks; and for sixty-two weeks it will be built again, with plaza and moat, though in distress of the times.

**9:26** After the sixty-two weeks, an anointed one will be cut off, and there will be nothing for him; and the people of the coming prince will destroy the city and the sanctuary. Its end will be with a flood, and until the end of the war desolations are decreed.

**9:27** And he will make a covenant strong with the many for one week, and for half of the week he will cause sacrifice and grain offering to cease; and upon the wing of abominations will be a desolator, until the decreed complete destruction is poured out upon the desolator."

## Translator Notes

- **9:12** — Ketiv reads plural "devarav" ("His words"), while Qere (translated here) reads singular "devaro" ("His word").
- **9:21** — Hebrew "mu'af bi'af" is lexically uncertain; deriving it from "uf" (Hophal participle) with "ye'af" yields "in swift flight," whereas connecting it to "ya'ef" yields "in utter weariness."
- **9:24** — Two Ketiv/Qere variants appear in the second infinitive clause: Ketiv reads "velachtom chatta'ot" ("and to seal up sins"), while Qere (translated here) reads Hiphil "ulehatem chatta't" ("and to bring sin to an end"). Hebrew "shavu'im" ("weeks") denotes periods of seven, and "nechtakh" ("have been decreed") is a Niphal hapax legomenon.
- **9:25** — The Masoretic disjunctive accent (athnach) separates "seven weeks" from "sixty-two weeks," linking the sixty-two weeks to the rebuilding; without that pause, "seven weeks and sixty-two weeks" together measure the span until an anointed prince. Hebrew "mashiach" literally means "anointed one" (echoing "anoint" in v. 24).
- **9:27** — The phrase "al kenaf shiqqutzim meshomem" ("upon the wing of abominations [will be] a desolator") is syntactically compressed and may refer to a pinnacle or extremity of the temple defiled by appalling abominations. At the end of the verse, Qal participle "shomem" can be read actively ("the desolator") or passively ("the desolate one").
