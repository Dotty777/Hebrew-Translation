## Text

**10:1** In the third year of Cyrus, king of Persia, a message was revealed to Daniel, whose name was called Belteshazzar. The message was true and involved a great conflict; he understood the message, and understanding came to him in the vision.

**10:2** In those days I, Daniel, was mourning for three full weeks.

**10:3** I ate no choice food, neither meat nor wine came into my mouth, and I did not anoint myself at all until three full weeks were fulfilled.

**10:4** On the twenty-fourth day of the first month, I was along the bank of the great river, which is the Tigris.

**10:5** I raised my eyes and looked, and look, there was a certain man dressed in linen, whose waist was belted with fine gold of Uphaz.

**10:6** His body resembled beryl, his face had the look of lightning, his eyes were like torches of fire, his arms and legs had the luster of polished bronze, and the sound of his words resembled the sound of a multitude.

**10:7** I, Daniel, alone saw the vision, and the men who were with me did not see the vision; yet a great trembling fell over them, so they fled to hide themselves.

**10:8** So I was left by myself and looked at this great vision. No strength was left in me; my vigor turned to ruin within me, and I retained no strength.

**10:9** Then I heard the sound of his words, and as I heard the sound of his words, I sank into a deep sleep on my face, with my face toward the ground.

**10:10** Then look, a hand touched me and set me trembling onto my knees and the palms of my hands.

**10:11** He said to me, "Daniel, greatly treasured man, understand the words that I am speaking to you and stand upright in your place, for I have now been sent to you." As he spoke this word with me, I stood up trembling.

**10:12** Then he said to me, "Do not be afraid, Daniel, for from the first day that you set your heart to understand and to humble yourself before your God, your words were heard, and I have come on account of your words.

**10:13** Yet the prince of the kingdom of Persia stood opposing me for twenty-one days; then look, Michael, one of the foremost princes, came to help me, and I was left there beside the kings of Persia.

**10:14** Now I have come to make you understand what will happen to your people in the latter days, for the vision still pertains to days to come."

**10:15** When he spoke with me according to these words, I set my face toward the ground and became speechless.

**10:16** Then look, one in the likeness of the sons of man touched my lips. I opened my mouth and spoke, saying to the one standing before me, "My lord, because of the vision, pangs of anguish have come upon me, and I have retained no strength.

**10:17** How can this servant of my lord speak with my lord here? As for me, right now no strength remains in me, and no breath is left in me."

**10:18** Then one who had the appearance of a man touched me again and strengthened me.

**10:19** He said, "Do not be afraid, greatly treasured man. Peace be to you; be strong, yes, be strong." As he spoke with me, I gained strength and said, "Let my lord speak, for you have strengthened me."

**10:20** Then he said, "Do you know why I have come to you? Now I will return to fight against the prince of Persia, and when I go out, look, the prince of Greece is coming.

**10:21** Nevertheless, I will tell you what is inscribed in the writing of truth. Yet there is no one who stands firmly with me against these except Michael, your prince."

## Translator Notes

- **10:1** — Hebrew tsava gadol literally means "great warfare" or "great hardship," pointing to the severity of the conflict revealed in the message.
- **10:2** — Hebrew literally reads "three weeks, days," adding "days" in apposition to specify three full weeks of days rather than weeks of years.
- **10:4** — Hebrew Hiddekel, the ancient name of the Tigris River.
- **10:16** — Literally "my pangs have turned upon me," an idiom using the noun for labor pains to express sudden, overwhelming anguish (cf. 1 Sam 4:19).
