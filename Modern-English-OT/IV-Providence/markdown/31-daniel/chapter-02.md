## Text

**2:1** Now in the second year of Nebuchadnezzar's reign, Nebuchadnezzar dreamed dreams, and his spirit became disturbed, and his sleep broke off from him.

**2:2** So the king commanded to call the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers, and the Chaldeans to recount his dreams to the king; and they came and stood in the king's presence.

**2:3** The king spoke to them: "I have dreamed a dream, and my spirit is disturbed to know the dream."

**2:4** And the Chaldeans spoke to the king in Aramaic: "King, live forever! Speak the dream to your servants, and we will report the interpretation."

**2:5** The king replied to the Chaldeans, saying, "My decree is settled: if you do not inform me of the dream and its interpretation, you will be cut to pieces, and your houses will be turned into a refuse heap.

**2:6** Yet if you report the dream and its interpretation, you will receive gifts, a reward, and great honor from me. So report to me the dream and its interpretation."

**2:7** They replied a second time, saying, "Let the king speak the dream to his servants, and we will report the interpretation."

**2:8** The king replied, saying, "I know for a fact that you are buying time, since you see that my decree is settled:

**2:9** that if you do not inform me of the dream, there is only one verdict for you; and you have conspired to speak false and corrupt talk before me until the season shifts. So speak the dream to me, and I will know that you are able to report its interpretation to me."

**2:10** The Chaldeans answered before the king and said, "There is not a person on the dry land who can report the king's matter, since no king, however great and powerful, has asked a thing like this of any magician, enchanter, or Chaldean.

**2:11** The thing that the king is asking is difficult, and there is no one else who can report it before the king except gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh."

**2:12** At this the king grew enraged and exceedingly furious, and he commanded to destroy all the wise men of Babylon.

**2:13** So the decree went out, and the wise men were to be killed; and they looked for Daniel and his companions, to kill them.

**2:14** Then Daniel responded with counsel and discretion to Arioch, the chief of the king's guard, who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.

**2:15** He answered and said to Arioch, the king's officer, "Why is the decree from the king so harsh?" Then Arioch made the matter known to Daniel.

**2:16** So Daniel went in and asked the king to give him time, so that he might report the interpretation to the king.

**2:17** Then Daniel went home and informed Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions, of the matter,

**2:18** to seek mercy from the God of heaven regarding this mystery, so that Daniel and his companions might not perish along with the remaining wise men of Babylon.

**2:19** Then in a night vision the mystery was uncovered to Daniel, and Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

**2:20** Daniel replied, saying, "Let the name of God be blessed from eternity to eternity, for wisdom and might belong to Him.

**2:21** It is He who alters the times and the seasons; He deposes kings and raises up kings; He grants wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who know discernment.

**2:22** He uncovers the deep and secret things; He knows what lies in the darkness, and light resides with Him.

**2:23** To You, God of my fathers, I give thanks and offer praise, because You have granted me wisdom and might; and now You have informed me of what we asked from You, for You have disclosed to us the king's matter."

**2:24** Accordingly Daniel went in to Arioch, whom the king had assigned to destroy the wise men of Babylon; he went and spoke to him like this: "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon! Lead me into the king's presence, and I will report the interpretation to the king."

**2:25** Then Arioch brought Daniel in haste before the king and spoke to him like this: "I have found a man from the exiles of Judah who will inform the king of the interpretation."

**2:26** The king replied to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, saying, "Are you able to inform me of the dream I saw and its interpretation?"

**2:27** Daniel answered before the king and said, "The mystery that the king is asking about, no wise men, enchanters, magicians, or diviners are able to report to the king.

**2:28** Yet there is a God in heaven who uncovers mysteries, and He has disclosed to King Nebuchadnezzar what will occur at the end of days. Your dream and the visions of your head on your bed are these:

**2:29** As for you, King, your thoughts came up on your bed about what will occur after this; and He who uncovers mysteries has disclosed to you what will occur.

**2:30** As for me, it is not because of wisdom present in me beyond all the living that this mystery has been uncovered to me, but so that the interpretation would be disclosed to the king and you would understand the thoughts of your heart.

**2:31** You, King, were watching, and look—one huge statue! That immense statue, extraordinary in brilliance, stood opposite you, and its look was terrifying.

**2:32** As for that statue, its head was of fine gold, its chest and its arms of silver, its belly and its thighs of bronze,

**2:33** its legs of iron, its feet part of iron and part of clay.

**2:34** You watched until a stone was broken off without hands, and it hit the statue on its feet of iron and clay and ground them to pieces.

**2:35** At that moment the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver, and the gold were ground down together and turned into chaff from the summer threshing floors; the wind swept them away, and no place at all was found for them. Yet the stone that hit the statue grew into a huge mountain and filled all the earth.

**2:36** This is the dream, and we will state its interpretation before the king.

**2:37** You, King, are king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has granted the kingship, the might, the power, and the honor;

**2:38** and wherever human beings, the wild animals of the field, and the birds of the heavens dwell, He has given them into your hand and set you as ruler over all of them. You yourself are the head of gold.

**2:39** After you another kingdom will arise, lower than you, and another, a third kingdom, of bronze, which will rule over all the earth.

**2:40** Then there will be a fourth kingdom, strong as iron, because iron grinds down and shatters everything; and like iron that smashes, it will grind down and smash all these.

**2:41** And in that you saw the feet and the toes part potter's clay and part iron, it will be a divided kingdom, yet something of the hardness of iron will be in it, just as you saw iron mingled with muddy clay.

**2:42** And the toes of the feet being part iron and part clay, a portion of the kingdom will be strong, and a portion of it will be fragile.

**2:43** And in that you saw iron mingled with muddy clay, they will mingle together through human seed, yet they will not cling to one another, this one to that one, just as iron does not mingle with clay.

**2:44** And in the days of those kings the God of heaven will establish a kingdom that will never be ruined, nor will the kingdom be passed on to another people; it will grind down and finish off all these kingdoms, while it itself will endure forever,

**2:45** just as you saw that a stone was broken off from the mountain without hands and ground down the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver, and the gold. A great God has disclosed to the king what will occur after this; and certain is the dream, and reliable is its interpretation."

**2:46** Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face and bowed down to Daniel, and he gave orders to present to him an offering and soothing fragrances.

**2:47** The king replied to Daniel, saying, "In truth, your God is God of gods, Lord of kings, and one who uncovers mysteries, because you were able to uncover this mystery."

**2:48** Then the king elevated Daniel and presented him with many large gifts, setting him as governor over the entire province of Babylon and chief of the prefects over all the wise men of Babylon.

**2:49** And Daniel petitioned the king, and he set Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego over the affairs of the province of Babylon, while Daniel remained at the king's court.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — Shenatho nihyethah alaw is literally "his sleep was upon him" or "came to an end on him"; the sense is that he could not sleep.
- **2:2** — The four classes are chartummim (magicians), ashshafim (enchanters or conjurers), mekhashshefim (sorcerers) and kasdim (Chaldeans, here a class of learned diviners).
- **2:4** — The Hebrew narrative switches to Aramaic after "in Aramaic"; the Aramaic continues through 7:28. Ketiv l'bdyk / qere le'avdakh both mean "to your servants"; the qere is translated.
- **2:5** — Ketiv/qere differ only in the spelling of "to the Chaldeans." Azda is a Persian loanword probably meaning "certain, publicly announced" (older translations took it as "gone"). Haddamin tit'avdun is literally "you will be made into pieces." Newali means "refuse heap."
- **2:8** — Idanna antun zavnin is literally "you are buying the time," that is, stalling.
- **2:9** — Chadah-hi dathkhon is literally "one is your law," meaning there is only one verdict for you. The Hithpael qere hizdemintun ("you have conspired / agreed together") is translated in place of the Haphel ketiv hizmantun ("you have prepared/planned").
- **2:10** — The ketiv/qere difference is only in the spelling of "Chaldeans." Kol-qovel di is a recurring Aramaic connective meaning "because, since, just as."
- **2:11** — "Flesh" (bisra) means mortal humanity.
- **2:14** — Rav-tabbachayya is literally "chief of the slaughterers"; the title came to mean the commander of the royal bodyguard.
- **2:18** — Elah shemayya, rendered "the God of heaven," is a regular title in Persian-period Aramaic; shemayya has the same plural form as Hebrew shamayim.
- **2:22** — The qere nehora ("light") is translated; the ketiv differs only in spelling.
- **2:25** — Bene galutha di Yehud is literally "the sons of the exile of Judah."
- **2:26** — The ketiv/qere difference (ha'ithakh, "are you") is one of spelling only.
- **2:27** — Gazrin, literally "cutters/deciders," are probably astrologers or diviners who determine fates.
- **2:28** — Be'acharith yomayya is literally "in the end of the days." The "visions of your head" are what passed through his mind.
- **2:29** — The qere ant ("you") is translated, and likewise in 2:31, 2:37 and 2:38. "He who uncovers mysteries" (gale razayya) refers to God, so the pronoun is capitalized.
- **2:31** — Wa'alu is an Aramaic interjection like "look!" (equivalent to Hebrew hinneh); it is rendered "and look."
- **2:33** — Chasaf is clay or earthenware; the qere forms minnehen (feminine "some of them") are translated as "part ... part" (and likewise in 2:41 and 2:42).
- **2:38** — Bene anasha is literally "sons of men" (human beings), here paired with wild animals and birds as what God places in the king's hand. The qere dayerin ("dwell") is translated.
- **2:39** — The qere ara' ("lower, inferior") replaces ketiv ar'a (which resembles the emphatic noun "earth"), while qere telitha'ah ("third") differs from the ketiv only in spelling.
- **2:40** — The qere revi'a'ah ("fourth") is translated.
- **2:41** — Nitsbetha means firmness or hardness. Chasaf di-fechar is "potter's clay," and chasaf tina is "clay of mud."
- **2:43** — The qere wedi ("and as") is translated. "Mix themselves with the seed of men" probably refers to intermarriage between royal houses, but the Aramaic is kept literal.
- **2:46** — Segid means to bow down or pay homage; "bowed down" keeps the act without deciding on its theology. Nichochin are pleasing or soothing aromas (incense).
- **2:47** — Mare malkhin is rendered "Lord of kings" because it refers to God.
- **2:48** — Rabbi is "made great," that is, promoted. Rav-signin is "chief of the prefects."
- **2:49** — Bithra malka is literally "in the gate of the king," meaning the royal court where officials served.
