## Text

**3:1** King Nebuchadnezzar made a statue of gold; its height was sixty cubits and its width six cubits. He set it up on the plain of Dura in the province of Babylon.

**3:2** Then King Nebuchadnezzar sent to gather the satraps, the prefects, and the governors, the advisers, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates, and all the rulers of the provinces to come to the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**3:3** Then the satraps, the prefects, and the governors, the advisers, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates, and all the rulers of the provinces gathered together for the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up, and they stood in front of the statue that Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**3:4** And the herald called out loudly, "To you it is declared, peoples, nations, and languages,

**3:5** that at the time you hear the sound of the horn, flute, lyre, trigon, harp, bagpipe, and every sort of music, you are to fall down and worship the golden statue that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.

**3:6** And whoever does not fall down and worship will at that very moment be thrown into the midst of the furnace of burning fire."

**3:7** Because of this, at that time, as soon as all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, flute, lyre, trigon, harp, and every sort of music, all the peoples, nations, and languages fell down, worshiping the golden statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**3:8** Because of this, at that time, Chaldean men came forward and maliciously accused the Jews.

**3:9** They spoke up and said to King Nebuchadnezzar, "King, live forever!

**3:10** You, King, have issued a decree that every person who hears the sound of the horn, flute, lyre, trigon, harp, bagpipe, and every sort of music is to fall down and worship the golden statue,

**3:11** and whoever does not fall down and worship will be thrown into the midst of the furnace of burning fire.

**3:12** There are Jewish men whom you appointed over the administration of the province of Babylon: Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. These men, King, have paid no regard to you; they do not serve your god, and they do not worship the golden statue that you have set up."

**3:13** Then Nebuchadnezzar, in rage and fury, ordered Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego to be brought; then those men were brought before the king.

**3:14** Nebuchadnezzar spoke up and said to them, "Is it true, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, that you are not serving my gods and are not worshiping the golden statue that I have set up?

**3:15** Now if you are ready, so that at the time you hear the sound of the horn, flute, lyre, trigon, harp, bagpipe, and every sort of music, you fall down and worship the statue that I have made—but if you do not worship, at that very moment you will be thrown into the midst of the furnace of burning fire. And what god is there who will rescue you from my hands?"

**3:16** Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered and said to the king, "Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to give you a reply concerning this.

**3:17** If it is so, our God whom we serve is able to rescue us; He will rescue us from the furnace of burning fire and from your hand, King.

**3:18** But if not, let it be known to you, King, that we do not serve your god and will not worship the golden statue that you have set up."

**3:19** Then Nebuchadnezzar was filled with fury, and the expression of his face changed toward Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. He spoke up and gave orders to heat the furnace seven times more than it was customary to heat it.

**3:20** And he ordered mighty men of strength who were in his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego and throw them into the furnace of burning fire.

**3:21** Then those men were bound in their mantles, their tunics, their caps, and their garments, and were thrown into the midst of the furnace of burning fire.

**3:22** Because of this, since the king's command was urgent and the furnace was exceedingly hot, the flame of the fire killed those men who carried up Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.

**3:23** And those three men, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, fell into the midst of the furnace of burning fire, bound.

**3:91** Then King Nebuchadnezzar was astounded and rose up in haste. He spoke up and said to his ministers, "Did we not throw three men into the midst of the fire, bound?" They answered and said to the king, "Certainly, King."

**3:92** He answered and said, "Look! I see four men unbound, walking about in the midst of the fire, and there is no harm on them; and the appearance of the fourth resembles a son of the gods."

**3:93** Then Nebuchadnezzar approached the door of the furnace of burning fire. He spoke up and said, "Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, servants of the Most High God, come out and come here!" Then Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego came out from the midst of the fire.

**3:94** And the satraps, the prefects, the governors, and the king's ministers gathered together and observed those men, that the fire had no power over their bodies: the hair of their heads was not singed, their mantles were not altered, and no smell of fire had come upon them.

**3:95** Nebuchadnezzar spoke up and said, "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, who sent His angel and rescued His servants who trusted in Him; they defied the king's command and surrendered their bodies so that they would not serve or worship any god except their own God.

**3:96** And by me a decree is issued that any people, nation, or language that speaks anything irreverent against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego will be cut limb from limb, and their house will be turned into a rubbish heap, because there is no other god who is able to deliver in this way."

**3:97** Then the king made Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego prosper in the province of Babylon.

## Translator Notes

- **3:8** — Literally 'ate the pieces of the Jews,' an Aramaic and Akkadian idiom meaning to slander or bring malicious accusations.
- **3:12** — The translation follows the Qere singular 'your god' (as also in 3:18); the Ketiv reads plural 'your gods' (contrast the unambiguous plural 'my gods' in 3:14).
- **3:15** — The Aramaic conditional sentence leaves its positive outcome unstated (aposiopesis), breaking directly into the warning 'but if you do not worship.'
- **3:17** — Following the Masoretic accentuation, Aramaic hen itay is rendered 'if it is so,' with both prepositional phrases governed by the second verb 'rescue'; alternatively, the clause may be construed as 'if our God whom we serve is able to rescue us, He will rescue us.'
- **3:92** — Literally 'a son of gods,' a Semitic idiom used by the pagan king for a divine or angelic being (cf. 'His angel' in 3:95 [MT 3:28]). Verses 3:91–97 correspond to MT 3:24–30.
