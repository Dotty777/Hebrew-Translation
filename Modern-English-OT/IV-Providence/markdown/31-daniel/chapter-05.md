## Text

**5:1** Belshazzar the king made a great banquet for a thousand of his nobles, and in front of the thousand he was drinking wine.

**5:2** While tasting the wine, Belshazzar gave orders to bring the vessels of gold and silver that his father Nebuchadnezzar had brought out from the temple that was in Jerusalem, so that the king and his nobles, his wives and his concubines, might drink out of them.

**5:3** Then they brought the vessels of gold that had been brought out from the temple of the house of God that was in Jerusalem, and the king and his nobles, his wives and his concubines, drank out of them.

**5:4** They drank the wine and gave praise to the gods of gold and silver, bronze, iron, wood, and stone.

**5:5** At that very moment fingers of a human hand came out and were writing opposite the lampstand on the plaster of the wall of the king's palace, and the king was watching the palm of the hand that was writing.

**5:6** Then the king's complexion changed, and his thoughts alarmed him; the joints of his hips were loosened, and his knees were knocking one against another.

**5:7** The king called out loudly to bring in the enchanters, the Chaldeans, and the diviners. The king responded and said to the wise men of Babylon, "Anyone who reads this writing and declares its interpretation to me will wear purple and have a chain of gold around his neck, and will rule as third in the kingdom."

**5:8** Then all the wise men of the king came in, yet they were not able to read the writing or to make its interpretation known to the king.

**5:9** Then King Belshazzar became deeply alarmed, and his complexion was changing, and his nobles were bewildered.

**5:10** Because of the words of the king and his nobles, the queen entered the banquet hall. The queen responded and said, "King, live forever! Do not let your thoughts alarm you, and do not let your complexion change.

**5:11** There is a man in your kingdom in whom is a spirit of the holy gods, and in the days of your father illumination, discernment, and wisdom like the wisdom of the gods were found in him; and King Nebuchadnezzar your father appointed him chief of the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans, and diviners—your father the king.

**5:12** Because an extraordinary spirit, understanding, and discernment—interpreting dreams, explaining riddles, and untying knots—were found in Daniel, whom the king named Belteshazzar, now let Daniel be summoned, and he will declare the interpretation."

**5:13** Then Daniel was brought in before the king. The king spoke up and said to Daniel, "Are you Daniel, one of the exiles of Judah whom the king my father brought from Judah?

**5:16** And I have heard about you that you are able to give interpretations and untie knots. Now if you are able to read the writing and make its interpretation known to me, you will wear purple and have a chain of gold around your neck, and you will rule as third in the kingdom."

**5:17** Then Daniel responded and said before the king, "May your gifts remain yours, and give your rewards to someone else; however, I will read the writing to the king and make the interpretation known to him.

**5:23** And you have exalted yourself against the Lord of heaven, and they brought the vessels of His house before you, and you and your nobles, your wives and your concubines, were drinking wine out of them; and you gave praise to the gods of silver and gold, bronze, iron, wood, and stone, which do not see, do not hear, and do not know, yet the God in whose hand is your breath, and to whom all your ways belong, you have not glorified.

**5:26** This is the interpretation of the word: 'Mene'—God has numbered your kingdom and brought it to a close.

**5:27** 'Tekel'—you have been weighed in the scales and found lacking.

**5:28** 'Peres'—your kingdom has been divided and given to Media and Persia."

**5:29** Then Belshazzar gave the order, and they clothed Daniel in purple, with a chain of gold around his neck, and proclaimed concerning him that he was to be the third ruler in the kingdom.

**5:30** On that same night Belshazzar, the Chaldean king, was killed.

## Translator Notes

- **5:5** — "fingers ... came out": Following the Qere nepaqah (Peal perfect 3fp), which agrees with the feminine plural subject "fingers," whereas the Ketiv reads masculine plural npqw. "palm of the hand": Aramaic pas yeda refers to the flat portion of the hand from wrist to fingers.
- **5:6** — "the joints of his hips were loosened": Aramaic qitrei chartseh mishtarayin (literally "the knots of his loins were untied," Hithpeel participle of shera) sets up an ironic wordplay with Daniel's reputation for "untying knots" (qitrin, shera) in 5:12 and 5:16.
- **5:7** — "third in the kingdom": Aramaic talti (and talta in 5:16, 29) means "third" or "third ruler."
- **5:8** — "its interpretation": Following the Qere ufishreh ("and its interpretation") rather than the Ketiv wpsr' ("and the interpretation").
- **5:24 *(additional)*** — Literally "the palm of the hand."
- **5:25 *(additional)*** — The Aramaic words name weights—mina, shekel, and half-minas (plural parsin, preceded by u-, "and")—which Daniel explains in 5:26-28 by wordplay on cognate verbs ("numbered," "weighed," "divided," using singular Peres) and the name Paras ("Persia").
