## Text

**2:1** At my watch post I will stand and position myself on the rampart, and I will watch to see what He will speak with me, and what response I will give regarding my complaint.

**2:2** And the LORD answered me and said, "Write the vision and inscribe it clearly on the tablets, so that whoever reads it may run.

**2:3** For still the vision is for the appointed time; it hurries toward the end and will not lie. If it lingers, wait for it, for it will surely come; it will not be late.

**2:4** Look, his soul is puffed up, not upright in him; but the righteous one will live by his faithfulness.

**2:5** Indeed, wine is treacherous; an arrogant man does not settle down, who widens his appetite like Sheol; he is like death and is not satisfied; he gathers to himself all the nations and assembles to himself all the peoples.

**2:6** Will not all of these take up a taunt against him, with satire and riddles about him, and say, 'Woe to the one who multiplies what does not belong to him—how long?—and weighs himself down with pledges!

**2:7** Will your creditors not suddenly rise up, and those who make you tremble wake up? And you will become plunder to them.

**2:8** Because you yourself have plundered many nations, all the rest of the peoples will plunder you, because of human bloodshed and violence against the land, against the city and all who live in it.

**2:9** Woe to the one who acquires evil gain for his house, to place his nest on high, to be rescued from the grasp of calamity!

**2:10** You have planned shame for your house, cutting off many peoples and sinning against your own life.

**2:11** For a stone from the wall will cry out, and a rafter from the timber will answer it.

**2:12** Woe to the one who builds a town with bloodshed and establishes a city with injustice!

**2:13** See, is it not from the LORD of hosts that peoples toil only for fire, and nations exhaust themselves only for emptiness?

**2:14** For the earth will be filled with knowing the LORD's glory, just as the waters cover over the sea.

**2:15** Woe to the one who makes his neighbor drink, mixing in your venom and even making them drunk, in order to look at their nakedness!

**2:16** You are sated with disgrace rather than glory. Drink, you too, and be exposed as uncircumcised! The cup of the LORD's right hand will pass around to you, and foul disgrace will be on your glory.

**2:17** For the violence done to Lebanon will cover you, and the devastation of beasts that terrified them, because of human bloodshed and violence against the land, against the city and all who live in it.

**2:18** What profit is a carved image when its maker has carved it—a cast image and a teacher of falsehood? For the maker of his own creation trusts in it, making mute idols!

**2:19** Woe to the one who says to wood, "Wake up!" and "Arise!" to a silent stone! Will it teach? Look, it is plated with gold and silver, and no breath at all is within it.

**2:20** But the LORD is in His holy temple; hush before Him, all the earth!'"

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — It hurries renders yapheah (from puah, breathe out, pant, or hasten); the form can also be parsed as a verbal adjective meaning testifies or witnesses (cf. Prov 14:5), pairing closely with will not lie.
- **2:4** — Puffed up renders the 3fs Pual perfect 'uppela, agreeing with feminine naphsho (his soul). By his faithfulness translates be'emunato, denoting steadfast fidelity and trust (quoted in the New Testament as by faith).
- **2:5** — Wine follows the Masoretic Hebrew (hayyayin), whereas the Dead Sea Pesher (1QpHab) reads wealth (hon). Settle down renders the rare verb yinveh (related to naveh, home or abode).
- **2:16** — Be exposed as uncircumcised follows the Masoretic Niphal imperative vehe'arel (root 'rl), whereas the Dead Sea Pesher and Septuagint read a metathesized form meaning and stagger. Foul disgrace renders the hapax qiqalon, an intensive form of qalon (disgrace) suggesting a wordplay on qi' (vomit).
- **2:17** — That terrified them follows the Masoretic verb yehitan (Hiphil imperfect of hatat with a 3fp suffix referring to the feminine plural beasts), whereas the Septuagint and Syriac read a 2ms suffix (will terrify you).
