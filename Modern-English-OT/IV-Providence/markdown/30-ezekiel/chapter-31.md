## Text

**31:1** Now in the eleventh year, in the third month, on the first of the month, the word of the LORD came to me, saying,

**31:2** "Son of man, say to Pharaoh king of Egypt and to his multitude: 'Whom are you like in your greatness?

**31:3** Look, Assyria was a cedar in Lebanon, with beautiful branches and forest shade, and tall in stature, and its top was among the dense boughs.

**31:4** Waters made it grow; the deep made it tall, with its rivers flowing around its planting place, and it sent out its channels to all the trees of the field.

**31:5** Therefore its height towered above all the trees of the field; its boughs multiplied and its branches grew long because of abundant waters as it sent them forth.

**31:6** In its boughs all the birds of the sky made their nests, and under its branches all the beasts of the field gave birth, and in its shade all the great nations dwelt.

**31:7** And it was beautiful in its greatness, in the length of its limbs, for its root reached to abundant waters.

**31:8** The cedars in the garden of God could not overshadow it; the cypresses did not compare to its boughs, and the plane trees were not like its branches; no tree in the garden of God resembled it in its beauty.

**31:9** I made it beautiful with its abundance of limbs, and all the trees of Eden that were in the garden of God envied it.

**31:10** Therefore, this is what the Lord GOD says: Because you towered in height, and it set its top among the dense boughs, and its heart was lifted up in its height,

**31:11** I gave it into the hand of a mighty ruler of nations; he would surely deal with it. According to its wickedness I drove it out.

**31:12** Foreigners, the most ruthless of nations, cut it down and abandoned it. Upon the mountains and in all the valleys its limbs fell, and its branches were broken in all the ravines of the earth; and all the peoples of the earth went down from its shade and abandoned it.

**31:13** Upon its fallen trunk all the birds of the sky dwell, and upon its branches are all the beasts of the field,

**31:14** so that none of the trees by the waters may tower in their height or set their top among the dense boughs, nor may their mighty ones, all those that drink water, stand up in their height; for all of them have been given over to death, to the earth below, in the midst of the sons of men, to those who go down to the pit.

**31:15** This is what the Lord GOD says: On the day it went down to Sheol, I caused mourning; I covered the deep for it and held back its rivers, and the abundant waters were restrained. I darkened Lebanon for it, and all the trees of the field fainted for it.

**31:16** At the sound of its downfall I made nations quake, when I brought it down to Sheol with those who go down to the pit; and all the trees of Eden, the choicest and best of Lebanon, all those that drink water, were comforted in the earth below.

**31:17** They also went down with it to Sheol, to those killed by the sword, and those who were its arm, who dwelt in its shade in the midst of the nations.

**31:18** Whom are you thus like in glory and in greatness among the trees of Eden? Yet you will be brought down with the trees of Eden to the earth below; in the midst of the uncircumcised you will lie, with those killed by the sword. This is Pharaoh and all his multitude, declares the Lord GOD.'"

## Translator Notes

- **31:3** — Hebrew avotim in 31:3, 31:10, and 31:14 is morphologically a plural of avot, dense or intertwined boughs, as in Ezekiel 19:11, though ancient versions render it clouds by connecting it to av.
- **31:10** — The Masoretic text shifts abruptly from second-person masculine singular gavahta, you towered, directly addressing the royal figure, to third-person masculine singular forms for the rest of the verse.
- **31:14** — Hebrew elehem is pointed with an initial tsere as the plural noun el or ayil with a suffix, their mighty ones or mighty trees, rather than the preposition alehem, to them.
- **31:15** — Hebrew ulpeh is a rare Pual-patterned stative or nominal form from the root alaph, meaning grew faint or wilted.
- **31:17** — Hebrew uzeroo literally means and its arm, referring collectively to the empire's military allies or supporters, which accounts for the plural verb yashevu, dwelt.
