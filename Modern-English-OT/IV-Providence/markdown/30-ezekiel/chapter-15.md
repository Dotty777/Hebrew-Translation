## Text

**15:1** Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying,

**15:2** "Son of man, how is the wood of the vine superior to any other wood—the vine branch that is among the trees of the forest?

**15:3** Is wood taken from it to be used for any work? Or do people take a peg from it on which to hang any vessel?

**15:4** Look, it has been given to the fire to be consumed; the fire has consumed both of its ends, and its middle is charred. Is it fit for any work?

**15:5** Look, while it was whole, it could not be used for any work; how much less, when fire has consumed it and it has been charred, can it still be used for any work?

**15:6** Therefore this is what the Lord GOD says: Just as the wood of the vine among the trees of the forest, which I have given to the fire to be consumed, so I have given over the inhabitants of Jerusalem.

**15:7** And I will set My face against them; from the fire they have come out, yet the fire will consume them, and you will know that I am the LORD when I set My face against them.

**15:8** And I will make the land a desolation, because they have committed treachery, declares the Lord GOD."

## Translator Notes

- **15:2** — The comparative idiom mah-yihyeh ... mikkol-ets literally asks "what is [the wood of the vine] more than any wood?" The feminine noun hazzemorah ("the vine branch") stands in apposition to masculine ets-haggefen ("the wood of the vine"), which accounts for the masculine verb hayah ("is") that follows.
- **15:3** — In vv. 3 and 5, the verb asah ("make, work") combined with limlakhah ("for work") denotes working or employing a material for a useful craft or object.
- **15:4** — The Hebrew repeats the root akhal ("eat, consume") across vv. 4-7, pairing the noun leokhlah ("to be consumed," lit. "for food/consumption," vv. 4, 6) with the fire consuming the wood and the inhabitants of Jerusalem (vv. 4, 5, 7). Both nachar (v. 4) and vayyechar (v. 5) are Niphal forms of charar ("be charred, scorched").
