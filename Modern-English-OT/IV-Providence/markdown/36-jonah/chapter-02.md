## Text

**2:1** Now the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah, and Jonah was in the fish's belly for three days and three nights.

**2:2** Then Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the fish's belly.

**2:3** He said, "From my distress I called to the LORD, and He answered me. From the belly of Sheol I cried for help; You heard my voice.

**2:4** And You cast me into the depths, into the heart of the seas, and the current encircled me; all Your breakers and Your waves passed over me.

**2:5** And I said, 'I have been driven out from before Your eyes,' yet I will look once more toward Your holy temple.

**2:6** Waters hemmed me in to my very life, the deep encircled me, seaweed was bound around my head.

**2:7** To the bases of the mountains I went down; the earth—its bars were behind me forever. Yet from the pit You brought up my life, LORD my God.

**2:8** When my soul grew faint within me, it was the LORD I remembered, and my prayer came to You, to Your holy temple.

**2:9** Those who pay heed to worthless vanities abandon their steadfast love.

**2:10** But as for me, with a voice of thanksgiving I will sacrifice to You; what I have vowed I will fulfill. Salvation is the LORD's!"

**2:11** Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah onto the dry ground.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — In the Masoretic Hebrew versification followed here, 2:1-11 corresponds to 1:17-2:10 in most English Bibles.
- **2:2** — Hebrew uses the feminine form ha-dagah here in place of the masculine ha-dag ("the fish") in 2:1 and 2:11, referring to the same great fish.
- **2:5** — The Masoretic Text reads akh ("yet" or "surely") followed by the auxiliary idiom osif lehabbit (Hiphil of yasaf plus Hiphil infinitive of nabat, "I will look once more"), expressing renewed hope in contrast to Jonah's despair.
- **2:9** — Hebrew meshamerim (the sole Piel occurrence of shamar in the Hebrew Bible) paired with havlei-shav (literally "vanities of emptiness," a poetic idiom for false gods) contrasts idol worship with "their steadfast love" (chasdam), which can mean either their own loyalty or the faithful love God extends to them.
