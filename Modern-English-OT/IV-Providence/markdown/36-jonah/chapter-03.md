## Text

**3:1** And the word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time, saying,

**3:2** "Rise, go to Nineveh, the great city, and proclaim to it the proclamation that I am speaking to you."

**3:3** Jonah rose and went to Nineveh in accordance with the word of the LORD. Now Nineveh was a great city to God, a three-day walk.

**3:4** Jonah began to enter the city, a one-day walk, and he proclaimed and said, "Forty more days, and Nineveh will be overturned!"

**3:5** And the people of Nineveh believed in God; they proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from their greatest to their least.

**3:6** And the word reached the king of Nineveh, and he rose from his throne, took off his mantle from himself, covered himself in sackcloth, and sat on the ashes.

**3:7** And he had it cried out and announced in Nineveh by the decree of the king and his nobles, saying, "Man and beast, herd and flock—let them not taste anything; let them not graze, and let them not drink water.

**3:8** Rather, let man and beast alike be covered in sackcloth, and let them call to God fervently; let each one turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in their hands.

**3:9** Who knows whether God will turn and relent, and turn back from His burning anger, so that we do not perish."

**3:10** And God saw their deeds, that they had turned from their evil way; so God relented concerning the calamity that He had spoken of doing to them, and He did not do it.

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — Hebrew ir-gedolah le-Elohim (literally "a great city to God") is a Hebrew superlative idiom meaning "an exceedingly great city," while also hinting at Nineveh's importance to God.
- **3:4** — Hebrew nehpakhet (Niphal participle of hafakh, "be overturned") conveys imminent judgment like the overthrow of Sodom (Gen 19:25), yet the verb can also mean "be turned around" or "transformed," creating an ironic double meaning.
- **3:7** — Hebrew ta'am ("decree") reflects an Aramaic official usage (cf. Ezra 6:14) and creates a wordplay with the verb yit'amu ("taste," from the same root ta'am) later in the verse.
- **3:10** — Hebrew repeats the same root ra'ah both for Nineveh's "evil" way and for the "calamity" God had spoken of bringing, highlighting the reversal of judgment upon their repentance.
