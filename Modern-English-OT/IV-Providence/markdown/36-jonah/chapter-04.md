## Text

**4:1** But it was a great displeasure to Jonah, and he burned with anger.

**4:2** He prayed to the LORD and said, "Please, LORD, was this not my word while I was still on my own soil? For this reason I acted beforehand to flee to Tarshish, for I knew that You are a God who is gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and great in loyal love, and relenting over the calamity.

**4:3** So now, LORD, please take my life from me, for my death is better than my life."

**4:4** And the LORD said, "Are you right to be angry?"

**4:5** Then Jonah went out from the city and sat east of the city; there he made himself a booth, and he sat beneath it in the shade until he saw what would happen in the city.

**4:6** Now the LORD God appointed a plant, and it came up above Jonah to be shade over his head, to rescue him from his misery. And Jonah rejoiced over the plant with great joy.

**4:7** But God appointed a worm as dawn came up on the next day, and it struck the plant so that it dried up.

**4:8** And as the sun rose, God appointed a stifling east wind, and the sun struck down on Jonah's head so that he grew faint. He asked for himself to die and said, "My death is better than my life."

**4:9** Then God said to Jonah, "Are you right to be angry over the plant?" And he said, "I am right to be angry, to the point of death."

**4:10** And the LORD said, "You had pity on the plant, over which you did not toil and which you did not make grow, which came to be in a single night and perished in a single night.

**4:11** And should I not have pity on Nineveh, the great city, where there are more than one hundred twenty thousand people who do not discern between their right hand and their left, and much livestock?"

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — Hebrew haheitev (interrogative prefix + Hiphil infinitive absolute of y-t-b, "do well") can mean either "Are you right to be angry?" or adverbially "Are you deeply angry?"; the same construction recurs in 4:9.
- **4:6** — The noun qiqayon occurs only in Jonah 4 and is of uncertain botanical identity, commonly identified with the fast-growing castor-oil plant. The verb wayya'al can be parsed as either Qal ("and it came up") or Hiphil ("and He caused it to come up") of the root alah.
- **4:8** — Hebrew charishit is a hapax legomenon modifying the feminine noun ruach ("wind"). Commonly linked to the root ch-r-sh ("be silent/still"), it denotes an oppressive, stifling heat, while ancient versions render it "scorching."
- **4:10** — Literally "which was a son of a night and as a son of a night perished," an idiomatic expression for something arising and perishing within a single night.
- **4:11** — Hebrew introduces this closing clause without an interrogative particle, forming an unmarked rhetorical question (literally "and I, I shall not have pity...?"). The phrase "more than 120,000" is literally "more than twelve myriads" (twelve ten-thousands).
