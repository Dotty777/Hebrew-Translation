## Text

**7:1** Woe to me, for I have become like the gatherings of summer fruit, like the gleanings of the grape harvest; there is not a cluster to eat, nor an early fig that my soul craves.

**7:2** The devout one has perished from the land, and there is no upright person among humankind; all of them lie in ambush for bloodshed; each man hunts his brother with a net.

**7:3** Both hands are on evil to do it skillfully: the official asks, and the judge asks for a payoff, and the prominent man speaks the craving of his soul; and they twist it together.

**7:4** Their best is like a brier, the most upright worse than a thorn hedge. The day of your watchmen, your reckoning, has come; now will be their bewilderment.

**7:5** Do not put trust in a neighbor; do not rely on a close friend; from her who lies in your embrace, guard the doorways of your mouth.

**7:6** For a son disdains a father, a daughter rises against her mother, a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law; the enemies of a man are the men of his house.

**7:7** Yet I will keep watch for the LORD; I will hope in the God of my salvation; my God will hear me.

**7:8** Do not rejoice over me, my enemy! Though I have fallen, I will rise up; though I sit in darkness, the LORD is a light to me.

**7:9** I will bear the LORD's indignation, for I have sinned against Him, until He argues my case and renders justice for me. He will bring me out into the light; I will gaze upon His righteousness.

**7:10** And my enemy will see, and shame will cover her, the one who said to me, "Where is the LORD your God?" My eyes will gaze upon her; now she will be trampled underfoot like mud of the streets.

**7:11** A day to build your walls! On that day the boundary will extend far.

**7:12** On that day they will come to you from Assyria and the cities of Egypt, and from Egypt as far as the River, and from sea to sea, and from mountain to mountain.

**7:13** Yet the earth will become a desolation on account of its inhabitants, from the fruit of their deeds.

**7:14** Shepherd Your people with Your rod, the flock of Your inheritance, dwelling alone in a woodland in the midst of fertile land; may they graze in Bashan and Gilead as in ancient days.

**7:15** As in the days of your coming out from the land of Egypt, I will show him wonders.

**7:16** The nations will see and be ashamed of all their strength; they will place a hand over the mouth; their ears will grow deaf.

**7:17** They will lick dust like a serpent; like creeping things of the earth, they will come trembling from their enclosures; they will turn in dread toward the LORD our God, and they will fear You.

**7:18** Who is a God like You, who forgives iniquity and passes over rebellion for the remnant of His inheritance? He does not hold fast to His anger forever, for He delights in loyal love.

**7:19** He will again show us compassion; He will subdue our iniquities; and You will hurl all their sins into the depths of the sea.

**7:20** You will give faithfulness to Jacob, loyal love to Abraham, which You swore to our fathers from days of old.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The opening clause is notably terse (literally "upon evil are both hands, to do well," with לְהֵיטִיב Hiphil infinitive construct of יטב). The final verb וַיְעַבְּתוּהָ is a Piel hapax legomenon from עבת (cognate with עֲבֹת, "cord"), picturing the corrupt leaders twisting the scheme together.
- **7:12** — מָצוֹר is the poetic singular designation for Egypt (as in 2 Kings 19:24; Isaiah 19:6), though the word can also mean "fortress," and נָהָר ("the River") denotes the Euphrates. The closing poetic merisms וְיָם מִיָּם וְהַר הָהָר literally read "and sea from sea, and mountain from the mountain."
- **7:14** — Anarthrous כַּרְמֶל can denote "fertile land" or "orchard" (paired with יַעַר, "woodland," as in Isaiah 29:17; 32:15), or the proper name "Carmel."
- **7:15** — The Masoretic text shifts from second-person singular צֵאתְךָ ("your coming out") to first-person divine speech with a third-person masculine singular object suffix in אַרְאֶנּוּ (Hiphil imperfect of ראה, "I will show him," referring collectively to the people of Israel).
- **7:19** — Following the Masoretic text strictly, the verse shifts from third person ("He") to second person ("You," Hiphil imperfect וְתַשְׁלִיךְ) and preserves the third-person plural suffix in חַטֹּאותָם ("their sins"), which several ancient versions harmonize to "our sins."
