## Text

**9:1** The LORD spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, in the second year after their departure from the land of Egypt, in the first month, saying,

**9:2** "Let the sons of Israel observe the Passover at its set time.

**9:3** On the fourteenth day of this month, at twilight, you shall observe it at its set time; in accordance with all its statutes and in accordance with all its regulations you shall observe it."

**9:4** So Moses spoke to the sons of Israel that they should observe the Passover.

**9:5** And they observed the Passover in the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month, at twilight, in the wilderness of Sinai; in accordance with everything that the LORD had commanded Moses, so the sons of Israel did.

**9:6** Now there were certain men who were impure because of a human corpse, and they could not observe the Passover on that day; so they drew near before Moses and before Aaron on that day.

**9:7** And those men said to him, "We are impure because of a human corpse. Why should we be held back from presenting the offering of the LORD at its set time in the midst of the sons of Israel?"

**9:8** And Moses said to them, "Stand still, and let me hear what the LORD will command concerning you."

**9:9** Then the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**9:10** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying, 'If any man of you or of your generations is impure because of a corpse, or is on a distant journey, he shall observe Passover to the LORD.

**9:11** In the second month, on the fourteenth day at twilight, they shall observe it; with unleavened bread and bitter herbs they shall eat it.

**9:12** They shall not leave any of it until morning, and they shall break no bone of it; in accordance with the entire statute of the Passover they shall observe it.

**9:13** But the man who is pure and was not on a journey, yet refrains from observing the Passover—that person shall be cut off from his people; because he did not present the offering of the LORD at its set time, that man shall bear his sin.

**9:14** And if a resident alien resides with you and observes Passover to the LORD, in accordance with the statute of the Passover and in accordance with its regulation, so he shall do. There shall be one statute for you, both for the resident alien and for the native of the land.'"

**9:15** On the day the tabernacle was raised up, the cloud covered the tabernacle, the tent of the testimony; and in the evening it would be above the tabernacle like the appearance of fire until morning.

**9:16** Thus it would be continually: the cloud would cover it, and the appearance of fire by night.

**9:17** And whenever the cloud was lifted up from above the tent, afterward the sons of Israel would set out; and at the place where the cloud rested, there the sons of Israel would encamp.

**9:18** At the command of the LORD the sons of Israel would set out, and at the command of the LORD they would encamp; all the days that the cloud rested above the tabernacle, they would stay encamped.

**9:19** And when the cloud lingered above the tabernacle many days, the sons of Israel would keep the charge of the LORD and would not set out.

**9:20** At times the cloud would be above the tabernacle for a few days; at the command of the LORD they would encamp, and at the command of the LORD they would set out.

**9:21** And at times the cloud would be there from evening until morning, and when the cloud was lifted up in the morning, they would set out; or for a day and a night, when the cloud was lifted up, they would set out.

**9:22** Whether two days, or a month, or a longer period, while the cloud lingered above the tabernacle, resting upon it, the sons of Israel would stay encamped and would not set out; but when it was lifted up, they would set out.

**9:23** At the command of the LORD they would encamp, and at the command of the LORD they would set out; they kept the charge of the LORD, at the command of the LORD by the hand of Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **9:3** — "At twilight" translates the Hebrew dual idiom beyn ha'arbayim (literally "between the two evenings"), referring to the interval between late afternoon or sunset and full darkness.
- **9:6** — "Because of a human corpse" renders lenephesh adam; in priestly legal contexts nephesh commonly denotes a dead body, in contrast to its sense "person" in 9:13.
- **9:10** — The Masoretic text places an extraordinary dot (punctum extraordinarium) over the final letter of rechoqah ("distant"), an ancient scribal mark indicating textual scrutiny or legal qualification.
- **9:15** — "Was raised up" translates the impersonal Hiphil infinitive construct haqim ("on the day of raising up the tabernacle"), and the preposition lamed on "the tent of the testimony" functions appositionally to specify the tabernacle.
- **9:16** — The Masoretic Hebrew omits "by day" (implied by contrast with "by night"), and verses 15b-23 employ habitual imperfect verbs to describe recurring past actions ("would cover," "would set out").
