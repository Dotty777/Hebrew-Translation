## Text

**22:1** Then the sons of Israel set out and camped in the plains of Moab, beyond the Jordan at Jericho.

**22:2** Now Balak son of Zippor saw everything that Israel had done to the Amorites.

**22:3** So Moab was greatly afraid because of the people, for they were numerous; and Moab felt dread because of the sons of Israel.

**22:4** Moab said to the elders of Midian, "Now the assembly will lick up everything around us, just as an ox licks up the green growth of the field." Now Balak son of Zippor was king of Moab at that time.

**22:5** So he sent messengers to Balaam son of Beor at Pethor, which is by the River, in the land of the sons of his people, to summon him, saying, "Look, a people has come out from Egypt; look, they have covered the surface of the land, and they are dwelling right opposite me.

**22:6** Now therefore, please come, curse this people for me, for they are too mighty for me; perhaps I may prevail, so that we strike them and I drive them out of the land. For I know that the one you bless is blessed, and the one you curse is cursed."

**22:7** So the elders of Moab and the elders of Midian set out with fees for divination in their hands, and they came to Balaam and spoke Balak's words to him.

**22:8** He said to them, "Spend the night here, and I will bring word back to you just as the LORD speaks to me." So the officials of Moab stayed with Balaam.

**22:9** Then God came to Balaam and said, "Who are these men with you?"

**22:10** Balaam said to God, "Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, sent to me:

**22:11** 'Look, the people coming out from Egypt have covered the surface of the land. Now come, curse them for me; perhaps I will be able to fight against them and drive them out.'"

**22:12** God said to Balaam, "You must not go with them; you must not curse the people, for they are blessed."

**22:13** So Balaam got up in the morning and said to Balak's officials, "Go to your land, for the LORD has refused to let me go with you."

**22:14** So the officials of Moab got up, came to Balak, and said, "Balaam refused to come with us."

**22:15** Then Balak again sent officials, more numerous and more distinguished than these.

**22:16** They came to Balaam and said to him, "This is what Balak son of Zippor says: 'Please do not hold back from coming to me,

**22:17** for I will indeed honor you very highly, and whatever you say to me I will do. And please come, curse this people for me.'"

**22:18** Balaam replied and said to Balak's servants, "Even if Balak were to give me his house full of silver and gold, I could not go beyond the command of the LORD my God to do anything small or great.

**22:19** Now please stay here tonight, you as well, so that I may know what more the LORD will speak with me."

**22:20** Then God came to Balaam by night and said to him, "If the men have come to summon you, rise and go with them; yet only the word that I speak to you, that you must do."

**22:21** So Balaam got up in the morning, saddled his female donkey, and went with the officials of Moab.

**22:22** But God's anger burned because he was going, and the angel of the LORD stationed himself in the road as an adversary against him. Now he was riding on his donkey, and his two attendants were with him.

**22:23** When the donkey saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road with his drawn sword in his hand, the donkey turned aside from the road and went into the field. So Balaam struck the donkey to turn her back onto the road.

**22:24** Then the angel of the LORD stood in a narrow path of the vineyards, with a wall on this side and a wall on that side.

**22:25** When the donkey saw the angel of the LORD, she pressed herself against the wall and pressed Balaam's foot against the wall; so he struck her again.

**22:26** Then the angel of the LORD passed on further and stood in a narrow place where there was no way to turn aside to the right or to the left.

**22:27** When the donkey saw the angel of the LORD, she lay down beneath Balaam. Then Balaam's anger burned, and he struck the donkey with his staff.

**22:28** Then the LORD opened the mouth of the donkey, and she said to Balaam, "What have I done to you that you have struck me these three times?"

**22:29** Balaam said to the donkey, "Because you have made sport of me! If only a sword were in my hand, right now I would have killed you!"

**22:30** The donkey said to Balaam, "Am I not your donkey on which you have ridden all your life up to this day? Have I ever been in the habit of doing this to you?" And he said, "No."

**22:31** Then the LORD uncovered Balaam's eyes, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road with his drawn sword in his hand; and he bowed low and prostrated himself on his face.

**22:32** The angel of the LORD said to him, "Why have you struck your donkey these three times? Look, I have come out as an adversary, because the way is reckless before me.

**22:33** The donkey saw me and turned aside before me these three times. If she had not turned aside from before me, surely by now I would have killed you and kept her alive."

**22:34** Balaam said to the angel of the LORD, "I have sinned, for I did not know that you were standing in the road to confront me. Now then, if it is evil in your eyes, I will turn back."

**22:35** The angel of the LORD said to Balaam, "Go with the men, yet only the word that I speak to you, that you must speak." So Balaam went with Balak's officials.

**22:36** When Balak heard that Balaam had come, he went out to meet him at the city of Moab, which is on the border of the Arnon, at the far edge of the border.

**22:37** Balak said to Balaam, "Did I not indeed send to you to summon you? Why did you not come to me? Am I truly unable to honor you?"

**22:38** Balaam said to Balak, "Look, I have come to you! Now, am I at all able to speak anything? The word that God puts in my mouth, that I will speak."

**22:39** Then Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kiriath-huzoth.

**22:40** And Balak sacrificed cattle and sheep, and sent from them to Balaam and to the officials who were with him.

**22:41** In the morning, Balak took Balaam and brought him up to Bamoth-baal, and from there he saw the outer edge of the people.

## Translator Notes

- **22:5** — Hebrew literally reads "the eye of the land," an idiom for the visible surface of the ground. The Masoretic Text reads "the sons of his people" rather than a specific tribal name.
- **22:6** — The Masoretic Text shifts from first-person singular in "I may prevail" (Qal) to first-person plural in "we strike" (Hiphil), then back to first-person singular in "I drive them out" (Piel).
- **22:24** — The noun rendered "narrow path" (mish'ol) occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and denotes a narrow, walled lane between vineyards.
- **22:32** — The rare Qal verb yarat ("is reckless" or "is perverse") occurs only here and in Job 16:11. The Masoretic Text reads "the way" rather than "your way."
- **22:33** — The particle ulay normally means "perhaps," but here functions as a counterfactual negative conditional ("if ... not," equivalent to lule).
