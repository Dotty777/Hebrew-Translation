## Text

**11:1** Now the people were acting like complainers of hardship in the ears of the LORD; and the LORD heard, and His anger flared, and the fire of the LORD burned among them and consumed part of the edge of the camp.

**11:2** Then the people cried out to Moses, and Moses prayed to the LORD, and the fire subsided.

**11:3** So he called the name of that place Taberah, because the fire of the LORD had burned among them.

**11:4** Now the rabble who were among them felt a fierce craving, and the sons of Israel also wept again and said, "Who will feed us meat?

**11:5** We remember the fish that we used to eat in Egypt for nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic.

**11:6** But now our appetite has dried up; there is nothing at all, only the manna before our eyes."

**11:7** Now the manna was like coriander seed, and its appearance was like the appearance of bdellium.

**11:8** The people would go about and gather it, and grind it between millstones or pound it in a mortar, and boil it in a pot and make it into cakes; and its taste was like the taste of rich oil.

**11:9** When the dew came down upon the camp at night, the manna would come down upon it.

**11:10** Then Moses heard the people weeping throughout their families, each man at the entrance of his tent; and the anger of the LORD flared greatly, and it was evil in the eyes of Moses.

**11:11** So Moses said to the LORD, "Why have You treated Your servant badly? And why have I not found favor in Your eyes, that You have laid the burden of all this people upon me?

**11:12** Was it I who conceived all this people? Or was it I who gave birth to them, that You say to me, 'Carry them in your bosom, as a nurse carries a nursing infant,' to the land that You swore to their fathers?

**11:13** Where am I to get meat to give to all this people? For they keep weeping before me, saying, 'Give us meat that we may eat.'

**11:14** I alone am not able to carry all this people, because it is too heavy for me.

**11:15** If this is how You are going to treat me, please kill me outright, if I have found favor in Your eyes, and do not let me look upon my own wretchedness."

**11:16** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Gather for Me seventy men from the elders of Israel, whom you know to be elders of the people and their officials, and take them to the tent of meeting, and let them take their stand there with you.

**11:17** And I will come down and speak with you there, and I will withdraw some of the Spirit that is upon you and place it upon them; and they shall carry the burden of the people with you, so that you will not carry it by yourself.

**11:18** And to the people you shall say, 'Consecrate yourselves for tomorrow, and you shall eat meat, for you have wept in the ears of the LORD, saying, "Who will feed us meat? For it was good for us in Egypt." So the LORD will give you meat, and you shall eat.

**11:19** You shall not eat for one day, nor two days, nor five days, nor ten days, nor twenty days,

**11:20** but for a full month, until it comes out through your nostrils and becomes repulsive to you, because you have rejected the LORD who is in your midst and have wept before Him, saying, "Why did we ever come out of Egypt?"'"

**11:21** But Moses said, "The people in whose midst I am are six hundred thousand on foot, and yet You have said, 'I will give them meat, and they shall eat for a full month.'

**11:22** Shall flocks and herds be slaughtered for them and be enough for them? Or shall all the fish of the sea be gathered for them and be enough for them?"

**11:23** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Is the hand of the LORD too short? Now you will see whether My word comes to pass for you or not."

**11:24** So Moses went out and spoke the words of the LORD to the people; and he gathered seventy men from the elders of the people and stationed them around the tent.

**11:25** Then the LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and withdrew some of the Spirit that was upon him and put it upon the seventy elders. And when the Spirit rested upon them, they prophesied, but they did not do so again.

**11:26** Now two men had remained in the camp; the name of the one was Eldad, and the name of the second was Medad. And the Spirit rested upon them—they were among those registered, though they had not gone out to the tent—and they prophesied in the camp.

**11:27** So a young man ran and reported to Moses and said, "Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp."

**11:28** Then Joshua son of Nun, Moses' attendant from his youth, spoke up and said, "My lord Moses, restrain them!"

**11:29** But Moses said to him, "Are you jealous on my behalf? If only all the people of the LORD were prophets, that the LORD would place His Spirit upon them!"

**11:30** Then Moses withdrew to the camp, he and the elders of Israel.

**11:31** Now a wind went out from the LORD, and it swept quail in from the sea and dropped them beside the camp, about a day's journey on this side and about a day's journey on that side, all around the camp, and about two cubits above the surface of the ground.

**11:32** And the people were up all that day and all the night and all the next day, and they gathered the quail; the one who gathered least gathered ten homers. And they spread them out widely for themselves all around the camp.

**11:33** While the meat was still between their teeth, before it was chewed, the anger of the LORD flared against the people, and the LORD struck the people with a very severe plague.

**11:34** So the name of that place was called Kibroth-hattaavah, because there they buried the people who had been craving.

**11:35** From Kibroth-hattaavah the people set out for Hazeroth, and they remained at Hazeroth.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — The participle kəmiṯʾōnənîm (Hithpolel of ʾnn, "acting like complainers") is rare, occurring elsewhere only in Lamentations 3:39. The noun raʿ ("hardship" or "evil") may be read either as the subject of their complaint or, following the Masoretic cantillation, as a predicate assessing it as "evil in the ears of the LORD."
- **11:8** — "Rich oil" translates ləšaḏ haššāmen (literally "the moisture/richness of oil"), using the rare noun ləšaḏ (elsewhere only in Psalm 32:4), which denotes fresh oil or an oil-rich delicacy.
- **11:15** — The second-person pronoun addressing God is unusually spelled as the short form ʾat (normally feminine singular) alongside the masculine singular participle ʿōśeh ("treating"), a rare Masoretic orthographic feature also attested in Deuteronomy 5 and Ezekiel 28.
- **11:17** — Hebrew haruach carries the definite article ("the Spirit" or "the spirit," also in vv. 25–26, referring back to the prophetic endowment on Moses; cf. rucho, "His Spirit," in v. 29).
- **11:25** — "But they did not do so again" translates wəlōʾ yāsāpû (Qal of ysp, "add/continue/repeat"), indicating that their ecstatic prophesying was a one-time sign; several ancient versions instead derived the verb from swp ("cease"), rendering "and did not cease."
- **11:34** — The place name Kibroth-hattaavah (qiḇrōṯ hattaʾăwāh) means "Graves of Craving," playing directly on the verbs "buried" (qāḇərû) and "who had been craving" (hammiṯʾawwîm, Hithpael participle of ʾwh).
