## Text

**21:1** The Canaanite, the king of Arad, residing in the Negev, heard that Israel was coming by the road of Atharim; so he fought against Israel and took captives from them.

**21:2** Israel vowed a vow to the LORD, saying, "If You certainly give this people into my hand, then I will devote their cities to utter destruction."

**21:3** And the LORD listened to the voice of Israel and gave over the Canaanites, and they devoted them and their cities to utter destruction; so they called the name of the place Hormah.

**21:4** They set out from Mount Hor along the road to the Red Sea in order to go around the land of Edom, and the soul of the people grew impatient on the way.

**21:5** The people spoke against God and against Moses, "Why did you bring us up from Egypt to die in the wilderness? For there is no bread and there is no water, and our soul loathes this wretched bread."

**21:6** So the LORD sent out fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people, and many of the people from Israel died.

**21:7** Then the people came to Moses and said, "We have sinned, for we spoke against the LORD and against you. Pray to the LORD so that He will remove the serpents from us." So Moses prayed on behalf of the people.

**21:8** The LORD said to Moses, "Make for yourself a fiery serpent and place it on a pole; and it shall be that every person who is bitten, when he sees it, will live."

**21:9** Moses made a serpent of bronze and placed it on the pole. And it happened that if a serpent bit a man, he gazed at the serpent of bronze and lived.

**21:10** The sons of Israel set out and encamped at Oboth.

**21:11** They set out from Oboth and encamped at Iye-abarim, in the wilderness facing Moab, toward the rising of the sun.

**21:12** From there they set out and encamped in the Wadi Zered.

**21:13** From there they set out and encamped across the Arnon, which is in the wilderness extending from the border of the Amorites, since the Arnon is the border of Moab, between Moab and the Amorites.

**21:14** That is why it is said in the Book of the Wars of the LORD: "Waheb in Suphah and the wadis of Arnon,

**21:15** and the slope of the wadis that inclines toward the settlement of Ar and leans against the border of Moab."

**21:16** From there they went to Beer; that is the well about which the LORD said to Moses, "Gather the people, and I will give water to them."

**21:17** Then Israel sang this song: "Rise up, well! Sing in response to it,

**21:18** a well that princes dug, that the nobles of the people excavated with a scepter, with their staffs." And from the wilderness they went to Mattanah,

**21:19** and from Mattanah to Nahaliel, and from Nahaliel to Bamoth,

**21:20** and from Bamoth to the valley that is in the country of Moab, at the summit of Pisgah, which looks out over the wasteland.

**21:21** Israel sent messengers to Sihon, the king of the Amorites, saying,

**21:22** "Let me pass through your land. We will not turn into any field or vineyard; we will not drink well water. We will travel by the King's Road until we pass through your territory."

**21:23** But Sihon did not let Israel pass through his territory. Instead, Sihon gathered all his people and went out into the wilderness to meet Israel; he arrived at Jahaz and fought against Israel.

**21:24** Israel struck him down with the edge of the sword and took possession of his land from the Arnon to the Jabbok, as far as the sons of Ammon, because the border of the sons of Ammon was strong.

**21:25** So Israel took all of these cities, and Israel settled in all the cities of the Amorites, in Heshbon and in all its towns.

**21:26** For Heshbon was the city of Sihon, the king of the Amorites; he had fought against the previous king of Moab and taken his entire land from his hand, as far as the Arnon.

**21:27** Therefore those who recite ballads say, "Come to Heshbon; may it be built and established, the city of Sihon!

**21:28** For fire went out from Heshbon, a flame from the town of Sihon; it consumed Ar of Moab, the lords of the heights of Arnon.

**21:29** Woe to you, Moab! You have perished, people of Chemosh; he has given his sons over as fugitives and his daughters into captivity to Sihon, an Amorite king.

**21:30** And we shot them down; Heshbon has perished as far as Dibon, and we laid waste as far as Nophah, which stretches to Medeba."

**21:31** So Israel settled in the land of the Amorites.

**21:32** Then Moses sent to scout out Jazer, and they seized its towns and drove out the Amorites who were there.

**21:33** Next they turned and went up along the road to Bashan. Og, the king of Bashan, came out to meet them, he and all his people, for battle at Edrei.

**21:34** The LORD said to Moses, "Do not be afraid of him, for into your hand I have given him, together with all his people and his land. You shall do to him just as you did to Sihon, the king of the Amorites, who resided in Heshbon."

**21:35** So they struck down him and his sons and all his people, until no survivor was left to him, and they took possession of his land.

## Translator Notes

- **21:5** — The adjective haqqeloqel ("wretched" or "worthless") is a hapax legomenon, a reduplicated form from the root qalal ("to be light, trifling, or contemptible").
- **21:14** — The quotation from the Book of the Wars of the LORD begins abruptly with the direct object marker et before Waheb in Suphah, reflecting a poetic fragment excerpted without its governing verb; Waheb and Suphah are otherwise unknown place names.
- **21:24** — The Masoretic Text reads the adjective az ("strong"); the Septuagint reads the place-name "Jazer."
- **21:30** — Follows the Masoretic vocalization throughout: wanniram is Qal wayyiqtol 1cp of yarah ("shoot") with a 3mp suffix ("we shot them down"), and wannashim is Hiphil wayyiqtol 1cp of shamam ("lay waste"). MT reads the relative pronoun asher ("which") with a Masoretic extraordinary dot over the resh, without which the consonants spell esh ("fire").
- **21:32** — Translates the Qere wayyoresh (Hiphil wayyiqtol 3ms of yarash, "drove out" or "dispossessed"); the Ketiv writes wyyrsh, corresponding to Qal wayyirash ("took possession of").
