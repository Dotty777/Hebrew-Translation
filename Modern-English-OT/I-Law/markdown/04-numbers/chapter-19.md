## Text

**19:1** Then the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

**19:2** "This is the statute of the law that the LORD has commanded, saying, 'Speak to the sons of Israel that they bring to you a red cow without defect, in which there is no blemish and upon which a yoke has never come.

**19:3** And you shall give it to Eleazar the priest, and he shall bring it outside the camp, and one shall slaughter it before him.

**19:4** Then Eleazar the priest shall take some of its blood on his finger and sprinkle some of its blood seven times directly toward the front of the tent of meeting.

**19:5** And one shall burn the cow in his sight; its hide and its flesh and its blood, together with its dung, he shall burn.

**19:6** Then the priest shall take cedar wood and hyssop and scarlet thread, and throw them into the midst of the burning of the cow.

**19:7** Then the priest shall wash his garments and bathe his body in water, and afterward he may come into the camp; yet the priest shall be unclean until evening.

**19:8** And the one who burns it shall wash his garments in water and bathe his body in water, and he shall be unclean until evening.

**19:9** Then a clean man shall gather the ashes of the cow and deposit them outside the camp in a clean place; and they shall be for safekeeping for the congregation of the sons of Israel, for water for impurity; it is a purification offering.

**19:10** And the one who gathers the ashes of the cow shall wash his garments and be unclean until evening. And this shall be for the sons of Israel and for the sojourner who sojourns in their midst as a lasting statute.

**19:11** The one who touches the dead body of any human person shall be unclean seven days.

**19:12** He shall purify himself with it on the third day and on the seventh day, and he will be clean; but if he does not purify himself on the third day and on the seventh day, he will not be clean.

**19:13** Everyone who touches a dead body, a human person who has died, and does not purify himself, has defiled the tabernacle of the LORD, and that person shall be cut off from Israel. Because water for impurity was not splashed on him, he shall be unclean; his uncleanness is still upon him.

**19:14** This is the law when a person dies in a tent: everyone who comes into the tent and all that is in the tent shall be unclean seven days.

**19:15** And every open vessel that has no lid bound with a cord over it is unclean.

**19:16** And anyone who in the open field touches one killed by a sword, or a dead body, or a human bone, or a grave, shall be unclean seven days.

**19:17** Then for the unclean person they shall take some of the dust from the burning of the purification offering, and one shall put running water over it in a vessel.

**19:18** Then a clean man shall take hyssop and dip it in the water, and sprinkle it on the tent and on all the vessels and on the persons who were there, and on the one who touched the bone or the killed person or the dead body or the grave.

**19:19** And the clean person shall sprinkle on the unclean person on the third day and on the seventh day; and on the seventh day he shall purify him, and he shall wash his garments and bathe in water, and he shall be clean in the evening.

**19:20** But the man who becomes unclean and does not purify himself, that person shall be cut off from the midst of the assembly, for he has defiled the sanctuary of the LORD; water for impurity was not splashed on him; he is unclean.

**19:21** And it shall be a lasting statute for them. The one who sprinkles the water for impurity shall wash his garments, and the one who touches the water for impurity shall be unclean until evening.

**19:22** And whatever the unclean person touches shall become unclean, and the person who touches it shall be unclean until evening.'"

## Translator Notes

- **19:2** — Hebrew parah adummah literally means "red cow"; parah denotes a cow and is traditionally rendered "heifer" following the Septuagint and Hebrews 9:13.
- **19:3** — The third-person masculine singular verbs "slaughter" (shachat) here and "burn" (saraf) in verse 5 are used impersonally ("one shall...") because Eleazar oversees the rite ("before him," "in his sight") rather than slaughtering or burning the cow himself.
- **19:9** — Hebrew chattat ("purification offering") is the same noun translated "sin offering" in 18:9; in this chapter it aligns with the privative Hithpael ("purify himself," vv. 12, 13, 20) and Piel ("purify him," v. 19) stems of chata.
- **19:12** — Following the Masoretic cantillation (tipcha on "seventh day"), both "third day" and "seventh day" modify the Hithpael verb "purify himself," consistent with verse 19; alternatively, "on the seventh day" can be grouped with "he will be clean" and "he will not be clean."
- **19:15** — Hebrew tsamid patil literally reads "lid, cord," an appositional idiom for a cover fastened down tightly with a cord.
