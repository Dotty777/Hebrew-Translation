## Text

**24:1** When Balaam saw that it was good in the eyes of the LORD to bless Israel, he did not go, as on previous occasions, to meet with omens, but instead set his face toward the wilderness.

**24:2** Balaam raised his eyes and saw Israel dwelling by its tribes, and the Spirit of God came upon him.

**24:3** He took up his oracle and said, "The declaration of Balaam, son of Beor, and the declaration of the man with an opened eye,

**24:4** the declaration of one who hears the sayings of God, who sees the vision of the Almighty, falling down, yet with eyes uncovered:

**24:5** How good are your tents, Jacob, your dwellings, Israel!

**24:6** Like valleys they spread out, like gardens along a river, like aloe trees that the LORD planted, like cedars along the waters.

**24:7** Water will pour from his buckets, and his seed will be in abundant waters; and his king will rise higher than Agag, and his kingdom will be lifted high.

**24:8** God brings him out of Egypt; he has towering horns like a wild ox. He will devour nations, his adversaries, crunch their bones, and strike through with his arrows.

**24:9** He crouches, he lies down like a lion and like a lioness; who will make him rise? Each one who blesses you is blessed, and each one who curses you is cursed."

**24:10** Then Balak's anger burned against Balaam, and he clapped his hands together. And Balak said to Balaam, "I summoned you to curse my enemies, yet look, you have thoroughly blessed them these three times.

**24:11** So now, flee away to your place. I said, 'I will greatly honor you,' yet look, the LORD has kept you from honor."

**24:12** And Balaam said to Balak, "Did I not also speak to your messengers whom you sent to me, saying,

**24:13** 'If Balak were to give me his house full of silver and gold, I would be unable to transgress the command of the LORD, to do good or bad from my own heart; whatever the LORD speaks, that I will speak'?

**24:14** And now, see, I am going to my people; come, let me counsel you about what this people will do to your people in the latter days."

**24:15** He took up his oracle and said, "The declaration of Balaam, son of Beor, and the declaration of the man with an opened eye,

**24:16** the declaration of one who hears the sayings of God and knows the knowledge of the Most High, who sees the vision of the Almighty, falling down, yet with eyes uncovered:

**24:17** I see him, yet not now; I gaze upon him, yet not near. A star will march forth from Jacob, and a scepter will arise from Israel; it will shatter the temples of Moab and demolish all the sons of Sheth.

**24:18** Edom will become a possession, and Seir, his enemies, will become a possession, while Israel acts with valor.

**24:19** And one from Jacob will hold dominion, and will destroy any survivor from a city."

**24:20** Then he saw Amalek, lifted up his discourse, and said, "First of the nations is Amalek, yet his end leads to destruction."

**24:21** Then he saw the Kenite, lifted up his discourse, and said, "Firm is your dwelling place, and set in the crag is your nest.

**24:22** Yet Kain will be consumed; how long until Asshur carries you away captive?"

**24:23** And he lifted up his discourse and said, "Alas, who will live when God appoints this?

**24:24** Ships from the shore of Kittim will afflict Asshur and will afflict Eber, and he also will go to destruction."

**24:25** Then Balaam got up, set out, and returned to his place, and Balak also went on his way.

## Translator Notes

- **24:3** — Hebrew beno Beor preserves an archaic construct ending (-o) meaning "son of Beor." The rare passive participle shetum in "with an opened eye" is disputed, understood either as "opened/unsealed" (paralleling "eyes uncovered" in v. 4) or, linking to satam, as "closed."
- **24:8** — The rare noun to'afot denotes heights, eminence, or the towering horns of a wild ox (cf. 23:22). The final clause literally reads "and his arrows he will shatter/strike through," understood either as an instrumental accusative ("strike through with his arrows") or as shattering the adversary's arrows.
- **24:17** — The Qal verb darak literally means "tread" or "march forth," and pa'atey ("temples") is a dual construct meaning the sides of the brow or edges of territory. The Pilpel verb qarqar means "tear down/demolish," while "sons of Sheth" may refer to a Transjordanian nomadic people or, comparing Jer 48:45, "sons of tumult."
- **24:22** — The Piel infinitive construct leba'er ("for burning/consuming") denotes utter devastation (cf. Isa 5:5; 6:13). Hebrew 'ad-mah literally asks "until what?" ("how long?"), introducing the impending capture of Kain by Asshur.
- **24:23** — Hebrew missumo 'El uses the Qal infinitive construct of sum ("set, appoint") with a proleptic third-person suffix anticipating the explicit subject "God," meaning "when God appoints/ordains this."
