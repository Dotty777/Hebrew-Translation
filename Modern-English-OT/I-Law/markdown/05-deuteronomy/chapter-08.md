## Text

**8:1** The entire commandment that I am commanding you today you must take care to do, so that you may live and multiply, and enter and take possession of the land that the LORD swore to your fathers.

**8:2** And you must remember the entire way along which the LORD your God led you these forty years in the wilderness, in order to humble you, to test you, to know what was in your heart—whether you would keep His commandments or not.

**8:3** So He humbled you, caused you to go hungry, and fed you the manna that you did not know and your fathers did not know, in order to make you know that not on bread alone does a human being live, but on everything that goes forth from the mouth of the LORD does a human being live.

**8:4** Your garment did not wear away on you, and your foot did not become swollen these forty years.

**8:5** You must know within your heart, then, that just as a man disciplines his son, the LORD your God is disciplining you.

**8:6** So keep the commandments of the LORD your God, walking in His ways and fearing Him.

**8:7** For the LORD your God is bringing you into a good land, a land of streams of water, of springs and deep waters flowing out in the valley and in the hill country;

**8:8** a land of wheat, barley, vines, fig trees, and pomegranates; a land of oil-bearing olives and honey;

**8:9** a land where you will not eat bread in scarcity and will not lack anything in it; a land whose stones are iron, and from whose mountains you will mine copper.

**8:10** Once you have eaten and are satisfied, you must bless the LORD your God on account of the good land that He gave you.

**8:11** Watch yourself so that you do not forget the LORD your God by failing to keep His commandments, His judgments, and His statutes that I command you today;

**8:12** or else, after you have eaten and are satisfied, and build good houses and inhabit them,

**8:13** and your cattle and your flock multiply, and silver and gold multiply for you, and everything that belongs to you multiplies,

**8:14** then your heart will become lifted up, and you will forget the LORD your God, who brought you out from the land of Egypt, from the house of slaves,

**8:15** who led you through the great and fearsome wilderness of fiery serpents, scorpions, and parched ground where there was no water; who brought water out for you from the flint rock,

**8:16** who fed you manna in the wilderness that your fathers did not know, in order to humble you and in order to test you, so as to do good to you in your future;

**8:17** and you will say in your heart, "My strength and the might of my hand have produced this wealth for me."

**8:18** Rather, you must remember the LORD your God, for it is He who gives you strength to produce wealth, in order to uphold His covenant that He swore to your fathers, as it is today.

**8:19** And it will come about that if you indeed forget the LORD your God, and walk after other gods, and serve them and bow down to them, I bear witness against you today that you will certainly perish.

**8:20** Just like the nations whom the LORD is causing to perish from before you, in the same way you will perish, because you will not listen to the voice of the LORD your God.

## Translator Notes

- **8:3** — The Masoretic text reads kol-motsa pi- followed by the divine name, everything that goes forth from the mouth of the LORD, without a separate noun for word as supplied in the Septuagint and later Greek citations.
- **8:7** — The plural tehomot, deep waters, derives from tehom, the deep, and denotes subterranean water sources emerging as springs across the landscape.
- **8:8** — The construct phrase zeit shemen, literally olive of oil, refers to oil-bearing olive trees rather than wild olives.
- **8:15** — The collective singular phrase nachash saraf, fiery serpents, pairs nachash, serpent, with saraf from the root meaning to burn, referring to venomous snakes whose bite burns or whose appearance is fiery.
- **8:20** — This verse pairs the Hiphil participle ma'avid, is causing to perish, with the Qal imperfect to'vedun, you will perish, from the same root avad to underscore the parallel between the fate of the dispossessed nations and a disobedient Israel.
