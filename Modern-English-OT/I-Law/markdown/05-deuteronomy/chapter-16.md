## Text

**16:1** Keep the month of Abib and celebrate Passover to the LORD your God, because in the month of Abib the LORD your God brought you out from Egypt at night.

**16:2** You shall sacrifice Passover to the LORD your God, of flock and herd, in the place that the LORD will choose to cause His name to dwell there.

**16:3** You shall not eat anything leavened with it; for seven days you shall eat unleavened bread with it, bread of affliction, because you went out from the land of Egypt in haste, so that you may remember the day of your going out from the land of Egypt all the days of your life.

**16:4** No leaven shall be seen with you within all your territory for seven days, and nothing of the meat that you sacrifice in the evening on the first day shall stay overnight until morning.

**16:5** You may not sacrifice the Passover in any one of your gates that the LORD your God is giving to you.

**16:6** Rather, at the place that the LORD your God will choose to cause His name to dwell, there you shall sacrifice the Passover in the evening as the sun goes down, the appointed time of your going out from Egypt.

**16:7** You shall cook and eat in the place that the LORD your God will choose, and in the morning you shall turn and go to your tents.

**16:8** Six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a solemn assembly to the LORD your God; you shall not do any work.

**16:9** Seven weeks you shall count for yourself; from the beginning of the sickle in the standing grain you shall begin to count seven weeks.

**16:10** Then you shall celebrate the Feast of Weeks to the LORD your God with the measure of a freewill offering from your hand, which you shall give according to how the LORD your God blesses you.

**16:11** And you shall rejoice before the LORD your God—you, your son and your daughter, your male servant and your female servant, the Levite who is in your gates, and the resident alien, the fatherless, and the widow who are in your midst—in the place that the LORD your God will choose to cause His name to dwell there.

**16:12** And you shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and you shall keep and carry out these statutes.

**16:13** You shall celebrate the Feast of Booths for yourself for seven days, when you gather in from your threshing floor and from your winepress.

**16:14** And you shall rejoice in your feast—you, your son and your daughter, your male servant and your female servant, and the Levite, the resident alien, the fatherless, and the widow who are in your gates.

**16:15** For seven days you shall hold a feast to the LORD your God in the place that the LORD will choose, because the LORD your God will bless you in all your yield and in all the work of your hands, and you will be nothing but joyful.

**16:16** Three times in the year all your males shall appear before the LORD your God in the place that He will choose: at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, at the Feast of Weeks, and at the Feast of Booths; and none shall appear before the LORD empty-handed.

**16:17** Each man shall give according to the gift of his hand, according to the blessing of the LORD your God that He has given to you.

**16:18** You shall appoint judges and officials for yourself in all your gates that the LORD your God is giving to you throughout your tribes, and they shall judge the people with just judgment.

**16:19** You shall not bend justice; you shall not be partial, and you shall not accept a bribe, because a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and overturns the words of the righteous.

**16:20** Justice, justice you shall pursue, so that you may live and take possession of the land that the LORD your God is giving to you.

**16:21** You shall not plant for yourself an Asherah of any tree beside the altar of the LORD your God that you will make for yourself.

**16:22** And you shall not erect for yourself a standing stone, which the LORD your God hates.

## Translator Notes

- **16:5** — Hebrew she'arekha ("your gates") is used metonymically throughout Deuteronomy for Israel's walled towns and settlements (compare 16:11, 14, 18; 17:2, 8; 18:6).
- **16:7** — The Piel verb bishalta (from bashal) commonly denotes boiling or cooking more broadly, rendered here generally as "cook."
- **16:10** — Hebrew missat ("the measure of") is a hapax legomenon occurring only here in the Hebrew Bible, denoting sufficiency, measure, or proportion (cognate with Aramaic missat, "enough").
- **16:16** — The Masoretic text vocalizes yera'eh as a Niphal ("be seen / appear") followed by the direct-object marker et-pene ("the face of"), a recurring festival idiom that some grammarians view as a reverential vocalization of an original Qal ("see the face of").
- **16:20** — Hebrew repeats the noun (tsedeq tsedeq, "justice, justice") for emphatic intensity and exclusivity, conveying "justice and nothing but justice."
