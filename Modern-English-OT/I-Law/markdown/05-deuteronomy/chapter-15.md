## Text

**15:1** At the end of seven years you shall observe a release.

**15:2** And these are the terms of the release: every holder of a loan shall release what he has lent to his neighbor; he must not demand payment from his neighbor or his brother, because a release to the LORD has been proclaimed.

**15:3** From a foreigner you may demand payment, but whatever belongs to you with your brother your hand shall release.

**15:4** Yet there will be no needy person among you, because the LORD will certainly bless you in the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess,

**15:5** if only you diligently listen to the voice of the LORD your God, taking care to perform this entire commandment that I am commanding you today.

**15:6** Because the LORD your God will bless you just as He promised you, you will lend to many nations, while you yourself will not borrow; and you will rule over many nations, yet over you they will not rule.

**15:7** If there is among you a needy person, one of your brothers in any of your gates within your land that the LORD your God is giving you, you must not harden your heart or clench your hand against your needy brother.

**15:8** Instead, you shall open your hand wide to him and surely lend him enough for his need, whatever he lacks.

**15:9** Watch yourself so that there is no worthless thought in your heart, saying, "The seventh year, the year of release, has drawn near," and your eye turns begrudging toward your needy brother and you give him nothing; then he will cry out against you to the LORD, and sin will be in you.

**15:10** You shall certainly give to him, and your heart must not be begrudging when you give to him; for on account of this matter the LORD your God will bless you in all your work and in everything to which you put your hand.

**15:11** For the needy will never cease from the midst of the land; therefore I am commanding you, saying, "You shall open your hand wide to your brother, to your poor, and to your needy in your land."

**15:12** If your brother—a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman—is sold to you, he shall serve you for six years, and in the seventh year you shall send him away free from you.

**15:13** And when you send him away free from you, you must not send him away empty-handed.

**15:14** You shall supply him generously from your flock, from your threshing floor, and from your winepress; from what the LORD your God has blessed you with, you shall give to him.

**15:15** And you shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and the LORD your God ransomed you; therefore I am commanding you this matter today.

**15:16** And it shall be that if he says to you, "I will not go out from you"—because he loves you and your household, since it goes well for him with you—

**15:17** then you shall take the awl and put it through his ear and into the door, and he shall become your slave forever; and to your female slave also you shall do likewise.

**15:18** It must not seem hard in your eyes when you send him away free from you, because he has served you six years, worth double the wages of a hired worker; and the LORD your God will bless you in everything that you do.

**15:19** Every firstborn male that is born in your herd and in your flock you shall consecrate to the LORD your God. You must not work with the firstborn of your ox, and you must not shear the firstborn of your flock.

**15:20** Before the LORD your God you shall eat it year by year in the place that the LORD chooses, you and your household.

**15:21** Yet if there is a defect in it—if it is lame or blind, any severe defect—you must not sacrifice it to the LORD your God.

**15:22** Within your gates you may eat it, the unclean and the clean together, like the gazelle and like the deer.

**15:23** Only its blood you must not eat; you shall pour it out onto the ground like water.

## Translator Notes

- **15:2** — The Qal infinitive absolute shamot ("release") functions jussively, followed by kol-ba'al mashsheh yado, literally "every holder of a loan of his hand" (that is, every creditor). In the final clause, the indefinite third-person verb qara ("one has proclaimed") is rendered passively ("has been proclaimed").
- **15:6** — Hebrew contrasts two stems of the same verb avat ("give or take a pledge"): the Hiphil veha'avatta ("you will lend") and the Qal lo ta'avot ("you will not borrow"), with the Hiphil recurring in v. 8.
- **15:9** — Hebrew vera'ah enekha (literally "and your eye is evil") is an idiom for stinginess or hostility; the same Qal verb ra'a ("be evil/begrudging") recurs in v. 10 with the heart (lo-yera levavekha).
- **15:14** — The Hiphil infinitive absolute and imperfect ha'aneq ta'aniq ("supply generously") derive from anaq ("necklace"), occurring in the Hiphil only here in the Hebrew Bible with the sense of adorning or loading someone generously with provisions.
- **15:18** — Hebrew mishneh sekhar sakhir literally means "double the wages of a hired worker," indicating that six years of continuous bondservice yielded twice the value of hiring a laborer (some interpret mishneh here as "equivalent to").
