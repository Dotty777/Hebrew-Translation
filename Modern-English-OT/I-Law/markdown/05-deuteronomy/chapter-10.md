## Text

**10:1** At that time the LORD said to me, "Carve for yourself two stone tablets like the first ones, and come up to Me on the mountain, and make for yourself a wooden ark.

**10:2** I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you shattered, and you are to place them in the ark."

**10:3** So I made an ark of acacia wood, carved two stone tablets like the first ones, and went up the mountain with the two tablets in my hand.

**10:4** And He wrote on the tablets, like the first writing, the Ten Words that the LORD spoke to you on the mountain from within the fire on the day of the assembly; and the LORD gave them to me.

**10:5** Then I turned and went down from the mountain, and placed the tablets in the ark that I had made; and there they remain, just as the LORD commanded me.

**10:6** And the sons of Israel traveled from Beeroth Bene-jaakan to Moserah. Aaron died there and was buried there, and his son Eleazar served as priest in his place.

**10:7** From there they traveled to Gudgodah, and from Gudgodah to Jotbathah, a land of streams of water.

**10:8** At that time the LORD separated the tribe of Levi to bear the ark of the covenant of the LORD, to stand before the LORD to minister to Him, and to bless in His name, down to this day.

**10:9** For this reason Levi has no share or inheritance with his brothers; the LORD Himself is his inheritance, just as the LORD your God spoke to him.

**10:10** And I remained on the mountain as during the first days, forty days and forty nights; and the LORD listened to me that time as well: the LORD was unwilling to destroy you.

**10:11** Then the LORD said to me, "Get up, go on the journey ahead of the people, so that they may enter and take possession of the land that I swore to their fathers to give to them."

**10:12** And now, Israel, what is the LORD your God asking from you, except to fear the LORD your God, to walk in all His ways, and to love Him, and to serve the LORD your God with your whole heart and with your whole soul,

**10:13** to observe the commandments of the LORD and His statutes that I am commanding you today, for your own good?

**10:14** See, to the LORD your God belong the heavens and the heaven of heavens, the earth and everything that is in it.

**10:15** Yet the LORD fixed His affection on your fathers alone, to love them, and He chose their seed after them—you—out of all the peoples, as it is this day.

**10:16** So circumcise the foreskin of your heart, and do not stiffen your neck any longer.

**10:17** For the LORD your God, He is God of gods and Lord of lords, the God who is great, mighty, and awe-inspiring, who shows no partiality and accepts no bribe.

**10:18** He carries out justice for the fatherless and the widow, and loves the foreign resident, giving him bread and clothing.

**10:19** Therefore you are to love the foreign resident, because you were foreign residents in the land of Egypt.

**10:20** The LORD your God you must fear; Him you must serve, and to Him you must cling, and by His name you must swear.

**10:21** He is your praise, and He is your God, who has done for you these great and awe-inspiring deeds that your eyes have seen.

**10:22** As seventy persons your fathers went down to Egypt, and now the LORD your God has made you like the stars of the heavens in multitude.

## Translator Notes

- **10:4** — Hebrew aseret haddevarim literally means "the Ten Words" (the Decalogue), echoing "the words" (haddevarim) in 9:10 and 10:2 and distinct from "commandments" (mitswot) in 10:13.
- **10:6** — Verses 6-7 form a brief third-person itinerary interlude before Moses's direct address to Israel resumes in verses 9-10; "served as priest" renders the Piel denominative verb from kohen ("priest").
- **10:12** — The Qal participle sho'el literally means "asking," and is answered by a chain of five lamed-infinitives construct ("to fear ... to walk ... to love ... to serve ... to observe") continuing into verse 13.
- **10:17** — "Shows no partiality" translates the Hebrew idiom lo'-yissa' fanim, literally "does not lift up faces," meaning not favoring one party over another in judgment.
