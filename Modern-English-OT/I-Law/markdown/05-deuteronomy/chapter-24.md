## Text

**24:1** If a man takes a wife and becomes her husband, and it happens that she does not find favor in his eyes because he has found an indecent matter in her, so he writes her a document of divorce, places it in her hand, and sends her away from his house,

**24:2** and she departs from his house, goes away, and becomes the wife of another man,

**24:3** and if the second man hates her, writes her a document of divorce, places it in her hand, and sends her away from his house, or if the second man who took her to himself as a wife dies,

**24:4** then her first husband, who sent her away, may not take her again to be his wife after she has been defiled, for that is an abomination before the LORD. And do not bring guilt of sin on the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.

**24:5** When a man takes a new wife, he must not go out with the army, nor may any duty be laid upon him. He is to be free for his home for one year and bring joy to his wife whom he has taken.

**24:6** No one may take a pair of millstones or an upper millstone as a pledge, because it is a life that he is taking as a pledge.

**24:7** If a man is found stealing a person from his brothers among the sons of Israel, and he deals brutally with him and sells him, then that thief must die. You must purge the evil from your midst.

**24:8** Guard yourself in an affliction of skin disease, observing very carefully and acting according to everything that the Levitical priests instruct you; just as I commanded them, so be careful to do.

**24:9** Remember what the LORD your God did to Miriam along the way when you came out from Egypt.

**24:10** When you make a loan of anything to your neighbor, do not enter his house to collect his pledge.

**24:11** Stand outside, and the man to whom you are lending shall bring the pledge outside to you.

**24:12** And if he is a poor man, do not lie down with his pledge.

**24:13** You must certainly return the pledge to him as the sun sets, so that he may lie down in his cloak and bless you, and for you it will be righteousness before the LORD your God.

**24:14** You must not exploit a poor and needy hired laborer, whether one of your brothers or one of your resident aliens who is in your land, within your gates.

**24:15** On that very day you must give him his wages, and do not let the sun go down on it, because he is poor and sets his soul on it, so that he does not cry out against you to the LORD and sin be upon you.

**24:16** Fathers are not to be put to death on account of children, nor are children to be put to death on account of fathers; every person is to be put to death for his own sin.

**24:17** Do not distort the justice due a resident alien or a fatherless child, and do not take a widow's garment as a pledge.

**24:18** And remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and the LORD your God redeemed you from there; that is why I am commanding you to do this thing.

**24:19** When you reap your harvest in your field and forget a sheaf in the field, you must not turn back to take it. It shall belong to the resident alien, the fatherless, and the widow, so that the LORD your God may bless you in every work of your hands.

**24:20** When you beat your olive tree, do not go through the branches again behind you. It shall belong to the resident alien, the fatherless, and the widow.

**24:21** When you harvest the grapes of your vineyard, do not glean behind you. It shall belong to the resident alien, the fatherless, and the widow.

**24:22** And remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt; that is why I am commanding you to do this thing.

## Translator Notes

- **24:1** — Hebrew erwat dabar literally means "nakedness of a matter," an idiom denoting something indecent or improper. Verses 1-3 form a single extended conditional protasis whose legal prohibition appears in verse 4.
- **24:4** — The verb rendered "has been defiled" is the rare Hothpaal passive-reflexive perfect of the root meaning to be unclean, emphasizing that the woman is regarded as defiled with respect to her first husband.
- **24:7** — The Hithpael verb hit'ammer, occurring only here and in Deuteronomy 21:14, denotes treating a captive brutally, tyrannically, or as a slave.
- **24:20** — The Piel verb tefa'er is a denominative from po'rah, meaning "bough" or "branch," and denotes going back through the olive branches a second time.
