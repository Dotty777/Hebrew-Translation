## Text

**25:1** When a dispute arises between men and they come to court, and the judges pass judgment on them, they must vindicate the innocent and condemn the guilty.

**25:2** Then if the guilty man deserves a beating, the judge shall have him lie down and be beaten in his presence, with a number of blows matching his guilt.

**25:3** He may give him forty blows, but no more; otherwise, if he goes further and strikes him beyond these with a heavy beating, your brother will be humiliated before your eyes.

**25:4** Do not muzzle an ox as it threshes.

**25:5** When brothers live together and one of them dies and has no son, the dead man's wife must not marry outside to an unrelated man. Her husband's brother shall go in to her, take her to himself as his wife, and fulfill the duty of a husband's brother toward her.

**25:6** And the firstborn whom she bears shall carry on the name of his dead brother, so that his name is not wiped out from Israel.

**25:7** And if the man does not want to take his brother's wife, then his brother's wife shall go up to the gate, to the elders, and say, "My husband's brother has refused to raise up a name for his brother in Israel; he is unwilling to fulfill the duty of a husband's brother toward me."

**25:8** Then the elders of his city shall summon him and speak with him. If he stands firm and says, "I do not want to take her,"

**25:9** then his brother's wife shall approach him before the eyes of the elders, draw his sandal off his foot, and spit in his face; and she shall answer and say, "This is what is to be done to the man who does not build his brother's house."

**25:10** And in Israel his name shall be called, "The house of the one whose sandal was drawn off."

**25:11** When men fight together, a man and his brother, and the wife of one approaches to rescue her husband from the hand of the one striking him, and she reaches out her hand and grasps his private parts,

**25:12** then you must cut off her hand; your eye shall show no pity.

**25:13** You must not have unequal stone weights in your pouch, one large and one small.

**25:14** You must not have unequal ephah measures in your house, one large and one small.

**25:15** You must have a complete and just stone weight, and you must have a complete and just ephah measure, so that your days may be lengthened on the land that the LORD your God is giving you.

**25:16** For every person who does these things, every person who commits injustice, is an abomination to the LORD your God.

**25:17** Remember what Amalek did to you along the way when you came out from Egypt,

**25:18** how he encountered you on the way and cut off from your rear all the feeble ones behind you while you were faint and exhausted, and he did not fear God.

**25:19** So when the LORD your God has given you rest from all your enemies on every side, in the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess, you must wipe out the memory of Amalek from beneath the heavens. You must not forget.

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — Hebrew bin hakkot literally means "a son of striking," using the Hiphil infinitive construct of the verb to strike as an idiom for deserving a beating.
- **25:5** — The Piel verb yibbem, appearing here and in verse 7, is a denominative from yabam, "husband's brother," denoting the levirate duty of marrying a deceased brother's childless widow to preserve his line.
- **25:13** — Hebrew eben wa'aben, literally "a stone and a stone," and efah we'efah in verse 14, literally "an ephah and an ephah," use repetition to denote dishonest dual standards for scale weights and dry grain measures.
- **25:18** — The Piel verb wayzanneb is a denominative from zanab, "tail," meaning to cut off the rear of a marching column; hanneheshalim is a Niphal participle occurring only here in Biblical Hebrew, denoting those who are feeble or lagging behind.
