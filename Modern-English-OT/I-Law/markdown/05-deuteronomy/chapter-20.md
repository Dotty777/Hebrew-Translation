## Text

**20:1** When you go out to war against your enemies and see horses and chariots, a people more numerous than you, do not be afraid of them, for the LORD your God is with you, the One who brought you up from the land of Egypt.

**20:2** And when you draw near to the battle, the priest shall step forward and speak to the people.

**20:3** And he shall say to them, "Hear, Israel, you are drawing near today for battle against your enemies. Do not let your heart grow faint; do not be afraid, do not panic, and do not be terrified before them,

**20:4** for the LORD your God is the One going with you, to fight for you against your enemies, to deliver you."

**20:5** Then the officers shall speak to the people, saying, "Who is the man who has built a new house and has not dedicated it? Let him go and return to his house, or else he might die in the battle and another man dedicate it.

**20:6** And who is the man who has planted a vineyard and has not put it to ordinary use? Let him go and return to his house, or else he might die in the battle and another man put it to ordinary use.

**20:7** And who is the man who has betrothed a woman and has not taken her? Let him go and return to his house, or else he might die in the battle and another man take her."

**20:8** And the officers shall speak further to the people and say, "Who is the man who is fearful and faint of heart? Let him go and return to his house, so that the heart of his brothers will not melt like his own heart."

**20:9** And when the officers finish speaking to the people, they are to appoint commanders of armies at the head of the people.

**20:10** When you draw near to a city to fight against it, you must call out to it for peace.

**20:11** And if it answers you with peace and opens to you, then all the people who are found in it shall become forced labor for you and serve you.

**20:12** But if it does not make peace with you, and makes war with you, then you shall besiege it.

**20:13** And when the LORD your God gives it into your hand, you are to strike down all its males with the edge of the sword.

**20:14** Only the women and the little ones and the livestock and everything that is in the city, all its spoil, you may plunder for yourself; and you may consume the spoil of your enemies, which the LORD your God has given to you.

**20:15** Thus you are to do to all the cities that are very far from you, which are not among the cities of these nations.

**20:16** Only from the cities of these peoples, which the LORD your God is giving to you as an inheritance, you must not keep alive anything that breathes.

**20:17** Rather, you must completely devote them to destruction, the Hittites and the Amorites, the Canaanites and the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, just as the LORD your God has commanded you,

**20:18** so that they will not teach you to do according to all their detestable practices that they have done for their gods, and you sin against the LORD your God.

**20:19** When you besiege a city for many days, fighting against it to capture it, you must not destroy its trees by swinging an axe against them, for you may eat from them, and you must not cut them down. For is the tree of the field a human being, that it should come under siege before you?

**20:20** Only trees that you know are not food trees, those you may destroy and cut down, and construct siegeworks against the city that is making war with you until it falls.

## Translator Notes

- **20:6** — The Piel verb hillel (literally "make common") refers to releasing the produce of a new vineyard for ordinary consumption after its initial consecrated period (cf. Lev 19:23-25).
- **20:8** — The Masoretic verb yimmas is Niphal ("melt") followed by the direct object marker et before "the heart of his brothers," an unusual construction where et introduces the subject of a passive/intransitive verb (or else the verb is read causatively, "he will not make the heart of his brothers melt").
- **20:19** — Although ha-adam is pointed with the definite article rather than an interrogative prefix, the clause is widely understood as an unmarked rhetorical question (literally "For is the human being a tree of the field, to come from before you into the siege?").
