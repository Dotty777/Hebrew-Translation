## Text

**27:1** Then the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**27:2** "Speak to the sons of Israel and say to them, 'When a man makes a special vow involving the assessment of persons to the LORD,

**27:3** then the assessment of the male from twenty years old up to sixty years old—the assessment shall be fifty shekels of silver, by the shekel of the sanctuary.

**27:4** And if it is a female, then the assessment shall be thirty shekels.

**27:5** And if one is from five years old up to twenty years old, then the assessment of the male shall be twenty shekels, and for the female, ten shekels.

**27:6** And if one is from one month old up to five years old, then the assessment of the male shall be five shekels of silver, and for the female the assessment shall be three shekels of silver.

**27:7** And if one is from sixty years old and upward, if a male, then the assessment shall be fifteen shekels, and for the female, ten shekels.

**27:8** But if he is too poor for the assessment, then he shall present him before the priest, and the priest shall assess him; according to what the one making the vow can afford, the priest shall assess him.

**27:9** And if it is an animal from which people bring an offering to the LORD, all that he gives of it to the LORD shall be holy.

**27:10** He shall not replace it or exchange it, good for bad or bad for good; and if he indeed exchanges animal for animal, then both it and the one exchanged for it shall be holy.

**27:11** And if it is any unclean animal from which people do not bring an offering to the LORD, then he shall present the animal before the priest.

**27:12** And the priest shall assess it, whether good or bad; according to the assessment of the priest, so it shall be.

**27:13** But if he indeed redeems it, then he shall add one-fifth to the assessment.

**27:14** And when a man consecrates his house as holy to the LORD, then the priest shall assess it, whether good or bad; just as the priest assesses it, so it shall stand.

**27:15** And if the one who consecrated it redeems his house, then he shall add one-fifth of the silver of the assessment to it, and it shall be his.

**27:16** And if a man consecrates to the LORD part of the field of his possession, then the assessment shall be according to its seed: a homer of barley seed at fifty shekels of silver.

**27:17** If he consecrates his field from the year of the Jubilee, it shall stand according to the assessment.

**27:18** But if he consecrates his field after the Jubilee, then the priest shall calculate for him the silver according to the years that remain until the year of the Jubilee, and a deduction shall be made from the assessment.

**27:19** And if the one who consecrated the field indeed redeems it, then he shall add one-fifth of the silver of the assessment to it, and it shall be confirmed to him.

**27:20** And if he does not redeem the field, or if he has sold the field to another man, it shall not be redeemed anymore.

**27:21** And the field, when it is released in the Jubilee, shall be holy to the LORD, like a devoted field; possession of it shall belong to the priest.

**27:22** And if he consecrates to the LORD a field he has purchased, which is not part of the field of his possession,

**27:23** then the priest shall calculate for him the amount of the assessment up to the year of the Jubilee, and he shall give the assessment on that day as a holy thing to the LORD.

**27:24** In the year of the Jubilee, the field shall return to the one from whom he purchased it, to the one to whom the possession of the land belongs.

**27:25** And every assessment shall be by the shekel of the sanctuary; the shekel shall be twenty gerahs.

**27:26** However, no one shall consecrate a firstborn among animals, which is born as firstborn to the LORD; whether ox or sheep, it belongs to the LORD.

**27:27** And if it is among the unclean animals, then he shall ransom it at the assessment and add one-fifth to it; and if it is not redeemed, then it shall be sold at the assessment.

**27:28** However, no devoted thing that a man devotes to the LORD from all that belongs to him, whether human being, animal, or part of the field of his possession, shall be sold or redeemed; every devoted thing is most holy to the LORD.

**27:29** No devoted one who is devoted from among humankind shall be ransomed; he shall surely be put to death.

**27:30** And every tithe of the land, of the seed of the land or of the fruit of the tree, belongs to the LORD; it is holy to the LORD.

**27:31** And if a man indeed redeems any of his tithe, he shall add one-fifth to it.

**27:32** And every tithe of herd and flock, everything that passes under the rod—the tenth one shall be holy to the LORD.

**27:33** He shall not inspect whether it is good or bad, nor shall he exchange it; and if he indeed exchanges it, then both it and the one exchanged for it shall be holy; it shall not be redeemed.'"

**27:34** These are the commandments that the LORD commanded Moses for the sons of Israel on Mount Sinai.

## Translator Notes

- **27:2** — Hebrew uses the crystallized Priestly noun erkeka (literally "your assessment") throughout Leviticus 27; its fossilized second-person suffix functions as part of a fixed technical term that even takes the definite article in 27:23 and stands before a following noun in 27:3, 5, 6, and 12.
- **27:8** — Literally "according to what the hand of the one making the vow reaches" (Hiphil imperfect of nsg), an established Hebrew idiom for what a person can afford.
- **27:26** — The Pual imperfect yebukkar (from bkr, occurring in the Pual only here in the Hebrew Bible) denotes an animal born into or accorded firstborn status as automatically belonging to the LORD.
- **27:28** — Hebrew herem and its cognate verbs (Hiphil in 27:28; Hophal in 27:29) denote irrevocable devotion to the LORD, whether permanent, unredeemable consecration to the sanctuary (27:21, 28) or, in judicial and warfare bans, consignment to death (27:29).
