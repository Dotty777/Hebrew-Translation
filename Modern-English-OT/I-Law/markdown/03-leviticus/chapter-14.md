## Text

**14:1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**14:2** "This will be the law for the leprous person on the day of his cleansing: he shall be brought to the priest.

**14:3** And the priest shall go outside the camp, and the priest shall inspect him; and look, the affliction of leprosy has been healed in the leprous person.

**14:4** Then the priest shall command that two live clean birds, cedar wood, crimson yarn, and hyssop be taken for the one being cleansed.

**14:5** And the priest shall command that the one bird be slaughtered into a clay vessel over living water.

**14:6** As for the live bird, he shall take it, and the cedar wood, the crimson yarn, and the hyssop, and he shall dip them and the live bird in the blood of the slaughtered bird over the living water.

**14:7** And he shall sprinkle seven times on the one being cleansed from the leprosy and declare him clean, and he shall release the live bird over the open field.

**14:8** Then the one being cleansed shall wash his garments, shave off all his hair, and bathe in water, and he shall be clean. Afterward he may enter the camp, yet he shall stay outside his tent seven days.

**14:9** And on the seventh day he shall shave off all his hair—his head, his beard, and his eyebrows; all his hair he shall shave off. Then he shall wash his garments and bathe his flesh in water, and he shall be clean.

**14:10** And on the eighth day he shall take two unblemished male lambs and one unblemished year-old ewe lamb, three tenth-measures of fine flour as a grain offering mixed with oil, and one log of oil.

**14:11** Then the priest who performs the cleansing shall present the man being cleansed and these things before the LORD at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**14:12** And the priest shall take the one male lamb and offer it as a guilt offering, together with the log of oil, and he shall wave them as a wave offering before the LORD.

**14:13** He shall slaughter the male lamb in the place where one slaughters the sin offering and the burnt offering, in the sacred place; for like the sin offering, the guilt offering belongs to the priest—it is most holy.

**14:14** Then the priest shall take some of the blood of the guilt offering, and the priest shall put it on the lobe of the right ear of the one being cleansed, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot.

**14:15** And the priest shall take some from the log of oil and pour it into the priest's own left palm.

**14:16** Then the priest shall dip his right finger into some of the oil that is in his left palm, and he shall sprinkle some of the oil with his finger seven times before the LORD.

**14:17** From the remainder of the oil that is in his palm, the priest shall put some on the lobe of the right ear of the one being cleansed, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot, on top of the blood of the guilt offering.

**14:18** And what remains of the oil that is in the priest's palm he shall put on the head of the one being cleansed; so the priest shall make atonement for him before the LORD.

**14:19** And the priest shall offer the sin offering, and he shall make atonement for the one being cleansed from his uncleanness; after that he shall slaughter the burnt offering.

**14:20** And the priest shall offer up the burnt offering and the grain offering onto the altar; thus the priest shall make atonement for him, and he shall be clean.

**14:21** But if he is poor and his means are insufficient, he shall take one male lamb as a guilt offering for a wave offering to make atonement for him, and one tenth-measure of fine flour mixed with oil for a grain offering, and a log of oil,

**14:22** and two turtledoves or two young pigeons, such as are within his means; one shall be a sin offering and the other a burnt offering.

**14:23** And he shall bring them on the eighth day for his cleansing to the priest, to the entrance of the tent of meeting, before the LORD.

**14:24** Then the priest shall take the male lamb of the guilt offering and the log of oil, and the priest shall wave them as a wave offering before the LORD.

**14:25** And he shall slaughter the male lamb of the guilt offering, and the priest shall take some of the blood of the guilt offering and put it on the lobe of the right ear of the one being cleansed, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot.

**14:26** And the priest shall pour some of the oil into the priest's own left palm.

**14:27** Then with his right finger the priest shall sprinkle some of the oil that is in his left palm seven times before the LORD.

**14:28** And the priest shall put some of the oil that is in his palm on the lobe of the right ear of the one being cleansed, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot, on the place of the blood of the guilt offering.

**14:29** And what remains of the oil that is in the priest's palm he shall put on the head of the one being cleansed, to make atonement for him before the LORD.

**14:30** Then he shall offer one of the turtledoves or of the young pigeons, from what is within his means,

**14:31** even what is within his means—the one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering, together with the grain offering; and the priest shall make atonement for the one being cleansed before the LORD.

**14:32** This is the law for the one who has an affliction of leprosy, whose means are insufficient in his cleansing."

**14:33** And the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

**14:34** "When you enter the land of Canaan, which I am giving to you as a possession, and I place an affliction of leprosy in a house in the land of your possession,

**14:35** then the one who owns the house shall come and report to the priest, saying, 'Something like an affliction has appeared to me in the house.'

**14:36** Then the priest shall command that they empty the house before the priest goes in to inspect the affliction, so that everything in the house will not become unclean; and after that the priest shall go in to inspect the house.

**14:37** And he shall inspect the affliction, and look, the affliction is in the walls of the house—sunken patches, greenish or reddish—and their appearance is lower than the wall.

**14:38** Then the priest shall go out of the house to the entrance of the house and shut up the house for seven days.

**14:39** And the priest shall return on the seventh day and inspect it; and look, the affliction has spread in the walls of the house.

**14:40** Then the priest shall command that they pull out the stones in which the affliction is and throw them outside the city into an unclean place.

**14:41** And he shall have the inside of the house scraped all around, and they shall pour out the plaster that they scraped off outside the city into an unclean place.

**14:42** Then they shall take other stones and bring them in place of the stones, and he shall take other plaster and plaster the house.

**14:43** But if the affliction returns and breaks out in the house after the stones have been pulled out, and after the house has been scraped, and after it has been plastered,

**14:44** then the priest shall go in and inspect it, and look, the affliction has spread in the house; it is a destructive leprosy in the house—it is unclean.

**14:45** And he shall tear down the house—its stones, its timbers, and all the plaster of the house—and carry them outside the city to an unclean place.

**14:46** And whoever enters the house during all the days that he has shut it up shall be unclean until the evening.

**14:47** And the one who lies down in the house shall wash his garments, and the one who eats in the house shall wash his garments.

**14:48** But if the priest indeed goes in and inspects it, and look, the affliction has not spread in the house after the house has been plastered, then the priest shall declare the house clean, for the affliction has been healed.

**14:49** And to purify the house he shall take two birds, cedar wood, crimson yarn, and hyssop.

**14:50** And he shall slaughter the one bird into a clay vessel over living water.

**14:51** Then he shall take the cedar wood, the hyssop, the crimson yarn, and the live bird, and dip them in the blood of the slaughtered bird and in the living water, and sprinkle onto the house seven times.

**14:52** Thus he shall purify the house with the blood of the bird, with the living water, with the live bird, with the cedar wood, with the hyssop, and with the crimson yarn.

**14:53** And he shall release the live bird outside the city into the open field; so he shall make atonement for the house, and it shall be clean.

**14:54** This is the law for every affliction of leprosy and for a scall,

**14:55** and for leprosy of a garment and of a house,

**14:56** and for a swelling, for a rash, and for a bright spot,

**14:57** to instruct concerning the day it is unclean and the day it is clean. This is the law of leprosy."

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — Hebrew tsara'at, traditionally rendered "leprosy," covers a range of ritually defiling skin disorders as well as discoloring or moldlike conditions on garments and plastered walls (vv. 34, 55), not solely modern Hansen's disease. "Healed in the leprous person" translates a pregnant Hebrew construction (literally "healed from the leprous person," i.e., healed away from him), using the Qal passive participle tsaru'a alongside the Pual participle metsora' in v. 2.
- **14:10** — Hebrew issaron ("tenth-measure") is one-tenth of an ephah (a dry measure of roughly 2.2 liters), and a log is a small liquid measure equal to one-twelfth of a hin (roughly one-third of a liter).
- **14:21** — "His means are insufficient" renders the Hebrew idiom 'en yado masseget (literally "his hand does not reach," Hiphil participle of nasag), which recurs in vv. 22, 30, 31, and 32 to express financial ability.
- **14:37** — "Sunken patches" translates the hapax legomenon sheqa'arurot (apparently from shaqa', "to sink"), and "lower than the wall" renders shaphal min-haqqir, meaning recessed beneath the wall surface.
- **14:49** — "To purify" renders the privative Piel infinitive lechatte' (from chata', "to sin," here meaning "to purge from defilement," also in v. 52), distinct from the Piel of taher ("to cleanse" or "declare clean," vv. 7, 11, 48).
