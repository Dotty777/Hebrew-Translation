## Text

**12:1** Now the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**12:2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying, 'When a woman conceives and gives birth to a male, she will be unclean for seven days; just as during the days of the impurity of her menstrual illness, she will be unclean.

**12:3** And on the eighth day, the flesh of his foreskin must be circumcised.

**12:4** And for thirty-three days she will remain in the blood of purification; she must not touch anything holy, and she must not enter the sanctuary until the completion of the days of her purification.

**12:5** But if she gives birth to a female, she will be unclean for two weeks, as during her menstrual impurity; and for sixty-six days she will remain in the blood of purification.

**12:6** And at the completion of the days of her purification, for a son or for a daughter, she must bring a lamb in its first year as a burnt offering and a young pigeon or a turtledove as a sin offering to the entrance of the tent of meeting, to the priest.

**12:7** And he will present it before the LORD and make atonement on her behalf, and she will become clean from the source of her blood. This is the law concerning the woman who gives birth, for a male or for a female.

**12:8** But if her hand does not reach enough for a sheep, she must take two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one as a burnt offering and one as a sin offering; and the priest will make atonement on her behalf, and she will become clean.'"

## Translator Notes

- **12:2** — Hebrew tazria (Hiphil imperfect of z-r-', literally "produces seed") is used here of a woman becoming pregnant or conceiving. The construct phrase "the impurity of her menstrual illness" translates niddat devotah (literally "the impurity/separation of her unwellness").
- **12:4** — The Masoretic Hebrew writes tohorah without a suffix ("purification") in "blood of purification" here and in v. 5 (where the preposition shifts from b- to 'al), in contrast to tohorah with a third-person feminine singular suffix ("her purification") at the end of v. 4 and in v. 6.
- **12:6** — Literally "a lamb, son of its year," a Hebrew idiom for a lamb within its first year of life. The noun chatta't ("sin offering," from the root ch-t-') here functions as a ritual purification offering rather than addressing moral transgression.
- **12:7** — The singular object suffix in vehiqrivo ("he will present it," Hiphil of q-r-b) treats the woman's offerings collectively as a single presentation. The phrase "the source of her blood" translates meqor dameiha (literally "the spring/source of her bloods," referring to postpartum bleeding).
- **12:8** — The clause "her hand does not reach enough for a sheep" translates the Hebrew idiom lo timtsa yadah dei seh, referring to insufficient financial means. The term seh denotes a single sheep or goat, referring back to the lamb in v. 6.
