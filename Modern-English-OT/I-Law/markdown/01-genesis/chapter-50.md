## Text

**50:1** Joseph fell onto his father's face; he wept over him and kissed him.

**50:2** Then Joseph ordered his servants, the physicians, to embalm his father, and the physicians embalmed Israel.

**50:3** Forty days were completed for him, for thus the days of embalming are completed, and Egypt wept for him seventy days.

**50:4** The days of weeping for him passed, and Joseph spoke to Pharaoh's household, saying, "Please, if I have found favor in your eyes, do speak in Pharaoh's ears, saying,

**50:5** 'My father made me swear, saying, "Look, I am dying. In my grave that I dug for myself in the land of Canaan, there you are to bury me." And now, let me go up, please, and bury my father, and I will return.'"

**50:6** Pharaoh replied, "Go up and bury your father, just as he made you swear."

**50:7** Joseph went up to bury his father, and all of Pharaoh's servants went up with him, the elders of his household, and all the elders of the land of Egypt,

**50:8** and all of Joseph's household, his brothers, and his father's household. Only their little ones, their flocks, and their herds did they leave in the land of Goshen.

**50:9** Both chariots and horsemen went up with him, and the company was very massive.

**50:10** They came as far as the threshing floor of Atad, which is beyond the Jordan, and there they lamented with a great and very heavy lamentation; and he observed mourning for his father for seven days.

**50:11** When the Canaanite inhabitants of the land saw the mourning at the threshing floor of Atad, they said, "This is a heavy mourning for Egypt." Therefore its name was called Abel-mizraim, which is beyond the Jordan.

**50:12** And his sons did for him in this way, just as he had ordered them.

**50:13** His sons carried him to the land of Canaan and buried him in the cave of the field of Machpelah, the field that Abraham had purchased as a burial possession from Ephron the Hittite, facing Mamre.

**50:14** Then Joseph returned to Egypt, he and his brothers and all who had gone up with him to bury his father, after he buried his father.

**50:15** Now Joseph's brothers saw that their father had died, and they said, "What if Joseph harbors a grudge against us and surely repays us for all the evil that we dealt to him?"

**50:16** So they sent word to Joseph, saying, "Your father gave orders before his death, saying,

**50:17** 'This is what you are to say to Joseph, "Please, do pardon the transgression of your brothers and their sin, for they dealt evil to you."' Now then, please pardon the transgression of the servants of your father's God." Joseph wept as they spoke to him.

**50:18** Then his brothers also went and fell before him, and they said, "Here we are as servants to you."

**50:19** Joseph replied to them, "Do not fear, for am I in God's place?

**50:20** You yourselves planned evil against me, yet God planned it for good, in order to act as on this day, to keep a numerous people alive.

**50:21** So now, do not fear; I myself will sustain you and your little ones." And he comforted them and spoke to their hearts.

**50:22** Joseph resided in Egypt, he and his father's household, and Joseph lived a hundred and ten years.

**50:23** Joseph saw Ephraim's sons of the third generation, and the sons of Machir, the son of Manasseh, were born upon Joseph's knees as well.

**50:24** Then Joseph said to his brothers, "I am dying, but God will surely attend to you and bring you up from this land to the land that He swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob."

**50:25** And Joseph made the sons of Israel swear, saying, "Surely God will attend to you, and you must bring up my bones from here."

**50:26** Joseph died at a hundred and ten years of age; they embalmed him, and he was placed in a coffin in Egypt.

## Translator Notes

- **50:11** — The name Abel-mizraim (meaning "meadow of Egypt" or "watercourse of Egypt") creates a wordplay with the similar-sounding Hebrew word for "mourning" used earlier in the verse. The verb rendered "its name was called" is literally an impersonal active singular, "one called its name."
- **50:13** — The clause rendered "the field that Abraham had purchased" treats the Hebrew phrase "the field" as a clarifying direct object; alternatively, reading the particle et as the preposition "with," it may be translated "which Abraham had purchased together with the field."
- **50:16** — The verb translated "sent word" is literally "ordered" or "charged" (Piel of tsavah) followed by "to Joseph," an idiomatic ellipsis for instructing messengers to deliver a message.
- **50:21** — Literally "spoke upon their heart" (with singular collective "heart"), a Hebrew idiom denoting tender, reassuring speech.
- **50:26** — The verb rendered "he was placed" is an unusual passive wayyiqtol form, generally analyzed as an archaic Qal passive related to the root sim ("to place/put"). In Hebrew, "coffin" carries the definite article to indicate the customary burial chest.
