## Text

**30:1** Rachel saw that she had borne no children to Jacob, and Rachel grew envious of her sister; so she said to Jacob, "Give me children, and if not, I will die."

**30:2** Jacob's anger blazed against Rachel, and he said, "Am I in God's place, who has held back the fruit of the womb from you?"

**30:3** She said, "Here is my maidservant Bilhah. Go in to her, that she may bear on my knees and I too may be built up from her."

**30:4** Then she gave him Bilhah, her female servant, as a wife, and Jacob went in to her.

**30:5** Bilhah conceived and bore a son to Jacob.

**30:6** Rachel said, "God has done me justice, and He has also listened to my voice and given me a son." For that reason she called his name Dan.

**30:7** Bilhah, Rachel's female servant, conceived again and bore a second son to Jacob.

**30:8** Rachel said, "With wrestlings of God I have wrestled with my sister, and I have also prevailed." So she called his name Naphtali.

**30:9** When Leah saw that she had stopped giving birth, she took Zilpah, her female servant, and gave her to Jacob as a wife.

**30:10** Zilpah, Leah's female servant, bore a son to Jacob.

**30:11** Leah said, "Fortune has come!" So she called his name Gad.

**30:12** Zilpah, Leah's female servant, bore a second son to Jacob.

**30:13** Leah said, "What happiness is mine! For women will call me happy." So she called his name Asher.

**30:14** Reuben went out during the days of the wheat harvest, found mandrakes in the field, and brought them to Leah, his mother. Rachel said to Leah, "Give me some of your son's mandrakes, please."

**30:15** But she said to her, "Is it too little that you have taken my husband, that you would also take my son's mandrakes?" And Rachel said, "Then he will lie with you tonight in exchange for your son's mandrakes."

**30:16** Jacob came from the field in the evening, and Leah went out to meet him and said, "You are to come in to me, for I have indeed hired you with my son's mandrakes." And he lay with her that night.

**30:17** God listened to Leah, and she conceived and bore a fifth son to Jacob.

**30:18** Leah said, "God has given me my wages, since I gave my female servant to my husband." So she called his name Issachar.

**30:19** Leah conceived again and bore a sixth son to Jacob.

**30:20** Leah said, "God has bestowed a good gift on me; this time my husband will honor me, for I have borne six sons to him." So she called his name Zebulun.

**30:21** After that she bore a daughter, and she called her name Dinah.

**30:22** God remembered Rachel; God listened to her and opened her womb.

**30:23** She conceived and bore a son, and she said, "God has removed my disgrace."

**30:24** She called his name Joseph, saying, "May the LORD add another son for me."

**30:25** Now when Rachel had given birth to Joseph, Jacob said to Laban, "Send me away, so that I may go to my place and to my land.

**30:26** Give me my wives and my children for whom I have served you, and let me go; for you yourself know my service with which I have served you."

**30:27** Laban said to him, "Please, if I have found favor in your eyes—I have learned through divination that the LORD has blessed me on account of you."

**30:28** And he said, "Specify your wages to me, and I will give them."

**30:29** He said to him, "You yourself know the way I have served you, and what your livestock has become with me.

**30:30** For what you had before I came was little, and it has increased into abundance, and the LORD has blessed you wherever I set foot. And now, when will I too provide for my household?"

**30:31** He said, "What should I give you?" And Jacob said, "Do not give me anything; if you will do this thing for me, I will again tend your flock and watch over it.

**30:32** Let me pass through all your flock today, removing from there every speckled and spotted animal, and every dark-colored animal among the sheep, and the spotted and speckled among the goats; and such will be my wages.

**30:33** And my righteousness will testify for me in time to come, when it comes before you regarding my wages: every one with me that is not speckled and spotted among the goats and dark-colored among the sheep is stolen."

**30:34** Laban said, "Yes, may it be according to your word."

**30:35** On that day he removed the striped and spotted male goats and all the speckled and spotted female goats, every one with white on it, and every dark-colored one among the sheep, and gave them into the hand of his sons.

**30:36** He set a three days' journey between himself and Jacob, while Jacob was tending the remaining flocks of Laban.

**30:37** Then Jacob took for himself fresh rods of poplar, almond, and plane, and peeled white strips on them, laying bare the white that was on the rods.

**30:38** He set the rods that he had peeled in the channels, in the water troughs where the flocks came to drink, directly opposite the flocks; and they bred when they came to drink.

**30:39** And the flocks bred before the rods, and the flocks bore striped, speckled, and spotted ones.

**30:40** Jacob separated the sheep, and turned the faces of the flocks toward the striped and every dark-colored one in Laban's flock; and he placed droves apart for himself, and did not place them with Laban's flock.

**30:41** Whenever the sturdy ones of the flocks were breeding, Jacob would put the rods before the eyes of the flocks in the channels, so that they would breed among the rods;

**30:42** but when the flocks were feeble, he would not put them. So the feeble ones became Laban's, and the sturdy ones became Jacob's.

**30:43** So the man increased exceedingly, and he came to have many flocks, and female servants and male servants, and camels and donkeys.

## Translator Notes

- **30:8** — The Hebrew phrase 'wrestlings of God' (naftule Elohim) pairs a cognate noun with the Niphal verb of patal ('wrestle') to play on the name Naphtali; 'of God' also functions as a Hebrew superlative idiom for mighty or fateful struggles.
- **30:11** — The translation follows the Qere (read text) ba gad ('Fortune has come') rather than the Ketiv (written text) begad ('In fortune' or 'How fortunate').
- **30:20** — Both the verb zabad ('bestow a gift') with its cognate noun zebed ('gift') and the verb zabal ('honor' or 'exalt,' traditionally 'dwell with') occur only here in the Hebrew Bible, forming a double wordplay on the name Zebulun.
- **30:27** — Laban's conditional clause breaks off without stating his request ('please stay'), a Hebrew rhetorical ellipsis; the Piel verb nichesh specifically denotes learning through divination or omens.
- **30:41** — In verses 41-42, 'sturdy' renders passive participles of qashar (literally 'bound tight,' Pual in v. 41 and Qal passive in v. 42), contrasted with 'feeble' from ataf (Hiphil infinitive and Qal passive participle in v. 42).
