## Text

**39:1** Now Joseph had been brought down to Egypt, and Potiphar, an official of Pharaoh, commander of the guard, an Egyptian man, bought him from the hand of the Ishmaelites who had brought him down there.

**39:2** And the LORD was with Joseph, so he became a prosperous man; and he was in the house of his Egyptian master.

**39:3** His master saw that the LORD was with him, and that everything he did, the LORD caused to prosper in his hand.

**39:4** So Joseph found favor in his eyes and attended him; he appointed him over his house, and all that belonged to him he placed in his hand.

**39:5** From the time he appointed him in his house and over all that belonged to him, the LORD blessed the house of the Egyptian because of Joseph, and the blessing of the LORD was on all that belonged to him, in the house and in the field.

**39:6** So he left all that belonged to him in Joseph's hand, and with him there he took no notice of anything except the food he ate. Now Joseph was handsome in build and handsome in appearance.

**39:7** Now after these things, his master's wife lifted her eyes to Joseph and said, "Lie with me."

**39:8** But he refused and said to his master's wife, "Look, with me here, my master takes no notice of what is in the house, and all that belongs to him he has placed in my hand.

**39:9** He is no greater than I in this house, and he has not held back anything from me except you, since you are his wife. And how could I commit this great evil and sin against God?"

**39:10** And day by day, as she spoke to Joseph, he did not listen to her to lie beside her or to be with her.

**39:11** Now on a certain day, he came into the house to do his work, and not one of the men of the house was there in the house.

**39:12** Then she seized him by his garment, saying, "Lie with me!" Yet he left his garment in her hand, fled, and went outside.

**39:13** And when she saw that he had left his garment in her hand and fled outside,

**39:14** she called to the men of her house and spoke to them, saying, "See, he has brought a Hebrew man to us to mock us! He came in to me to lie with me, and I cried out in a loud voice.

**39:15** And when he heard that I lifted my voice and cried out, he left his garment beside me, fled, and went outside."

**39:16** Then she laid his garment beside her until his master came to his house.

**39:17** And she spoke to him with words like these, saying, "The Hebrew servant whom you brought to us came in to me to mock me.

**39:18** And when I lifted my voice and cried out, he left his garment beside me and fled outside."

**39:19** Now when his master heard his wife's words that she spoke to him, saying, "Things like these your servant did to me," his anger burned.

**39:20** Then Joseph's master took him and placed him in the prison, the place where the prisoners of the king were confined; and he was there in the prison.

**39:21** But the LORD was with Joseph and extended steadfast kindness to him, and granted him favor in the eyes of the chief of the prison.

**39:22** And the chief of the prison placed into Joseph's hand all the prisoners who were in the prison, and everything that was being done there, he himself was the one doing it.

**39:23** The chief of the prison did not look into anything at all in his hand, because the LORD was with him; and whatever he did, the LORD caused to prosper.

## Translator Notes

- **39:9** — The negative particle with a third-person masculine singular suffix (einennu, lit. "he is not") refers directly to Joseph's master, though some render it impersonally as "there is no one."
- **39:11** — Hebrew kehayyom hazzeh (lit. "like this day") is an idiom denoting an unspecified day in the narrative ("on a certain day").
- **39:14** — The Piel infinitive construct of tsachaq with the preposition be- literally means "to laugh or play at" and carries the sense of mocking, insulting, or taking improper liberties (compare Gen 26:8; 39:17).
- **39:20** — Ketiv/Qere variant: the written ketiv (asurei, Qal passive participle construct plural of asar) means "the bound ones of," while the read qere (asirei, noun construct plural of asir, translated here) means "the prisoners of"; both convey the same sense.
