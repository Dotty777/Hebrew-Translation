## Text

**28:1** Then Isaac called for Jacob and blessed him, and he commanded him and said to him, "You must not take a wife from the daughters of Canaan.

**28:2** Rise, go to Paddan-aram, to the house of Bethuel your mother's father, and take a wife for yourself from there, from the daughters of Laban your mother's brother.

**28:3** And may God Almighty bless you, and make you fruitful and multiply you, so that you will become an assembly of peoples.

**28:4** May He also give to you the blessing of Abraham, to you and to your seed with you, for you to possess the land of your sojournings, which God gave to Abraham."

**28:5** So Isaac sent Jacob off, and he went to Paddan-aram, to Laban the son of Bethuel the Aramean, brother of Rebekah, mother of Jacob and Esau.

**28:6** Now Esau saw that Isaac had blessed Jacob and had sent him away to Paddan-aram to take a wife for himself from there, and that in blessing him he had commanded him, saying, "You must not take a wife from the daughters of Canaan."

**28:7** And Jacob listened to his father and to his mother, and went to Paddan-aram.

**28:8** So Esau saw that the daughters of Canaan were displeasing in the eyes of Isaac his father.

**28:9** And Esau went to Ishmael and took Mahalath, the daughter of Ishmael the son of Abraham, sister of Nebaioth, as a wife for himself in addition to his wives.

**28:10** Now Jacob went out from Beersheba and went toward Haran.

**28:11** And he came upon a certain place and stayed overnight there, because the sun had set. He took one of the stones of the place and set it at his head, and he lay down in that place.

**28:12** And he dreamed, and look, a stairway was set up on the earth, with its top reaching to heaven; and look, angels of God were going up and coming down on it.

**28:13** And look, the LORD was standing above it and said, "I am the LORD, the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac. The land on which you are lying, to you I will give it, and to your seed.

**28:14** And your seed will be like the dust of the earth, and you will spread out westward and eastward and northward and southward; and in you all the families of the earth will be blessed, and in your seed.

**28:15** And look, I am with you, and I will guard you wherever you go, and I will bring you back to this land; for I will not abandon you until I have done what I have spoken to you."

**28:16** Then Jacob woke from his sleep and said, "Truly, the LORD is in this place, and I did not know it!"

**28:17** And he was afraid and said, "How awe-inspiring is this place! This is nothing other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven."

**28:18** Then Jacob rose early in the morning, and he took the stone that he had set at his head and set it up as a pillar, and poured oil over its top.

**28:19** And he called the name of that place Bethel; however, Luz was the name of the city formerly.

**28:20** Then Jacob vowed a vow, saying, "If God will be with me, and will guard me on this way that I am going, and will give me bread to eat and clothing to wear,

**28:21** and I return in peace to my father's house, then the LORD will be my God,

**28:22** and this stone, which I have set as a pillar, will be God's house; and of everything that You give me, I will certainly give a tenth of it to You."

## Translator Notes

- **28:11** — Hebrew literally reads "the place" with the definite article, signaling a specific location in the narrator's mind. The noun rendered "at his head" denotes the area around the head or a headrest rather than necessarily a pillow beneath it.
- **28:12** — The noun sullam ("stairway") occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible; derived from a root meaning "to heap up," it denotes a stepped ramp, stairway, or ladder.
- **28:13** — The prepositional phrase 'alav ("above it") can refer either to the stairway ("above it") or to Jacob ("beside him").
- **28:21** — Following standard Hebrew conditional syntax, the weqatal clause "then the LORD will be my God" begins the apodosis (result) of Jacob's vow; alternatively, it may close the condition ("and the LORD is my God"), with the vow's commitment beginning at v. 22.
