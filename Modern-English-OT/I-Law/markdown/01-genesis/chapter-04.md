## Text

**4:1** Now the man knew Eve, his wife, and she conceived and gave birth to Cain, and said, "I have acquired a man with the LORD."

**4:2** Then she went on to give birth to his brother Abel. Abel became a shepherd of flocks, while Cain was a worker of the ground.

**4:3** And it happened at the end of some days that Cain brought from the fruit of the ground an offering to the LORD.

**4:4** And Abel too brought from the firstborn of his flock and from their fat portions, and the LORD showed regard for Abel and for his offering,

**4:5** but for Cain and for his offering He showed no regard. And Cain burned with great anger, and his face fell.

**4:6** Then the LORD said to Cain, "Why are you burning with anger, and why has your face fallen?

**4:7** If you do well, will there not be a lifting up? And if you do not do well, at the doorway sin is crouching; its desire is toward you, yet you are to rule over it."

**4:8** And Cain spoke to his brother Abel. Now it happened while they were in the field that Cain rose against his brother Abel and killed him.

**4:9** And the LORD said to Cain, "Where is Abel, your brother?" And he said, "I do not know. Am I my brother's guardian?"

**4:10** And He said, "What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood cries out to Me from the ground.

**4:11** And now, cursed are you from the ground, which opened wide its mouth to take your brother's blood from your hand.

**4:12** When you work the ground, it will no longer give its strength to you; you will be restless and wandering on the earth."

**4:13** Then Cain said to the LORD, "Too great is my punishment to bear!

**4:14** Look, today You have driven me out from the face of the ground, and I will be hidden from Your face; I will be restless and wandering on the earth, and it will be that anyone who finds me will kill me."

**4:15** And the LORD said to him, "Therefore, vengeance will be exacted sevenfold on anyone who kills Cain." And the LORD set a sign for Cain, so that no one who found him would strike him down.

**4:16** So Cain went out from before the LORD and lived in the land of Nod, east of Eden.

**4:17** And Cain knew his wife, and she conceived and gave birth to Enoch. He was building a city, and he called the name of the city after the name of his son Enoch.

**4:18** And Irad was born to Enoch. Irad fathered Mehujael, Mehujael fathered Methushael, and Methushael fathered Lamech.

**4:19** And Lamech took for himself two wives; the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the second was Zillah.

**4:20** And Adah gave birth to Jabal; he was the father of those who live in tents and own livestock.

**4:21** And the name of his brother was Jubal; he was the father of all who play the lyre and flute.

**4:22** And Zillah too gave birth to Tubal-cain, a forger of every cutting tool of bronze and iron; and Tubal-cain's sister was Naamah.

**4:23** And Lamech said to his wives, "Adah and Zillah, listen to my voice; wives of Lamech, give ear to my speech: for I have killed a man for wounding me, and a youth for bruising me.

**4:24** If Cain will be avenged sevenfold, then Lamech seventy-sevenfold!"

**4:25** And Adam knew his wife again, and she gave birth to a son and called his name Seth, "For God has appointed another offspring for me in place of Abel, since Cain killed him."

**4:26** And to Seth as well a son was born, and he called his name Enosh. Then people began to call on the name of the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Hebrew plays on the name Cain (qayin) and the Qal verb qaniti ("I have acquired"). The phrase et-YHWH literally reads "with the LORD," often understood as "with the help of the LORD."
- **4:7** — Hebrew se'et is the Qal infinitive construct of nasa ("lift up"), likely denoting the lifting of a fallen face or acceptance. Although chatta't ("sin") is grammatically feminine, the Qal participle rovets ("crouching") and the following pronouns are masculine, personifying sin as a lurking predator.
- **4:8** — The Masoretic Hebrew uses wayyomer ("said/spoke") without recording any spoken words; ancient versions such as the Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch supply "Let us go out to the field."
- **4:13** — Hebrew awon encompasses both guilt/iniquity and its resulting punishment, and minneso (preposition min plus Qal infinitive construct of nasa) can mean either "too great to bear" or "too great to forgive."
- **4:22** — The phrase lotesh kol-choresh (two Qal participles, literally "forging/sharpening every cutting-implement/craftsman") is difficult and is understood either as "forger of every cutting tool" or "sharpener/instructor of every craftsman."
