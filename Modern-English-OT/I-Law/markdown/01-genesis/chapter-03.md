## Text

**3:1** Now the serpent was more shrewd than any animal of the field that the LORD God had made. He said to the woman, "Is it really so that God said, 'You must not eat from any tree of the garden'?"

**3:2** The woman said to the serpent, "We may eat from the fruit of the trees of the garden,

**3:3** but from the fruit of the tree that is in the middle of the garden, God has said, 'You must not eat from it, and you must not touch it, or you will die.'"

**3:4** The serpent said to the woman, "You certainly will not die!

**3:5** Because God knows that on the day you eat from it, your eyes will open, and you will become like God, knowing good and evil."

**3:6** When the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was desirable for gaining insight, she took from its fruit and ate; and she also gave some to her husband with her, and he ate.

**3:7** Then the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked; so they sewed fig leaves together and made coverings for themselves.

**3:8** Then they heard the sound of the LORD God walking about in the garden at the breeze of the day, and the man and his wife hid from the LORD God's presence among the trees of the garden.

**3:9** And the LORD God called out to the man and said to him, "Where are you?"

**3:10** He said, "I heard Your sound in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked; so I hid."

**3:11** And He said, "Who told you that you were naked? Have you eaten from the tree that I commanded you not to eat from?"

**3:12** The man answered, "The woman You gave to be with me—she herself gave me from the tree, and I ate."

**3:13** The LORD God said to the woman, "What is this that you have done?" And the woman replied, "The serpent misled me, and I ate."

**3:14** The LORD God said to the serpent, "Because you have done this, cursed are you more than all the livestock and more than every animal of the field; on your belly you will go, and dust you will eat all the days of your life.

**3:15** And I will put hostility between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring; he will strike your head, and you will strike his heel."

**3:16** To the woman He said, "I will greatly multiply your pain and your conception; with pain you will give birth to children; yet your desire will be for your husband, and he will rule over you."

**3:17** Then to Adam He said, "Because you listened to the voice of your wife and ate from the tree about which I commanded you, saying, 'You must not eat from it,' cursed is the ground because of you; with pain you will eat from it all the days of your life.

**3:18** Thorns and thistles it will sprout for you, and you will eat the plants of the field.

**3:19** By the sweat of your face you will eat bread until you return to the ground, since from it you were taken; for dust you are, and to dust you will return."

**3:20** The man called his wife's name Eve, because she became the mother of all the living.

**3:21** And the LORD God made tunics of skin for Adam and for his wife, and He clothed them.

**3:22** Then the LORD God said, "See, the man has become like one of Us, knowing good and evil; and now, he might reach out his hand and take also from the tree of life, and eat, and live forever—"

**3:23** So the LORD God sent him out from the garden of Eden to work the ground from which he had been taken.

**3:24** He drove the man out; and east of the garden of Eden He stationed the cherubim and the flame of the sword that turned this way and that, to guard the way to the tree of life.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Arum ("shrewd") echoes arummim ("naked") in 2:25. The serpent's opening, af ki, expresses surprise or doubt, so it is rendered "Is it really so that."
- **3:4** — The serpent negates God's emphatic phrase from 2:17 (lo moth temuthun), rendered "You certainly will not die."
- **3:5** — Kelohim can mean "like God" or "like gods"; the singular sense fits the reference to God earlier in the verse.
- **3:6** — Lehaskil means to make wise or to give insight.
- **3:7** — Chagoroth are belts or loin coverings.
- **3:8** — Leruach hayyom is literally "at the wind/breeze of the day," probably the cooler evening breeze; the literal phrase is kept.
- **3:15** — Zera ("offspring," literally "seed") is a collective singular, and hu ("he") agrees with it in the masculine. The same verb shuf is used for both actions, so "strike" is used for both; its exact sense (crush, bruise, strike) is debated.
- **3:16** — Itsavon ("pain") is the same word used for the man in 3:17; etsev ("pain") in the next line is from the same root. Harbah arbeh is emphatic: "I will greatly multiply." "Your pain and your conception" may be a hendiadys meaning "your pain in pregnancy."
- **3:17** — Le-adam is pointed without the article, as in 2:20, so it is rendered "Adam." Itsavon ("pain") echoes the woman's sentence in 3:16.
- **3:19** — Appayim (literally "nostrils") here means the face.
- **3:20** — Chawwah (Eve) sounds like chai, "living"; the Hebrew gives the name itself, not a translation of it.
- **3:22** — The sentence breaks off after pen ("lest"), and the action follows in 3:23; the dash marks this unfinished sentence.
