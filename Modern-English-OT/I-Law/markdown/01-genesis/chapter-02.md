## Text

**2:1** So the heavens and the earth were completed, and all their array.

**2:2** On the seventh day God completed His work that He had done, and on the seventh day He ceased from all His work that He had done.

**2:3** Then God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it He ceased from all His work that God had created and made.

**2:4** These are the generations of the heavens and the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made earth and heavens.

**2:5** Now no shrub of the field was yet on the earth, and no plant of the field had yet sprung up, for the LORD God had not sent rain on the earth, and there was no man to work the ground.

**2:6** But a mist would rise from the earth and water the whole face of the ground.

**2:7** Then the LORD God formed the man, dust from the ground, and blew into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living creature.

**2:8** And the LORD God planted a garden in Eden, toward the east, and He set there the man whom He had formed.

**2:9** Out of the ground the LORD God made every tree grow that is pleasing to the sight and good for food, with the tree of life in the middle of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

**2:10** A river was flowing out of Eden to water the garden, and from there it divided and became four headstreams.

**2:11** The name of the first is Pishon; it is the one that encircles the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold.

**2:12** The gold of that land is good; bdellium and onyx stone are there.

**2:13** The name of the second river is Gihon; it is the one that encircles the whole land of Cush.

**2:14** The name of the third river is Tigris; it is the one that flows east of Assyria. And the fourth river is the Euphrates.

**2:15** Then the LORD God took the man and settled him in the garden of Eden to work it and to guard it.

**2:16** And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, "From every tree of the garden you may freely eat,

**2:17** but from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you must not eat, for on the day that you eat from it you will certainly die."

**2:18** Then the LORD God said, "It is not good for the man to be alone; I will make for him a helper corresponding to him."

**2:19** So out of the ground the LORD God formed every animal of the field and every bird of the heavens, and He brought them to the man to see what he would call each one; and whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name.

**2:20** The man gave names to all the livestock, and to the birds of the heavens, and to every animal of the field; but for Adam there was not found a helper corresponding to him.

**2:21** So the LORD God brought a deep sleep over the man, and he fell asleep; then He took one of his ribs and closed up flesh in its place.

**2:22** The LORD God built the rib that He had taken from the man into a woman, and He brought her to the man.

**2:23** Then the man said, "This time this one is bone from my bones and flesh from my flesh; this one will be called 'woman,' because this one was taken out of man."

**2:24** Therefore a man will leave his father and his mother and cling to his wife, and they will become one flesh.

**2:25** And the two of them were naked, the man and his wife, and they felt no shame.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — The Hebrew reads "on the seventh day" in both clauses (contrast the Greek "sixth day" in the first clause). Shabath means to stop or cease, the root of "Sabbath"; "ceased" keeps that sense rather than suggesting tiredness.
- **2:3** — The Hebrew has "which God created to make," which is rendered "had created and made" so that it reads naturally.
- **2:4** — Toledoth ("generations") introduces a new section, as at 5:1 and elsewhere in Genesis. The divine name YHWH first appears here, joined to Elohim, so it is rendered "the LORD God."
- **2:6** — The meaning of ed is uncertain (a mist, or an underground spring or stream); "mist" is the traditional sense.
- **2:7** — There is a wordplay between adam (man) and adamah (ground). Nefesh chayyah is the same phrase used for animals in 1:20, 24, so it is rendered "living creature" to keep the term consistent. Wayyippach means "blew" or "breathed."
- **2:10** — Rashim, literally "heads," is rendered "headstreams," meaning the sources of four rivers.
- **2:12** — Bdellium is an aromatic resin; the identity of the shoham stone is uncertain (onyx or carnelian).
- **2:14** — The Hebrew name is Hiddeqel, which is the Tigris (compare Dan 10:4); Perath is the Euphrates. Ashur is rendered "Assyria."
- **2:15** — Wayyannichehu, from the verb "to rest," means to set down or settle. Leshomrah means "to keep, guard."
- **2:17** — Moth tamuth is an emphatic infinitive construction ("dying you will die"), rendered "you will certainly die."
- **2:18** — Ezer kenegdo is literally "a helper as opposite him," that is, matching or corresponding to him. Ezer implies no lower rank; it is used of God in Ps 33:20.
- **2:20** — Here the Masoretic pointing has le-adam without the definite article (unlike ha-adam earlier in the chapter), marking the first personal-name use "Adam."
- **2:21** — Tsela usually means "side"; the traditional "rib" is kept.
- **2:23** — There is a wordplay between ishah (woman) and ish (man). The Hebrew repeats zoth, "this one," three times, and the repetition is kept.
