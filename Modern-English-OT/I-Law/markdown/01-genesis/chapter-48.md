## Text

**48:1** Now it happened after these things that someone said to Joseph, "Look, your father is sick." So he took his two sons with him, Manasseh and Ephraim.

**48:2** Then someone informed Jacob and said, "Look, your son Joseph is coming to you." So Israel strengthened himself and sat up on the bed.

**48:3** Jacob said to Joseph, "God Almighty appeared to me at Luz in the land of Canaan and blessed me.

**48:4** And He said to me, 'See, I am going to make you fruitful and multiply you, and I will make you into an assembly of peoples, and I will give this land to your seed after you as a perpetual possession.'

**48:5** So now, your two sons born to you in the land of Egypt before my coming to you into Egypt belong to me; Ephraim and Manasseh shall be mine, like Reuben and Simeon.

**48:6** But the children whom you father after them shall belong to you; they shall be called under the name of their brothers in their inheritance.

**48:7** As for me, when I was coming from Paddan, Rachel died, to my grief, in the land of Canaan along the road, while there was still a stretch of land before reaching Ephrath, and I buried her there on the road to Ephrath, that is, Bethlehem."

**48:8** Then Israel saw the sons of Joseph, and he said, "Who are these?"

**48:9** Joseph replied to his father, "They are my sons, whom God has given me in this place." So he said, "Bring them over to me, please, so that I may bless them."

**48:10** Now the eyes of Israel had grown heavy from old age; he was unable to see. So he brought them near to him, and he kissed them and embraced them.

**48:11** Then Israel said to Joseph, "I did not suppose I would see your face, yet look, God has let me see your seed too."

**48:12** Then Joseph brought them out from beside his knees and bowed low with his face to the ground.

**48:13** Joseph took both of them, Ephraim in his right hand on Israel's left, and Manasseh in his left hand on Israel's right, and brought them near to him.

**48:14** Israel stretched out his right hand and set it on the head of Ephraim, though he was the younger, and his left hand on the head of Manasseh, crossing his hands, for Manasseh was the firstborn.

**48:15** Then he blessed Joseph and said, "The God in whose presence my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked, the God who has shepherded me throughout my life until this day,

**48:16** the Angel who has redeemed me from all harm—may He bless the boys, and may my name be called in them, and the name of my fathers Abraham and Isaac, and may they teem into a multitude in the midst of the earth."

**48:17** When Joseph saw that his father was setting his right hand on Ephraim's head, it was displeasing in his sight; so he took hold of his father's hand to move it from Ephraim's head onto Manasseh's head.

**48:18** Then Joseph said to his father, "Not like this, my father, because this one is the firstborn; lay your right hand on his head."

**48:19** Yet his father refused and said, "I know, my son, I know; he too will become a people, and he too will grow great. Nevertheless, his younger brother will grow greater than he, and his seed will become a fullness of nations."

**48:20** So he blessed them on that day, saying, "By you Israel will pronounce blessing, saying, 'May God make you like Ephraim and like Manasseh.'" Thus he set Ephraim ahead of Manasseh.

**48:21** Then Israel said to Joseph, "Look, I am about to die, yet God will be with you and bring you back to the land of your fathers.

**48:22** And as for me, I have given you one ridge above your brothers, which I took from the hand of the Amorites with my sword and with my bow."

## Translator Notes

- **48:7** — Hebrew metah alai (lit. "died upon me") is an idiom expressing bereavement and painful personal loss.
- **48:14** — The rare Piel verb sikkel occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and is generally understood as "crossing" (from a Semitic cognate meaning "intertwine"), though some connect it with the root for insight ("guiding knowingly").
- **48:16** — The Piel jussive verb yevarekh ("may He bless") is singular, following the series "the God ... the God ... the Angel" (vv. 15-16); the unique Qal verb yidgu ("may they teem") is related to the noun for fish, conveying prolific growth.
- **48:22** — Hebrew shekhem ("ridge, shoulder, portion") forms a deliberate wordplay with the place name Shechem, where Joseph was later buried (Josh 24:32).
