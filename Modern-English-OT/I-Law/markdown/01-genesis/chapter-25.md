## Text

**25:1** Then Abraham again took a wife, and her name was Keturah.

**25:2** She bore to him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah.

**25:3** Jokshan fathered Sheba and Dedan. The sons of Dedan were Asshurim, Letushim, and Leummim.

**25:4** The sons of Midian were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah. All of these were the sons of Keturah.

**25:5** Abraham gave everything that belonged to him to Isaac.

**25:6** But to the sons of the concubines who belonged to Abraham, Abraham gave gifts; and while he was still alive, he sent them away from his son Isaac eastward, to the land of the east.

**25:7** Now these are the days of the years of Abraham's life that he lived: one hundred and seventy-five years.

**25:8** Then Abraham breathed his last and died at a good old age, old and satisfied, and he was gathered to his people.

**25:9** His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah, in the field of Ephron son of Zohar the Hittite, which faces Mamre.

**25:10** The field that Abraham bought from the sons of Heth—there Abraham was buried, and Sarah his wife.

**25:11** After Abraham's death, God blessed his son Isaac; and Isaac settled near Beer-lahai-roi.

**25:12** Now these are the generations of Ishmael, Abraham's son, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's maidservant, bore to Abraham.

**25:13** These are the names of the sons of Ishmael, by their names according to their generations: the firstborn of Ishmael, Nebaioth, and Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam,

**25:14** Mishma, Dumah, Massa,

**25:15** Hadad, Tema, Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah.

**25:16** These are the sons of Ishmael, and these are their names, by their villages and their encampments: twelve princes according to their tribes.

**25:17** These are the years of Ishmael's life: one hundred and thirty-seven years. He breathed his last and died, and he was gathered to his people.

**25:18** They dwelt from Havilah to Shur, which faces Egypt as you go toward Assyria; he settled opposite all his brothers.

**25:19** Now these are the generations of Isaac, Abraham's son: Abraham fathered Isaac.

**25:20** Isaac was forty years old when he took Rebekah, daughter of Bethuel the Aramean from Paddan-aram, the sister of Laban the Aramean, to be his wife.

**25:21** Isaac pleaded with the LORD on behalf of his wife, because she was barren; and the LORD granted his plea, and Rebekah his wife conceived.

**25:22** The children struggled together within her, and she said, "If this is so, why then am I here?" So she went to inquire of the LORD.

**25:23** The LORD said to her, "Two nations are in your womb, and two peoples will be separated from inside you; one people will prove stronger than the other, and the older will serve the younger."

**25:24** When her days to give birth were fulfilled, look, there were twins in her womb.

**25:25** The first came out reddish, all over like a cloak of hair; so they called his name Esau.

**25:26** Afterward his brother came out, with his hand grasping Esau's heel, and his name was called Jacob. Now Isaac was sixty years old at their birth.

**25:27** The boys grew up, and Esau became a man skilled at hunting, a man of the field, while Jacob was a mild man, dwelling in tents.

**25:28** Isaac loved Esau, because game was in his mouth; but Rebekah loved Jacob.

**25:29** Now Jacob boiled a stew, and Esau came in from the field, and he was faint.

**25:30** Esau said to Jacob, "Please let me gulp down some of that red, red stuff, for I am faint." Therefore his name was called Edom.

**25:31** Jacob said, "Sell your birthright to me right now."

**25:32** Esau said, "Look, I am about to die; what then is a birthright to me?"

**25:33** Jacob said, "Swear to me right now." So he swore to him and sold his birthright to Jacob.

**25:34** Then Jacob gave Esau bread and lentil stew; and he ate and drank, and got up and went away. Thus Esau despised the birthright.

## Translator Notes

- **25:8** — Hebrew literally reads "old and satisfied" (or "full"), without the phrase "of days" or "of years" supplied by some ancient versions.
- **25:18** — The verb rendered "he settled" is literally "he fell" (Qal perfect of naphal), used idiomatically of encamping or having one's territorial lot fall; "opposite" (literally "upon the face of") can also carry the nuance "east of" or "in defiance of."
- **25:21** — The verbs "pleaded" and "granted his plea" pair the Qal and Niphal stems of the same root ('atar), the Niphal carrying a tolerative sense ("let Himself be pleaded with").
- **25:23** — The ketiv (written form) reads gyym, likely an unusual spelling variant, while the qere (read form) is goyim ("nations"), which is translated here.
- **25:30** — The verb "let me gulp down" (Hiphil imperative of la'at) occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and suggests greedy swallowing; the name Edom ("red") plays on Esau's repeated word 'adom ("red").
