## Text

**40:1** Now after these things, the cupbearer of the king of Egypt and the baker offended their master, the king of Egypt.

**40:2** And Pharaoh grew furious with his two officials, with the chief cupbearer and with the chief baker.

**40:3** So he placed them in custody at the house of the commander of the guard, in the prison, the place where Joseph was confined.

**40:4** And the commander of the guard assigned Joseph to be with them, and he attended them; and they remained in custody for some days.

**40:5** And the two of them dreamed a dream, each man his own dream in one night, each according to the interpretation of his dream—the cupbearer and the baker of the king of Egypt, who were confined in the prison.

**40:6** When Joseph came to them in the morning, he saw them, and look, they were downcast.

**40:7** So he asked the officials of Pharaoh who were with him in custody at his master's house, saying, "Why are your faces sad today?"

**40:8** And they said to him, "We have dreamed a dream, and there is no interpreter for it." Then Joseph said to them, "Do interpretations not belong to God? Please recount it to me."

**40:9** So the chief cupbearer recounted his dream to Joseph and said to him, "In my dream, look, a vine was before me,

**40:10** and on the vine were three branches. As it budded, its blossoms sprang up; its clusters ripened into grapes.

**40:11** And Pharaoh's cup was in my hand, so I took the grapes, squeezed them into Pharaoh's cup, and set the cup on Pharaoh's palm."

**40:12** And Joseph said to him, "This is its interpretation: the three branches are three days.

**40:13** Within three days Pharaoh will lift up your head and restore you to your post; and you will place Pharaoh's cup in his hand according to the former custom, when you were his cupbearer.

**40:14** Only remember me when it goes well for you, and please show me loyal love; make mention of me to Pharaoh, and bring me out of this house.

**40:15** For I was truly stolen from the land of the Hebrews, and even here I have not done anything for which they should place me in the pit."

**40:16** When the chief baker saw that he had interpreted well, he said to Joseph, "I also, in my dream—look, three baskets of white bread were on my head.

**40:17** And in the top basket were all kinds of food for Pharaoh, the work of a baker, and the birds were eating them out of the basket from off my head."

**40:18** Joseph answered and said, "This is its interpretation: the three baskets are three days.

**40:19** Within three days Pharaoh will lift up your head from off you and hang you on a tree, and the birds will eat your flesh from off you."

**40:20** Now it happened on the third day, Pharaoh's birthday, that he held a feast for all his servants, and he lifted up the head of the chief cupbearer and the head of the chief baker in the midst of his servants.

**40:21** And he restored the chief cupbearer to his post as cupbearer, and he set the cup on Pharaoh's palm.

**40:22** But the chief baker he hanged, just as Joseph had interpreted for them.

**40:23** And yet the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph; he forgot him.

## Translator Notes

- **40:13** — The idiom "lift up the head" here signifies restoring the cupbearer to favor and rank, setting up a grim wordplay in v. 19 where adding "from off you" (me'alekha) turns the phrase into decapitation, and in v. 20 where it encompasses both officials' reckoning.
- **40:15** — The rare Pual infinitive absolute paired with the Pual finite verb (gunnov gunnavti) intensifies the passive statement ("truly stolen"). "The pit" (habbor) designates a subterranean dungeon and verbally echoes the pit into which Joseph's brothers threw him in Gen 37:24.
- **40:16** — The rare word chori is most often linked to a root meaning "white," denoting white bread or fine white cakes, though another view connects it to "hole" and renders "openwork" or "wicker baskets."
- **40:20** — Hebrew yom hulledet et-par'oh (lit. "the day of the being-born of Pharaoh") uses the Hophal infinitive construct of yalad with the marker et introducing the grammatical subject of the passive verb, a recognized Classical Hebrew construction.
