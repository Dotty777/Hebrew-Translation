## Text

**33:1** Jacob raised his eyes and looked, and now Esau was coming, and with him were four hundred men. So he divided the children among Leah, Rachel, and the two female servants.

**33:2** He placed the female servants and their children first, Leah and her children after them, and Rachel and Joseph last.

**33:3** He himself passed in front of them, bowing down to the ground seven times until he approached his brother.

**33:4** Yet Esau ran to meet him, embraced him, fell upon his neck, and kissed him; and they wept.

**33:5** Then he raised his eyes and saw the women and the children, and he said, "Who are these with you?" He replied, "The children with whom God has favored your servant."

**33:6** Then the female servants, they and their children, approached and bowed down.

**33:7** Leah also approached with her children, and they bowed down; and afterward Joseph and Rachel approached and bowed down.

**33:8** Then he asked, "What do you mean by this entire camp that I met?" And he replied, "To find favor in my lord's sight."

**33:9** But Esau said, "I have plenty, my brother; let what is yours remain yours."

**33:10** Jacob replied, "No, please! If indeed I have found favor in your sight, then take my gift from my hand, for truly I have seen your face as though seeing the face of God, and you have accepted me favorably.

**33:11** Please take my blessing that has been brought to you, because God has favored me and because I have everything." So he pressed him, and he took it.

**33:12** Then he said, "Let us set out and go on, and I will go in front of you."

**33:13** But he said to him, "My lord knows that the children are delicate, and the flocks and the herds are nursing under my care; if they are driven hard for a single day, all the flocks will die.

**33:14** May my lord please pass ahead of his servant, and I myself will move along slowly, matching the pace of the livestock in front of me and the pace of the children, until I come to my lord at Seir."

**33:15** Then Esau said, "Let me station with you some of the people who are with me, please." But he replied, "Why is that needed? May I find favor in my lord's sight."

**33:16** So on that day Esau turned back on his way to Seir.

**33:17** Jacob, however, traveled to Succoth; he built a house for himself, and for his livestock he made shelters. Therefore he called the name of the place Succoth.

**33:18** Jacob arrived safely at the city of Shechem, which is in the land of Canaan, upon arriving from Paddan-aram, and he encamped facing the city.

**33:19** He purchased the portion of the field on which he had pitched his tent from the hand of the sons of Hamor, the father of Shechem, for a hundred kesitahs.

**33:20** He set up an altar there and called it El-Elohe-Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **33:4** — In the Masoretic Text, the verb "and kissed him" (wayyishshaqehu) bears six scribal dots (puncta extraordinaria) above its letters, a traditional mark indicating a textual or interpretive question concerning the word.
- **33:14** — Literally, "at the foot of the livestock ... and at the foot of the children"; the Hebrew idiom leregel signifies matching the walking pace of another, and melakah ("work" or "property") here denotes movable livestock.
- **33:18** — The Hebrew word shalem is translated here as an adverbial adjective meaning "safely" or "intact," echoing Jacob's vow in Genesis 28:21, though some ancient versions treat it as a place name, "Shalem."
- **33:19** — The Hebrew word qesitah (kesitah), appearing only three times in the Hebrew Bible (Gen 33:19; Josh 24:32; Job 42:11), refers to an ancient unit of weighed payment or monetary value of uncertain worth.
- **33:20** — The altar name El-Elohe-Israel means "God, the God of Israel" or "El is the God of Israel."
