## Text

**37:1** Jacob settled in the land of his father's sojournings, in the land of Canaan.

**37:2** These are the generations of Jacob. Joseph, when he was seventeen years old, was tending the flock with his brothers; he was a youth with the sons of Bilhah and with the sons of Zilpah, his father's wives. And Joseph brought a bad report about them to their father.

**37:3** Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his sons, because he was the son of his old age, and he made for him a long-sleeved tunic.

**37:4** When his brothers saw that their father loved him above all his brothers, they hated him and were unable to speak to him in peace.

**37:5** Then Joseph dreamed a dream and told it to his brothers, and they hated him all the more.

**37:6** He said to them, "Please listen to this dream that I have dreamed.

**37:7** Look, we were binding sheaves in the middle of the field, and see, my sheaf rose up and stood upright too; and look, your sheaves gathered around and bowed down to my sheaf."

**37:8** His brothers said to him, "Will you really reign over us, or will you truly rule over us?" So they hated him all the more on account of his dreams and on account of his words.

**37:9** Then he dreamed yet another dream and recounted it to his brothers. He said, "Look, I have dreamed a dream again, and see, the sun and the moon and eleven stars were bowing down to me."

**37:10** He recounted it to his father and to his brothers, and his father rebuked him and said to him, "What is this dream that you have dreamed? Shall we truly come—I and your mother and your brothers—to bow down to you to the ground?"

**37:11** His brothers envied him, while his father held the matter in mind.

**37:12** Now his brothers went to pasture their father's flock in Shechem.

**37:13** Israel said to Joseph, "Are your brothers not shepherding in Shechem? Come, and I will send you to them." And he said to him, "Here I am."

**37:14** So he said to him, "Please go, see to the welfare of your brothers and the welfare of the flock, and bring word back to me." He sent him from the valley of Hebron, and he came to Shechem.

**37:15** A man found him, and look, he was wandering in the field; and the man asked him, saying, "What are you seeking?"

**37:16** He said, "It is my brothers I am seeking. Please tell me where they are shepherding."

**37:17** The man said, "They have moved on from here, for I heard them saying, 'Let us go to Dothan.'" So Joseph went after his brothers and found them in Dothan.

**37:18** They saw him from a distance, and before he approached them, they schemed craftily against him to put him to death.

**37:19** They said to one another, "Look, that master of dreams is coming!

**37:20** Now then, come, let us kill him and throw him into one of the cisterns, and we will say, 'A fierce beast has devoured him.' Then we will see what becomes of his dreams."

**37:21** Reuben heard this and rescued him from their hand, and he said, "Let us not strike him fatally."

**37:22** And Reuben said to them, "Do not shed blood. Throw him into this cistern that is in the wilderness, and do not stretch out a hand against him"—in order to rescue him from their hand, to restore him to his father.

**37:23** So it happened, as soon as Joseph came to his brothers, that they stripped Joseph of his tunic, the long-sleeved tunic that was on him.

**37:24** They took him and threw him into the cistern. Now the cistern was empty; there was no water in it.

**37:25** Then they sat down to eat bread. And they lifted up their eyes and looked, and see, a caravan of Ishmaelites was coming from Gilead, with their camels carrying gum, balm, and ladanum, going to bring them down to Egypt.

**37:26** Judah said to his brothers, "What gain is there if we kill our brother and cover up his blood?

**37:27** Come, let us sell him to the Ishmaelites, and let not our hand be against him, for he is our brother, our own flesh." And his brothers listened.

**37:28** Then Midianite traders passed by; and they pulled and brought Joseph up out of the cistern, and sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver. And they brought Joseph to Egypt.

**37:29** When Reuben returned to the cistern, look, Joseph was not in the cistern! So he tore his garments.

**37:30** He returned to his brothers and said, "The boy is gone! And I—where can I go?"

**37:31** Then they took Joseph's tunic, slaughtered a male goat, and dipped the tunic in the blood.

**37:32** They sent the long-sleeved tunic and brought it to their father, and said, "This is what we found. Please identify it: is it your son's tunic or not?"

**37:33** He recognized it and said, "My son's tunic! A fierce beast has devoured him! Surely Joseph has been torn to pieces!"

**37:34** Then Jacob tore his clothes, put sackcloth around his waist, and mourned over his son for many days.

**37:35** All his sons and all his daughters rose up to comfort him, yet he refused to be comforted and said, "Indeed, I will go down to my son, mourning, to Sheol." Thus his father wept for him.

**37:36** Meanwhile, the Medanites sold him into Egypt to Potiphar, an official of Pharaoh, commander of the guard.

## Translator Notes

- **37:3** — Hebrew ketonet passim ("long-sleeved tunic") literally denotes a tunic of extremities, reaching the wrists and ankles (cf. 2 Sam 13:18-19), though ancient versions rendered it as an ornamented or multicolored garment.
- **37:12** — The Masoretic Text marks the direct-object particle et before "their father's flock" with extraordinary dots (puncta extraordinaria), an ancient scribal sign indicating traditional textual doubt or pause without altering the reading.
- **37:28** — The subject of the plural verbs "pulled," "brought up," and "sold" is grammatically unspecified in Hebrew and may refer either to the Midianite traders or to Joseph's brothers (cf. 37:36; 45:4).
- **37:36** — The Masoretic Text reads "Medanites" (from Medan, brother of Midian in Gen 25:2), whereas verse 28 has "Midianites"; scholars take this either as a kindred tribe or as a variant spelling of Midianites.
