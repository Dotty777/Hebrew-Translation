## Text

**14:1** Now it happened in the days of Amraphel king of Shinar, Arioch king of Ellasar, Chedorlaomer king of Elam, and Tidal king of Goiim,

**14:2** that they waged war against Bera king of Sodom, Birsha king of Gomorrah, Shinab king of Admah, Shemeber king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela—that is, Zoar.

**14:3** All of these united at the Valley of Siddim—that is, the Salt Sea.

**14:4** For twelve years they served Chedorlaomer, yet in the thirteenth year they rebelled.

**14:5** In the fourteenth year, Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him arrived and struck down the Rephaim in Ashteroth-karnaim, the Zuzim in Ham, the Emim in Shaveh-kiriathaim,

**14:6** and the Horites in their mountain country of Seir, as far as El-paran, which is beside the wilderness.

**14:7** Then they turned back and came to En-mishpat—that is, Kadesh—and struck all the territory of the Amalekites, as well as the Amorites living in Hazazon-tamar.

**14:8** Then the king of Sodom, the king of Gomorrah, the king of Admah, the king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela—that is, Zoar—marched out, and they lined up for battle against them in the Valley of Siddim,

**14:9** against Chedorlaomer king of Elam, Tidal king of Goiim, Amraphel king of Shinar, and Arioch king of Ellasar—four kings against the five.

**14:10** Now the Valley of Siddim had pit after pit of bitumen, and the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled and fell into them, while those who remained fled to the hill country.

**14:11** Then they took all the possessions of Sodom and Gomorrah and all their food, and went away.

**14:12** They also took Lot, Abram's brother's son, and his possessions, and went away; for he was living in Sodom.

**14:13** Then an escapee came and told Abram the Hebrew. Now he was dwelling by the oaks of Mamre the Amorite, brother of Eshcol and brother of Aner, and they were covenant allies of Abram.

**14:14** When Abram heard that his relative had been captured, he mustered his trained men, born in his household—three hundred and eighteen—and pursued as far as Dan.

**14:15** He split up against them at night—he and his servants—and struck them down, pursuing them as far as Hobah, which is north of Damascus.

**14:16** He brought back all the possessions; he also brought back Lot his relative and his possessions, as well as the women and the people.

**14:17** After his return from striking down Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him, the king of Sodom came out to meet him at the Valley of Shaveh—that is, the King's Valley.

**14:18** Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine, and he was priest of God Most High.

**14:19** He blessed him and said, "Blessed be Abram by God Most High, Creator of heaven and earth,

**14:20** and blessed be God Most High, who has delivered your adversaries into your hand." And he gave him a tenth of everything.

**14:21** Then the king of Sodom said to Abram, "Give me the people, and take the possessions for yourself."

**14:22** And Abram said to the king of Sodom, "I have raised my hand to the LORD, God Most High, Creator of heaven and earth,

**14:23** that I will take nothing—not from a thread to a sandal strap—out of all that belongs to you, so that you will not say, 'I have made Abram rich.'

**14:24** Nothing for me, only what the young men have eaten, and the portion of the men who went with me. Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre—let them take their portion."

## Translator Notes

- **14:6** — Hebrew behareram literally reads "in their mountain/hill country," with a third-person plural possessive suffix, standing in apposition to Seir.
- **14:14** — Hebrew wayyareq (Hiphil of riq, literally "poured out" or "unsheathed," used elsewhere for drawing a sword) is used here idiomatically for mobilizing troops. Hebrew achiv (literally "his brother") denotes a close relative, here Abram's nephew Lot (v. 12).
- **14:19** — Hebrew qoneh (Qal active participle of qanah) carries the dual sense of "Creator" and "Possessor/Owner" (similarly in v. 22).
- **14:23** — The Hebrew conditional particle im ("if") following an oath gesture ("raised my hand," v. 22) introduces an emphatic negative vow ("I will certainly not...").
