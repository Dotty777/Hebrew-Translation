## Text

**22:1** Now it happened after these events that God tested Abraham, and He said to him, "Abraham." And he said, "Here I am."

**22:2** And He said, "Please take your son, your only one, whom you love—Isaac—and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him up there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains that I will tell you about."

**22:3** So Abraham got up early in the morning, saddled his donkey, and took with him two of his young men and Isaac his son. He split wood for a burnt offering, then rose and went to the place that God had told him about.

**22:4** On the third day, Abraham lifted his eyes and saw the place from far off.

**22:5** And Abraham said to his young men, "Remain here with the donkey; the boy and I will go over there, and we will bow down in worship and return to you."

**22:6** Then Abraham took the wood for the burnt offering and placed it on Isaac his son; he took the fire and the knife in his hand, and the two of them walked together.

**22:7** Then Isaac spoke to Abraham his father and said, "My father!" And he replied, "Here I am, my son." And he said, "Here are the fire and the wood, yet where is the lamb for a burnt offering?"

**22:8** Abraham said, "God will see to the lamb for Himself as a burnt offering, my son." And the two of them walked together.

**22:9** And they arrived at the place that God had told him about; there Abraham built the altar, set the wood in order, bound Isaac his son, and placed him on the altar, atop the wood.

**22:10** And Abraham extended his hand and took the knife to slaughter his son.

**22:11** But the angel of the LORD called out to him from heaven and said, "Abraham! Abraham!" And he answered, "Here I am."

**22:12** He said, "Do not extend your hand against the boy, nor do anything to him; for now I know that you are one who fears God, and you have not held back your son, your only one, from Me."

**22:13** Then Abraham lifted his eyes and looked, and there, behind him, was a ram caught by its horns in the thicket. So Abraham went, took the ram, and offered it up as a burnt offering in place of his son.

**22:14** And Abraham called the name of that place The LORD Will See to It, as it is said today, "On the mountain of the LORD it will be seen to."

**22:15** And the angel of the LORD called out to Abraham a second time from heaven.

**22:16** He said, "By Myself I have sworn—declares the LORD—because you have done this thing and have not held back your son, your only one,

**22:17** I will most certainly bless you, and I will abundantly multiply your seed like the stars of the heavens and like the sand that is on the shore of the sea; and your seed will take possession of the gate of his enemies.

**22:18** And all the nations of the earth will bless themselves by your seed, because you have listened to My voice."

**22:19** Then Abraham returned to his young men; they got up and went together to Beersheba, and Abraham settled in Beersheba.

**22:20** Now it happened after these events that it was reported to Abraham, saying, "Look, Milcah too has borne sons to Nahor your brother:

**22:21** Uz his firstborn, and Buz his brother, and Kemuel the father of Aram,

**22:22** and Chesed, Hazo, Pildash, Jidlaph, and Bethuel."

**22:23** And Bethuel fathered Rebekah. Milcah bore these eight to Nahor, the brother of Abraham.

**22:24** And his concubine—her name was Reumah—she also bore Tebah, Gaham, Tahash, and Maacah.

## Translator Notes

- **22:8** — The idiom 'ra'ah lo' literally means "see for oneself," conveying "see to" or "provide," and introduces the repeated 'ra'ah' ("see") wordplay that culminates in the naming of the site in v. 14.
- **22:13** — The Masoretic Text reads 'achar' ("behind"), rather than the variant 'echad' ("a" or "one") found in the Samaritan Pentateuch, Septuagint, and some Hebrew manuscripts.
- **22:14** — The place name pairs the divine name with 'yir'eh' (Qal imperfect of 'ra'ah', "see," echoing v. 8's idiom "see to/provide"), playing on 'yera'eh' (Niphal imperfect of the same verb), which can mean either "it will be seen to/provided" or "He will be seen/appear."
- **22:18** — Hebrew 'vehithbarekhu' is the Hithpael of 'barakh' (in contrast to the Niphal used in Gen 12:3 and 18:18), which may be understood reflexively ("bless themselves") or as a middle/passive ("obtain blessing / be blessed").
