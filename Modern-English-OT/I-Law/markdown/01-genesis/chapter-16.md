## Text

**16:1** Now Sarai, the wife of Abram, had borne no children to him; she had an Egyptian maidservant, and her name was Hagar.

**16:2** Then Sarai said to Abram, "See now, the LORD has kept me from giving birth. Please go in to my maidservant; perhaps I will be built up through her." And Abram heeded the voice of Sarai.

**16:3** So Sarai, Abram's wife, took Hagar the Egyptian, her maidservant—at the end of ten years of Abram living in the land of Canaan—and gave her to Abram her husband as a wife for him.

**16:4** He went in to Hagar, and she conceived. When she saw that she had conceived, her mistress became despised in her eyes.

**16:5** Then Sarai said to Abram, "The wrong done to me is on you! I myself gave my maidservant into your embrace, and when she saw that she had conceived, I became despised in her eyes. May the LORD judge between me and you!"

**16:6** Abram said to Sarai, "Look, your maidservant is in your hand; do to her what is good in your eyes." Then Sarai afflicted her, and she fled from her presence.

**16:7** Then the angel of the LORD found her beside a spring of water in the wilderness—beside the spring along the road to Shur.

**16:8** He said, "Hagar, maidservant of Sarai, where have you come from, and where are you going?" She said, "From the presence of Sarai my mistress I am fleeing."

**16:9** Then the angel of the LORD said to her, "Return to your mistress and submit yourself under her hands."

**16:10** And the angel of the LORD said to her, "I will greatly multiply your offspring, so that they cannot be counted for their abundance."

**16:11** And the angel of the LORD said to her, "Look, you are pregnant and about to bear a son; you will call his name Ishmael, for the LORD has heard your affliction.

**16:12** He will be a wild donkey of a man—his hand against everyone, and the hand of everyone against him—and he will dwell opposite all his brothers."

**16:13** So she called the name of the LORD who was speaking to her, "You are a God of Seeing," for she said, "Have I really even here seen after Him who sees me?"

**16:14** Therefore the well was called Beer-lahai-roi; see, it lies between Kadesh and Bered.

**16:15** Then Hagar gave birth to a son for Abram, and Abram called the name of his son, whom Hagar had borne, Ishmael.

**16:16** Now Abram was eighty-six years old when Hagar gave birth to Ishmael for Abram.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — Hebrew ibbaneh (Niphal imperfect of banah, "build") literally means "be built up," playing on ben ("son") to express building a family by obtaining children through Hagar.
- **16:5** — In the Masoretic Text, uveynekha ("and you") bears an extraordinary dot (punctum extraordinarium) over the second yod, marking an unusual spelling with an extra yod.
- **16:9** — Hebrew hit'anni (Hithpael imperative of anah, "humble/submit oneself") uses the reflexive stem of the same verb translated "afflicted" in v. 6 and the noun "affliction" in v. 11.
- **16:12** — Hebrew al-peney (literally "upon the face of" or "opposite") carries both a geographical sense ("to the east of") and a relational sense ("in opposition to").
- **16:13** — The Masoretic pointing distinguishes El Ro'i (noun ro'i, "seeing") from ro'i at the end of the verse (Qal active participle with first-person suffix, "Him who sees me"); "seen after Him who sees me" conveys either gazing after God's departing presence or remaining alive to see after encountering Him. In v. 14, Beer-lahai-roi means "Well of the Living One who sees me."
