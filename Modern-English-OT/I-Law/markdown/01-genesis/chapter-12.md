## Text

**12:1** Then the LORD said to Abram, "Go out from your land, from your relatives, and from your father's house, to the land that I will show you.

**12:2** And I will make you into a great nation, I will bless you, and I will make your name great; and be a blessing.

**12:3** And I will bless those who bless you, and the one who reviles you I will curse; and all the families of the earth will be blessed in you."

**12:4** So Abram went just as the LORD had spoken to him, and Lot went with him; Abram was seventy-five years old when he went out from Haran.

**12:5** And Abram took his wife Sarai, his brother's son Lot, all the possessions that they had amassed, and the persons whom they had acquired in Haran; and they went out to go to the land of Canaan, and they came into the land of Canaan.

**12:6** And Abram passed through the land as far as the place of Shechem, as far as the oak of Moreh; now the Canaanites were in the land at that time.

**12:7** And the LORD appeared to Abram and said, "I will give this land to your offspring." And there he built an altar to the LORD, who had appeared to him.

**12:8** Then he relocated from there to the hill country east of Bethel and set up his tent with Bethel to the west and Ai to the east; he built an altar to the LORD there and called on the name of the LORD.

**12:9** Then Abram journeyed on, going steadily toward the Negev.

**12:10** And there was a famine in the land, and Abram went down into Egypt to stay there as a foreigner, because the famine in the land was severe.

**12:11** And as he drew near to enter Egypt, he said to his wife Sarai, "Look now, I know that you are a woman of beautiful appearance.

**12:12** And it will happen that when the Egyptians see you, they will say, 'This is his wife,' and they will kill me, but they will keep you alive.

**12:13** Say, please, that you are my sister, so that it may go well for me on your account and that my soul may live because of you."

**12:14** Now when Abram entered Egypt, the Egyptians saw the woman, that she was very beautiful.

**12:15** And the officials of Pharaoh saw her, and they praised her to Pharaoh; so the woman was taken into the house of Pharaoh.

**12:16** And he dealt well with Abram on her account, and he came to have flocks and herds, male donkeys, male servants and female servants, female donkeys, and camels.

**12:17** But the LORD plagued Pharaoh and his house with great plagues because of Sarai, the wife of Abram.

**12:18** Then Pharaoh summoned Abram and said, "What is this that you have done to me? Why did you not inform me that she is your wife?

**12:19** Why did you say, 'She is my sister,' so that I took her to myself as a wife? And now, here is your wife—take her and go!"

**12:20** Then Pharaoh commanded men concerning him, and they sent him away, along with his wife and all that was his.

## Translator Notes

- **12:2** — "And be a blessing" renders a Qal imperative (weheyeh), which following the preceding cohortative and imperfect verbs carries both an exhortative and a consequential force ("so that you will be a blessing").
- **12:3** — Hebrew contrasts plural "those who bless you" with singular "the one who reviles you" (Piel participle of qalal, "treat lightly/disdain/revile"), answered by the stronger verb arar ("curse"). The Niphal verb "will be blessed" (wenivreku) can be read as passive ("be blessed") or middle/reflexive ("find blessing / bless themselves").
- **12:5** — "The persons whom they had acquired" translates literally "the soul that they had made" (hannephesh asher-'asu), using collective singular nephesh for household members or servants and 'asah in the sense of acquiring (cf. Gen 31:1; Deut 8:17).
- **12:8** — The word for "his tent" (here and in 13:3) is written with an archaic final -h spelling for the masculine singular suffix -o; "west" translates Hebrew yam, literally "sea."
