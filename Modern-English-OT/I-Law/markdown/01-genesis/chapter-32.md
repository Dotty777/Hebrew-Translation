## Text

**32:1** Laban got up early in the morning, kissed his sons and his daughters, and blessed them. Then Laban left and returned to his place.

**32:2** And Jacob went on his way, and the angels of God encountered him.

**32:3** When Jacob saw them, he said, "This is the camp of God." And he called the name of that place Mahanaim.

**32:4** Then Jacob sent messengers ahead of him to Esau his brother, to the land of Seir, the territory of Edom.

**32:5** He commanded them, saying, "Say this to my lord Esau: 'Your servant Jacob says this: I have sojourned with Laban and remained until now.

**32:6** I now have oxen and donkeys, flocks, and male and female servants, and I have sent to inform my lord, so that I may find favor in your sight.'"

**32:7** The messengers returned to Jacob and said, "We went to your brother Esau, and moreover he is coming to meet you, and four hundred men are with him."

**32:8** Jacob became very afraid and distressed, and he divided the people who were with him, along with the flocks, the herds, and the camels, into two camps.

**32:9** He said, "If Esau comes to the one camp and strikes it, then the remaining camp will escape."

**32:10** Then Jacob said, "God of my father Abraham and God of my father Isaac, LORD, who said to me, 'Return to your land and to your relatives, and I will deal well with you,'

**32:11** I am unworthy of all the acts of loyal love and of all the faithfulness that You have shown to Your servant, for with my staff I crossed over this Jordan, and now I have become two camps.

**32:12** Rescue me, please, from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau, for I am afraid of him, that he may come and strike me, mother along with children.

**32:13** Yet You Yourself said, 'I will surely deal well with you, and I will make your offspring like the sand of the sea, which cannot be counted because of its abundance.'"

**32:14** So he lodged there that night, and from what had come into his hand he took a gift for Esau his brother:

**32:15** two hundred female goats and twenty male goats; two hundred ewes and twenty rams;

**32:16** thirty nursing camels along with their young; forty cows and ten bulls; twenty female donkeys and ten male donkeys.

**32:17** He placed them in the hand of his servants, each herd by itself, and said to his servants, "Pass ahead of me, and leave a space between one herd and the next."

**32:18** He commanded the first one, saying, "When Esau my brother meets you and asks you, 'Whose are you, where are you going, and to whom do these ahead of you belong?'

**32:19** then you shall say, 'They belong to your servant Jacob; it is a gift sent to my lord Esau, and look, he also is behind us.'"

**32:20** He commanded the second as well, and the third, and all those walking behind the herds, saying, "You shall speak this same message to Esau when you find him,

**32:21** and you shall also say, 'Look, your servant Jacob is behind us.'" For he said, "Let me appease his face with the gift going ahead of me, and afterward I will see his face; perhaps he will accept me."

**32:22** So the gift passed ahead of him, while he himself lodged in the camp that night.

**32:23** During that night he got up, took his two wives, his two female servants, and his eleven children, and crossed over the ford of the Jabbok.

**32:24** He took them and had them cross the stream, and he sent across what belonged to him.

**32:25** Jacob was left alone, and a man wrestled with him until the rising of dawn.

**32:26** When he saw that he could not prevail over him, he touched the socket of his hip, and the socket of Jacob's hip became dislocated during his wrestling with him.

**32:27** Then he said, "Release me, because the dawn has risen." But he said, "I will not release you unless you bless me."

**32:28** He said to him, "What is your name?" And he replied, "Jacob."

**32:29** Then he said, "No longer will your name be called Jacob, but Israel, because you have contended with God and with men, and you have prevailed."

**32:30** Then Jacob asked, saying, "Tell me your name, please." And he said, "Why do you ask about my name?" Then he blessed him there.

**32:31** So Jacob called the name of the place Peniel: "For I have seen God face to face, yet my life has been rescued."

**32:32** The sun rose over him as he passed by Penuel, while he was limping on his hip.

**32:33** Therefore the sons of Israel do not eat the sciatic sinew that is on the socket of the hip to this day, because he touched the socket of Jacob's hip at the sciatic sinew.

## Translator Notes

- **32:3** — The Hebrew name Mahanaim is a dual form meaning "two camps," recalling the camp of God's angels and anticipating Jacob's division of his company into two camps in verse 8.
- **32:11** — Literally, "I am too small for all the acts of loyal love," using the Qal stative perfect of the root meaning "to be small" with a comparative preposition as an idiom for humility and unworthiness.
- **32:21** — The Hebrew repeats the noun panim, "face," four times in this verse: "appease his face," "ahead of me" (lit. "to my face"), "see his face," and "accept me" (lit. "lift up my face," an idiom for showing favor).
- **32:32** — The Masoretic Text spells the place name Peniel ("face of God") in verse 31 and Penuel in verse 32, using an archaic variant connecting vowel in the construct form.
- **32:33** — The rare Hebrew expression gid hannasheh occurs only in this verse and designates the sciatic nerve or hip sinew located along the socket of the hip joint.
