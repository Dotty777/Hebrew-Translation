## Text

**42:1** And Jacob saw that there was grain in Egypt, and Jacob said to his sons, "Why are you looking at one another?"

**42:2** He said, "Look, I have heard that there is grain in Egypt. Go down there and buy grain for us from there, so that we may live and not die."

**42:3** And ten of Joseph's brothers went down to buy grain from Egypt.

**42:4** But Jacob did not send Benjamin, Joseph's brother, along with his brothers, for he said, "Harm might happen to him."

**42:5** And the sons of Israel came to buy grain in the midst of those who came, because the famine was in the land of Canaan.

**42:6** Now Joseph was the ruler over the land; it was he who sold grain to all the people of the land. And Joseph's brothers came and bowed low to him, faces to the ground.

**42:7** Joseph saw his brothers and recognized them, yet he acted as a stranger toward them and spoke harshly with them. He said to them, "Where have you come from?" They said, "From the land of Canaan, to buy food."

**42:8** Thus Joseph recognized his brothers, yet they did not recognize him.

**42:9** Joseph remembered the dreams that he had dreamed concerning them, and he said to them, "You are spies! To see the nakedness of the land you have come."

**42:10** They said to him, "No, my lord; rather, your servants have come to buy food.

**42:11** All of us are sons of one man; we are honest men, and your servants have not been spies."

**42:12** But he said to them, "No! Rather, you have come to see the nakedness of the land."

**42:13** And they said, "Your servants are twelve brothers, the sons of one man in the land of Canaan. Look, the youngest is with our father today, and one is no more."

**42:14** Joseph said to them, "This is what I spoke to you, saying, 'You are spies!'

**42:15** By this you will be tested: by the life of Pharaoh, you will certainly not go out from here unless your youngest brother comes here.

**42:16** Send one from among you and let him fetch your brother, while you remain bound, so that your words may be tested, whether truth is with you. And if not, by the life of Pharaoh, you are certainly spies."

**42:17** Then he gathered them into custody for three days.

**42:18** And Joseph said to them on the third day, "Do this and live; I fear God.

**42:19** If you are honest men, let one of your brothers remain bound in your house of custody, while you go and bring grain for the famine of your households.

**42:20** And bring your youngest brother to me, that your words may be proven true and you will not die." And they did so.

**42:21** And they said, each to his brother, "Indeed, we are guilty regarding our brother, in that we saw the distress of his soul when he pleaded with us for mercy, and we did not listen. For this reason this distress has come upon us."

**42:22** And Reuben answered them, saying, "Did I not say to you, 'Do not sin against the boy'? Yet you did not listen. And look, his blood also is being required."

**42:23** Now they did not know that Joseph understood, because the interpreter was between them.

**42:24** He turned aside from them and wept. Then he came back to them and spoke to them; and he took Simeon from them and bound him before their eyes.

**42:25** And Joseph gave orders to fill their containers with grain, to return each man's silver to his sack, and to give them provisions for the journey. And he did so for them.

**42:26** Then they loaded their grain onto their donkeys and went from there.

**42:27** When one of them opened his sack to give fodder to his donkey at the lodging place, he saw his silver; and look, it was in the mouth of his bag.

**42:28** So he said to his brothers, "My silver has been returned, and indeed, here it is in my bag!" Then their hearts failed them, and trembling they turned to one another, saying, "What is this God has done to us?"

**42:29** And they came to Jacob their father, to the land of Canaan, and reported to him all the things that had happened to them, saying,

**42:30** "The man, the lord of the land, spoke harshly with us and treated us as those spying out the land.

**42:31** And we said to him, 'We are honest men; we have not been spies.

**42:32** We are twelve brothers, the sons of our father; one is no more, and the youngest is with our father today in the land of Canaan.'

**42:33** Then the man, the lord of the land, said to us, 'By this I will know that you are honest men: leave one of your brothers with me, take grain for the famine of your households, and go.

**42:34** And bring your youngest brother to me, so that I may know that you are not spies, but rather that you are honest men. Then I will give your brother to you, and you may trade in the land.'"

**42:35** Now it happened, as they were emptying their sacks, that look, each man's bundle of silver was in his sack! And they and their father saw their bundles of silver, and they were afraid.

**42:36** Then Jacob their father said to them, "You have left me bereaved! Joseph is no more, and Simeon is no more, and Benjamin you would take. All of these things have come upon me."

**42:37** Then Reuben said to his father, "You may put my two sons to death if I do not bring him to you. Place him in my hand, and I myself will return him to you."

**42:38** But he said, "My son will not go down with you, because his brother is dead, and he alone remains. If harm befalls him along the way you are going, then you will bring down my gray hair in sorrow to Sheol."

## Translator Notes

- **42:7** — Wordplay on the Hebrew root nkr: the Hiphil verb ('recognized them') contrasts directly with the Hithpael ('acted as a stranger toward them').
- **42:9** — 'The nakedness of the land' is a literal Hebrew idiom referring to the land's exposed or undefended weak points.
- **42:28** — Literally 'their heart went out,' an idiom for losing courage in terror; the following clause ('trembling they turned to one another') uses a pregnant Hebrew construction combining the verb 'tremble' with the preposition 'to.'
- **42:33** — The Masoretic Text omits the noun 'grain' here, reading literally 'and the famine of your households take and go,' an elliptical phrasing for 'grain for the famine of your households' as in verse 19.
