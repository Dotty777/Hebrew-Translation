## Text

**21:1** "Now these are the legal rulings that you are to place before them.

**21:2** When you buy a Hebrew slave, for six years he is to serve, and in the seventh he is to go out free, at no cost.

**21:3** If he comes in by himself, he is to go out by himself; if he has a wife, then his wife is to go out with him.

**21:4** If his master gives a wife to him, and she bears sons or daughters for him, the woman and her children are to belong to her master, while he is to go out by himself.

**21:5** Yet if the slave firmly says, 'I love my master, my wife, and my children; I am not going out free,'

**21:6** then his master must bring him near to God, and bring him near to the door or to the doorpost; and his master must pierce his ear with an awl, and he will serve him forever.

**21:7** Now when a man sells his daughter as a female slave, she must not go out in the manner that the male slaves go out.

**21:8** If she is displeasing in the eyes of her master, who designated her for himself, then he must allow her to be redeemed. He has no right to sell her to a foreign people, because he has dealt treacherously with her.

**21:9** And if he designates her for his son, he must act toward her according to the legal right of daughters.

**21:10** If he takes another woman for himself, he must not diminish her food, her clothing, or her marital rights.

**21:11** And if he does not do these three things for her, then she is to go out at no cost, without money.

**21:12** One who strikes a man so that he dies must certainly be put to death.

**21:13** Yet if he did not lie in wait, and God caused it to come into his hand, then I will appoint a place for you where he can flee.

**21:14** But if a man acts presumptuously against his neighbor to kill him with cunning, you must take him from beside My altar to die.

**21:15** And one who strikes his father or his mother must certainly be put to death.

**21:16** And one who curses his father or his mother must certainly be put to death.

**21:17** And one who steals a person, whether he has sold him or he is found in his hand, must certainly be put to death.

**21:18** And when men quarrel, and one strikes his neighbor with a stone or with a fist, and he does not die but falls bedridden,

**21:19** if he rises and walks about outside on his staff, then the one who struck him is to be acquitted; only he must pay for his lost time and see that he is completely healed.

**21:20** And when a man strikes his male slave or his female slave with a rod, and the slave dies under his hand, the slave must certainly be avenged.

**21:21** However, if the slave survives a day or two, he is not to be avenged, for the slave is his money.

**21:22** And when men fight each other and strike a pregnant woman so that her children come out, yet no harm occurs, he must certainly be fined according to what the woman's husband imposes upon him, and he must pay as assessed by the judges.

**21:23** But if harm occurs, then you must give a life for a life,

**21:24** an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth, a hand for a hand, a foot for a foot,

**21:25** a burn for a burn, a wound for a wound, a welt for a welt.

**21:26** And when a man strikes the eye of his male slave or the eye of his female slave and destroys it, he must release him into freedom in place of his eye.

**21:27** And if he knocks out a tooth of his male slave or a tooth of his female slave, he must release him into freedom in place of his tooth.

**21:28** And when an ox gores a man or a woman so that he or she dies, the ox must certainly be stoned and its flesh must not be eaten, while the owner of the ox is guiltless.

**21:29** However, if it was an ox given to goring in the past, and its owner had been warned yet did not keep it under guard, and it kills a man or a woman, the ox must be stoned, and its owner as well must be put to death.

**21:30** If a ransom is imposed upon him, then he must pay as the redemption price for his life all that is imposed upon him.

**21:31** Whether it gores a son or gores a daughter, according to this ruling it must be done to him.

**21:32** If the ox gores a male slave or a female slave, the owner must pay thirty shekels of silver to the slave's master, and the ox must be stoned.

**21:33** And when a man uncovers a pit, or when a man digs a pit and does not cover it, and an ox or a donkey falls into it,

**21:34** the owner of the pit must make restitution; he must pay back money to its owner, and the dead animal will belong to him.

**21:35** And when a man's ox strikes his neighbor's ox so that it dies, then they must sell the live ox and divide its money in half, and the dead animal as well they must divide in half.

**21:36** Or if it was known that it was an ox given to goring in the past, yet its owner did not keep it under guard, he must certainly make restitution of an ox in place of the ox, and the dead animal will belong to him.

**21:37** When a man steals an ox or a sheep and slaughters it or sells it, he must make restitution of five from the herd in place of the ox and four from the flock in place of the sheep.

## Translator Notes

- **21:6** — Hebrew ha'elohim literally means "God," pointing to a solemn legal ratification before God, though the Targums and later Jewish tradition understand it as "the judges."
- **21:8** — Ketiv/Qere: the written text (ketiv) reads lo' ("not," i.e., "whom he has not designated"), whereas the read text (qere) translated here reads lo ("for himself," i.e., "whom he designated for himself").
- **21:10** — Hebrew onatah is a hapax legomenon (occurring only here in the Hebrew Bible); it is understood here as "her marital rights," though some lexicographers propose "oil / ointments" or "dwelling."
- **21:16** — The Masoretic Text places kidnapping in v. 16 and cursing one's parents in v. 17; this edition's verse order places cursing one's parents in v. 16 and kidnapping in v. 17, matching the sequence of the Septuagint.
- **21:22** — Hebrew weyatse'u yeladeyha literally means "and her children come out," leaving open whether premature live birth or miscarriage is envisioned; biphlilim ("as assessed by the judges") derives from the judicial root pll.
