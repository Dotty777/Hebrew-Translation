## Text

**12:1** And the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron in the land of Egypt, saying,

**12:2** "This month is for you the beginning of months; it shall be first for you of the months of the year.

**12:3** Speak to the entire congregation of Israel, saying, 'On the tenth of this month, they shall take for themselves, each man a lamb for a household of fathers, a lamb for each household.

**12:4** And if the household is too small for a lamb, then he and his neighbor closest to his house shall take one by a reckoning of persons; each man according to what he eats, you shall reckon for the lamb.

**12:5** You shall have a lamb without defect, a male one year old; from the sheep or from the goats you may take it.

**12:6** And it shall be in your keeping until the fourteenth day of this month, and the entire assembly of the congregation of Israel shall slaughter it at twilight.

**12:7** And they shall take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and on the lintel, on the houses where they will eat it.

**12:8** And they shall eat the meat on that night; roasted over fire, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, they shall eat it.

**12:9** Do not eat any of it raw or boiled in any way in water, but rather roasted over fire, its head with its legs and with its inner parts.

**12:10** You shall not leave any of it until morning; whatever remains of it until morning you shall burn in the fire.

**12:11** And this is how you shall eat it: with your waist belted, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand; and you shall eat it in haste. It is a Passover to the LORD.

**12:12** And I will pass through the land of Egypt on that night, and I will strike every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from man to beast; and on all the gods of Egypt I will carry out judgments. I am the LORD.

**12:13** And the blood shall be for you a sign on the houses where you are; I will see the blood and pass over you, and there shall be no destructive plague among you when I strike in the land of Egypt.

**12:14** And this day shall be for you a memorial, and you shall celebrate it as a festival to the LORD; throughout your generations, as a perpetual statute, you shall celebrate it.

**12:15** For seven days you shall eat unleavened bread; indeed, on the first day you shall clear leaven from your houses, for everyone who eats anything leavened from the first day until the seventh day, that person shall be cut off from Israel.

**12:16** And on the first day you shall have a sacred assembly, and on the seventh day a sacred assembly; no work of any kind shall be done on them, only what is to be eaten by every person, that alone may be prepared by you.

**12:17** And you shall observe the unleavened bread, for on this very day I brought out your hosts from the land of Egypt; and you shall observe this day throughout your generations as a perpetual statute.

**12:18** In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month in the evening, you shall eat unleavened bread until the twenty-first day of the month in the evening.

**12:19** For seven days no leaven shall be found in your houses, for everyone who eats anything leavened, that person shall be cut off from the congregation of Israel, whether a resident alien or a native of the land.

**12:20** You shall eat nothing leavened; in all your dwelling places you shall eat unleavened bread.'"

**12:21** Then Moses summoned all the elders of Israel and said to them, "Draw out and take for yourselves from the flock according to your clans, and slaughter the Passover.

**12:22** And you shall take a bundle of hyssop, dip it into the blood that is in the basin, and touch the lintel and the two doorposts with some of the blood that is in the basin; and none of you shall go out of the doorway of his house until morning.

**12:23** For the LORD will pass through to strike Egypt, and when He sees the blood on the lintel and on the two doorposts, the LORD will pass over the doorway and will not permit the destroyer to come into your houses to strike.

**12:24** And you shall observe this thing as a statute for you and for your children forever.

**12:25** And when you enter the land that the LORD will give you, just as He has spoken, you shall observe this service.

**12:26** And it shall be, when your children say to you, 'What is this service to you?'

**12:27** then you shall say, 'It is a Passover sacrifice to the LORD, who passed over the houses of the sons of Israel in Egypt when He struck Egypt and rescued our houses.'" Then the people bowed down and worshiped.

**12:28** And the sons of Israel went and carried it out; just as the LORD commanded Moses and Aaron, they did so.

**12:29** And it happened at midnight that the LORD struck every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh sitting on his throne to the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon, and every firstborn of the livestock.

**12:30** And Pharaoh got up during the night, he and all his servants and all Egypt, and there was a great outcry in Egypt, for there was no house in which there was not someone dead.

**12:31** And he called Moses and Aaron during the night and said, "Get up, go out from the midst of my people, both you and the sons of Israel, and go, serve the LORD as you spoke.

**12:32** Both your flocks and your herds, take them as you have spoken, and go; and bless me as well."

**12:33** And Egypt pressed hard on the people to hasten to send them out of the land, for they said, "All of us are dead!"

**12:34** So the people carried their dough before it was leavened, with their kneading bowls bundled in their cloaks on their shoulders.

**12:35** And the sons of Israel had done according to the word of Moses, and they asked from the Egyptians silver articles and gold articles and garments.

**12:36** And the LORD had given the people favor in the eyes of the Egyptians, so they granted their request, and they plundered the Egyptians.

**12:37** And the sons of Israel set out from Rameses to Succoth, around six hundred thousand men on foot, apart from little ones.

**12:38** And a great mixed multitude also went up with them, along with flocks and herds, very abundant livestock.

**12:39** And they baked into unleavened cakes the dough that they had brought out from Egypt, for it had not leavened, because they had been driven out from Egypt and could not linger, and they had not even made provisions for themselves.

**12:40** And the stay of the sons of Israel, during which they lived in Egypt, was four hundred and thirty years.

**12:41** And it happened at the end of four hundred and thirty years, on that very day, all the hosts of the LORD came out from the land of Egypt.

**12:42** It was a night of vigil by the LORD to bring them out from the land of Egypt; this very night belongs to the LORD, a vigil for all the sons of Israel throughout their generations.

**12:43** Then the LORD said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the statute concerning the Passover: no foreigner may eat of it;

**12:44** but every slave of a man who is bought with money, once you have circumcised him, then he may eat of it.

**12:45** A temporary resident or a hired worker shall not eat of it.

**12:46** In one house it shall be eaten; you shall not bring any of the meat outside the house, and you shall not break a bone of it.

**12:47** The entire congregation of Israel shall observe it.

**12:48** And if a resident alien resides with you and wishes to observe the Passover to the LORD, every male belonging to him must be circumcised, and then he may draw near to observe it, and he shall be like a native of the land; but no uncircumcised male may eat of it.

**12:49** One law shall apply to the native and to the resident alien who resides in your midst."

**12:50** And all the sons of Israel carried it out; just as the LORD commanded Moses and Aaron, they did so.

**12:51** And it happened on that very day that the LORD brought out the sons of Israel from the land of Egypt according to their hosts.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — Hebrew "seh" denotes a single individual animal from the flock, either a sheep or a goat, as verse 5 clarifies.
- **12:6** — "At twilight" renders the literal Hebrew dual idiom "ben ha-arbayim" ("between the two evenings"), variously understood as the period between afternoon and sunset or between sunset and full dark.
- **12:22** — Hebrew "saf" can mean either "basin" or "threshold"; translating "basin" follows its use for a vessel holding liquids, though some ancient versions and interpreters understand the blood as held at the doorway threshold.
- **12:40** — The Masoretic Text states simply "in Egypt"; the Samaritan Pentateuch and Septuagint expand the phrase to include the patriarchs' stay in the land of Canaan.
- **12:42** — Hebrew "shimmurim" (an intensive plural noun from "shamar," to watch or keep) carries a double resonance here: the LORD's own protective vigil on the night of the exodus, and Israel's ongoing night of observance through every generation.
