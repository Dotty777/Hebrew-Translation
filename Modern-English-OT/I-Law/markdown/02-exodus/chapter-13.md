## Text

**13:1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**13:2** "Consecrate to Me every firstborn, whatever opens every womb among the sons of Israel, both of man and of livestock; it is Mine."

**13:3** And Moses said to the people, "Remember this day on which you went out of Egypt, from the house of slaves, because by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from here; and leavened bread shall not be eaten.

**13:4** Today you are going out, in the month of Abib.

**13:5** And it shall come about, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites, which He swore to your fathers to give to you, a land flowing with milk and honey, that you shall perform this service in this month.

**13:6** Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a festival to the LORD.

**13:7** Unleavened bread shall be eaten throughout the seven days; no leavened bread shall be seen with you, and no leaven shall be seen with you within all your borders.

**13:8** And you shall tell your son on that day, saying, 'It is on account of what the LORD did for me when I went out of Egypt.'

**13:9** And it shall serve you as a sign on your hand and as a reminder between your eyes, so that the instruction of the LORD may be in your mouth; for with a strong hand the LORD brought you out of Egypt.

**13:10** And you shall keep this statute at its appointed time, year after year.

**13:11** And it shall come about, when the LORD brings you into the land of the Canaanites, just as He swore to you and to your fathers, and gives it to you,

**13:12** that you shall hand over to the LORD all that opens the womb, along with every first young of livestock that belongs to you; the males belong to the LORD.

**13:13** As for every first young of a donkey, you shall redeem it with a sheep or goat, and if you do not redeem it, you shall break its neck; and you shall redeem every human firstborn among your sons.

**13:14** And it shall come about, when your son asks you in time to come, saying, 'What is this?' that you shall say to him, 'By strength of hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt, from the house of slaves.

**13:15** And it happened, when Pharaoh hardened himself against letting us go, that the LORD killed every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of man to the firstborn of livestock. Therefore I sacrifice to the LORD all that opens the womb, the males, while every firstborn of my sons I redeem.

**13:16** And it shall serve as a sign on your hand and as frontlets between your eyes, because by strength of hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt.'"

**13:17** Now it came about, when Pharaoh sent the people out, that God did not lead them by the road of the land of the Philistines, although it was near; for God said, "Otherwise the people might change their minds when they see war and turn back to Egypt."

**13:18** So God brought the people around by the road of the wilderness toward the Red Sea; and equipped for battle, the sons of Israel went up from the land of Egypt.

**13:19** And Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, because Joseph had solemnly made the sons of Israel swear, saying, "God will certainly attend to you, and you shall bring up my bones from here with you."

**13:20** And they broke camp from Succoth and encamped at Etham, at the edge of the wilderness.

**13:21** Now the LORD was going ahead of them by day in a pillar of cloud to lead them on the road, and by night in a pillar of fire to shine light for them, so that they could travel day and night.

**13:22** The pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night did not withdraw from ahead of the people.

## Translator Notes

- **13:8** — Hebrew literally reads ba'avur zeh asah YHWH li ("on account of this [which] the LORD did for me"), with the demonstrative zeh introducing an elliptical relative clause explaining the observance.
- **13:10** — Literally "from days to days" (miyyamim yamimah), a Hebrew idiom denoting an annual cycle.
- **13:17** — The particle ki functions concessively ("although/even though"). The Hebrew also plays on sound between naham (Qal of nhh, "led them") and yinnahem (Niphal of nhm, "change their minds/regret").
- **13:18** — Hebrew yam-suph literally means "Sea of Reeds." The Qal passive participle hamushim ("equipped for battle" or "in battle array") is a rare military term of debated etymology (cf. Josh 1:14; 4:12; Judg 7:11).
- **13:22** — The Hiphil imperfect yamish (from mush) is rendered intransitively ("did not withdraw"), though it can also be read transitively with the LORD as implied subject ("He did not remove").
