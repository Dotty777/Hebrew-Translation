## Text

**9:1** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Go in to Pharaoh and speak to him, 'This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Send My people away so that they may serve Me.

**9:2** For if you refuse to send them away and are still keeping hold of them,

**9:3** look, the hand of the LORD is coming upon your livestock that are in the field—on the horses, on the donkeys, on the camels, on the herds, and on the flocks—a very heavy pestilence.

**9:4** Yet the LORD will make a distinction between the livestock of Israel and the livestock of Egypt, so that nothing will die of all that belongs to the sons of Israel.'"

**9:5** And the LORD set an appointed time, saying, "Tomorrow the LORD will do this thing in the land."

**9:6** So the LORD did this thing on the next day, and all the livestock of Egypt died; yet of the livestock of the sons of Israel, not one died.

**9:7** And Pharaoh sent, and look, not even one of the livestock of Israel had died. Yet Pharaoh's heart became heavy, and he did not send the people away.

**9:8** Then the LORD said to Moses and to Aaron, "Take for yourselves handfuls of soot from a kiln, and let Moses toss it toward the sky in the sight of Pharaoh.

**9:9** It will become fine dust over all the land of Egypt, and it will become boils breaking out into blisters on people and on animals throughout all the land of Egypt."

**9:10** So they took soot from the kiln and stood before Pharaoh, and Moses tossed it toward the sky; and it became boils with blisters breaking out on people and on animals.

**9:11** And the magicians were not able to stand before Moses because of the boils, for the boils were on the magicians and on all the Egyptians.

**9:12** Yet the LORD made Pharaoh's heart hard, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had spoken to Moses.

**9:13** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Rise early in the morning and station yourself before Pharaoh, and say to him, 'This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Send My people away so that they may serve Me.

**9:14** For this time I am sending all My plagues against your heart, and upon your servants and your people, so that you may know that there is no one like Me in all the earth.

**9:15** For by now I could have stretched out My hand and struck you and your people with pestilence, and you would have been wiped out from the earth.

**9:16** However, for this reason I have kept you standing: in order to show you My power, and so that My name may be recounted throughout all the earth.

**9:17** You are still exalting yourself against My people by not sending them away.

**9:18** Look, about this time tomorrow I will rain down very heavy hail, such as has never been in Egypt from the day it was founded until now.

**9:19** So now, send and bring your livestock to safety, along with everything that belongs to you in the field. Every person and animal that is found in the field and is not gathered into the house—the hail will come down on them, and they will die.'"

**9:20** Whoever among Pharaoh's servants feared the word of the LORD made his servants and his livestock flee into the houses;

**9:21** but whoever did not set his heart on the word of the LORD left his servants and his livestock in the field.

**9:22** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky so that there may be hail throughout all the land of Egypt, on people, on animals, and on every plant of the field in the land of Egypt."

**9:23** So Moses stretched out his staff toward the sky, and the LORD sent thunder and hail, and fire ran down to the earth; and the LORD rained hail upon the land of Egypt.

**9:24** So there was hail, and fire flaring continuously in the midst of the hail—very heavy, such as had never been in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation.

**9:25** And the hail struck throughout all the land of Egypt everything that was in the field, from people to animals; and the hail struck every plant of the field and shattered every tree of the field.

**9:26** Only in the land of Goshen, where the sons of Israel were, there was no hail.

**9:27** Then Pharaoh sent and summoned Moses and Aaron and said to them, "This time I have sinned; the LORD is the righteous One, and I and my people are the wicked.

**9:28** Plead with the LORD, for there has been enough of God's thunder and hail; and I will send you away, and you shall stay no longer."

**9:29** And Moses said to him, "As soon as I leave the city, I will spread out my palms to the LORD; the thunder will cease and there will be no more hail, in order that you may know that the earth belongs to the LORD.

**9:30** Yet as for you and your servants, I know that you do not yet fear the LORD God."

**9:31** Now the flax and the barley were struck down, for the barley was in the ear and the flax was in bud.

**9:32** But the wheat and the emmer were not struck down, for they ripen late.

**9:33** So Moses went out from Pharaoh and out of the city, and he spread out his palms to the LORD; and the thunder and the hail ceased, and rain did not pour down to the earth.

**9:34** When Pharaoh saw that the rain, the hail, and the thunder had ceased, he sinned again and made his heart heavy, he and his servants.

**9:35** So Pharaoh's heart grew hard, and he did not send the sons of Israel away, just as the LORD had spoken by the hand of Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **9:3** — The form הוֹיָה is a rare Qal feminine participle of the archaic root הוה (a by-form of היה, "to be"), the only such participle of this verb in the Hebrew Bible, expressing imminent action ("is coming").
- **9:15** — The construction כִּי עַתָּה followed by a perfect verb (שָׁלַחְתִּי) and waw-consecutive forms expresses a counterfactual past condition ("by now I could have... and you would have been wiped out").
- **9:16** — The Hiphil verb הֶעֱמַדְתִּיךָ (from עמד, "stand") literally means "I have caused you to stand" or "kept you standing," contrasting with being wiped out in 9:15. The Piel infinitive construct סַפֵּר has an indefinite subject ("be recounted").
- **9:17** — The rare Hithpoel participle מִסְתּוֹלֵל (from סלל, "heap up a mound," with metathesis of ת and ס) vividly portrays Pharaoh raising himself up as a barrier against God's people.
- **9:31** — The agricultural terms גִּבְעֹל ("in bud," 9:31) and אֲפִילֹת ("ripen late," 9:32) are hapax legomena in the Hebrew Bible; כֻּסֶּמֶת (9:32) refers to emmer wheat, a late-ripening grain cultivated in Egypt.
