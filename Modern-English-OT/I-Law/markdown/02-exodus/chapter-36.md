## Text

**36:1** So Bezalel and Oholiab, and every wise-hearted man in whom the LORD has placed wisdom and understanding to know how to do all the work of the service of the sanctuary, shall work according to everything that the LORD has commanded.

**36:2** Then Moses called Bezalel and Oholiab and every wise-hearted man in whose heart the LORD had placed wisdom, everyone whose heart moved him to draw near to the work to do it.

**36:3** They received from before Moses the entire contribution that the sons of Israel had brought for the work of the service of the sanctuary, to do it. Still, they kept bringing to him freewill offerings morning by morning.

**36:4** Then all the wise ones who were doing all the work of the sanctuary came, each man from his work that they were doing.

**36:5** And they spoke to Moses, saying, "The people are bringing much more than enough for the service of the work that the LORD commanded to be done."

**36:6** So Moses gave a command, and they passed a proclamation through the camp, saying, "Let neither man nor woman do any more work for the contribution of the sanctuary." And the people were held back from bringing more.

**36:7** For the material was enough for them for all the work, to do it, and to spare.

**36:8** From the blue, purple, and crimson yarn they made finely woven garments for ministering in the sanctuary, and they made the holy garments for Aaron, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:9** He made the ephod of gold, blue, purple, and crimson yarn, and twisted fine linen.

**36:10** They hammered out thin sheets of gold, and he cut them into threads to work into the blue, into the purple, into the crimson yarn, and into the fine linen, the work of a skilled designer.

**36:11** They made attaching shoulder pieces for it; at its two ends it was joined together.

**36:12** And the woven band that fastened it, which was on it, was of one piece with it and of the same workmanship: gold, blue, purple, and crimson yarn, and twisted fine linen, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:13** They fashioned the onyx stones, enclosed in gold filigree settings, engraved like the engravings of a signet according to the names of the sons of Israel.

**36:14** He set them on the shoulder pieces of the ephod as stones of reminder for the sons of Israel, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:15** He made the breastpiece as the work of a skilled designer, like the work of the ephod, of gold, blue, purple, and crimson yarn, and twisted fine linen.

**36:16** It was square; they made the breastpiece folded double, a span in its length and a span in its width when folded double.

**36:17** They set in it four rows of stones: a row of carnelian, topaz, and emerald was the first row.

**36:18** The second row was turquoise, lapis lazuli, and chalcedony.

**36:19** The third row was jacinth, agate, and amethyst.

**36:20** And the fourth row was beryl, onyx, and jasper; they were enclosed in gold filigree settings in their mountings.

**36:21** The stones corresponded to the names of the sons of Israel, twelve of them according to their names, like the engravings of a signet, each according to his name, for the twelve tribes.

**36:22** They made on the breastpiece braided chains of corded work, of pure gold.

**36:23** They made two gold filigree settings and two gold rings, and they placed the two rings on the two ends of the breastpiece.

**36:24** Then they put the two gold cords onto the two rings at the ends of the breastpiece.

**36:25** The two ends of the two cords they put onto the two filigree settings, and they placed them on the shoulder pieces of the ephod at its front.

**36:26** They made two gold rings and set them on the two ends of the breastpiece, on its edge facing the ephod on the inside.

**36:27** They made two gold rings and placed them on the two shoulder pieces of the ephod below, at its front, right beside its seam, above the woven band of the ephod.

**36:28** They tied the breastpiece by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a blue cord, so that it would rest upon the woven band of the ephod and the breastpiece would not come loose from the ephod, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:29** He made the robe of the ephod, the work of a weaver, entirely of blue.

**36:30** The opening of the robe was in its center, like the opening of a coat of mail, with an edging around its opening so that it would not tear.

**36:31** On the hem of the robe they made pomegranates of twisted blue, purple, and crimson yarn.

**36:32** They made bells of pure gold and placed the bells between the pomegranates all around the hem of the robe, between the pomegranates.

**36:33** A bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate, all around the hem of the robe for ministering, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:34** They made the tunics of fine linen, the work of a weaver, for Aaron and for his sons,

**36:35** and the turban of fine linen, and the ornate caps of fine linen, and the linen undergarments of twisted fine linen,

**36:36** and the sash of twisted fine linen, and blue, purple, and crimson yarn, the work of an embroiderer, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:37** They made the plate of the holy diadem of pure gold, and they inscribed on it an inscription like the engravings of a signet: "Holiness to the LORD."

**36:38** They put a blue cord on it to place it high on the turban, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **36:7** — The Hebrew noun melakah ("work") is used twice, first in the extended sense of contributed "material" and then as "work"; wehoter is a Hiphil infinitive absolute of yatar ("to leave over"), used adverbially to express a surplus ("and to spare").
- **36:8** — The rare expression bigde-serad ("finely woven garments") is of uncertain etymology, variously understood as finely woven or braided textiles or as official garments of service.
- **36:11** — The Ketiv writes the noun with a third-person masculine singular suffix lacking yod (qtswwtw), while the Qere reads the standard plural form qetsotayw ("its ends"), which is translated here.
- **36:17** — The precise mineralogical identification of several of the twelve Hebrew gemstone names in verses 17-20 remains uncertain; the renderings here reflect established lexical and historical identifications.
- **36:31** — The Hophal participle moshzar ("twisted") appears here in the Hebrew text without shesh ("fine linen"), directly modifying the dyed yarns.
