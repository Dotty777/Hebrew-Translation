## Text

**5:1** And afterward Moses and Aaron came in and said to Pharaoh, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Let My people go, so that they may celebrate a festival for Me in the wilderness."

**5:2** And Pharaoh said, "Who is the LORD, that I should listen to His voice to let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, and furthermore, I will not let Israel go."

**5:3** So they said, "The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Please let us go a journey of three days into the wilderness and sacrifice to the LORD our God, or else He may strike us with pestilence or with the sword."

**5:4** And the king of Egypt said to them, "Why, Moses and Aaron, are you pulling the people away from their tasks? Go to your burdens!"

**5:5** Pharaoh also said, "Look, the people of the land are numerous now, and you are making them rest from their burdens!"

**5:6** So on that day Pharaoh commanded the taskmasters over the people and their foremen, saying,

**5:7** "You shall no longer give straw to the people for making bricks as before; they themselves shall go and gather straw for themselves.

**5:8** Yet the quota of bricks that they were making before, you shall impose on them; you shall not reduce it. For they are idle; therefore they are crying out, saying, 'Let us go, let us sacrifice to our God.'

**5:9** Let the labor weigh heavy on the men, that they may work at it and pay no attention to words of falsehood."

**5:10** Then the taskmasters of the people and their foremen went out and spoke to the people, saying, "This is what Pharaoh says: I am not giving you straw.

**5:11** Go yourselves, get straw for yourselves from wherever you find it, for not a thing will be reduced from your labor."

**5:12** Then the people spread out across all the land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw.

**5:13** And the taskmasters were pressing hard, saying, "Complete your tasks, each day's quota in its day, just as when there was straw."

**5:14** And the foremen of the sons of Israel, whom Pharaoh's taskmasters had appointed over them, were beaten and asked, "Why have you not completed your prescribed share in making bricks as before, either yesterday or today?"

**5:15** So the foremen of the sons of Israel came and cried out to Pharaoh, saying, "Why do you do this to your servants?

**5:16** No straw is given to your servants, yet they say to us, 'Make bricks!' And look, your servants are being beaten, yet your own people are at fault."

**5:17** But he said, "Idle, you are idle! Therefore you are saying, 'Let us go, let us sacrifice to the LORD.'

**5:18** Now then, go, work! Straw will not be given to you, yet you shall deliver the tally of bricks."

**5:19** And the foremen of the sons of Israel saw themselves in trouble when they were told, "You shall not reduce your bricks, each day's quota in its day."

**5:20** Then they encountered Moses and Aaron, who were standing to meet them as they came out from Pharaoh.

**5:21** And they said to them, "May the LORD look upon you and judge, because you have made our scent stink in the eyes of Pharaoh and in the eyes of his servants, to put a sword in their hand to kill us."

**5:22** So Moses returned to the LORD and said, "Lord, why have You caused harm to this people? Why is it that You sent me?

**5:23** From the time I came to Pharaoh to speak in Your name, he has caused harm to this people, and You have not rescued Your people at all."

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — The verb "has met" (niqra) is a Niphal perfect spelled with aleph for the III-He root qarah ("meet, encounter"), referring to a divine encounter.
- **5:12** — The Hebrew pairs a Poel infinitive construct with a cognate noun, leqoshesh qash ("to gather stubble"), vivid wordplay highlighting the Israelites' desperate scavenging.
- **5:16** — The Masoretic vocalization wechatat is a rare third-person feminine singular Qal perfect of chata ("sin, be at fault") with "your people" (ammeka) as subject, indicating that Pharaoh's own Egyptian officials are to blame rather than the Israelite laborers.
- **5:23** — Hebrew pairs the Hiphil infinitive absolute with the negated finite verb (hatstsel lo-hitstsalta, from natsal) for strong emphasis ("not rescued at all"), while the Hiphil of raa ("caused harm") echoes Moses's question to the LORD in v. 22.
