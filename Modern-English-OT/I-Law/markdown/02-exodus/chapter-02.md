## Text

**2:1** And a man from the house of Levi went and took a daughter of Levi.

**2:2** And the woman conceived and gave birth to a son; and she saw that he was good, so she hid him for three months.

**2:3** And when she could no longer hide him, she took a basket of papyrus for him and coated it with bitumen and pitch; she placed the child in it and placed it among the reeds along the bank of the Nile.

**2:4** And his sister stationed herself at a distance to learn what would be done to him.

**2:5** Then Pharaoh's daughter went down to bathe at the Nile, while her young women were walking alongside the Nile; and she saw the basket in the midst of the reeds, and sent her maidservant, who took it.

**2:6** She opened it and saw the child, and look, a boy was crying; and she felt compassion for him and said, "This is one of the children of the Hebrews."

**2:7** Then his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Should I go and call for you a nursing woman from the Hebrew women, so she can nurse the child for you?"

**2:8** Pharaoh's daughter told her, "Go." So the young woman went and called the mother of the child.

**2:9** Then Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child with you and nurse him for me, and I myself will pay your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

**2:10** And the child grew, and she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became a son to her. And she called his name Moses, and she said, "Because I drew him from the water."

**2:11** And it happened in those days, after Moses had grown up, that he went out to his brothers and looked upon their burdens; and he saw an Egyptian man striking a Hebrew man from among his brothers.

**2:12** So he turned this way and that, and when he saw that no one was there, he struck the Egyptian down and hid him in the sand.

**2:13** He went out on the second day, and look, two Hebrew men were fighting together; and he said to the wrongdoer, "Why are you striking your neighbor?"

**2:14** And he replied, "Who set you as a ruler and a judge over us? Do you intend to kill me just as you killed the Egyptian?" So Moses was afraid and said, "Indeed, the matter is known."

**2:15** When Pharaoh heard about this matter, he sought to kill Moses; yet Moses fled from Pharaoh and settled in the land of Midian, and he sat down by the well.

**2:16** And the priest of Midian had seven daughters; they came, drew water, and filled the troughs in order to water the flock of their father.

**2:17** Yet the shepherds came and drove them off; so Moses rose up and saved them, and he watered their flock.

**2:18** And they came to their father Reuel, and he said, "Why have you come so quickly today?"

**2:19** And they replied, "An Egyptian man rescued us from the hand of the shepherds; moreover, he actually drew water for us and watered the flock."

**2:20** And he said to his daughters, "And where is he? Why then did you leave the man? Call for him so that he may eat bread."

**2:21** And Moses consented to live with the man, and he gave Zipporah his daughter to Moses.

**2:22** And she gave birth to a son, and he called his name Gershom, because he said, "I have become a sojourner in a foreign land."

**2:23** And it happened during those many days that the king of Egypt died; and the sons of Israel groaned because of the servitude and cried out, and their cry for help went up to God from the servitude.

**2:24** So God heard their moaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.

**2:25** And God saw the sons of Israel, and God knew.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Hebrew ki-tov hu ("that he was good") uses the same wording as Genesis 1:4, conveying wholeness, beauty, and delight.
- **2:3** — Hebrew tevah ("basket," an Egyptian loanword meaning chest or box) is the same word used for Noah's ark in Genesis 6-9.
- **2:10** — The name Moses (Mosheh) is linked by wordplay to the Hebrew verb mashah ("draw out"), while also corresponding to the Egyptian element meaning "child" or "born of."
- **2:22** — The name Gershom plays on the Hebrew words ger ("sojourner") and sham ("there").
- **2:25** — Hebrew wayyeda elohim ("and God knew") uses the verb yada absolutely without a stated object, expressing deep personal awareness and concern.
