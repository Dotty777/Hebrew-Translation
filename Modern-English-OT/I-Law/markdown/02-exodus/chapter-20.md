## Text

**20:1** Now God spoke all of these words, saying:

**20:2** "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out from the land of Egypt, from the house of slaves.

**20:3** You must not have other gods before My face.

**20:4** You must not make for yourself a carved image or any form of what is in the heavens above, or what is on the earth beneath, or what is in the waters below the earth.

**20:5** You must not bow down to them nor serve them, for I am the LORD your God, a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of fathers upon children, to the third and fourth generations of those who hate Me,

**20:6** yet showing loyal love to thousands, to those who love Me and to those who keep My commands.

**20:7** You must not take up the name of the LORD your God in vain, for the LORD will not acquit the one who takes up His name in vain.

**20:8** Remember the Sabbath day, to set it apart as holy.

**20:9** For six days you are to labor and do all your work,

**20:10** but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the LORD your God. You must not do any work, neither you, nor your son or your daughter, your male slave or your female slave, your livestock, nor your resident foreigner who is within your gates.

**20:11** For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, the sea and everything that is in them, and He rested on the seventh day. For this reason, the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and set it apart as holy.

**20:12** Honor your father and your mother, so that your days may lengthen upon the land that the LORD your God is giving to you.

**20:13** You must not commit adultery.

**20:14** You must not steal.

**20:15** You must not murder.

**20:16** You must not testify as a false witness against your neighbor.

**20:17** You must not covet your neighbor's house; you must not covet your neighbor's wife, nor his male slave or his female slave, nor his ox or his donkey, nor anything belonging to your neighbor."

**20:18** Now all the people were seeing the thunder, the lightning flashes, the sound of the ram's horn, and the mountain smoking. When the people saw it, they shook and stood far off.

**20:19** And they said to Moses, "You speak with us, and we will listen; but do not let God speak with us, or else we will die."

**20:20** Then Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid, for God has come to test you, and so that the fear of Him will be before your faces, in order that you do not sin."

**20:21** So the people stood far off, and Moses approached the thick darkness where God was.

**20:22** Then the LORD said to Moses, "This is what you are to say to the sons of Israel: 'You yourselves have seen that I have spoken with you from the heavens.

**20:23** You must not make gods alongside Me; gods of silver or gods of gold you must not make for yourselves.

**20:24** You are to make for Me an altar of earth and sacrifice upon it your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your flock and your herd. At every place where I make My name remembered, I will come to you and bless you.

**20:25** And if you make an altar of stones for Me, you must not build it out of cut stones, for if you swing your chisel against it, you will profane it.

**20:26** And you must not climb up by steps onto My altar, so that your nakedness will not be uncovered upon it.'"

## Translator Notes

- **20:13** — Native Masoretic order arranges these three prohibitions as murder (20:13), adultery (20:14), and theft (20:15). In the Saints.Bible parallel display, they map to slots 20:15 (murder), 20:13 (adultery), and 20:14 (theft).
- **20:18** — Hebrew literally says the people were seeing (Qal participle ro'im) both visible phenomena and sounds, using "see" in a broad sense of sensory perception; likewise the Masoretic verb wayyar' ("and saw") is followed rather than the Septuagint reading "feared."
- **20:23** — The Masoretic pause (atnah) falls after "alongside Me" (itti), creating an elliptical opening clause whose implied object ("gods") is supplied from the following "gods of silver or gods of gold."
- **20:25** — Hebrew cherev ordinarily means "sword," but here denotes a metal stone-cutting tool or chisel; the Hiphil perfect henafta ("swing") with the following Piel wayyiqtol ("profane") expresses a conditional consequence.
