## Text

**18:1** Now Jethro, priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law, heard of everything God had done for Moses and for Israel His people, that the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.

**18:2** So Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, took Zipporah, Moses' wife, after she had been sent away,

**18:3** along with her two sons; the name of the one was Gershom, for he said, "I have been a sojourner in a foreign land,"

**18:4** and the name of the other was Eliezer, "For the God of my father was my help, and He rescued me from the sword of Pharaoh."

**18:5** Then Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, came with his sons and his wife to Moses into the wilderness where he was camped, at the mountain of God.

**18:6** And he said to Moses, "I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you, and your wife and her two sons with her."

**18:7** So Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, and he bowed down and kissed him; they asked each other about their well-being, and then they went into the tent.

**18:8** Then Moses recounted to his father-in-law all that the LORD had done to Pharaoh and to Egypt on account of Israel, all the hardship that had befallen them along the way, and how the LORD had rescued them.

**18:9** And Jethro rejoiced over all the good that the LORD had done for Israel, whom He had rescued from the hand of Egypt.

**18:10** And Jethro said, "Blessed be the LORD, who rescued you from the hand of Egypt and from the hand of Pharaoh, who rescued the people from under the hand of Egypt.

**18:11** Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods; indeed, in the matter in which they acted arrogantly against them."

**18:12** Then Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, brought a burnt offering and sacrifices for God; and Aaron came with all the elders of Israel to eat bread with Moses' father-in-law before God.

**18:13** And on the next day, Moses sat to judge the people, while the people stood around Moses from morning until evening.

**18:14** When Moses' father-in-law saw all that he was doing for the people, he said, "What is this thing that you are doing for the people? Why are you sitting alone while all the people stand around you from morning until evening?"

**18:15** Moses said to his father-in-law, "Because the people come to me to inquire of God.

**18:16** Whenever a matter arises for them, it comes to me, and I judge between a man and his neighbor, and I make known the statutes of God and His instructions."

**18:17** Moses' father-in-law said to him, "The thing you are doing is not good.

**18:18** You will surely wear out, both you and this people who are with you, for the matter is too heavy for you; you cannot do it by yourself.

**18:19** Now listen to my voice; I will advise you, and may God be with you. You act on behalf of the people before God, and you bring the matters to God.

**18:20** And you shall teach them the statutes and the instructions, and make known to them the way in which they should walk and the work that they should do.

**18:21** Moreover, you shall look out from all the people capable men who fear God, men of truth who hate dishonest gain, and appoint them over the people as officials of thousands, officials of hundreds, officials of fifties, and officials of tens.

**18:22** They shall judge the people at all times; every major matter they shall bring to you, while every minor matter they themselves shall judge. So make it lighter for yourself, and let them bear it with you.

**18:23** If you do this thing, and God commands you so, then you will be able to endure, and all this people too will go to their place in peace."

**18:24** Then Moses heeded the voice of his father-in-law and did everything that he had said.

**18:25** Moses chose capable men from all Israel and appointed them as heads over the people: officials of thousands, officials of hundreds, officials of fifties, and officials of tens.

**18:26** And they judged the people at all times; the difficult matter they would bring to Moses, while every minor matter they themselves would judge.

**18:27** Then Moses sent his father-in-law off, and he went his way to his own land.

## Translator Notes

- **18:2** — The phrase 'after she had been sent away' translates achar shillucheiha, from the plural verbal noun shilluchim (related to Piel shalach, 'to send away'), which leaves unspecified when or why Moses had sent Zipporah back to Midian.
- **18:6** — The Masoretic Text literally reads 'And he said to Moses,' expressing idiomatically that Jethro sent word ahead to Moses before Moses went out to meet him in v. 7.
- **18:11** — The final clause is syntactically elliptical in Hebrew (literally 'for in the matter in which they acted arrogantly against them'), implying that the LORD proved His supremacy over Egypt precisely in the arrogance they displayed against Israel.
- **18:20** — The verb 'teach' translates Hiphil zahar (vehizhartah), which normally carries the stronger sense of 'warn' or 'admonish' and here denotes careful, solemn instruction in God's statutes and instructions.
