## Text

**11:1** Now the LORD said to Moses, "One more plague I will bring upon Pharaoh and upon Egypt; after that he will let you go from here. When he lets you go completely, he will surely drive you out from here.

**11:2** Speak now in the ears of the people, so that each man may ask his neighbor and each woman her neighbor for articles of silver and articles of gold."

**11:3** And the LORD gave the people favor in the eyes of the Egyptians. Moreover, the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the eyes of Pharaoh's servants and in the eyes of the people.

**11:4** And Moses said, "This is what the LORD says: 'About midnight I am going out into the midst of Egypt,

**11:5** and every firstborn in the land of Egypt will die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits on his throne to the firstborn of the female servant who is behind the millstones, and every firstborn of the animals.

**11:6** And there will be a great outcry in all the land of Egypt, the like of which has never happened before, and the like of which will never happen again.

**11:7** But against any of the sons of Israel not a dog will sharpen its tongue, from man to animal, so that you may know that the LORD makes a distinction between Egypt and Israel.'

**11:8** And all these servants of yours will come down to me and bow down to me, saying, 'Go out, you and all the people who follow at your feet.' And after that I will go out." Then he went out from Pharaoh in burning anger.

**11:9** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Pharaoh will not listen to you, so that My wonders may multiply in the land of Egypt."

**11:10** Now Moses and Aaron performed all these wonders before Pharaoh, yet the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not let the sons of Israel go from his land.

## Translator Notes

- **11:1** — The adverbial noun כָּלָה ("completely/altogether") may modify either "when he lets you go" or the following emphatic Piel infinitive absolute construction גָּרֵשׁ יְגָרֵשׁ ("he will surely drive out").
- **11:6** — In the Masoretic Text, the masculine singular suffix in כָּמֹהוּ ("the like of it," twice) refers back to the grammatically feminine noun צְעָקָה ("outcry"), while the verbs נִהְיָתָה (Niphal) and תֹסִף (Hiphil) remain feminine singular.
- **11:7** — The idiom לֹא יֶחֱרַץ־כֶּלֶב לְשֹׁנוֹ (literally "not a dog will sharpen its tongue") expresses total absence of hostility, meaning not even a dog will snarl or bark against them (compare Josh 10:21).
- **11:8** — The phrase אֲשֶׁר־בְּרַגְלֶיךָ (literally "who are at your feet") is a Hebrew idiom for followers under someone's leadership. The direct quotation of the LORD's words ends at verse 7, with verse 8 resuming Moses' own address to Pharaoh.
