## Text

**1:1** And these are the names of the sons of Israel who came into Egypt with Jacob; each man and his household came:

**1:2** Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah;

**1:3** Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin;

**1:4** Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.

**1:5** And all the persons who came out of Jacob's loins were seventy persons, while Joseph was already in Egypt.

**1:6** And Joseph died, along with all his brothers and all of that generation.

**1:7** Yet the sons of Israel were fruitful and teemed, and they multiplied and grew immensely mighty, so that the land became filled with them.

**1:8** Then a new king rose over Egypt, one who did not know Joseph.

**1:9** And he said to his people, "Look, the people of the sons of Israel are more numerous and mightier than we are.

**1:10** Come, let us act shrewdly toward them, or else they will multiply, and should war occur, they too will join those who hate us, fight against us, and go up from the land."

**1:11** So they placed overseers of forced labor over them in order to afflict them with their burdens; and they built storage cities for Pharaoh, Pithom and Rameses.

**1:12** Yet the more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and the more they spread; and they felt dread because of the sons of Israel.

**1:13** And the Egyptians forced the sons of Israel to labor with brutality.

**1:14** They made their lives bitter with severe labor, with mortar and bricks and with every kind of labor in the field—all their labor that they exacted from them with brutality.

**1:15** Then the king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives, of whom the name of the one was Shiphrah and the name of the second was Puah.

**1:16** And he said, "When you assist the Hebrew women in childbirth and look upon the birthstool, if it is a son, put him to death; and if it is a daughter, let her live."

**1:17** Yet the midwives feared God and did not do as the king of Egypt had spoken to them; instead, they kept the boys alive.

**1:18** So the king of Egypt summoned the midwives and said to them, "Why have you done this thing and kept the boys alive?"

**1:19** And the midwives said to Pharaoh, "Because the Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women; for they are vigorous—before the midwife comes to them, they have given birth."

**1:20** And God did good to the midwives, and the people multiplied and grew very mighty.

**1:21** And because the midwives feared God, He made households for them.

**1:22** Then Pharaoh commanded all his people, saying, "Every son who is born, you shall throw into the Nile, yet every daughter you shall keep alive."

## Translator Notes

- **1:7** — Hebrew wayyishretsu (Qal of sh-r-ts) literally means "teemed" or "swarmed," echoing the abundant multiplication of living creatures in Genesis 1 and 9:7.
- **1:14** — The Hebrew construction avdu bahem (Qal of avad with the preposition b-) is a technical idiom meaning to exact forced labor from someone, paired here and in v. 13 with bepharekh, "with brutality" or "ruthlessly."
- **1:16** — Hebrew ha'ovnayim is a dual noun literally meaning "the two stones," referring to the birthing stones or birthstool on which a woman crouched during delivery.
- **1:19** — Hebrew chayot ("vigorous" or "lively") plays on the verb chayah ("live" or "keep alive") repeated throughout vv. 16-22.
- **1:22** — Following the Masoretic Text strictly, Pharaoh says "Every son who is born," without the added phrase "to the Hebrews" found in the Samaritan Pentateuch and Septuagint.
