## Text

**5:1** Then I turned and lifted up my eyes, and looked, and behold, a flying scroll.

**5:2** And he said to me, "What do you see?" So I answered, "I see a flying scroll; its length is twenty cubits and its breadth is ten cubits."

**5:3** Then he said to me, "This is the curse that goes forth over the face of the whole earth: for every thief shall be purged away according to this side of it, and every swearer shall be purged away according to that side of it.

**5:4** 'I have sent it forth,' says the LORD of hosts, 'and it shall enter the house of the thief and the house of the one who swears falsely by My name. It shall abide in the midst of his house and consume it, both its timber and its stones.'"

**5:5** Then the angel who spoke with me came forward and said to me, "Lift up your eyes now, and see what this is that goes forth."

**5:6** And I said, "What is it?" And he said, "This is the ephah that goes forth." He also said, "This is their appearance throughout all the earth."

**5:7** And behold, a round disk of lead was lifted up, and there was a woman sitting in the midst of the ephah.

**5:8** Then he said, "This is Wickedness!" And he thrust her down into the midst of the ephah, and cast the leaden weight upon its mouth.

**5:9** Then I lifted up my eyes and looked, and behold, two women came forth with the wind in their wings, for they had wings like the wings of a stork; and they lifted up the ephah between the earth and the heavens.

**5:10** So I said to the angel who spoke with me, "Where are they carrying the ephah?"

**5:11** And he said to me, "To build a house for it in the land of Shinar; and when it is prepared, it shall be set down there upon its own base."

## Translator Notes

- **5:3** — "Purged away" translates a Hebrew verb meaning to be cleaned out or swept away in judgment according to the curse inscribed on both sides of the scroll.
- **5:6** — An ephah was a dry-measure basket holding roughly half a bushel. "Their appearance" literally reads "their eye" in the Masoretic Text, denoting likeness or aspect, whereas some ancient versions read "their iniquity."
- **5:11** — Shinar is the ancient biblical name for the plain of Babylonia.
