## Text

**2:1** Now the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah, and Jonah remained within the belly of the fish three days and three nights.

**2:2** Then Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from within the belly of the fish.

**2:3** And he said, "Out of my distress I called to the LORD, and He answered me; from the belly of Sheol I cried for help, and You heard my voice.

**2:4** For You cast me into the deep, into the heart of the seas, and the flood encompassed me; all Your breakers and Your billows swept over me.

**2:5** And I said, 'I have been driven away from before Your eyes,' yet I shall again gaze toward Your holy temple.

**2:6** The waters closed in upon me, even to my soul; the deep surrounded me; seaweed was bound about my head.

**2:7** To the bases of the mountains I descended; the earth with its bars closed behind me forever. Yet You brought up my life from the pit, O LORD my God.

**2:8** When my soul grew faint within me, I remembered the LORD; and my prayer reached You, into Your holy temple.

**2:9** Those who cling to vain idols forsake their own lovingkindness.

**2:10** But I, with the voice of thanksgiving, will sacrifice to You; what I have vowed I will fulfill. Salvation belongs to the LORD!"

**2:11** Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — In the Masoretic Hebrew text, chapter 2 begins here; most English versions number this verse as 1:17 and number Hebrew 2:2-11 as 2:1-10. The verb 'appointed' recurs in chapter 4 for the plant, the worm, and the east wind.
- **2:2** — Here the Hebrew briefly shifts from the masculine noun for fish (2:1, 2:11) to the feminine form.
- **2:6** — The Hebrew noun rendered 'soul' can also denote the throat or neck, conveying both waters rising to the throat and peril to life itself.
- **2:9** — The phrase 'their own lovingkindness' refers either to the covenant mercy of God that idolaters forfeit or to the loyal devotion they owe to Him.
