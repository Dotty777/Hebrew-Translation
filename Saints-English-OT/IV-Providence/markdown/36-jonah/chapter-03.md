## Text

**3:1** And the word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time, saying,

**3:2** "Arise, go to Nineveh, the great city, and proclaim to it the proclamation that I speak to you."

**3:3** So Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD. Now Nineveh was a great city before God, a journey of three days.

**3:4** And Jonah began to enter the city a journey of one day, and he cried out and said, "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!"

**3:5** And the men of Nineveh believed God; they proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them.

**3:6** When the word reached the king of Nineveh, he arose from his throne, laid aside his robe, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat upon ashes.

**3:7** And he issued a proclamation and announced in Nineveh, by the decree of the king and his nobles, saying, "Let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock, taste anything; let them neither graze nor drink water.

**3:8** Rather, let both man and beast be covered with sackcloth, and let them cry out mightily to God; let each one turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in their hands.

**3:9** Who knows? God may turn and relent, and turn away from His fierce wrath, so that we may not perish."

**3:10** Then God saw their deeds, that they turned from their evil way; and God relented concerning the calamity that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — The Hebrew phrase 'a great city before God' (literally 'great to God') serves as a superlative for extraordinary size while also suggesting Nineveh's importance in God's sight.
- **3:4** — The Hebrew participle translated 'shall be overthrown' commonly denotes sudden destruction, as of Sodom, yet it also carries the sense of being turned around or transformed.
- **3:10** — The Hebrew verb 'relented' (also in 3:9) expresses God's compassionate turning back from threatened judgment in response to Nineveh's turning from its evil way.
