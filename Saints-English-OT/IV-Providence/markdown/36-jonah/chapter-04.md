## Text

**4:1** Yet it was exceedingly displeasing to Jonah, and he burned with anger.

**4:2** Then he prayed to the LORD and said, "O LORD, I pray, was this not my word while I was still in my own land? Therefore I made haste to flee to Tarshish, for I knew that You are a gracious and merciful God, slow to anger and great in lovingkindness, who relents from bringing calamity.

**4:3** And now, O LORD, please take my life from me, for my death is better than my life."

**4:4** And the LORD said, "Is it well for you to be angry?"

**4:5** Then Jonah went out from the city and sat to the east of the city; and he made a booth for himself there and sat beneath it in the shade, until he should see what would happen in the city.

**4:6** And the LORD God appointed a plant, and it grew up over Jonah to be a shade over his head, to deliver him from his distress. And Jonah rejoiced over the plant with great joy.

**4:7** But God appointed a worm at the rising of the dawn on the next day, and it struck the plant so that it withered.

**4:8** And it came to pass, when the sun rose, that God appointed a scorching east wind; and the sun struck upon Jonah's head so that he grew faint. And he asked for himself that he might die, and said, "My death is better than my life."

**4:9** Then God said to Jonah, "Is it well for you to be angry over the plant?" And he said, "It is well for me to be angry, even to death!"

**4:10** And the LORD said, "You have had compassion on the plant for which you did not labor, nor did you make it grow, which came to be in a night and perished in a night.

**4:11** And should I not have compassion on Nineveh, the great city, in which are more than one hundred and twenty thousand people who do not know their right hand from their left, and much livestock?"

## Translator Notes

- **4:4** — The Hebrew idiom combines the infinitive absolute meaning doing well or rightly with the expression for burning anger, questioning whether Jonah has any right to be angry, and is echoed verbatim in 4:9.
- **4:6** — The Hebrew word qiqayon appears only in Jonah 4 and denotes a fast-growing, broad-leaved plant, commonly identified as the castor-oil plant or a gourd vine.
- **4:8** — The rare Hebrew adjective charishit occurs only here and is understood to describe a scorching, sultry, or fierce east wind.
- **4:10** — Literally "son of a night," a Hebrew idiom expressing that the plant came into existence in a single night and perished just as rapidly.
