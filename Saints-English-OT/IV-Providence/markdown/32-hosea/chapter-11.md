## Text

**11:1** When Israel was a youth, then I loved him, and out of Egypt I called My son.

**11:2** As they called to them, so they went away from them; they sacrificed to the Baals and burned incense to carved images.

**11:3** Yet I Myself taught Ephraim to walk; He took them up in His arms, yet they did not know that I healed them.

**11:4** I drew them with cords of a man, with bands of love; and I was to them as those who lift the yoke from their jaws, and I bent down to him and fed him.

**11:5** He shall not return to the land of Egypt, but Assyria shall be his king, because they refused to return.

**11:6** And the sword shall whirl through his cities, and shall consume his gate bars and devour them because of their own counsels.

**11:7** My people are bent on turning away from Me. Though they call them to the Most High, none at all will exalt Him.

**11:8** How can I give you up, O Ephraim? How can I hand you over, O Israel? How can I make you like Admah? How can I set you like Zeboiim? My heart turns within Me; all My compassions are kindled together.

**11:9** I will not execute the fierceness of My anger; I will not return to destroy Ephraim. For I am God, and not man, the Holy One in your midst; and I will not come in wrath.

**11:10** They shall walk after the LORD; He will roar like a lion. When He roars, then the children shall come trembling from the west.

**11:11** They shall come trembling like a bird from Egypt, and like a dove from the land of Assyria; and I will cause them to dwell in their houses, says the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **11:2** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads third-person plural, they called to them and from them, referring to the prophets whom the LORD sent to summon Israel.
- **11:3** — The Masoretic text shifts briefly from first person to third person in the middle clause, He took them up in His arms, before returning to first person.
- **11:7** — The difficult Hebrew phrase el-al is understood here as to the Most High, though it may also mean upward.
- **11:9** — The Hebrew be'ir can be rendered either in wrath and terror or into the city.
