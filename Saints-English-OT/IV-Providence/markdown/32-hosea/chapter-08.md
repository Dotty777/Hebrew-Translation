## Text

**8:1** Put the trumpet to your mouth! One swoops like an eagle upon the house of the LORD, because they have transgressed My covenant and rebelled against My law.

**8:2** To Me they cry out, "My God, we, Israel, know You!"

**8:3** Israel has cast off what is good; an enemy shall pursue him.

**8:4** They set up kings, yet not from Me; they appointed princes, yet I did not know it. From their silver and their gold they made idols for themselves, that they might be cut off.

**8:5** Your calf is spurned, O Samaria! My anger burns against them; how long shall they be incapable of innocence?

**8:6** For it is from Israel; a craftsman made it, and it is not God. Surely the calf of Samaria shall be reduced to splinters.

**8:7** For they sow the wind, and they shall reap the whirlwind. It has no standing grain; the sprout yields no meal; and even if it should yield, strangers shall swallow it up.

**8:8** Israel is swallowed up; now they have become among the nations like a vessel in which there is no delight.

**8:9** For they have gone up to Assyria, a wild donkey wandering alone by itself; Ephraim has hired lovers.

**8:10** Though they hire allies among the nations, now I will gather them together; and they shall writhe for a little while under the burden of the king of princes.

**8:11** Because Ephraim has multiplied altars for sinning, they have become to him altars for sinning.

**8:12** Were I to write for him the myriads of My law, they are counted as a strange thing.

**8:13** As for the sacrifices of My offerings, they sacrifice flesh and eat it, yet the LORD does not accept them. Now He will remember their iniquity and punish their sins; they shall return to Egypt.

**8:14** For Israel has forgotten his Maker and built palaces, and Judah has multiplied fortified cities; yet I will send fire upon his cities, and it shall devour her citadels.

## Translator Notes

- **8:10** — The phrase "writhe for a little while" may also be read "begin to diminish"; "king of princes" reflects the Assyrian monarch's boastful royal title (cf. Isaiah 10:8).
- **8:12** — Translates the Masoretic qere readings ekhtav-lo ("I write for him") and rubbei ("myriads of"); the ketiv spells the verb plene and reads the singular rubbo ("a myriad").
- **8:13** — "My offerings" renders a rare reduplicated Hebrew noun derived from the root "to give," referring to sacrificial gifts brought to the sanctuary.
