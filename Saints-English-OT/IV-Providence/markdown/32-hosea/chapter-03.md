## Text

**3:1** Then the LORD said to me, "Go again, love a woman who is loved by a lover and is an adulteress, even as the LORD loves the children of Israel, though they turn to other gods and love raisin cakes."

**3:2** So I bought her for myself for fifteen shekels of silver, and a homer of barley and a lethek of barley.

**3:3** And I said to her, "You shall abide for me many days; you shall not play the harlot, nor shall you belong to a man; and so shall I also be toward you."

**3:4** For the children of Israel shall abide many days without king or prince, without sacrifice or sacred pillar, without ephod or teraphim.

**3:5** Afterward the children of Israel shall return and seek the LORD their God and David their king, and they shall come trembling to the LORD and to His goodness in the latter days.

## Translator Notes

- **3:2** — The Hebrew literally reads "fifteen of silver" (standard idiom for shekels). A homer was a large dry measure of roughly six bushels, and a lethek was half a homer.
- **3:4** — An ephod was a priestly vestment associated with seeking divine guidance, and teraphim were household idols or divinatory images.
- **3:5** — The Hebrew verb rendered "come trembling to" literally means "fear toward," expressing eager, reverent awe before the LORD.
