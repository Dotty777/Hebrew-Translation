## Text

**7:1** Woe to me! For I have become like the gatherings of summer fruit, like the gleanings of the vintage; there is no cluster to eat, nor a first-ripe fig that my soul craves.

**7:2** The godly man has perished from the land, and there is no upright person among mankind; all of them lie in wait for bloodshed, and every man hunts his brother with a net.

**7:3** Both hands are upon evil, to do it skillfully: the prince makes his demand, and the judge asks for a reward; the great man speaks the wicked desire of his soul, and thus they weave it together.

**7:4** The best of them is like a brier; the most upright is worse than a thorn hedge. The day of your watchmen, your visitation, has come; now shall be their confusion.

**7:5** Do not trust in a neighbor; do not put confidence in a close friend. Guard the doors of your mouth from her who lies in your bosom.

**7:6** For a son treats his father with contempt, a daughter rises up against her mother, a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law; a man's enemies are the men of his own house.

**7:7** But as for me, I will watch for the LORD; I will wait for the God of my salvation; my God will hear me.

**7:8** Do not rejoice over me, O my enemy; though I have fallen, I shall rise; though I sit in darkness, the LORD is a light to me.

**7:9** I will endure the indignation of the LORD, for I have sinned against Him, until He pleads my cause and executes justice on my behalf; He will bring me out into the light, and I shall behold His righteousness.

**7:10** Then my enemy shall see it, and shame shall cover her who said to me, "Where is the LORD your God?" My eyes shall look upon her; now she shall be trampled down like the mire of the streets.

**7:11** A day for building your walls—on that day the boundary shall be extended far.

**7:12** On that day they shall come to you from Assyria and the cities of Egypt, and from Egypt even to the River, from sea to sea and from mountain to mountain.

**7:13** Yet the earth shall become a desolation because of those who dwell in it, on account of the fruit of their deeds.

**7:14** Shepherd Your people with Your staff, the flock of Your inheritance, who dwell alone in a forest in the midst of Carmel; let them pasture in Bashan and Gilead, as in days of old.

**7:15** As in the days of your coming out from the land of Egypt, I will show him wondrous things.

**7:16** The nations shall see and be ashamed because of all their might; they shall lay their hand upon their mouth, and their ears shall grow deaf.

**7:17** They shall lick the dust like a serpent; like crawling things of the earth they shall come trembling out of their strongholds; they shall turn in dread to the LORD our God, and they shall be afraid of You.

**7:18** Who is a God like You, forgiving iniquity and passing over transgression for the remnant of His inheritance? He does not keep His anger forever, because He delights in lovingkindness.

**7:19** He will again have compassion on us; He will tread our iniquities underfoot, and You will cast all their sins into the depths of the sea.

**7:20** You will grant faithfulness to Jacob and lovingkindness to Abraham, which You swore to our fathers from the days of old.

## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — The Hebrew infinitive literally means to do well or skillfully, used here ironically of practiced wrongdoing; the final verb depicts corrupt leaders weaving their schemes together like a braided cord.
- **7:11** — The Hebrew noun rendered boundary can also mean decree or statute, yielding the alternate sense that an oppressive decree will be far removed.
- **7:12** — The Hebrew term matsor, literally fortress or stronghold, serves here as a poetic name for Egypt, paired with Assyria and the River, meaning the Euphrates.
- **7:18** — The opening question, Who is a God like You, echoes the prophet's own name, Micah, which means Who is like the LORD.
- **7:19** — The Masoretic Hebrew shifts from first-person plural us and our to third-person plural their sins, referring to the remnant of God's inheritance mentioned in the preceding verse.
