## Text

**1:1** The burden concerning Nineveh: the book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite.

**1:2** A jealous and avenging God is the LORD; the LORD avenges and is filled with wrath. The LORD takes vengeance on His adversaries, and He keeps wrath for His enemies.

**1:3** The LORD is slow to anger and great in might, and He will by no means leave the guilty unpunished. The LORD has His way in the whirlwind and in the tempest, and the clouds are the dust of His feet.

**1:4** He rebukes the sea and makes it dry, and He parches all the rivers; Bashan and Carmel wither, and the blossom of Lebanon languishes.

**1:5** The mountains quake before Him, and the hills melt away; the earth heaves at His presence, indeed, the world and all who dwell in it.

**1:6** Who can stand before His indignation, and who can endure the fierceness of His anger? His wrath is poured out like fire, and the rocks are shattered by Him.

**1:7** The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of distress; and He knows those who take refuge in Him.

**1:8** But with an overflowing flood He will make an utter end of her place, and He will pursue His enemies into darkness.

**1:9** What do you devise against the LORD? He will bring utter destruction; affliction shall not rise up a second time.

**1:10** For though they are entangled like thorns, and drenched as with their drink, they shall be devoured like stubble that is completely dry.

**1:11** From you has gone forth one who plots evil against the LORD, a counselor of wickedness.

**1:12** Thus says the LORD: "Though they are at full strength and likewise numerous, even so they shall be cut down, and he shall pass away. Though I have afflicted you, I will afflict you no more.

**1:13** And now I will break his yoke from upon you, and tear your bonds apart."

**1:14** And the LORD has issued a command concerning you: "No longer shall any of your name be sown; from the house of your gods I will cut off the carved image and the molten image. I will prepare your grave, for you are vile."

## Translator Notes

- **1:3** — The qere reads the construct form ugdol-koach, meaning great in might, whereas the ketiv reads the full spelling ugdol without construct shortening.
- **1:10** — The Hebrew wording is famously terse, picturing Assyria's defenders as hopelessly tangled like thorns and sodden with drink, yet instantly consumed like dry stubble.
