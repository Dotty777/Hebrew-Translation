## Text

**32:1** Now when the people saw that Moses delayed in coming down from the mountain, the people assembled around Aaron and said to him, "Arise, make for us gods that shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him."

**32:2** And Aaron said to them, "Take off the rings of gold that are in the ears of your wives, your sons, and your daughters, and bring them to me."

**32:3** So all the people took off the rings of gold that were in their ears and brought them to Aaron.

**32:4** And he received it from their hand, shaped it with a graving tool, and made it a molten calf. Then they said, "These are your gods, O Israel, that brought you up out of the land of Egypt!"

**32:5** When Aaron saw this, he built an altar before it. And Aaron made proclamation and said, "Tomorrow shall be a feast to the LORD."

**32:6** So they rose early on the following day, offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings; and the people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to revel.

**32:7** Then the LORD spoke to Moses, "Go, descend, for your people whom you brought up out of the land of Egypt have acted corruptly.

**32:8** They have quickly turned aside from the way that I commanded them. They have made for themselves a molten calf, bowed down to it, sacrificed to it, and said, 'These are your gods, O Israel, that brought you up out of the land of Egypt!'

**32:10** Now therefore, leave Me alone, that My wrath may burn hot against them and that I may consume them; and I will make of you a great nation."

**32:11** Then Moses implored the favor of the LORD his God and said, "Why, O LORD, does Your wrath burn hot against Your people, whom You brought forth out of the land of Egypt with great power and with a mighty hand?

**32:12** Why should the Egyptians speak, saying, 'With evil intent He brought them out, to slay them in the mountains and to consume them from the face of the earth'? Turn from Your fierce wrath, and relent from this calamity against Your people.

**32:13** Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, Your servants, to whom You swore by Yourself and spoke to them, 'I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven, and all this land of which I have spoken I will give to your seed, and they shall inherit it forever.'"

**32:14** So the LORD relented concerning the calamity that He had spoken of doing to His people.

**32:15** Then Moses turned and went down from the mountain, with the two tablets of the Testimony in his hand—tablets written on both their sides; on the one side and on the other they were written.

**32:16** Now the tablets were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, engraved upon the tablets.

**32:17** When Joshua heard the sound of the people as they shouted, he said to Moses, "There is a sound of war in the camp!"

**32:18** But he said, "It is not the sound of the shout of victory, nor the sound of the cry of defeat; it is the sound of singing that I hear."

**32:19** And it came to pass, as soon as he drew near to the camp, that he saw the calf and the dancing. Then Moses' wrath burned hot, and he cast the tablets out of his hands and shattered them at the foot of the mountain.

**32:20** Then he took the calf that they had made, burned it in the fire, and ground it until it was fine powder; and he scattered it upon the face of the water and made the children of Israel drink it.

**32:21** And Moses said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you, that you have brought upon them so great a sin?"

**32:22** So Aaron said, "Do not let the anger of my lord burn hot; you yourself know the people, that they are set on evil.

**32:23** For they said to me, 'Make for us gods that shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.'

**32:24** And I said to them, 'Whoever has any gold, take it off!' So they gave it to me, and I cast it into the fire, and out came this calf."

**32:25** Now Moses saw that the people were unrestrained, for Aaron had let them loose to become a derision among their adversaries.

**32:26** Then Moses stood at the gate of the camp and said, "Whoever is on the LORD's side, come to me!" And all the sons of Levi gathered together to him.

**32:27** And he said to them, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: 'Let every man fasten his sword upon his side, and go to and fro from gate to gate throughout the camp, and let every man slay his brother, every man his companion, and every man his neighbor.'"

**32:28** So the sons of Levi did according to the word of Moses, and about three thousand men of the people fell on that day.

**32:29** Then Moses said, "Consecrate yourselves today to the LORD, for each man has stood against his son and against his brother, that He may bestow a blessing upon you this day."

**32:30** Now it came to pass on the following day that Moses said to the people, "You have sinned a great sin. And now I will go up to the LORD; perhaps I may make atonement for your sin."

**32:31** Then Moses returned to the LORD and said, "Alas, this people has sinned a great sin, and has made for themselves gods of gold!

**32:32** Yet now, if You will forgive their sin—but if not, I pray, blot me out of Your book that You have written."

**32:33** And the LORD said to Moses, "Whoever has sinned against Me, him I will blot out of My book.

**32:34** Now therefore, go, lead the people to the place of which I have spoken to you. Behold, My angel shall go before you. Nevertheless, in the day of My visitation, I will visit their sin upon them."

**32:35** So the LORD struck the people with a plague, because they had made the calf that Aaron made.

## Translator Notes

- **32:4** — The Hebrew noun rendered "graving tool" denotes a chisel or stylus used to shape metal, though some interpreters understand the related root as binding the gold in a pouch or mold.
- **32:14** — The Hebrew verb translated "relented" expresses the LORD's compassionate turning back from executing a threatened judgment in response to Moses' intercession.
- **32:18** — Moses' reply employs a wordplay between the Qal infinitive meaning "shouting" (in victory or defeat) and the Piel infinitive of the same root meaning "singing" or "chanting."
- **32:29** — Literally "Fill your hand today to the LORD," the customary Hebrew idiom for priestly consecration and ordination.
- **32:32** — Moses' plea breaks off mid-sentence in a solemn Hebrew ellipsis ("if You will forgive their sin—") before stating his willingness to be blotted out.
