## Text

**2:1** Now a man of the house of Levi went and took as his wife a daughter of Levi.

**2:2** And the woman conceived and bore a son; and when she saw that he was a goodly child, she hid him three months.

**2:3** But when she could no longer hide him, she took for him an ark of bulrushes and coated it with bitumen and pitch; then she placed the child in it and set it among the reeds by the bank of the Nile.

**2:4** And his sister stationed herself at a distance, to learn what would be done to him.

**2:5** Then the daughter of Pharaoh came down to bathe at the Nile, while her maidens walked beside the Nile; and she saw the ark in the midst of the reeds, and she sent her maidservant to take it.

**2:6** And when she opened it, she saw the child, and behold, the boy was weeping. So she took pity on him and said, "This is one of the children of the Hebrews."

**2:7** Then his sister said to the daughter of Pharaoh, "Shall I go and call a nursing woman for you from among the Hebrew women, that she may nurse the child for you?"

**2:8** And the daughter of Pharaoh said to her, "Go." So the maiden went and called the child's mother.

**2:9** And the daughter of Pharaoh said to her, "Take this child away and nurse him for me, and I myself will give you your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

**2:10** And the child grew, and she brought him to the daughter of Pharaoh, and he became her son. And she called his name Moses, saying, "Because I drew him out of the water."

**2:11** Now it came to pass in those days, when Moses had grown up, that he went out to his brethren and looked upon their burdens; and he saw an Egyptian man striking a Hebrew man, one of his brethren.

**2:12** So he turned this way and that, and when he saw that there was no one, he struck down the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.

**2:13** And he went out on the second day, and behold, two Hebrew men were quarreling together; and he said to the one who was in the wrong, "Why do you strike your companion?"

**2:14** And he answered, "Who appointed you a prince and a judge over us? Do you intend to slay me as you slew the Egyptian?" Then Moses was afraid and said, "Surely this matter has become known!"

**2:15** When Pharaoh heard of this matter, he sought to slay Moses; but Moses fled from the presence of Pharaoh and dwelt in the land of Midian, and he sat down by a well.

**2:16** Now the priest of Midian had seven daughters; and they came and drew water, and filled the troughs to water their father's flock.

**2:17** Then the shepherds came and drove them away; but Moses rose up and rescued them, and watered their flock.

**2:18** When they came to Reuel their father, he said, "Why have you returned so soon today?"

**2:19** And they answered, "An Egyptian man delivered us from the hand of the shepherds; moreover, he even drew water for us and watered the flock."

**2:20** So he said to his daughters, "And where is he? Why is it that you have left the man behind? Call him, that he may eat bread."

**2:21** And Moses consented to dwell with the man, and he gave Zipporah his daughter to Moses.

**2:22** And she bore a son, and he called his name Gershom; for he said, "I have been a sojourner in a foreign land."

**2:23** Now it came to pass during those many days that the king of Egypt died; and the sons of Israel groaned because of the bondage and cried out, and their cry for help ascended to God because of the bondage.

**2:24** So God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.

**2:25** And God beheld the sons of Israel, and God knew.

## Translator Notes

- **2:3** — "Ark" renders the same Hebrew word used for Noah's ark in Genesis 6-9, highlighting Moses's rescue through the waters as an act of divine preservation.
- **2:10** — The name Moses resembles the Hebrew verb meaning "to draw out," while also echoing an Egyptian element meaning "child" or "born of."
- **2:25** — The Hebrew ends starkly with "and God knew," expressing God's intimate concern for Israel's plight and His readiness to act.
