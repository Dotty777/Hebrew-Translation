## Text

**36:1** And Bezalel and Oholiab, and every wise-hearted man in whom the LORD has placed wisdom and understanding to know how to perform all the work of the service of the sanctuary, shall work according to all that the LORD has commanded.

**36:2** Then Moses summoned Bezalel and Oholiab, and every wise-hearted man in whose heart the LORD had put wisdom, everyone whose heart moved him to come near to the work to perform it.

**36:3** And they received from before Moses all the offering that the children of Israel had brought for the work of the service of the sanctuary, to perform it. Still they kept bringing to him freewill offerings morning by morning.

**36:4** Then all the skilled men who were performing all the work of the sanctuary came, each man from the work that they were doing.

**36:5** And they spoke to Moses, saying, "The people are bringing much more than enough for the service of the work that the LORD has commanded to be done."

**36:6** So Moses gave a command, and they sent a proclamation throughout the camp, saying, "Let neither man nor woman prepare any more work for the offering of the sanctuary." Thus the people were restrained from bringing.

**36:7** For the material was sufficient for them for all the work to do it, and even to spare.

**36:8** And of the blue, purple, and scarlet yarn they made finely woven garments for ministering in the sanctuary, and they made the holy garments for Aaron, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:9** He made the ephod of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and finely twisted linen.

**36:10** And they hammered out thin plates of gold, and he cut them into threads to work into the blue, into the purple, into the scarlet yarn, and into the fine linen, the work of a skilled designer.

**36:11** They made shoulder pieces for it, joined together; at its two edges it was joined.

**36:12** And the intricately woven band of the ephod that was upon it was of one piece with it and of the same workmanship: of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and finely twisted linen, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:13** And they fashioned the onyx stones, set in mountings of gold, engraved like the engravings of a signet with the names of the sons of Israel.

**36:14** And he placed them on the shoulder pieces of the ephod as stones of memorial for the sons of Israel, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:15** He also made the breastplate, the work of a skilled designer, like the workmanship of the ephod: of gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and finely twisted linen.

**36:16** It was square; they made the breastplate doubled over: a span was its length and a span its width when doubled.

**36:17** And they set in it four rows of stones: a row of sardius, topaz, and emerald was the first row;

**36:18** and the second row: turquoise, sapphire, and diamond;

**36:19** and the third row: jacinth, agate, and amethyst;

**36:20** and the fourth row: beryl, onyx, and jasper; they were enclosed in gold settings in their mountings.

**36:21** And the stones were according to the names of the sons of Israel, twelve according to their names, like the engravings of a signet, each according to its name, for the twelve tribes.

**36:22** And they made upon the breastplate braided chains of corded work, of pure gold.

**36:23** They also made two settings of gold and two rings of gold, and they put the two rings on the two edges of the breastplate.

**36:24** And they put the two braided cords of gold into the two rings on the edges of the breastplate.

**36:25** And the two ends of the two braided cords they fastened to the two settings, and put them on the shoulder pieces of the ephod toward its front.

**36:26** And they made two rings of gold and placed them on the two edges of the breastplate, on its border that faces inward toward the ephod.

**36:27** And they made two rings of gold and put them on the two shoulder pieces of the ephod underneath, toward its front, close by its seam, above the woven band of the ephod.

**36:28** And they bound the breastplate by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a cord of blue, so that it would rest upon the woven band of the ephod and the breastplate would not come loose from the ephod, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:29** He made the robe of the ephod of woven work, entirely of blue.

**36:30** And the opening of the robe was in its midst, like the opening of a coat of mail, with a binding around its opening so that it would not be torn.

**36:31** And they made on the hem of the robe pomegranates of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, finely twisted.

**36:32** They also made bells of pure gold, and placed the bells between the pomegranates on the hem of the robe all around, between the pomegranates:

**36:33** a bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate, all around the hem of the robe for ministering, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:34** And they made the tunics of fine linen, of woven work, for Aaron and for his sons,

**36:35** and the turban of fine linen, and the ornamental caps of fine linen, and the linen trousers of finely twisted linen,

**36:36** and the sash of finely twisted linen, and of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, the work of an embroiderer, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**36:37** And they made the plate of the holy crown of pure gold, and inscribed upon it an inscription like the engravings of a signet: "Holiness to the LORD."

**36:38** And they fastened a cord of blue to it, to attach it high upon the turban, just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **36:1** — Literally "wise of heart," the Hebrew idiom denotes both practical craftsmanship and God-given artistic discernment.
- **36:8** — In the Saints Hebrew Critical Text, the making of the priestly garments follows directly after the freewill offerings (corresponding to Exodus 39:1-31 in the traditional Masoretic order). The phrase "finely woven garments" renders Hebrew bigdei serad, referring to braided or specially worked liturgical cloths.
- **36:11** — The translation follows the Qere (read form) reading the plural suffix "its two edges," whereas the Ketiv (written form) preserves an older singular-like orthography.
- **36:17** — The exact ancient mineralogical identifications of several gemstones in the breastplate remain debated; traditional English equivalents are used here for the twelve stones.
