## Text

**18:1** Now Jethro, the priest of Midian, the father-in-law of Moses, heard of all that God had done for Moses and for Israel His people—how the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.

**18:2** Then Jethro, the father-in-law of Moses, took Zipporah, the wife of Moses, after he had sent her away,

**18:3** along with her two sons, of whom the name of the one was Gershom, for he had said, "I have been a sojourner in a foreign land,"

**18:4** and the name of the other was Eliezer, meaning, "The God of my father was my help, and He delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh."

**18:5** So Jethro, the father-in-law of Moses, came with his sons and his wife to Moses in the wilderness, where he was encamped at the mountain of God.

**18:6** And he said to Moses, "I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you, together with your wife and her two sons with her."

**18:7** Then Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, bowed down, and kissed him. And they asked one another of their welfare, and went into the tent.

**18:8** And Moses recounted to his father-in-law all that the LORD had done to Pharaoh and to Egypt for the sake of Israel, all the hardship that had come upon them along the way, and how the LORD had delivered them.

**18:9** And Jethro rejoiced over all the good that the LORD had done for Israel, whom He had delivered out of the hand of Egypt.

**18:10** And Jethro said, "Blessed be the LORD, who has delivered you out of the hand of Egypt and out of the hand of Pharaoh, and who has delivered the people from beneath the hand of Egypt.

**18:11** Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods, even in the very matter in which they dealt arrogantly against them."

**18:12** Then Jethro, the father-in-law of Moses, brought a burnt offering and sacrifices to God. And Aaron came, with all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with the father-in-law of Moses before God.

**18:13** And it came to pass on the next day that Moses sat to judge the people; and the people stood before Moses from the morning until the evening.

**18:14** Now when the father-in-law of Moses saw all that he was doing for the people, he said, "What is this thing that you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone, while all the people stand before you from morning until evening?"

**18:15** And Moses said to his father-in-law, "Because the people come to me to inquire of God.

**18:16** Whenever they have a dispute, they come to me, and I judge between a man and his neighbor; and I make known the statutes of God and His laws."

**18:17** Then the father-in-law of Moses said to him, "The thing that you are doing is not good.

**18:18** You will surely wear away, both you and this people who are with you; for the matter is too heavy for you—you are not able to perform it by yourself.

**18:19** Now heed my voice; I will counsel you, and God shall be with you: represent the people before God, and bring the matters to God.

**18:20** And you shall instruct them in the statutes and the laws, and make known to them the way in which they must walk and the work which they must do.

**18:21** Moreover you shall select from all the people capable men, who fear God, men of truth, hating unjust gain; and appoint such over them to be rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens.

**18:22** And let them judge the people at all times; and it shall be that every great matter they shall bring to you, while every small matter they themselves shall judge. Thus lighten the burden from yourself, and they shall bear it with you.

**18:23** If you do this thing, and God so commands you, then you will be able to endure, and all this people also will go to their place in peace."

**18:24** So Moses heeded the voice of his father-in-law and did all that he had spoken.

**18:25** And Moses chose capable men out of all Israel, and appointed them as heads over the people—rulers of thousands, rulers of hundreds, rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens.

**18:26** So they judged the people at all times: the difficult cases they brought to Moses, while every small matter they themselves judged.

**18:27** Then Moses sent his father-in-law on his way, and he departed to his own land.

## Translator Notes

- **18:3** — The name Gershom plays on the Hebrew word ger ("sojourner" or "resident alien"), recalling Moses' years as an exile in Midian.
- **18:4** — The name Eliezer means "My God Is Help," honoring God's deliverance of Moses from Pharaoh's wrath.
- **18:11** — The final clause is deliberately elliptical in Hebrew, underscoring that the LORD manifested His supremacy over all gods in the very matter by which the Egyptians acted arrogantly against Israel.
- **18:26** — The Masoretic text preserves an unusual archaic imperfect vocalization for "they judged" (yishputu), reflecting customary, ongoing judicial action.
