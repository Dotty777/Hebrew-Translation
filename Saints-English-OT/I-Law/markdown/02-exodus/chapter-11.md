## Text

**11:1** And the LORD said to Moses, "Yet one more plague I will bring upon Pharaoh and upon Egypt. Afterward he will let you go from this place; when he lets you go altogether, he will surely drive you out from here.

**11:2** Speak now in the ears of the people, and let every man ask from his neighbor, and every woman from her neighbor, articles of silver and articles of gold."

**11:3** And the LORD gave the people favor in the sight of the Egyptians. Moreover, the man Moses was exceedingly great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants and in the sight of the people.

**11:4** Then Moses said, "Thus says the LORD: 'About midnight I will go forth into the midst of Egypt,

**11:5** and every firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits upon his throne, even to the firstborn of the maidservant who is behind the handmill, and every firstborn of the beasts.

**11:6** And there shall be a great outcry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as has never been before, nor shall there ever be the like again.

**11:7** But against none of the children of Israel shall a dog move its tongue, from man even to beast, that you may know that the LORD makes a distinction between Egypt and Israel.'

**11:8** And all these your servants shall come down to me and bow down to me, saying, 'Depart, you and all the people who follow at your feet!' And after that I will go out." Then he went out from Pharaoh in burning anger.

**11:9** And the LORD said to Moses, "Pharaoh will not heed you, so that My wonders may be multiplied in the land of Egypt."

**11:10** So Moses and Aaron performed all these wonders before Pharaoh; yet the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he did not let the children of Israel go out of his land.

## Translator Notes

- **11:7** — "Move its tongue" renders a vivid Hebrew idiom literally meaning "sharpen its tongue," portraying complete stillness and protection in which not even a dog snarls or barks.
- **11:8** — "Who follow at your feet" translates the Hebrew idiom "who are at your feet," denoting the whole assembly marching under Moses' leadership.
- **11:10** — This summary verse closes the entire cycle of wonders performed before Pharaoh prior to the institution of the Passover and the final plague.
