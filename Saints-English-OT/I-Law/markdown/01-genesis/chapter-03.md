## Text

**3:1** Now the serpent was craftier than every beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said to the woman, "Has God indeed said, 'You shall not eat of any tree of the garden'?"

**3:2** And the woman said to the serpent, "Of the fruit of the trees of the garden we may eat;

**3:3** but of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God has said, 'You shall not eat of it, nor shall you touch it, lest you die.'"

**3:4** Then the serpent said to the woman, "You shall surely not die;

**3:5** for God knows that in the day you eat of it, your eyes shall be opened, and you shall be as God, knowing good and evil."

**3:6** And when the woman beheld that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be coveted to give wisdom, she took of its fruit and ate; and she gave also to her husband with her, and he ate.

**3:7** Then the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together and made for themselves girdles.

**3:8** And they heard the sound of the LORD God walking to and fro in the garden in the breeze of the day; and the man and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amidst the trees of the garden.

**3:9** Then the LORD God called to the man and said to him, "Where are you?"

**3:10** And he said, "I heard Your voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; therefore I hid myself."

**3:11** And He said, "Who declared to you that you were naked? Have you eaten from the tree from which I commanded you not to eat?"

**3:12** And the man said, "The woman whom You gave to be with me, she gave to me from the tree, and I ate."

**3:13** Then the LORD God said to the woman, "What is this that you have done?" And the woman said, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate."

**3:14** So the LORD God said to the serpent, "Because you have done this, cursed are you above all livestock and above every beast of the field; upon your belly you shall go, and dust you shall eat all the days of your life.

**3:15** And enmity I will set between you and the woman, and between your seed and her seed: he shall strike your head, and you shall strike his heel."

**3:16** To the woman He said, "I will greatly multiply your sorrow and your conception; in pain you shall bear children; your desire shall be toward your husband, yet he shall rule over you."

**3:17** And to Adam He said, "Because you have heeded the voice of your wife, and have eaten from the tree concerning which I commanded you, saying, 'You shall not eat of it,' cursed is the ground on your account; in toil you shall eat of it all the days of your life.

**3:18** Thorns also and thistles it shall bring forth for you, and you shall eat the herb of the field.

**3:19** By the sweat of your face you shall eat bread, until you return to the ground, for out of it you were taken; for dust you are, and to dust you shall return."

**3:20** And the man called the name of his wife Eve, because she was the mother of all living.

**3:21** And the LORD God made for Adam and for his wife tunics of skin, and clothed them.

**3:22** Then the LORD God said, "Behold, the man has become as one of Us, knowing good and evil; and now, lest he stretch forth his hand and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live forever—"

**3:23** therefore the LORD God sent him forth from the garden of Eden to till the ground from which he had been taken.

**3:24** So He drove out the man; and He stationed at the east of the garden of Eden the cherubim, and the flaming blade of the revolving sword, to guard the way to the tree of life.

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — Hebrew arum ('crafty') plays on arumim ('naked') in 2:25. Throughout vv. 1-5 the serpent and the woman use second-person plural verbs and suffixes.
- **3:8** — Hebrew leruach hayyom literally means 'at the breeze/wind of the day,' denoting the cool afternoon breeze.
- **3:15** — Masoretic Hebrew uses masculine hu ('he,' agreeing with masculine zera, 'seed') and repeats the same rare verb shuph ('strike' or 'bruise') in both clauses.
- **3:19** — Hebrew appekha is literally 'your nostrils' or 'your face.'
- **3:20** — Hebrew Chawwah ('Eve') is linked by sound with chay ('living').
