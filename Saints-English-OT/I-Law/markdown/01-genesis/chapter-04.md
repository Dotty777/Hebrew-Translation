## Text

**4:1** Now Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and bore Cain, and said, "I have acquired a man with the help of the LORD."

**4:2** And she bore again, his brother Abel. Now Abel became a shepherd of flocks, while Cain was a tiller of the ground.

**4:3** And it came to pass in the course of time that Cain brought an offering from the fruit of the ground to the LORD.

**4:4** And Abel also brought of the firstborn of his flock and of their fat. And the LORD showed regard for Abel and his offering,

**4:5** but for Cain and his offering He showed no regard. And Cain was exceedingly angry, and his countenance fell.

**4:6** Then the LORD said to Cain, "Why are you angered, and why has your countenance fallen?

**4:7** If you do well, shall you not be accepted? Yet if you do not do well, sin crouches at the door; its desire is toward you, but you must rule over it."

**4:8** And Cain spoke to Abel his brother; and it came to pass, while they were in the field, that Cain rose up against Abel his brother and slew him.

**4:9** Then the LORD said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" And he replied, "I do not know. Am I the keeper of my brother?"

**4:10** And He said, "What have you done? The voice of your brother's blood cries out to Me from the ground.

**4:11** Now therefore you are cursed from the ground, which has opened its mouth to receive the blood of your brother from your hand.

**4:12** When you till the ground, it shall no longer yield its strength to you; a fugitive and a wanderer you shall be upon the earth."

**4:13** And Cain said to the LORD, "My punishment is too great to bear.

**4:14** Behold, You have driven me out this day from the face of the ground, and from Your presence I shall be hidden; I shall be a fugitive and a wanderer upon the earth, and whoever finds me will slay me."

**4:15** And the LORD said to him, "Therefore, whoever slays Cain, vengeance shall be exacted on him sevenfold." And the LORD appointed a mark for Cain, lest anyone who found him should strike him down.

**4:16** So Cain departed from the presence of the LORD and dwelt in the land of Nod, to the east of Eden.

**4:17** And Cain knew his wife, and she conceived and bore Enoch. And he built a city, and he called the name of the city after the name of his son, Enoch.

**4:18** To Enoch was born Irad; and Irad fathered Mehujael, and Mehujael fathered Methushael, and Methushael fathered Lamech.

**4:19** And Lamech took for himself two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the second was Zillah.

**4:20** And Adah bore Jabal; he was the father of those who dwell in tents and keep livestock.

**4:21** His brother's name was Jubal; he was the father of all who play the lyre and pipe.

**4:22** Zillah also bore Tubal-Cain, the forger of every instrument of bronze and iron; and the sister of Tubal-Cain was Naamah.

**4:23** Then Lamech said to his wives, "Adah and Zillah, hear my voice; wives of Lamech, give ear to my speech! For I have slain a man for wounding me, and a young man for bruising me.

**4:24** If Cain is avenged sevenfold, then Lamech seventy-sevenfold."

**4:25** And Adam knew his wife again, and she bore a son and called his name Seth, "For God has appointed for me another seed in place of Abel, because Cain slew him."

**4:26** And to Seth also a son was born, and he called his name Enosh. At that time men began to call upon the name of the LORD.

## Translator Notes

- **4:1** — Hebrew Qayin (Cain) plays on the verb qaniti ("I have acquired"). The Hebrew preposition literally reads "with the LORD," conveying "with the help of the LORD."
- **4:7** — Hebrew se'et literally means "a lifting up," likely referring to the lifting up of one's countenance or acceptance before God.
- **4:8** — The Masoretic Text records no spoken words after "spoke to Abel his brother." Ancient versions including the Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch, and Vulgate supply "Let us go out into the field."
- **4:13** — Hebrew avon can signify either "iniquity" or the "punishment" for iniquity, and the closing phrase can mean either "too great to bear" or "too great to be forgiven."
- **4:25** — Hebrew Shet (Seth) echoes the verb shat ("has appointed"), tying the son's name directly to Eve's words.
