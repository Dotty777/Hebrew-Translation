## Text

**25:1** And Abraham again took a wife, and her name was Keturah.

**25:2** And she bore him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah.

**25:3** Jokshan fathered Sheba and Dedan. And the sons of Dedan were the Asshurim, the Letushim, and the Leummim.

**25:4** And the sons of Midian were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah. All these were the sons of Keturah.

**25:5** Now Abraham gave all that belonged to him to Isaac.

**25:6** But to the sons of the concubines whom Abraham had, Abraham gave gifts; and while he was still living, he sent them away from Isaac his son, eastward to the land of the east.

**25:7** These are the days of the years of Abraham's life which he lived: one hundred seventy-five years.

**25:8** Then Abraham breathed his last and died in a good old age, an old man and full of years, and was gathered to his people.

**25:9** And his sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah, in the field of Ephron the son of Zohar the Hittite, which lies before Mamre.

**25:10** The field which Abraham purchased from the sons of Heth—there Abraham was buried, and Sarah his wife.

**25:11** And it came to pass after the death of Abraham that God blessed Isaac his son; and Isaac dwelt by Beer Lahai Roi.

**25:12** Now these are the generations of Ishmael, Abraham's son, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's maidservant, bore to Abraham.

**25:13** And these are the names of the sons of Ishmael, by their names according to their generations: the firstborn of Ishmael, Nebajoth; then Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam,

**25:14** Mishma, Dumah, Massa,

**25:15** Hadad, Tema, Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah.

**25:16** These are the sons of Ishmael, and these are their names by their villages and by their encampments—twelve princes according to their tribes.

**25:17** And these were the years of the life of Ishmael: one hundred thirty-seven years; and he breathed his last and died, and was gathered to his people.

**25:18** They dwelt from Havilah as far as Shur, which lies east of Egypt as one goes toward Assyria; he settled over against all his brethren.

**25:19** Now these are the generations of Isaac, Abraham's son: Abraham fathered Isaac.

**25:20** Isaac was forty years old when he took Rebekah, the daughter of Bethuel the Aramean of Paddan Aram, the sister of Laban the Aramean, to be his wife.

**25:21** And Isaac pleaded with the LORD on behalf of his wife, because she was barren; and the LORD granted his plea, and Rebekah his wife conceived.

**25:22** But the children struggled together within her, and she said, "If it is so, why am I thus?" So she went to inquire of the LORD.

**25:23** And the LORD said to her, "Two nations are in your womb, and two peoples shall be separated from your body; one people shall be stronger than the other, and the older shall serve the younger."

**25:24** When her days to give birth were fulfilled, behold, there were twins in her womb.

**25:25** And the first came forth red, all over like a hairy garment; so they called his name Esau.

**25:26** Afterward his brother came forth, and his hand grasped Esau's heel; so his name was called Jacob. And Isaac was sixty years old when she bore them.

**25:27** So the boys grew; and Esau became a skillful hunter, a man of the field, while Jacob was a quiet man, dwelling in tents.

**25:28** And Isaac loved Esau because he had a taste for wild game, but Rebekah loved Jacob.

**25:29** Now Jacob was boiling a stew; and Esau came in from the field, and he was weary.

**25:30** And Esau said to Jacob, "Please let me eat some of that red—that red stew—for I am weary." Therefore his name was called Edom.

**25:31** And Jacob said, "Sell me your birthright this very day."

**25:32** And Esau said, "Behold, I am about to die; so of what use is a birthright to me?"

**25:33** Then Jacob said, "Swear to me this very day." So he swore to him, and sold his birthright to Jacob.

**25:34** And Jacob gave Esau bread and lentil stew; and he ate and drank, arose and went his way. Thus Esau despised the birthright.

## Translator Notes

- **25:18** — Hebrew nafal literally means "he fell," used here idiomatically for settling or taking up one's allotted dwelling place opposite his kindred, echoing Genesis 16:12.
- **25:23** — The Masoretic text preserves a ketiv/qere orthographic variant (ketiv gyym, qere goyim), both meaning "nations"; the translation follows the qere.
- **25:27** — The Hebrew adjective tam describes Jacob as "complete," "mild," or "quiet," contrasting his domestic temperament with Esau's life in the open field.
- **25:30** — Esau's repeated call for "that red—that red stew" (Hebrew adom) creates a wordplay explaining his name Edom, while the verb rendered "let me eat" literally carries the coarse sense of gulping down food.
