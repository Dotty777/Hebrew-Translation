## Text

**2:1** Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them.

**2:2** And by the seventh day God had completed His work which He had made; and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had made.

**2:3** And God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, because on it He rested from all His work which God had created to make.

**2:4** These are the generations of the heavens and of the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made earth and heavens.

**2:5** Now no shrub of the field was yet in the earth, and no herb of the field had yet sprung up, for the LORD God had not caused it to rain upon the earth, and there was no man to till the ground;

**2:6** but a mist rose up from the earth and watered the whole face of the ground.

**2:7** Then the LORD God fashioned man of dust from the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.

**2:8** And the LORD God planted a garden in Eden toward the east, and there He placed the man whom He had fashioned.

**2:9** And out of the ground the LORD God caused to spring up every tree pleasant to the sight and good for food, and the tree of life in the midst of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

**2:10** Now a river flowed forth from Eden to water the garden, and from there it divided and became four headwaters.

**2:11** The name of the first is Pishon; it is the one that winds around all the land of Havilah, where the gold is.

**2:12** And the gold of that land is good; bdellium and the onyx stone are there.

**2:13** And the name of the second river is Gihon; it is the one that winds around all the land of Cush.

**2:14** And the name of the third river is Tigris; it is the one that flows east of Assyria. And the fourth river is Euphrates.

**2:15** So the LORD God took the man and settled him in the garden of Eden to till it and to keep it.

**2:16** And the LORD God laid a command upon the man, saying, "Of every tree of the garden you may freely eat;

**2:17** yet of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die."

**2:18** And the LORD God said, "It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make for him a helper corresponding to him."

**2:19** Now out of the ground the LORD God had formed every beast of the field and every fowl of the heavens, and He brought them to the man to see what he would call them; and whatever the man called each living creature, that was its name.

**2:20** So the man gave names to all livestock, and to the fowl of the heavens, and to every beast of the field; yet for man there was not found a helper corresponding to him.

**2:21** Then the LORD God caused a deep slumber to fall upon the man, and he slept; and He took one of his ribs, and closed up flesh in its place.

**2:22** And the rib which the LORD God had taken from the man He built into a woman, and brought her to the man.

**2:23** And the man said, "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh; she shall be called Woman, for out of Man was she taken."

**2:24** Therefore a man shall forsake his father and his mother and cleave to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.

**2:25** And both of them were naked, the man and his wife, yet they felt no shame.

## Translator Notes

- **2:2** — Masoretic Hebrew reads 'the seventh day' (differing from the Septuagint's 'sixth day'); the completed verb wayekhal is rendered pluperfect ('had completed') to convey that God's work stood finished as the seventh day began.
- **2:3** — Literally 'which God created to make' (bara Elohim la'asot), a Hebrew idiom summarizing all that God had creatively fashioned.
- **2:7** — Hebrew plays on ha'adam ('the man') and ha'adamah ('the ground'); nephesh chayyah ('living soul') denotes a living, breathing being.
- **2:14** — Hebrew Hiddekel is rendered by its standard English name Tigris; qidmat Ashshur means 'east of Assyria.'
- **2:18** — Hebrew ezer kenegdo literally means 'a helper as opposite/corresponding to him,' denoting an equal, fitting counterpart.
- **2:23** — Hebrew wordplay pairs ishshah ('woman') with ish ('man').
