## Translator Notes

- **7:3** — Hebrew eglot tsav denotes covered or litter-style wagons suited for hauling the heavy components of the tabernacle.
- **7:13** — The unit "shekels" is supplied after "one hundred and thirty" from the following clause; "the shekel of the sanctuary" refers to the official standard weight kept at the sanctuary.
- **7:14** — Hebrew kaf (literally "palm") refers to a small, shallow incense pan or ladle; "ten of gold" signifies a weight of ten shekels of gold.
- **7:19** — The Masoretic Hebrew varies the opening of the offering formula across the first three days: "And his offering was" (7:13), "He presented as his offering" (7:19), and "His offering was" (7:25, 31, 37, 43).
- **7:72** — Hebrew expresses ordinals above ten with a cardinal construction—literally "on the day of eleven day" (and similarly in 7:78 for the twelfth day)—rendered idiomatically as "On the eleventh day."
- **7:85** — The total of two thousand four hundred shekels is the sum of twelve silver dishes at one hundred and thirty shekels each and twelve silver basins at seventy shekels each.
- **7:89** — Hebrew employs the rare Hithpael participle middabber ("speaking") to convey the solemn resonance of the divine voice speaking to Moses from above the mercy seat.
