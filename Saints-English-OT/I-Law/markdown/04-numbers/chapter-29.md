## Translator Notes

- **29:1** — The Hebrew phrase yom teruah denotes a day of loud sounding or trumpet blasts, inaugurating the seventh month; the prohibition of laborious work here and in verses 12 and 35 is narrower than the ban on all work on the Day of Atonement in verse 7.
- **29:3** — Hebrew issaron literally means a tenth-measure, referring to one-tenth of an ephah as specified in Numbers 28:5.
- **29:15** — The Masoretic Text places an extraordinary scribal dot over the vav in the first occurrence of issaron here, flagging an ancient textual note on the repeated distributive term.
- **29:19** — The Masoretic Text reads plural their drink offerings here and plural its drink offerings in verse 31, in contrast to singular its drink offering on the other days of the seven-day feast.
