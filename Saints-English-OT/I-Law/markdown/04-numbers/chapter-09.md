## Text

**9:1** Now the LORD spoke to Moses in the Wilderness of Sinai, in the second year after they came out of the land of Egypt, in the first month, saying,

**9:2** "Let the children of Israel observe the Passover at its appointed time.

**9:3** On the fourteenth day of this month, at twilight, you shall observe it at its appointed time; according to all its statutes and according to all its ordinances you shall observe it."

**9:4** So Moses spoke to the children of Israel that they should observe the Passover.

**9:5** And they observed the Passover in the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month, at twilight, in the Wilderness of Sinai; according to all that the LORD had commanded Moses, so the children of Israel did.

**9:6** Now there were certain men who were unclean because of the dead body of a man, so that they could not observe the Passover on that day; and they drew near before Moses and before Aaron on that day.

**9:7** And those men said to him, "We are unclean because of the dead body of a man. Why should we be held back from presenting the offering of the LORD at its appointed time among the children of Israel?"

**9:8** And Moses said to them, "Stand still, and I will hear what the LORD will command concerning you."

**9:9** Then the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**9:10** "Speak to the children of Israel, saying, 'If any man among you or among your generations is unclean because of a dead body, or is on a distant journey, he shall still observe the Passover to the LORD.

**9:11** In the second month, on the fourteenth day, at twilight, they shall observe it; with unleavened bread and bitter herbs they shall eat it.

**9:12** They shall leave none of it until morning, nor break any bone of it; according to every statute of the Passover they shall observe it.

**9:13** But the man who is clean and is not on a journey, yet neglects to observe the Passover, that soul shall be cut off from among his people, because he did not present the offering of the LORD at its appointed time; that man shall bear his sin.

**9:14** And if a stranger sojourns among you and would observe the Passover to the LORD, according to the statute of the Passover and according to its ordinance, so he shall do; you shall have one statute, both for the stranger and for the native of the land.'"

**9:15** Now on the day that the tabernacle was raised up, the cloud covered the tabernacle, the tent of the Testimony; and in the evening it was over the tabernacle like the appearance of fire until morning.

**9:16** So it was continually: the cloud covered it, and the appearance of fire by night.

**9:17** And whenever the cloud was lifted up from above the tent, after that the children of Israel would set out; and in the place where the cloud settled, there the children of Israel would encamp.

**9:18** At the command of the LORD the children of Israel would set out, and at the command of the LORD they would encamp; all the days that the cloud rested upon the tabernacle, they would remain encamped.

**9:19** Even when the cloud lingered upon the tabernacle many days, the children of Israel kept the charge of the LORD and did not set out.

**9:20** And sometimes the cloud was upon the tabernacle a few days; at the command of the LORD they would encamp, and at the command of the LORD they would set out.

**9:21** And sometimes the cloud remained from evening until morning; when the cloud was lifted up in the morning, then they would set out; or whether by day and by night, whenever the cloud was lifted up, they would set out.

**9:22** Whether two days, or a month, or a longer time that the cloud lingered upon the tabernacle, resting over it, the children of Israel would remain encamped and not set out; but when it was lifted up, they would set out.

**9:23** At the command of the LORD they encamped, and at the command of the LORD they set out; they kept the charge of the LORD, at the command of the LORD by the hand of Moses.

## Translator Notes

- **9:3** — Hebrew "bein ha-arbayim" literally means "between the two evenings," referring to twilight between sunset and darkness.
- **9:10** — The Masoretic Text places an extraordinary scribal dot over the final letter of "rechoqah" ("distant"), an ancient textual mark traditionally associated with questions over what distance qualified as a far journey.
- **9:16** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads simply "the cloud covered it," without the words "by day" found in some ancient versions.
- **9:18** — Hebrew "al-pi YHWH" literally means "at the mouth of the LORD," an idiom for "at the command of the LORD."
- **9:22** — Hebrew "yamim" literally means "days," and is used idiomatically here to indicate a longer period or a full year.
