## Text

**24:1** And Balaam saw that it was good in the sight of the LORD to bless Israel, so he did not go, as at previous times, to meet with omens, but set his face toward the wilderness.

**24:2** And Balaam lifted up his eyes and saw Israel dwelling according to its tribes, and the Spirit of God was upon him.

**24:3** Then he took up his oracle and said, "The utterance of Balaam the son of Beor, even the utterance of the man whose eye is opened,

**24:4** the utterance of him who hears the sayings of God, who beholds the vision of the Almighty, falling down, yet with eyes uncovered:

**24:5** How fair are your tents, O Jacob, your habitations, O Israel!

**24:6** Like valleys that stretch forth, like gardens beside a river, like aloes which the LORD has planted, like cedars beside the waters.

**24:7** Water shall flow from his buckets, and his seed shall be in abundant waters; his king shall be exalted above Agag, and his kingdom shall be lifted high.

**24:8** God brings him forth out of Egypt; he has strength like the wild ox. He shall devour the nations, his adversaries; he shall crush their bones and pierce them through with his arrows.

**24:9** He crouches, he lies down like a lion, and like a lioness—who shall rouse him? Blessed is everyone who blesses you, and cursed is everyone who curses you."

**24:10** Then the anger of Balak burned against Balaam, and he struck his hands together; and Balak said to Balaam, "I summoned you to curse my enemies, and behold, you have surely blessed them these three times.

**24:11** Now therefore, flee to your own place! I said I would greatly honor you, yet behold, the LORD has kept you back from honor."

**24:12** And Balaam said to Balak, "Did I not also speak to your messengers whom you sent to me, saying,

**24:13** 'If Balak were to give me his house full of silver and gold, I could not transgress the command of the LORD, to do good or evil of my own heart; what the LORD speaks, that I must speak.'

**24:14** And now, behold, I am going to my people. Come, I will counsel you concerning what this people shall do to your people in the latter days."

**24:15** Then he took up his oracle and said, "The utterance of Balaam the son of Beor, even the utterance of the man whose eye is opened,

**24:16** the utterance of him who hears the sayings of God, and knows the knowledge of the Most High, who beholds the vision of the Almighty, falling down, yet with eyes uncovered:

**24:17** I see him, yet not now; I behold him, yet not near: a Star shall come forth from Jacob, and a Scepter shall rise out of Israel, and shall crush the brow of Moab and tear down all the sons of Sheth.

**24:18** And Edom shall become a possession, and Seir also, his enemies, shall become a possession, while Israel does valiantly.

**24:19** Out of Jacob one shall exercise dominion, and shall destroy the survivor from the city."

**24:20** Then he looked on Amalek, and took up his oracle and said, "Amalek was first among the nations, but his latter end shall be destruction."

**24:21** Then he looked on the Kenites, and took up his oracle and said, "Enduring is your dwelling place, and your nest is set in the crag;

**24:22** nevertheless Kain shall be laid waste; how long until Asshur carries you away captive?"

**24:23** Then he took up his oracle and said, "Alas, who shall live when God ordains this?

**24:24** Yet ships shall come from the coast of Kittim, and they shall afflict Asshur and afflict Eber; and he also shall come to destruction."

**24:25** Then Balaam arose and departed, and returned to his place; and Balak also went on his way.

## Translator Notes

- **24:3** — The rare Hebrew phrase shetum ha-ayin is traditionally understood as 'whose eye is opened' to prophetic vision, though some interpret the adjective as 'closed' or 'piercing.'
- **24:17** — The phrase 'sons of Sheth' may designate a Transjordanian people associated with Moab, or it may be read as 'sons of tumult' in parallel with Jeremiah 48:45.
- **24:21** — The Hebrew contains a deliberate wordplay between ha-Qeni ('the Kenites'), qinneka ('your nest'), and Qayin ('Kain') in verse 22.
- **24:24** — Kittim originally referred to Cyprus and its coastal settlements, and later came to signify western Mediterranean seafaring powers more broadly.
