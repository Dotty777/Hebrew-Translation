## Text

**11:1** Now the people became like those who complain of misfortune in the hearing of the LORD; and the LORD heard it, and His wrath was kindled. So the fire of the LORD burned among them and consumed the outskirts of the camp.

**11:2** Then the people cried out to Moses, and Moses prayed to the LORD, and the fire died down.

**11:3** So he called the name of that place Taberah, because the fire of the LORD had burned among them.

**11:4** Now the mixed multitude who were among them yielded to an intense craving; and the children of Israel also wept again and said, "Who will give us meat to eat?

**11:5** We remember the fish which we ate freely in Egypt, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions, and the garlic;

**11:6** but now our soul is dried up; there is nothing at all except this manna before our eyes!"

**11:7** Now the manna was like coriander seed, and its appearance was like the appearance of bdellium.

**11:8** The people went about and gathered it, ground it on millstones or beat it in a mortar, boiled it in a pot, and made cakes of it; and its taste was like the taste of pastry baked with oil.

**11:9** And when the dew fell upon the camp in the night, the manna fell upon it.

**11:10** Then Moses heard the people weeping throughout their families, each man at the doorway of his tent; and the wrath of the LORD was greatly kindled, and in the sight of Moses it was also grievous.

**11:11** So Moses said to the LORD, "Why have You dealt harshly with Your servant? And why have I not found favor in Your sight, that You have laid the burden of all this people upon me?

**11:12** Did I conceive all this people? Did I bring them forth, that You should say to me, 'Carry them in your bosom, as a guardian carries a nursing child,' to the land which You swore to their fathers?

**11:13** Where am I to get meat to give to all this people? For they weep before me, saying, 'Give us meat, that we may eat!'

**11:14** I am not able to bear all this people alone, for the burden is too heavy for me.

**11:15** And if You deal thus with me, please slay me at once—if I have found favor in Your sight—and do not let me look upon my own wretchedness."

**11:16** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Gather to Me seventy men of the elders of Israel, whom you know to be the elders of the people and their officers; bring them to the tent of meeting, and let them take their stand there with you.

**11:17** Then I will come down and speak with you there; and I will take of the Spirit that is upon you and put the same upon them, and they shall bear the burden of the people with you, so that you shall not bear it by yourself alone.

**11:18** And to the people you shall say, 'Consecrate yourselves for tomorrow, and you shall eat meat; for you have wept in the hearing of the LORD, saying, "Who will give us meat to eat? For it was well with us in Egypt." Therefore the LORD will give you meat, and you shall eat.

**11:19** You shall eat not one day, nor two days, nor five days, nor ten days, nor twenty days,

**11:20** but for a whole month, until it comes out of your nostrils and becomes loathsome to you, because you have rejected the LORD who is among you, and have wept before Him, saying, "Why did we ever come forth from Egypt?"'"

**11:21** And Moses said, "The people among whom I am number six hundred thousand men on foot; yet You have said, 'I will give them meat, that they may eat for a whole month.'

**11:22** Shall flocks and herds be slaughtered for them, to be sufficient for them? Or shall all the fish of the sea be gathered together for them, to be sufficient for them?"

**11:23** And the LORD said to Moses, "Has the hand of the LORD been shortened? Now you shall see whether My word will come to pass for you or not."

**11:24** So Moses went out and spoke the words of the LORD to the people; and he gathered seventy men of the elders of the people and stationed them all around the tent.

**11:25** Then the LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and took of the Spirit that was upon him and placed the same upon the seventy elders; and it came to pass, when the Spirit rested upon them, that they prophesied, though they did not do so again.

**11:26** Yet two men had remained in the camp—the name of the one was Eldad, and the name of the other Medad—and the Spirit rested upon them. Now they were among those who were registered, though they had not gone out to the tent; and they prophesied in the camp.

**11:27** And a young man ran and told Moses, and said, "Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp."

**11:28** Then Joshua the son of Nun, the attendant of Moses from his youth, answered and said, "My lord Moses, restrain them!"

**11:29** And Moses said to him, "Are you jealous for my sake? Oh, that all the LORD's people were prophets, and that the LORD would put His Spirit upon them!"

**11:30** Then Moses returned to the camp, both he and the elders of Israel.

**11:31** Now a wind went forth from the LORD and drove quail from the sea, and let them fall beside the camp, about a day's journey on this side and about a day's journey on the other side, all around the camp, and about two cubits above the surface of the earth.

**11:32** And the people rose up all that day, and all that night, and all the next day, and gathered the quail—he who gathered least gathered ten homers—and they spread them out all around the camp for themselves.

**11:33** While the meat was still between their teeth, before it was chewed, the wrath of the LORD was kindled against the people, and the LORD struck the people with an exceedingly great plague.

**11:34** So he called the name of that place Kibroth Hattaavah, because there they buried the people who had yielded to craving.

**11:35** From Kibroth Hattaavah the people journeyed to Hazeroth, and they remained at Hazeroth.

## Translator Notes

- **11:3** — Taberah means Burning in Hebrew, echoing the verb for the fire of the LORD that burned among the people.
- **11:15** — The Masoretic text unusually writes the second-person pronoun in the defective feminine form att alongside a masculine participle as Moses addresses the LORD in distress.
- **11:25** — The Hebrew phrase velo yasafu is ordinarily derived from yasaf, meaning they did not continue or do so again, though some ancient versions connect it to suf, meaning they did not cease.
- **11:32** — A homer was a large dry measure of volume, approximately five to six bushels or about two hundred twenty liters.
- **11:34** — Kibroth Hattaavah means Graves of Craving in Hebrew.
