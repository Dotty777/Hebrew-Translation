## Text

**24:1** When a man takes a wife and marries her, and it comes to pass that she does not find favor in his eyes because he has found some unseemly thing in her, and he writes her a certificate of divorce, places it in her hand, and sends her away from his house,

**24:2** and she goes out from his house and departs and becomes the wife of another man,

**24:3** and the latter husband hates her, and writes her a certificate of divorce, places it in her hand, and sends her away from his house, or if the latter husband who took her as his wife dies,

**24:4** then her former husband who sent her away may not take her again to be his wife after she has been defiled, for that is an abomination before the LORD; and you shall not cause the land to sin, which the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.

**24:5** When a man takes a new wife, he shall not go out with the army, nor shall any duty be imposed upon him; he shall be free for his house for one year, and shall bring joy to his wife whom he has taken.

**24:6** No man shall take a millstone or an upper millstone in pledge, for he takes a life in pledge.

**24:7** If a man is found stealing one of his brethren from among the children of Israel, and treats him harshly and sells him, then that thief shall die; thus you shall purge the evil from your midst.

**24:8** Take heed concerning a plague of leprosy, to observe diligently and to do according to all that the priests, the Levites, shall instruct you; as I commanded them, so you shall take care to do.

**24:9** Remember what the LORD your God did to Miriam on the way when you came out of Egypt.

**24:10** When you make a loan of anything to your neighbor, you shall not enter his house to take his pledge.

**24:11** You shall stand outside, and the man to whom you are making the loan shall bring the pledge out to you.

**24:12** And if the man is poor, you shall not lie down to sleep with his pledge.

**24:13** You shall surely return the pledge to him as the sun goes down, that he may sleep in his own garment and bless you; and it shall be righteousness to you before the LORD your God.

**24:14** You shall not oppress a hired servant who is poor and needy, whether one of your brethren or one of your sojourners who are in your land within your gates.

**24:15** On his day you shall give him his wages, and the sun shall not go down upon it, for he is poor and sets his heart upon it; lest he cry out against you to the LORD, and it be sin in you.

**24:16** Fathers shall not be put to death for children, nor shall children be put to death for fathers; every man shall be put to death for his own sin.

**24:17** You shall not pervert the justice due to the sojourner or the fatherless, nor take a widow's garment in pledge.

**24:18** But you shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and the LORD your God redeemed you from there; therefore I am commanding you to do this thing.

**24:19** When you reap your harvest in your field and forget a sheaf in the field, you shall not go back to get it; it shall be for the sojourner, for the fatherless, and for the widow, that the LORD your God may bless you in all the work of your hands.

**24:20** When you beat your olive trees, you shall not search the boughs again; it shall be for the sojourner, for the fatherless, and for the widow.

**24:21** When you gather the grapes of your vineyard, you shall not glean it afterward; it shall be for the sojourner, for the fatherless, and for the widow.

**24:22** And you shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt; therefore I am commanding you to do this thing.

## Translator Notes

- **24:1** — "Some unseemly thing" renders the Hebrew phrase literally meaning "nakedness of a matter," indicating a serious impropriety distinct from adultery, which carried a separate penalty in 22:22.
- **24:6** — Hebrew literally reads "a pair of millstones or an upper millstone"; seizing either part disabled a household's daily grinding of grain, thereby taking a family's livelihood or "life."
- **24:15** — "Sets his heart upon it" translates the Hebrew idiom "lifts up his soul toward it," expressing the poor laborer's urgent dependence on the day's wages.
