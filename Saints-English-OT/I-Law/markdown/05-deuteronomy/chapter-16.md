## Text

**16:1** Keep the month of Abib, and celebrate the Passover to the LORD your God, because in the month of Abib the LORD your God brought you forth out of Egypt by night.

**16:2** And you shall sacrifice the Passover offering to the LORD your God, of the flock and of the herd, at the place that the LORD shall choose to cause His name to dwell there.

**16:3** You shall eat no leavened bread with it; seven days you shall eat unleavened bread with it, the bread of affliction—for you departed from the land of Egypt in haste—so that you may remember the day of your departure from the land of Egypt all the days of your life.

**16:4** And no leaven shall be seen with you throughout all your borders for seven days, nor shall any of the flesh that you sacrifice in the evening on the first day remain overnight until morning.

**16:5** You may not sacrifice the Passover inside any of your gates that the LORD your God is giving you.

**16:6** Rather, at the place that the LORD your God shall choose to cause His name to dwell, there you shall sacrifice the Passover in the evening, at the going down of the sun, at the appointed time of your departure from Egypt.

**16:7** And you shall cook and eat it in the place that the LORD your God shall choose; and in the morning you shall turn and go to your tents.

**16:8** Six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a solemn assembly to the LORD your God; you shall do no work.

**16:9** Seven weeks you shall count for yourself; from the time the sickle first begins in the standing grain, you shall begin to count seven weeks.

**16:10** Then you shall keep the Feast of Weeks to the LORD your God with the measure of a freewill offering from your hand, which you shall give according as the LORD your God blesses you.

**16:11** And you shall rejoice before the LORD your God—you and your son and your daughter, your manservant and your maidservant, the Levite who is within your gates, and the sojourner, the fatherless, and the widow who are in your midst—at the place that the LORD your God shall choose to cause His name to dwell there.

**16:12** And you shall remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and you shall take care to perform these statutes.

**16:13** You shall celebrate the Feast of Tabernacles for yourself seven days, when you gather in from your threshing floor and from your winepress.

**16:14** And you shall rejoice in your feast: you and your son and your daughter, your manservant and your maidservant, and the Levite, the sojourner, the fatherless, and the widow who are within your gates.

**16:15** Seven days you shall keep a feast to the LORD your God at the place that the LORD shall choose; because the LORD your God shall bless you in all your produce and in all the work of your hands, so that you shall be altogether joyful.

**16:16** Three times in the year all your males shall appear before the LORD your God in the place that He shall choose: at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, at the Feast of Weeks, and at the Feast of Tabernacles; and none shall appear before the LORD empty-handed.

**16:17** Every man shall give according to the gift of his hand, according to the blessing of the LORD your God which He has bestowed upon you.

**16:18** Judges and officers you shall appoint for yourself in all your gates that the LORD your God is giving you, according to your tribes, and they shall judge the people with righteous judgment.

**16:19** You shall not pervert justice; you shall not show partiality, nor shall you take a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and subverts the words of the righteous.

**16:20** Justice, justice you shall pursue, so that you may live and possess the land that the LORD your God is giving you.

**16:21** You shall not plant for yourself an Asherah, any tree, beside the altar of the LORD your God that you shall make for yourself.

**16:22** Nor shall you set up for yourself a sacred pillar, which the LORD your God hates.

## Translator Notes

- **16:7** — The Hebrew verb bishalta broadly means to cook or boil, though in the context of the Passover offering it is traditionally understood alongside the roasting instruction of Exodus 12:9.
- **16:10** — The rare Hebrew noun missat occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and denotes a sufficient measure or proportionate tribute given freely in response to the LORD's blessing.
- **16:20** — The emphatic Hebrew repetition tsedeq tsedeq ("Justice, justice") stresses an uncompromising pursuit of pure righteousness and impartial judgment.
- **16:21** — An "Asherah" was a sacred wooden pole or planted tree linked with Canaanite fertility worship, strictly prohibited beside the altar of the LORD.
