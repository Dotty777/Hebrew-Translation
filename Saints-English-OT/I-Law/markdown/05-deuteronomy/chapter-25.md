## Text

**25:1** If there is a dispute between men, and they approach the court and the judges judge them, then they shall justify the righteous and condemn the wicked.

**25:2** Then it shall be, if the wicked man deserves to be beaten, that the judge shall have him lie down and be beaten in his presence with a fixed number of stripes, according to his wickedness.

**25:3** Forty stripes he may give him, and no more; lest he exceed these and beat him with many more stripes, and your brother be degraded in your sight.

**25:4** You shall not muzzle an ox while it is threshing.

**25:5** If brothers dwell together, and one of them dies and has no son, the wife of the dead man shall not be married outside the family to a stranger; her husband's brother shall go in to her, take her as his wife, and perform the duty of a husband's brother to her.

**25:6** And it shall be that the firstborn whom she bears shall succeed to the name of his dead brother, that his name may not be blotted out from Israel.

**25:7** But if the man does not desire to take his brother's wife, then his brother's wife shall go up to the gate to the elders and say, "My husband's brother refuses to raise up a name for his brother in Israel; he is not willing to perform the duty of a husband's brother to me."

**25:8** Then the elders of his city shall summon him and speak to him. If he stands firm and says, "I do not desire to take her,"

**25:9** then his brother's wife shall approach him in the sight of the elders, remove his sandal from his foot, spit in his face, and answer and say, "So shall it be done to the man who will not build up his brother's house."

**25:10** And his name shall be called in Israel, "The house of him whose sandal was removed."

**25:11** When men strive together, a man and his brother, and the wife of the one draws near to rescue her husband from the hand of the one striking him, and she puts forth her hand and seizes him by his private parts,

**25:12** then you shall cut off her hand; your eye shall not show pity.

**25:13** You shall not have in your bag differing weights, a large and a small.

**25:14** You shall not have in your house differing measures, a large and a small.

**25:15** You shall have a full and just weight; you shall have a full and just measure, that your days may be prolonged in the land which the LORD your God is giving you.

**25:16** For all who do such things, all who act unrighteously, are an abomination to the LORD your God.

**25:17** Remember what Amalek did to you on the way as you came out of Egypt,

**25:18** how he met you on the way and attacked your rear, all the stragglers behind you, when you were faint and weary; and he did not fear God.

**25:19** Therefore it shall be, when the LORD your God has given you rest from all your enemies all around, in the land which the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess, that you shall blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven. You shall not forget.

## Translator Notes

- **25:2** — "Deserves to be beaten" renders the Hebrew idiom literally meaning "a son of striking."
- **25:13** — "Differing weights" literally reads "a stone and a stone" in Hebrew, referring to stone weights used in balance scales; similarly, verse 14 literally reads "an ephah and an ephah" for dry grain measures.
- **25:18** — "Attacked your rear" translates a Hebrew verb literally meaning "cut off the tail," describing an ambush on the weary stragglers trailing behind Israel's march.
