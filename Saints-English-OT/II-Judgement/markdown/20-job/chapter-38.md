## Text

**38:1** Then the LORD answered Job from the whirlwind and said:

**38:2** "Who is this who obscures counsel with words without knowledge?

**38:3** Gird up your loins now like a mighty man; I will question you, and you shall inform Me.

**38:4** Where were you when I laid the foundation of the earth? Declare it, if you possess understanding.

**38:5** Who fixed its measurements—surely you know!—or who stretched the measuring line over it?

**38:6** Upon what were its bases sunk, or who laid its cornerstone,

**38:7** when the morning stars sang out together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy?

**38:8** And who enclosed the sea with doors when it burst forth, issuing from the womb,

**38:9** when I appointed the cloud as its garment, and thick gloom as its swaddling band,

**38:10** when I prescribed My boundary for it, and set in place a bar and doors,

**38:11** and said, 'Thus far you shall come, and no farther; and here the swelling pride of your waves shall be halted'?

**38:12** Since your days began, have you commanded the morning, and caused the dawn to know its place,

**38:13** that it might take hold of the skirts of the earth, and the wicked be shaken out of it?

**38:14** It is transformed like clay beneath a seal, and they stand forth like a garment.

**38:15** From the wicked their light is withheld, and the uplifted arm is broken.

**38:16** Have you come to the springs of the sea, or walked about in the recesses of the deep?

**38:17** Have the gates of death been revealed to you, or have you beheld the gates of the shadow of death?

**38:18** Have you comprehended the expanses of the earth? Declare it, if you know all of it.

**38:19** Where is the way to where light dwells? And darkness—where is its place,

**38:20** that you might take it to its territory, and that you might discern the paths to its house?

**38:21** You know it, for you were born then, and the number of your days is great!

**38:22** Have you entered the storehouses of the snow, or have you beheld the storehouses of the hail,

**38:23** which I have kept in reserve for the time of distress, for the day of battle and war?

**38:24** By what way is the light distributed, and the east wind dispersed over the earth?

**38:25** Who has cleft a channel for the pouring flood, or a path for the thunderbolt,

**38:26** to send rain upon a land where there is no man, upon a wilderness in which no mortal is,

**38:27** to satisfy the waste and desolate ground, and to cause the shoots of tender grass to spring forth?

**38:28** Does the rain have a father? Or who has fathered the drops of dew?

**38:29** From whose womb did the ice come forth? And the frost of heaven—who gave birth to it?

**38:30** The waters become concealed like stone, and the face of the deep is locked fast together.

**38:31** Can you bind the chains of the Pleiades, or loosen the cords of Orion?

**38:32** Can you lead forth the Mazzaroth in their season, or guide the Bear with her children?

**38:33** Do you know the statutes of the heavens, or can you establish their dominion upon the earth?

**38:34** Can you lift up your voice to the clouds, so that an abundance of waters may cover you?

**38:35** Can you send forth lightnings, that they may go and say to you, 'Here we are'?

**38:36** Who has placed wisdom in the inward parts, or who has granted understanding to the heart?

**38:37** Who can number the clouds in wisdom, or who can tilt the waterskins of heaven,

**38:38** when the dust hardens into a solid mass, and the clods cleave fast together?

**38:39** Can you hunt prey for the lioness, or fill the appetite of the young lions,

**38:40** when they crouch in their dens, and abide in the thicket to lie in wait?

**38:41** Who prepares for the raven its provision, when its young cry out to God, and wander about for lack of food?"

## Translator Notes

- **38:1** — The ketiv writes the preposition and noun as a single word, while the qere separates them as min hasearah, translated here as from the whirlwind.
- **38:12** — The qere supplies the definite article before dawn and uses the standard second-person verb spelling, whereas the ketiv omits the article and appends a final he to the verb.
- **38:32** — Mazzaroth is a transliteration of a rare Hebrew astronomical term referring to a prominent constellation or the constellations of the zodiac in their seasonal circuit.
- **38:36** — The rare Hebrew words translated inward parts and heart follow traditional interpretation; some scholars alternatively understand them as cloud layers and celestial signs or weather-sensing birds.
