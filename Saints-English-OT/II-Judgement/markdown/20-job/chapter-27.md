## Text

**27:1** And Job again took up his discourse and said:

**27:2** "As God lives, who has set aside my right, and the Almighty, who has embittered my soul,

**27:3** as long as my breath remains within me, and the spirit of God is in my nostrils,

**27:4** my lips shall not speak unrighteousness, nor shall my tongue utter deceit.

**27:5** Far be it from me that I should justify you! Until I die, I will not remove my integrity from myself.

**27:6** I hold fast to my righteousness and will not release it; my heart does not reproach me for any of my days.

**27:7** Let my enemy be as the wicked, and the one opposing me as the unrighteous.

**27:8** For what is the hope of the godless when he is cut off, when God takes away his soul?

**27:9** Will God listen to his outcry when distress comes upon him?

**27:10** Will he find his delight in the Almighty? Will he call upon God at all times?

**27:11** I will instruct you concerning the hand of God; what is with the Almighty I will not hide.

**27:12** Behold, all of you yourselves have seen it; why then are you so utterly vain?

**27:13** This is the portion of a wicked man with God, and the inheritance that ruthless men receive from the Almighty:

**27:14** If his children increase, it is for the sword, and his offspring will not have their fill of bread.

**27:15** His survivors will be buried by death, and his widows will not weep.

**27:16** Though he piles up silver like dust and lays up apparel like clay,

**27:17** he may lay it up, but the righteous will wear it, and the innocent will divide the silver.

**27:18** He builds his house like the moth, and like a shelter that a watchman fashions.

**27:19** He lies down rich, yet he will not be gathered; he opens his eyes, and he is gone.

**27:20** Terrors overtake him like waters; by night a whirlwind steals him away.

**27:21** The east wind lifts him up, and he departs; it hurls him out of his place.

**27:22** It hurls at him and shows no pity; he flees headlong from its power.

**27:23** It claps its hands at him, and hisses him out of his place."

## Translator Notes

- **27:8** — The Hebrew verb yivtsa' can mean either "when he is cut off" or "though he gains dishonest wealth," highlighting the futility of the godless when God claims his life.
- **27:15** — "Buried by death" reflects the Masoretic idiom bammawet, where "death" often denotes deadly pestilence leaving survivors without honorable burial or mourning.
- **27:19** — Follows the Masoretic Hebrew lo' ye'asef ("will not be gathered," meaning denied honorable burial or gathering to his fathers), whereas some ancient versions read "will do so no more."
- **27:22** — The grammatical subject in verses 22 and 23 continues the personified east wind from verse 21, though some interpreters understand the verbs impersonally or as referring to divine judgment.
