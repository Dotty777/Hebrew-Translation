## Text

**39:1** "Do you know the time when the wild goats of the rock bring forth? Do you watch when the does travail in birth?

**39:2** Can you number the months that they fulfill, and do you know the time when they give birth?

**39:3** They bow down, they bring forth their young, and they cast off their birth pangs.

**39:4** Their young grow strong, they mature in the open field; they go forth and do not return to them.

**39:5** Who has sent the wild donkey out free? And who has loosed the bonds of the swift wild donkey,

**39:6** whose house I have made the wilderness, and the salt land his dwelling places?

**39:7** He laughs at the tumult of the city; the shouts of the driver he does not hear.

**39:8** He ranges the mountains as his pasture, and seeks after every green herb.

**39:9** Will the wild ox be willing to serve you, or will he abide through the night beside your manger?

**39:10** Can you bind the wild ox in the furrow with his rope, or will he harrow the valleys behind you?

**39:11** Will you rely upon him because his strength is great, and leave your labor to him?

**39:12** Will you trust him that he will bring back your grain, and gather it to your threshing floor?

**39:13** The wing of the ostrich waves exultantly, yet are her pinions and plumage those of the stork?

**39:14** For she abandons her eggs to the ground, and lets them warm in the dust,

**39:15** forgetting that a foot may crush them, or a wild beast of the field may trample them.

**39:16** She treats her young harshly, as though they were not hers; though her labor is in vain, she feels no dread,

**39:17** because God has deprived her of wisdom, and has not apportioned understanding to her.

**39:18** When she lifts herself up on high, she laughs at the horse and his rider.

**39:19** Do you bestow might upon the horse? Do you clothe his neck with thunder?

**39:20** Do you make him bound like the locust? The majesty of his snorting is terror.

**39:21** He paws in the valley and exults in strength; he goes forth to meet the weapons.

**39:22** He mocks at dread and is not dismayed, and he does not turn back before the sword.

**39:23** Against him the quiver rattles, the flashing spear and the javelin.

**39:24** With quivering and rage he swallows the ground, and he cannot stand still when the trumpet sounds.

**39:25** At the sound of the trumpet he cries, 'Aha!' From afar he scents the battle, the thunder of the captains and the shouting.

**39:26** Does the hawk take wing by your understanding, spreading his wings toward the south?

**39:27** Is it at your command that the eagle mounts upward, and sets his nest on high?

**39:28** On the rock he dwells and lodges, upon the crag of the rock and the stronghold.

**39:29** From there he spies out his food; from afar his eyes behold it.

**39:30** His young ones gulp down blood; and where the slain are, there he is."

## Translator Notes

- **39:5** — The two Hebrew terms rendered wild donkey and swift wild donkey are poetic synonyms designating the untamed desert onager.
- **39:12** — The translation follows the qere, a transitive Hiphil verb meaning to bring back, whereas the ketiv reads the intransitive Qal form meaning to return.
- **39:13** — The Hebrew noun for stork derives from the root for kindness or devotion, creating an intentional contrast between the stork's maternal care and the ostrich's harshness toward her young.
- **39:19** — The Hebrew term rendered thunder literally evokes quivering or thunderous motion and is also widely understood to describe the warhorse's flowing, trembling mane.
