## Text

**3:1** After this Job opened his mouth and cursed his day.

**3:2** And Job answered and said:

**3:3** "May the day perish on which I was born, and the night that declared, 'A male child is conceived.'

**3:4** May that day be darkness; may God above not seek after it, and may no light shine upon it.

**3:5** May darkness and the shadow of death claim it; may a thick cloud settle over it; may the blackness of the day terrify it.

**3:6** As for that night, may thick gloom seize it; may it not rejoice among the days of the year; may it not enter into the number of the months.

**3:7** Behold, may that night be barren; may no sound of gladness enter into it.

**3:8** May those who curse the day denounce it, those who are ready to rouse Leviathan.

**3:9** May the stars of its twilight grow dark; may it hope for light and find none, nor behold the eyelids of the dawn;

**3:10** because it did not shut the doors of my mother's womb, nor hide trouble from my eyes.

**3:11** Why did I not die at birth, and expire as I came forth from the womb?

**3:12** Why did knees receive me, and why were there breasts, that I should nurse?

**3:13** For by now I would have lain down and been quiet; I would have slept, and then I would have found rest—

**3:14** with kings and counselors of the earth, who rebuilt ruins for themselves,

**3:15** or with princes who possessed gold, who filled their houses with silver;

**3:16** or why was I not hidden like a stillborn child, like infants who never saw the light?

**3:17** There the wicked cease from raging, and there the weary in strength find rest.

**3:18** Together the captives are at ease; they hear no voice of the taskmaster.

**3:19** The small and the great are there, and the servant is free from his master.

**3:20** Why is light given to one in misery, and life to those bitter of soul,

**3:21** who wait for death, yet it does not come, and dig for it more than for hidden treasures,

**3:22** who rejoice with exultation and are glad when they find the grave—

**3:23** to a man whose way is hidden, whom God has hedged in?

**3:24** For my sighing comes before my bread, and my groanings pour out like water.

**3:25** For the dread that I feared has come upon me, and what I trembled at has befallen me.

**3:26** I am not at ease, nor am I quiet, nor do I have rest, yet turmoil has come."

## Translator Notes

- **3:1** — His day refers idiomatically to the day of Job's birth, as unfolded in 3:3.
- **3:6** — Rejoice follows the Masoretic vocalization of the Hebrew verb; a different vocalization of the same consonants yields be joined or be united.
- **3:8** — Leviathan is a formidable sea creature of biblical poetic imagery representing untamed cosmic chaos (compare Job 41 and Psalm 74:14).
- **3:10** — My mother's womb renders the concise Hebrew idiom literally reading my womb, meaning the womb that bore Job.
