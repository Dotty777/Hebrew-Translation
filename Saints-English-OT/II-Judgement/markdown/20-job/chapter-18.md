## Text

**18:1** Then Bildad the Shuhite answered and said:

**18:2** How long until you put an end to words? Consider, and afterward we will speak.

**18:3** Why are we counted as beasts, and regarded as senseless in your sight?

**18:4** You who tear yourself in your anger—shall the earth be forsaken for your sake, or shall the rock be moved from its place?

**18:5** Indeed, the light of the wicked shall be extinguished, and the flame of his fire shall not shine.

**18:6** The light grows dark in his tent, and his lamp above him is put out.

**18:7** His vigorous strides are straitened, and his own counsel casts him down.

**18:8** For he is thrust into a net by his own feet, and he walks upon a snare.

**18:9** A trap seizes him by the heel, and a snare lays fast hold upon him.

**18:10** A noose is hidden for him in the ground, and a trap for him upon the path.

**18:11** Terrors frighten him on every side and chase him at his heels.

**18:12** His strength is famished, and calamity stands ready at his side.

**18:13** It devours the parts of his skin; the firstborn of death devours his limbs.

**18:14** He is torn from the tent in which he trusted, and brought before the king of terrors.

**18:15** What is none of his dwells in his tent; brimstone is scattered upon his habitation.

**18:16** His roots dry up beneath, and his branch withers above.

**18:17** His remembrance perishes from the earth, and he has no name upon the street.

**18:18** He is driven from light into darkness, and chased out of the inhabited world.

**18:19** He has neither offspring nor posterity among his people, nor any survivor where he dwelt.

**18:20** Those in the west are appalled at his day, and those in the east are seized with horror.

**18:21** Surely such are the dwellings of the unrighteous, and this is the place of him who does not know God.

## Translator Notes

- **18:13** — "The firstborn of death" is a poetic personification of the deadliest disease or most fatal affliction.
- **18:20** — The Hebrew terms for "those in the west" and "those in the east" literally mean those behind and those before, and may alternatively denote succeeding and preceding generations.
