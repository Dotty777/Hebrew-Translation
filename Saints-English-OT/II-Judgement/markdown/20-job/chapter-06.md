## Text

**6:1** Then Job answered and said:

**6:2** "Oh, that my vexation were surely weighed, and my calamity lifted together with it in the balances!

**6:3** For now it would be heavier than the sand of the seas; therefore my words have been rash.

**6:4** For the arrows of the Almighty are in me, and my spirit drinks their venom; the terrors of God marshal themselves against me.

**6:5** Does the wild donkey bray over green grass, or does the ox low over its fodder?

**6:6** Can that which is tasteless be eaten without salt, or is there any flavor in the white of an egg?

**6:7** My soul refuses to touch them; they are as loathsome food to me.

**6:8** Oh, that my petition might come to pass, and that God would grant what I long for:

**6:9** even that God would be pleased to crush me, that He would loose His hand and cut me off!

**6:10** Then this would still be my consolation—yes, I would exult in anguish though He does not spare—for I have not denied the words of the Holy One.

**6:11** What is my strength, that I should still wait? And what is my end, that I should prolong my life?

**6:12** Is my strength the strength of stones, or is my flesh made of bronze?

**6:13** Is it not so that there is no help within me, and that resourcefulness has been driven from me?

**6:14** To the despairing man, lovingkindness is due from his friend, even though he should forsake the fear of the Almighty.

**6:15** My brethren have dealt treacherously like a brook, like the channels of streams that pass away,

**6:16** which are darkened by reason of the ice, and in which the snow conceals itself.

**6:17** In the season when they grow warm, they vanish; when it is hot, they disappear from their place.

**6:18** The courses of their way turn aside; they go up into the wasteland and perish.

**6:19** The caravans of Tema looked; the companies of Sheba waited in hope for them.

**6:20** They were put to shame because they had been confident; they came there and were confounded.

**6:21** For now you have become as nothing; you behold terror and are afraid.

**6:22** Did I ever say, 'Give to me, or from your substance offer a bribe on my behalf,

**6:23** or deliver me from the hand of the adversary, or redeem me from the hand of the ruthless'?

**6:24** Teach me, and I will hold my peace; make me understand how I have erred.

**6:25** How forceful are upright words! Yet what does your reproving prove?

**6:26** Do you intend to reprove mere words, when the speeches of a despairing man are as wind?

**6:27** Indeed, you would even cast lots over the fatherless, and bargain over your friend.

**6:28** Now therefore, be pleased to look upon me; for surely I will not lie to your face.

**6:29** Turn now, let there be no injustice; yes, turn again, for my righteousness is still in this cause.

**6:30** Is there injustice upon my tongue? Cannot my palate discern calamity?"

## Translator Notes

- **6:2** — The translation follows the qere reading meaning 'and my calamity'; the ketiv preserves an older orthographic spelling of the same word.
- **6:6** — The rare Hebrew phrase traditionally rendered 'the white of an egg' is also understood by some lexicons as the bland juice of the purslane or mallow plant.
- **6:29** — The translation follows the qere plural imperative 'and turn again' addressed to Job's friends, rather than the ketiv spelling.
