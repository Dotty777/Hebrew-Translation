## Text

**7:1** "Is there not an appointed hard service for mortal man upon the earth? Are not his days like the days of a hired laborer?

**7:2** Like a servant who pants for the shade, and like a hired laborer who eagerly awaits his wages,

**7:3** so I have been allotted months of emptiness, and nights of misery have been appointed to me.

**7:4** When I lie down, I say, 'When shall I arise?' Yet the night stretches long, and I am filled with restless tossing until the dawn.

**7:5** My flesh is clothed with worms and crusts of dust; my skin hardens and breaks open afresh.

**7:6** My days are swifter than a weaver's shuttle, and they come to an end without hope.

**7:7** Remember that my life is but a breath; my eye will never again behold good.

**7:8** The eye of him who sees me shall gaze upon me no longer; Your eyes shall be upon me, and I shall be no more.

**7:9** As a cloud fades and passes away, so he who goes down to Sheol shall not ascend.

**7:10** He shall never return to his house, nor shall his place recognize him anymore.

**7:11** Indeed, I will not hold back my mouth; I will speak out in the distress of my spirit; I will utter my complaint in the bitterness of my soul.

**7:12** Am I the sea, or a sea monster, that You set a guard over me?

**7:13** When I say, 'My bed will comfort me, and my couch will bear away my complaint,'

**7:14** then You frighten me with dreams, and through visions You terrify me,

**7:15** so that my soul would choose strangling, and death rather than these bones of mine.

**7:16** I loathe my life; I would not live forever. Leave me alone, for my days are a mere breath.

**7:17** What is mortal man, that You should magnify him, and that You should set Your heart upon him,

**7:18** that You should visit him morning by morning, and test him moment by moment?

**7:19** How long will You not turn Your gaze from me, nor leave me alone until I swallow my saliva?

**7:20** If I have sinned, what have I done to You, O Watcher of mankind? Why have You made me Your target, so that I have become a burden to myself?

**7:21** Why then do You not forgive my transgression and cause my iniquity to pass away? For soon I shall lie down in the dust; You will search for me diligently, but I shall not be."

## Translator Notes

- **7:1** — The translation follows the qere poetic preposition 'upon' rather than the shorter prose ketiv spelling, with no difference in meaning.
- **7:5** — The translation follows the qere reading 'and crusts of dust' (literally clods of dust); the ketiv reflects an orthographic variant of the same noun.
- **7:20** — The Masoretic text reads 'to myself,' which ancient scribal tradition lists among the Tiqqune Soferim as a reverent substitution for 'to You.'
