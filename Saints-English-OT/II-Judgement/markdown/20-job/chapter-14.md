## Text

**14:1** Man, born of a woman, is short of days and filled with turmoil.

**14:2** Like a blossom he comes forth and withers away; he flees like a shadow and does not abide.

**14:3** Yet even upon such a one do You open Your eyes, and do You bring me into judgment with Yourself?

**14:4** Who can bring forth the clean from the unclean? Not one.

**14:5** Since his days are decreed, and the number of his months is with You, You have set his bounds so that he cannot cross beyond.

**14:6** Turn Your gaze from him that he may rest, until he fulfills his day like a hired laborer.

**14:7** For a tree possesses hope: if it is hewn down, it will sprout again, and its tender shoot will not cease.

**14:8** Though its root grows old in the earth, and its stump dies in the dust,

**14:9** yet at the scent of water it will blossom and put forth boughs like a young plant.

**14:10** But a mighty man dies and is laid low; indeed, man breathes his last, and where is he?

**14:11** As waters vanish from a sea, and a river dwindles and dries up,

**14:12** so a man lies down and does not rise again; until the heavens are no more, they will not awaken, nor will they be stirred from their sleep.

**14:13** Oh, that You would conceal me in Sheol, that You would keep me hidden until Your wrath turns away, that You would set an appointed time for me and remember me!

**14:14** If a man dies, will he live once more? All the days of my appointed service I would wait, until my relief should come.

**14:15** You would call, and I myself would answer You; You would yearn for the work of Your hands.

**14:16** For now You count my steps; do You not keep watch over my sin?

**14:17** Sealed up in a pouch is my transgression, and You coat over my iniquity.

**14:18** Yet surely a falling mountain crumbles away, and a rock is removed from its place;

**14:19** waters wear down stones, and their torrents wash away the dust of the earth; thus You destroy the hope of mortal man.

**14:20** You overpower him forever, and he departs; You alter his countenance and send him away.

**14:21** His sons rise to honor, and he does not know it; they are brought low, and he takes no notice of them.

**14:22** Only his flesh upon him feels pain, and his soul within him mourns.

## Translator Notes

- **14:3** — The Masoretic Text reads 'me,' shifting directly from mortal humanity in general to Job's own trial before God.
- **14:13** — Sheol is the Hebrew term for the subterranean realm of the dead.
- **14:14** — The Hebrew terms for 'appointed service' and 'relief' draw on the imagery of hard military duty and the awaited replacement of a soldier on watch.
- **14:16** — The second clause may be read as a rhetorical question depicting Job's present distress, or as continuing the wish of verses thirteen through fifteen ('For then You would count my steps; You would not keep watch over my sin').
