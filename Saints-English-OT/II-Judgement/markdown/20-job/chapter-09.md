## Text

**9:1** Then Job answered and said:

**9:2** "Truly I know that it is so; but how can a man be righteous before God?

**9:3** If one desired to contend with Him, he could not answer Him one time out of a thousand.

**9:4** He is wise in heart and mighty in strength; who has hardened himself against Him and prospered?

**9:5** He removes the mountains, and they do not know it, when He overturns them in His wrath;

**9:6** He shakes the earth out of its place, and its pillars tremble;

**9:7** He commands the sun, and it does not rise, and He seals up the stars;

**9:8** He alone stretches out the heavens and treads upon the waves of the sea;

**9:9** He makes the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades, and the chambers of the south;

**9:10** He does great things beyond searching out, and marvelous things without number.

**9:11** Behold, He passes by me, and I do not see Him; He moves on, yet I do not perceive Him.

**9:12** Behold, if He snatches away, who can turn Him back? Who can say to Him, 'What are You doing?'

**9:13** God will not turn back His anger; beneath Him the helpers of Rahab bow low.

**9:14** How much less then can I answer Him, and choose out my words with Him?

**9:15** Though I were righteous, I could not answer; I would make supplication to my Judge.

**9:16** If I called and He answered me, I would not believe that He had given ear to my voice.

**9:17** For He crushes me with a tempest and multiplies my wounds without cause.

**9:18** He will not allow me to catch my breath, but fills me with bitterness.

**9:19** If it is a matter of strength, behold, He is mighty; and if of justice, who will appoint a time for me?

**9:20** Though I were righteous, my own mouth would condemn me; though I were blameless, it would prove me perverse.

**9:21** I am blameless; I have no regard for myself; I despise my life.

**9:22** It is all one; therefore I say, 'He destroys both the blameless and the wicked.'

**9:23** If a scourge slays suddenly, He mocks at the trial of the innocent.

**9:24** The earth is given into the hand of the wicked; He covers the faces of its judges. If it is not He, who then could it be?

**9:25** Now my days are swifter than a runner; they flee away and see no good.

**9:26** They glide by like skiffs of reed, like an eagle swooping down upon its prey.

**9:27** If I say, 'I will forget my complaint, I will put off my sad countenance and be of good cheer,'

**9:28** I dread all my sufferings; I know that You will not hold me innocent.

**9:29** I shall be accounted wicked; why then do I labor in vain?

**9:30** If I wash myself with snow water and cleanse my hands with lye,

**9:31** yet You will plunge me into the pit, and my own garments will abhor me.

**9:32** For He is not a man, as I am, that I should answer Him, and that we should come together in judgment.

**9:33** There is no arbiter between us, who might lay his hand upon us both.

**9:34** Let Him take His rod away from me, and let His dread not terrify me.

**9:35** Then I would speak and not fear Him; for it is not so with me."

## Translator Notes

- **9:9** — The Hebrew names Ash, Kesil, and Kimah denote constellations traditionally identified as the Bear, Orion, and the Pleiades, while "the chambers of the south" refers to the stars of the southern hemisphere.
- **9:13** — Rahab is a poetic Hebrew name for a mythical sea monster symbolizing proud, rebellious power subdued beneath God.
- **9:19** — Translated according to the Masoretic Hebrew first-person suffix ("appoint a time for me"); some ancient versions read a third-person suffix ("summon Him").
- **9:26** — "Skiffs of reed" renders Hebrew oniyyot eveh, light papyrus boats renowned in antiquity for their speed.
- **9:30** — Translated according to the Masoretic Qere ("with snow water"); the Ketiv reads "in snow."
