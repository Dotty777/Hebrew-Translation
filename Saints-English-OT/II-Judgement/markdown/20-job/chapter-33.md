## Text

**33:1** "But now, Job, hear my speech, I pray, and give ear to all my words.

**33:2** Behold now, I have opened my mouth; my tongue speaks in my palate.

**33:3** My sayings come from the uprightness of my heart, and my lips utter knowledge purely.

**33:4** The Spirit of God made me, and the breath of the Almighty gives life to me.

**33:5** If you are able, answer me; set your case in order before me, and take your stand.

**33:6** Behold, before God I am just as you are; I also have been pinched out of clay.

**33:7** Behold, no dread of me shall terrify you, nor shall my pressure weigh heavy upon you.

**33:8** Surely you have spoken in my hearing, and I have heard the sound of your words, saying,

**33:9** 'I am pure, without transgression; I am clean, and there is no iniquity in me.

**33:10** Behold, He finds pretexts against me; He counts me as His enemy;

**33:11** He sets my feet in the stocks; He watches all my paths.'

**33:12** Behold, in this you are not right; I will answer you, for God is greater than mortal man.

**33:13** Why do you contend against Him, because He does not answer for any of His words?

**33:14** For God speaks in one way, and in two, though one does not perceive it.

**33:15** In a dream, a vision of the night, when deep sleep falls upon men, in slumberings upon the bed,

**33:16** then He opens the ears of men, and seals their instruction,

**33:17** in order to turn man from his deed, and to conceal pride from a man;

**33:18** He keeps back his soul from the Pit, and his life from passing away by the sword.

**33:19** A man is also reproved with pain upon his bed, with unceasing ache in the multitude of his bones,

**33:20** so that his life loathes bread, and his soul abhors choice food.

**33:21** His flesh wastes away out of sight, and his bones that were unseen are laid bare.

**33:22** Indeed, his soul draws near to the Pit, and his life to the bringers of death.

**33:23** If there is for him a messenger, a mediator, one among a thousand, to declare to man his uprightness,

**33:24** then He shows grace to him and says, 'Rescue him from descending into the Pit; I have found a ransom.'

**33:25** His flesh shall become fresher than in youth; he shall return to the days of his youthful vigor.

**33:26** When he prays to God, He will accept him; he shall behold His face with shouts of joy, and He will restore to mortal man his righteousness.

**33:27** He looks upon men and says, 'I have sinned and perverted what is right, and it did not profit me.'

**33:28** He has redeemed his soul from passing into the Pit, and his life shall look upon the light.

**33:29** Behold, God does all these things, twice, even three times, with a man,

**33:30** to turn back his soul from the Pit, that he may be enlightened with the light of the living.

**33:31** Pay heed, O Job, listen to me; keep silent, and I will speak.

**33:32** If you have words to say, answer me; speak, for I desire to justify you.

**33:33** If not, then listen to me; keep silent, and I will teach you wisdom."

## Translator Notes

- **33:6** — Pinched out renders a rare verb describing a potter pinching off a portion of clay to form a vessel.
- **33:19** — Following the qere (read form), the multitude of his bones; the ketiv (written form) reads the strife of his bones.
- **33:21** — Following the qere (read form), are laid bare (or protrude); the ketiv (written form) reads the noun bareness.
- **33:27** — The verb looks upon follows the Masoretic vocalization from the root meaning to behold; others connect it with the verb to sing (he sings before men).
- **33:28** — Following the qere (read form), third-person his soul and his life; the ketiv (written form) reads first-person my soul and my life, continuing the direct confession of verse 27.
