## Text

**41:1** "Behold, any hope concerning him is in vain; is one not cast down even at the sight of him?

**41:2** No one is so fierce that he dares to stir him up; who then is able to stand before Me?

**41:3** Who has first given to Me, that I should repay him? All that is under the whole heaven is Mine.

**41:4** Concerning him, shall I keep silent about his limbs, or the matter of his mighty power, or the grace of his frame?

**41:5** Who can strip off the face of his outer covering? Who can enter within his double bridle?

**41:6** Who can open the doors of his face? All around his teeth is terror.

**41:7** Rows of shields are his pride, shut up closely as with a tight seal.

**41:8** One lies so close to another that no air can pass between them.

**41:9** They are joined one to another; they interlock and cannot be separated.

**41:10** His sneezings flash forth light, and his eyes are like the eyelids of the dawn.

**41:11** Out of his mouth proceed flaming torches; sparks of fire leap forth.

**41:12** Out of his nostrils goes forth smoke, as from a boiling pot and burning rushes.

**41:13** His breath kindles coals, and a flame goes forth from his mouth.

**41:14** Strength abides in his neck, and dismay dances before him.

**41:15** The folds of his flesh cleave tightly together; they are cast firm upon him and cannot be moved.

**41:16** His heart is cast hard as a stone, yes, cast hard as a lower millstone.

**41:17** When he rises up, the mighty are terrified; because of his crashings, they lose their bearings.

**41:18** Though the sword reaches him, it cannot prevail, nor the spear, the dart, or the javelin.

**41:19** He counts iron as straw, and bronze as rotten wood.

**41:20** The arrow cannot put him to flight; slingstones are turned into stubble for him.

**41:21** Clubs are regarded as stubble, and he laughs at the rattling of the javelin.

**41:22** His underparts are sharp potsherds; he spreads himself like a threshing sledge upon the mire.

**41:23** He causes the deep to boil like a pot; he makes the sea like a cauldron of ointment.

**41:24** Behind him he makes a shining wake; one would think the deep to be white-haired.

**41:25** Upon the earth there is none like him, one made without fear.

**41:26** He looks upon every lofty thing; he is king over all the sons of pride."

## Translator Notes

- **41:4** — The Masoretic Qere reads lo, meaning concerning him or for him, rendered here as a rhetorical question; the Ketiv reads lo with an alef, meaning not, yielding I will not keep silent concerning his limbs.
- **41:5** — The expression double bridle literally renders kefel risno and refers poetically to Leviathan's formidable double jaws or layered armor.
- **41:17** — The Hebrew elim denotes the mighty or heavenly beings, and the verb yitchatta'u means to lose one's bearings or shrink back in terror.
- **41:26** — Sons of pride renders bene shachats, an idiom for the proudest and fiercest wild creatures.
