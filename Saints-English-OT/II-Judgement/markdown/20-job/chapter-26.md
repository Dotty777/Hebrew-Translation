## Text

**26:1** Then Job answered and said:

**26:2** "How you have helped the one lacking strength, and delivered the arm without might!

**26:3** How you have counseled the one lacking wisdom, and made sound insight known in abundance!

**26:4** To whom have you uttered words, and whose breath has come forth from you?

**26:5** The shades of the dead tremble beneath the waters and those who dwell in them.

**26:6** Sheol lies naked before Him, and Abaddon has no covering.

**26:7** He stretches out the north over the void; He suspends the earth upon nothing.

**26:8** He binds up the waters in His thick clouds, yet the cloud does not burst beneath them.

**26:9** He veils the face of the throne, and spreads His cloud over it.

**26:10** He has inscribed a circle upon the face of the waters, to the boundary of light and darkness.

**26:11** The pillars of the heavens shake, and are astounded at His rebuke.

**26:12** With His might He stilled the sea, and by His understanding He crushed Rahab.

**26:13** By His Spirit the heavens became fair; His hand pierced through the fleeing serpent.

**26:14** Behold, these are only the outer edges of His ways, and how faint a whisper is heard of Him! But who can comprehend the thunder of His mighty power?"

## Translator Notes

- **26:4** — The Hebrew phrase 'et-mi may be understood either as "To whom" or "With whose help," ironically questioning the source of Bildad's speech.
- **26:5** — "The shades of the dead" translates Hebrew rephaim, referring to the departed spirits inhabiting the underworld beneath the cosmic waters.
- **26:6** — "Abaddon" means "Destruction" and stands in poetic parallel with Sheol as a designation for the realm of the dead.
- **26:12** — Translates the Masoretic Qere ubitvunato ("and by His understanding") rather than the Ketiv spelling ubetovanto. The verb raga' can mean either "stilled" or "stirred up," and "Rahab" denotes a primordial sea monster symbolizing chaotic pride subdued by God.
