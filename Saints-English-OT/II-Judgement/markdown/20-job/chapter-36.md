## Text

**36:1** And Elihu continued and said:

**36:2** "Wait a little for me, and I will make it known to you, for there are yet words on behalf of God.

**36:3** I will bring my knowledge from afar, and I will ascribe righteousness to my Maker.

**36:4** For indeed my words are not falsehood; one complete in knowledge is with you.

**36:5** Behold, God is mighty, yet He does not despise any; He is mighty in strength of heart.

**36:6** He does not keep the wicked alive, but grants justice to the afflicted.

**36:7** He does not withhold His eyes from the righteous, but with kings upon the throne He seats them forever, and they are exalted.

**36:8** And if they are bound in chains, caught in the cords of affliction,

**36:9** then He declares to them their deeds and their transgressions, that they have acted arrogantly.

**36:10** He also opens their ears to discipline, and commands that they turn back from iniquity.

**36:11** If they listen and serve Him, they shall fulfill their days in prosperity, and their years in pleasantness.

**36:12** But if they do not listen, they shall pass away by the sword, and expire without knowledge.

**36:13** But the godless in heart harbor wrath; they do not cry for help when He binds them.

**36:14** Their soul dies in youth, and their life ends among the cult prostitutes.

**36:15** He delivers the afflicted by his affliction, and opens their ear by oppression.

**36:16** Indeed, He also drew you out of the mouth of distress into a broad place where there is no confinement, and what was set upon your table was full of rich food.

**36:17** Yet you are full of the judgment of the wicked; judgment and justice lay hold upon you.

**36:18** Take heed lest wrath entice you under a blow, and do not let a great ransom turn you aside.

**36:19** Will your cry for help avail to keep you out of distress, or all the exertions of strength?

**36:20** Do not long for the night, when peoples are taken away in their place.

**36:21** Take heed; do not turn to iniquity, for you have chosen this rather than affliction.

**36:22** Behold, God is exalted in His might; who is an instructor like Him?

**36:23** Who has prescribed His way for Him, or who has said, 'You have done iniquity'?

**36:24** Remember to extol His work, of which men have sung.

**36:25** All mankind has looked upon it; mortal man beholds it from afar.

**36:26** Behold, God is great, and we do not know Him; the number of His years is unsearchable.

**36:27** For He draws up the drops of water; they distill as rain from His mist,

**36:28** which the clouds pour down and shower abundantly upon mankind.

**36:29** Indeed, can anyone understand the spreading of the clouds, or the thunderings of His pavilion?

**36:30** Behold, He spreads His lightning around Him, and covers the roots of the sea.

**36:31** For by these He judges the peoples; He provides food in abundance.

**36:32** He covers both hands with lightning, and charges it to strike the mark.

**36:33** Its thunder declares concerning Him, and the cattle also concerning the storm that rises."

## Translator Notes

- **36:14** — Hebrew qedeshim denotes male cult prostitutes associated with pagan shrine practices, symbolizing a life wasted and cut short in moral ruin.
- **36:18** — The Hebrew wording is terse; the noun safeq can refer either to a chastising blow or to derisive hand-clapping and scoffing.
- **36:30** — Hebrew or literally means light and is used in this storm passage (36:30, 32; 37:3, 11, 15) for lightning; "roots of the sea" poetically denotes the ocean's deepest foundations.
- **36:33** — Following the Masoretic Text, "Its thunder" (re'o, roar) announces God's coming in the storm, and even the livestock sense what is rising (oleh).
