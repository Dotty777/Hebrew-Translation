## Text

**35:1** Again Elihu answered and said:

**35:2** "Do you consider this to be just, that you have said, 'My righteousness is more than that of God'?

**35:3** For you ask, 'What advantage is it to you? What profit shall I gain, more than if I had sinned?'

**35:4** I will answer you with words, and your companions with you.

**35:5** Look to the heavens and see; and behold the clouds, which are higher than you.

**35:6** If you sin, what do you accomplish against Him? And if your transgressions are multiplied, what do you do to Him?

**35:7** If you are righteous, what do you bestow upon Him? Or what does He receive from your hand?

**35:8** Your wickedness affects a man like yourself, and your righteousness a son of man.

**35:9** Because of the multitude of oppressions they cry out; they cry for help from the arm of the mighty.

**35:10** Yet no one says, 'Where is God my Maker, who gives songs in the night,

**35:11** who instructs us more than the beasts of the earth, and makes us wiser than the birds of the heavens?'

**35:12** There they cry out, yet He does not answer, because of the pride of evil men.

**35:13** Surely God will not listen to an empty cry, nor will the Almighty regard it.

**35:14** Although you say that you do not behold Him, yet the case is before Him; therefore wait for Him.

**35:15** And now, because His anger has not punished, nor has He taken great notice of arrogance,

**35:16** therefore Job opens his mouth in vain; he multiplies words without knowledge."

## Translator Notes

- **35:2** — Hebrew literally reads "my righteousness more than God," expressing Elihu's charge that Job claims to be more righteous than God.
- **35:3** — The Hebrew shifts abruptly from second person ("to you") to first person ("shall I gain"), and literally reads "from my sin," meaning "more than if I had sinned."
- **35:15** — The Hebrew noun rendered "arrogance" occurs only here in the Old Testament and is variously understood as "arrogance," "transgression," or "folly."
