## Text

**21:1** Then Job answered and said:

**21:2** "Listen attentively to my words, and let this be your consolation.

**21:3** Bear with me that I may speak, and after I have spoken, keep on mocking.

**21:4** As for me, is my complaint against man? And why then should my spirit not be impatient?

**21:5** Look upon me and be appalled, and lay your hand over your mouth.

**21:6** Even when I think of it, I am dismayed, and trembling takes hold of my flesh.

**21:7** Why do the wicked live on, grow old, and also increase in power?

**21:8** Their offspring are established in their presence with them, and their descendants before their eyes.

**21:9** Their houses are safe from dread, and the rod of God is not upon them.

**21:10** His bull breeds without fail; his cow calves and does not miscarry.

**21:11** They send out their little ones like a flock, and their children dance about.

**21:12** They sing to the tambourine and harp, and rejoice at the sound of the flute.

**21:13** They finish their days in prosperity, and in an instant they go down to Sheol.

**21:14** Yet they say to God, 'Depart from us, for we do not desire the knowledge of Your ways.

**21:15** Who is the Almighty, that we should serve Him? And what profit do we have if we pray to Him?'

**21:16** Behold, their prosperity is not in their own hand; the counsel of the wicked is far from me.

**21:17** How often is the lamp of the wicked put out, and their calamity comes upon them—the sorrows He allots in His anger?

**21:18** Are they like straw before the wind, and like chaff that a storm carries away?

**21:19** God lays up his iniquity for his children; let Him repay him himself, so that he may know it.

**21:20** Let his own eyes behold his ruin, and let him drink of the wrath of the Almighty.

**21:21** For what concern has he for his household after him, when the number of his months is cut off?

**21:22** Shall anyone teach God knowledge, seeing He judges those on high?

**21:23** One dies in the fullness of his vigor, wholly at ease and tranquil;

**21:24** his pails are full of milk, and the marrow of his bones is well moistened.

**21:25** And another dies in bitterness of soul, never having tasted good things.

**21:26** Together they lie down in the dust, and the worms cover over them.

**21:27** Behold, I know your thoughts, and the schemes by which you would wrong me.

**21:28** For you say, 'Where is the house of the nobleman, and where is the tent of the dwellings of the wicked?'

**21:29** Have you not asked those who travel the way, and do you not recognize their signs?

**21:30** that the wicked man is spared in the day of calamity, and they are led away in the day of wrath?

**21:31** Who declares his way to his face, and who repays him for what he has done?

**21:32** Yet he is borne to the grave, and watch is kept over his tomb.

**21:33** The clods of the valley are sweet to him; everyone follows after him, just as those before him are without number.

**21:34** How then can you comfort me with emptiness, since in your answers nothing remains but falsehood?"

## Translator Notes

- **21:13** — Following the Masoretic qere ("they finish" or "complete"); the ketiv reads "they wear out" or "spend."
- **21:19** — The opening clause is widely understood as Job citing his friends' own argument before rebutting it in the second half of the verse.
- **21:24** — The rare Hebrew term rendered "his pails" is uncertain and is alternatively understood by some interpreters as "his body" or "his sides."
- **21:30** — The Hebrew phrasing may mean either that the wicked man is "spared in" the day of calamity or that he is "reserved for" it; the former fits the flow of Job's argument in verses 29 through 33.
