## Text

**1:1** Now it came to pass after the death of Saul, when David had returned from striking down the Amalekites, that David remained in Ziklag two days.

**1:2** And it came to pass on the third day, behold, a man came from the camp, from Saul, with his garments torn and earth upon his head. And when he came to David, he fell to the ground and bowed down.

**1:3** And David said to him, "From where are you coming?" And he replied to him, "I have escaped out of the camp of Israel."

**1:4** And David said to him, "How did the matter turn out? Tell me, please." And he replied, "The people have fled from the battle, and moreover, many of the people have fallen and died; and Saul and Jonathan his son are dead as well."

**1:5** Then David said to the young man who told him, "How do you know that Saul and Jonathan his son are dead?"

**1:6** And the young man who told him said, "As I happened by chance to be upon Mount Gilboa, behold, Saul leaned upon his spear; and behold, the chariots and the horsemen pressed hard after him.

**1:7** And when he looked behind him, he saw me and called to me. And I answered, 'Here I am.'

**1:8** And he said to me, 'Who are you?' And I said to him, 'I am an Amalekite.'

**1:9** Then he said to me, 'Stand over me, please, and slay me, for anguish has seized me, yet my life is still wholly in me.'

**1:10** So I stood over him and slew him, because I knew that he could not live after he had fallen. And I took the crown that was upon his head and the armlet that was upon his arm, and have brought them here to my lord."

**1:11** Then David took hold of his own garments and tore them, and likewise did all the men who were with him.

**1:12** And they mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul and for Jonathan his son, and for the people of the LORD and for the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

**1:13** Then David said to the young man who had told him, "Where are you from?" And he answered, "I am the son of a sojourner, an Amalekite."

**1:14** And David said to him, "How is it that you were not afraid to stretch out your hand to destroy the LORD's anointed?"

**1:15** Then David called one of the young men and said, "Draw near and strike him down!" And he struck him so that he died.

**1:16** And David said to him, "Your blood be upon your own head, for your own mouth has testified against you, saying, 'I have slain the LORD's anointed.'"

**1:17** Then David lamented with this lamentation over Saul and over Jonathan his son,

**1:18** and he commanded them to teach the children of Judah the Bow; behold, it is written in the Book of Jashar:

**1:19** "The splendor of Israel lies slain upon your high places! How the mighty have fallen!

**1:20** Do not tell it in Gath, do not proclaim it in the streets of Ashkelon—lest the daughters of the Philistines rejoice, lest the daughters of the uncircumcised exult.

**1:21** O mountains of Gilboa, let there be neither dew nor rain upon you, nor fields of offerings! For there the shield of the mighty was defiled—the shield of Saul, not anointed with oil.

**1:22** From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan did not turn back, and the sword of Saul did not return empty.

**1:23** Saul and Jonathan were beloved and pleasant in their lives, and in their death they were not parted; they were swifter than eagles, they were mightier than lions.

**1:24** O daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who clothed you in scarlet with finery, who set ornaments of gold upon your apparel.

**1:25** How the mighty have fallen in the midst of the battle! Jonathan lies slain upon your high places!

**1:26** I am distressed for you, my brother Jonathan; you have been exceedingly dear to me; your love to me was more wondrous than the love of women.

**1:27** How the mighty have fallen, and the weapons of war have perished!"

## Translator Notes

- **1:8** — The Ketiv reads "and he said," whereas the Qere (translated here) reads "and I said," matching the context of the Amalekite's reply.
- **1:9** — The rare Hebrew noun translated "anguish" occurs only here in the Hebrew Bible and may denote agony, convulsion, or dizziness.
- **1:16** — The Ketiv uses the plural "your bloods" (an idiom for bloodguilt), while the Qere (translated here) reads the singular "your blood."
- **1:18** — Literally "Bow," referring to the title of David's lament; the Book of Jashar ("Book of the Upright") is an ancient collection of Hebrew poems also cited in Joshua 10:13.
