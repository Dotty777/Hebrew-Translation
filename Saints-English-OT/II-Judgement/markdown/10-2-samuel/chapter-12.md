## Text

**12:1** Then the LORD sent Nathan to David. And he came to him and said to him, "There were two men in one city, the one rich and the other poor.

**12:2** The rich man had flocks and herds in exceeding abundance.

**12:3** But the poor man had nothing at all, except one small ewe lamb which he had bought and reared; and it grew up together with him and with his children. It would eat of his own morsel and drink from his own cup and lie in his bosom, and it was to him like a daughter.

**12:4** Now a wayfarer came to the rich man, and he was unwilling to take from his own flock or from his own herd to prepare for the traveler who had come to him; but he took the poor man's ewe lamb and prepared it for the man who had come to him."

**12:5** Then David's anger was kindled exceedingly against the man, and he said to Nathan, "As the LORD lives, the man who has done this is worthy of death!

**12:6** And he shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing and because he showed no pity."

**12:7** Then Nathan said to David, "You are the man! Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: 'I anointed you as king over Israel, and I rescued you from the hand of Saul.

**12:8** And I gave you the house of your master and the wives of your master into your bosom, and I gave you the house of Israel and Judah; and if that were too little, I would also have added to you as much again and more.

**12:9** Why have you despised the word of the LORD, to do what is evil in My sight? You have struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword; you have taken his wife to be your wife, and him you have slain by the sword of the sons of Ammon.

**12:10** Now therefore, the sword shall not depart from your house forever, because you have despised Me and have taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife.'

**12:11** Thus says the LORD: 'Behold, I am raising up calamity against you out of your own house; and I will take your wives before your eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he shall lie with your wives in the sight of this sun.

**12:12** For you acted in secret, but I will do this thing before all Israel and before the sun.'"

**12:13** Then David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the LORD." And Nathan said to David, "The LORD also has taken away your sin; you shall not die.

**12:14** Nevertheless, because by this deed you have given the enemies of the LORD great occasion to blaspheme, the son also who is born to you shall surely die."

**12:15** Then Nathan departed to his house. And the LORD struck the child whom Uriah's wife bore to David, and he became grievously ill.

**12:16** Therefore David sought God on behalf of the child; and David kept a fast, and went in and passed the night lying upon the ground.

**12:17** And the elders of his house stood over him to raise him up from the ground, but he was unwilling, nor would he eat bread with them.

**12:18** Now it came to pass on the seventh day that the child died. And the servants of David were afraid to tell him that the child was dead, for they said, "Behold, while the child was still alive, we spoke to him, and he would not listen to our voice. How then can we say to him, 'The child is dead'? He may do himself harm!"

**12:19** When David saw that his servants were whispering among themselves, David perceived that the child was dead. Therefore David said to his servants, "Is the child dead?" And they said, "He is dead."

**12:20** Then David arose from the ground, washed and anointed himself, and changed his garments; and he went into the house of the LORD and worshiped. Afterward he came to his own house, and when he asked, they set food before him, and he ate.

**12:21** Then his servants said to him, "What is this thing that you have done? For the sake of the child while he was alive you fasted and wept, but when the child died, you arose and ate bread."

**12:22** And he said, "While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept; for I said, 'Who knows? The LORD may be gracious to me, that the child may live.'

**12:23** But now he is dead; why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me."

**12:24** Then David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and went in to her and lay with her; and she bore a son, and she called his name Solomon. And the LORD loved him.

**12:25** And He sent word by the hand of Nathan the prophet, who called his name Jedidiah, because of the LORD.

**12:26** Now Joab fought against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and captured the royal city.

**12:27** And Joab sent messengers to David, and said, "I have fought against Rabbah, and I have also captured the city of waters.

**12:28** Now therefore, gather the rest of the people together, encamp against the city, and capture it, lest I myself capture the city and my name be called over it."

**12:29** So David gathered all the people together and went to Rabbah, and fought against it and captured it.

**12:30** And he took the crown of their king from off his head; its weight was a talent of gold, with precious stones, and it was placed upon David's head. Also he brought out the spoil of the city in exceedingly great abundance.

**12:31** And he brought out the people who were in it, and set them to the saw, and to iron picks, and to iron axes, and made them serve at the brickkiln. Thus he did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

## Translator Notes

- **12:9** — The Qere reads 'in My sight' (followed here), whereas the Ketiv reads 'in His sight.'
- **12:14** — The Hebrew literally reads 'the enemies of the LORD,' traditionally recognized as a scribal euphemism avoiding direct mention of scorning the LORD Himself.
- **12:22** — The Qere reads a waw-consecutive perfect ('and the LORD will be gracious to me'), while the Ketiv reads an imperfect ('will the LORD be gracious to me'); both convey David's hope for divine mercy.
- **12:24** — The Qere reads 'and she called' (Bathsheba, followed here), whereas the Ketiv reads 'and he called' (David).
- **12:31** — The Qere reads 'at the brickkiln' (followed here), whereas the Ketiv reads a consonantal form of uncertain meaning; the clause describes assigning Ammonite captives to forced labor.
