## Text

**18:1** And David mustered the people who were with him, and set captains of thousands and captains of hundreds over them.

**18:2** Then David sent out the people: one third under the hand of Joab, one third under the hand of Abishai the son of Zeruiah, the brother of Joab, and one third under the hand of Ittai the Gittite. And the king said to the people, "I also will surely go out with you."

**18:3** But the people answered, "You shall not go out! For if we indeed flee, they will not give a thought to us; and if half of us perish, they will not care about us. For now you are worth ten thousand of us; therefore now it is better that you be ready to aid us from the city."

**18:4** And the king said to them, "Whatever seems good in your eyes I will do." So the king stood beside the gate, while all the people marched out by hundreds and by thousands.

**18:5** Now the king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, saying, "Deal gently for my sake with the young man Absalom." And all the people heard when the king charged all the captains concerning Absalom.

**18:6** So the people went out into the field to meet Israel, and the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim.

**18:7** There the people of Israel were defeated before the servants of David, and the slaughter there on that day was great—twenty thousand.

**18:8** For the battle there spread out over the face of all the countryside, and the forest devoured more of the people than the sword devoured on that day.

**18:9** Then Absalom chanced to encounter the servants of David. Now Absalom was riding on a mule, and the mule went beneath the thick boughs of a great terebinth tree, and his head caught fast in the terebinth; so he was left hanging between heaven and earth, while the mule that was under him went on.

**18:10** And a certain man saw it and reported to Joab, saying, "Behold, I saw Absalom hanging in a terebinth tree!"

**18:11** Then Joab said to the man who had told him, "Behold, you saw him! Why then did you not strike him down to the ground there? I would have given you ten pieces of silver and a belt."

**18:12** But the man said to Joab, "Even though I were to weigh a thousand pieces of silver into my palms, I would not stretch forth my hand against the king's son; for in our hearing the king commanded you and Abishai and Ittai, saying, 'Take care, whoever you are, of the young man Absalom!'

**18:13** Otherwise I would have dealt treacherously against my own life—for nothing is hidden from the king—and you yourself would have stood aloof."

**18:14** Then Joab said, "I will not linger thus before you." And he took three javelins in his hand and thrust them into the heart of Absalom while he was still alive in the heart of the terebinth tree.

**18:15** And ten young men who bore Joab's armor surrounded Absalom, and struck him and slew him.

**18:16** Then Joab blew the trumpet, and the people returned from pursuing after Israel; for Joab restrained the people.

**18:17** And they took Absalom and cast him into the great pit in the forest, and heaped a very great pile of stones over him. Meanwhile all Israel fled, every man to his tent.

**18:18** Now Absalom in his lifetime had taken and set up for himself a pillar, which is in the King's Valley; for he had said, "I have no son to keep my name in remembrance." He called the pillar after his own name, and to this day it is called Absalom's Monument.

**18:19** Then Ahimaaz the son of Zadok said, "Please let me run and bring tidings to the king, that the LORD has vindicated him from the hand of his enemies."

**18:20** And Joab said to him, "You shall not be a bearer of tidings today, but you may bear tidings on another day. Today you shall bear no tidings, because the king's son is dead."

**18:21** Then Joab said to the Cushite, "Go, tell the king what you have seen." So the Cushite bowed low to Joab and ran.

**18:22** Then Ahimaaz the son of Zadok spoke yet again to Joab, saying, "Come what may, please let me also run after the Cushite." And Joab said, "Why would you run, my son, since no reward for the tidings awaits you?"

**18:23** "Come what may, I will run." So he said to him, "Run." Then Ahimaaz ran by the way of the plain and outran the Cushite.

**18:24** Now David was sitting between the two gates. And the watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall, and lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a man was running alone.

**18:25** Then the watchman cried out and told the king. And the king said, "If he is alone, there are tidings in his mouth." And he kept coming nearer and nearer.

**18:26** Then the watchman saw another man running, and the watchman called to the gatekeeper and said, "Behold, a man is running alone!" And the king said, "This one also brings tidings."

**18:27** And the watchman said, "I observe that the running of the first is like the running of Ahimaaz the son of Zadok." And the king said, "He is a good man, and he comes with good tidings."

**18:28** Then Ahimaaz cried out and said to the king, "All is well!" And he bowed down before the king with his face to the earth, and said, "Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delivered up the men who lifted up their hand against my lord the king!"

**18:29** And the king asked, "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" And Ahimaaz answered, "I saw a great tumult when Joab sent the king's servant and your servant, but I did not know what it was."

**18:30** And the king said, "Turn aside and stand here." So he turned aside and stood still.

**18:31** And behold, the Cushite arrived; and the Cushite said, "Let my lord the king receive tidings, for the LORD has vindicated you this day from the hand of all those who rose up against you."

**18:32** And the king said to the Cushite, "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" And the Cushite answered, "May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who rise up against you for evil, be like that young man!"

## Translator Notes

- **18:3** — The Masoretic Qere reads the Qal infinitive 'to aid' (la'azor), whereas the Ketiv writes a Hiphil form with the same sense. The Masoretic text also reads 'for now' ('attah) rather than 'for you' ('attah), with the subject 'you' understood from the context of the plea.
- **18:8** — The Masoretic Qere reads the feminine singular participle 'spread out' (nafotset) in grammatical agreement with the singular noun 'battle,' while the Ketiv writes a feminine plural form.
- **18:12** — The Masoretic Qere reads 'even though' (welu) rather than the Ketiv's orthographic variant spelling (welo). The Hebrew text also reads 'whoever you may be' (mi) rather than 'for my sake' (li) as in verse 5.
- **18:13** — The Masoretic Qere reads 'against my own life' (benafshi), whereas the Ketiv reads 'against his life' (benafsho).
- **18:20** — In the Masoretic text, the word 'so' (ken) stands in the consonantal text (Ketiv) but is omitted in the traditional reading (Qere), yielding the causal clause 'because the king's son is dead.'
