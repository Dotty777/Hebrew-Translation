## Text

**16:1** Now David had passed a little beyond the summit, and behold, Ziba the servant of Mephibosheth came to meet him with a pair of saddled donkeys, and upon them two hundred loaves of bread, one hundred clusters of raisins, one hundred summer fruits, and a skin of wine.

**16:2** Then the king said to Ziba, "What do you mean by these?" And Ziba said, "The donkeys are for the king's household to ride on, the bread and summer fruit are for the young men to eat, and the wine is for the weary to drink in the wilderness."

**16:3** Then the king said, "And where is your master's son?" And Ziba said to the king, "Behold, he remains in Jerusalem, for he said, 'Today the house of Israel will restore to me the kingdom of my father.'"

**16:4** So the king said to Ziba, "Behold, all that belongs to Mephibosheth is yours." And Ziba said, "I bow down; may I find favor in your sight, my lord the king."

**16:5** Now when King David came as far as Bahurim, behold, a man from the family of the house of Saul came out from there, whose name was Shimei the son of Gera; he came forth, cursing continually as he came.

**16:6** And he cast stones at David and at all the servants of King David, though all the people and all the mighty men were on his right hand and on his left.

**16:7** And thus Shimei spoke as he cursed: "Get out, get out, you man of blood and worthless man!

**16:8** The LORD has brought back upon you all the blood of the house of Saul, in whose place you have reigned, and the LORD has delivered the kingdom into the hand of Absalom your son. And behold, you are in your own calamity, because you are a man of blood!"

**16:9** Then Abishai the son of Zeruiah said to the king, "Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Please let me go over and take off his head."

**16:10** But the king said, "What have I to do with you, you sons of Zeruiah? So let him curse, because the LORD has said to him, 'Curse David.' Who then shall say, 'Why have you done this?'"

**16:11** And David said to Abishai and to all his servants, "Behold, my son who came forth from my own body seeks my life; how much more now may this Benjamite! Leave him alone, and let him curse, for the LORD has spoken to him.

**16:12** Perhaps the LORD will look upon my eye, and the LORD will repay me good in return for his cursing this day."

**16:13** So David and his men went along the road, while Shimei walked along the hillside opposite him, cursing as he went, casting stones toward him, and flinging dust.

**16:14** And the king and all the people who were with him arrived weary, and he refreshed himself there.

**16:15** Meanwhile Absalom and all the people, the men of Israel, came to Jerusalem, and Ahithophel was with him.

**16:16** And it came to pass, when Hushai the Archite, the friend of David, came to Absalom, that Hushai said to Absalom, "Long live the king! Long live the king!"

**16:17** So Absalom said to Hushai, "Is this your loyalty to your friend? Why did you not go with your friend?"

**16:18** And Hushai said to Absalom, "No, rather, the one whom the LORD and this people and all the men of Israel have chosen, his I will be, and with him I will abide.

**16:19** And secondly, whom should I serve? Should it not be before his son? As I have served before your father, so will I be before you."

**16:20** Then Absalom said to Ahithophel, "Give your counsel: what shall we do?"

**16:21** And Ahithophel said to Absalom, "Go in to your father's concubines, whom he left to keep the house; and all Israel will hear that you have made yourself odious to your father, and the hands of all who are with you will be strengthened."

**16:22** So they pitched a tent for Absalom upon the roof, and Absalom went in to his father's concubines in the sight of all Israel.

**16:23** Now the counsel of Ahithophel, which he gave in those days, was as when a man inquires of the word of God; so was all the counsel of Ahithophel both with David and with Absalom.

## Translator Notes

- **16:2** — The Hebrew Qere reads "and the bread," whereas the Ketiv has a scribal form that appears as "and to fight."
- **16:10** — Following the Hebrew Qere ("So let him curse, because the LORD has said to him"); the Ketiv reads "If he curses, and if the LORD has said to him."
- **16:12** — The Hebrew Qere reads "upon my eye," traditionally understood as referring to David's tears; the Ketiv reads "upon my iniquity" or "upon my affliction."
- **16:18** — Following the Hebrew Qere ("his"); the Ketiv is spelled identically to the negative particle ("not").
- **16:23** — The word "a man" is supplied by the Masoretic reading tradition (Qere without Ketiv) to complete the idiom.
