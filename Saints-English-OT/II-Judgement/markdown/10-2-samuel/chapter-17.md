## Text

**17:1** Moreover Ahithophel said to Absalom, "Please let me choose twelve thousand men, and I will arise and pursue David tonight.

**17:2** I will come upon him while he is weary and weak of hands, and I will throw him into terror; then all the people who are with him will flee, and I will strike down the king alone.

**17:3** Then I will bring back all the people to you; the return of everyone depends upon the man whom you seek, and all the people will be at peace."

**17:4** And the saying seemed right in the eyes of Absalom and in the eyes of all the elders of Israel.

**17:5** Then Absalom said, "Please call Hushai the Archite also, and let us hear what is in his mouth as well."

**17:6** When Hushai came to Absalom, Absalom spoke to him, saying, "Ahithophel has spoken in this manner. Shall we carry out his word? If not, you speak."

**17:7** Then Hushai said to Absalom, "The counsel that Ahithophel has given is not good at this time."

**17:8** Moreover Hushai said, "You know your father and his men, that they are mighty men, and they are bitter of soul, like a bear robbed of her cubs in the field. And your father is a man of war, and he will not lodge with the people.

**17:9** Behold, even now he is hidden in one of the pits or in some other place. And it will come to pass, when some fall among them at the outset, that whoever hears of it will say, 'There has been a slaughter among the people who follow Absalom.'

**17:10** And even the valiant man, whose heart is like the heart of a lion, will utterly melt; for all Israel knows that your father is a mighty man, and that those who are with him are men of valor.

**17:11** Therefore I counsel that all Israel be surely gathered to you, from Dan even to Beersheba, like the sand that is by the sea for multitude, and that you yourself go forth into battle.

**17:12** Then we will come upon him in one of the places where he may be found, and we will settle upon him as the dew falls upon the ground; and of him and of all the men who are with him, not even one shall be left.

**17:13** Moreover, if he withdraws into a city, then all Israel shall bring ropes to that city, and we will drag it into the valley until not even a pebble is found there."

**17:14** Then Absalom and all the men of Israel said, "The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the counsel of Ahithophel." For the LORD had ordained to thwart the good counsel of Ahithophel, in order that the LORD might bring disaster upon Absalom.

**17:15** Then Hushai said to Zadok and to Abiathar the priests, "Thus and thus Ahithophel has counseled Absalom and the elders of Israel, and thus and thus I have counseled.

**17:16** Now therefore, send quickly and tell David, saying, 'Do not lodge tonight in the plains of the wilderness, but by all means cross over, lest the king and all the people who are with him be swallowed up.'"

**17:17** Now Jonathan and Ahimaaz were waiting at En Rogel; and a maidservant went and told them, and they were to go and tell King David, for they could not let themselves be seen entering the city.

**17:18** However, a young man saw them and told Absalom. So both of them went away quickly and came to the house of a man in Bahurim, who had a well in his courtyard; and they went down into it.

**17:19** Then the woman took a covering and spread it over the mouth of the well, and scattered crushed grain upon it; and the matter was not known.

**17:20** When the servants of Absalom came to the woman at the house, they said, "Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?" And the woman said to them, "They have crossed over the brook of water." So they searched, and when they did not find them, they returned to Jerusalem.

**17:21** And it came to pass, after they had gone, that they came up out of the well and went and told King David. And they said to David, "Arise and cross over the water quickly, for thus has Ahithophel counseled against you."

**17:22** Then David and all the people who were with him arose and crossed over the Jordan. By morning light, not even one was missing who had not crossed over the Jordan.

**17:23** Now when Ahithophel saw that his counsel had not been carried out, he saddled his donkey, arose, and went to his house, to his own city. Then he set his household in order and hanged himself, and died; and he was buried in his father's tomb.

**17:24** Then David came to Mahanaim. And Absalom crossed over the Jordan, he and all the men of Israel with him.

**17:25** And Absalom set Amasa over the army in place of Joab. Now Amasa was the son of a man whose name was Ithra the Israelite, who had gone in to Abigail the daughter of Nahash, sister of Zeruiah, the mother of Joab.

**17:26** So Israel and Absalom encamped in the land of Gilead.

**17:27** Now it came to pass, when David had come to Mahanaim, that Shobi the son of Nahash from Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, Machir the son of Ammiel from Lo Debar, and Barzillai the Gileadite from Rogelim,

**17:28** brought beds and basins, earthen vessels and wheat, barley and flour, parched grain and beans, lentils and parched seeds,

**17:29** honey and curds, sheep and cheese of the herd, to David and to the people who were with him to eat; for they said, "The people are hungry and weary and thirsty in the wilderness."

## Translator Notes

- **17:3** — The Masoretic Hebrew is unusually concise here, literally "like the returning of all is the man whom you seek," meaning that dealing with David alone ensures the peaceful return of the entire people.
- **17:12** — The Hebrew Qere adjusts the numeral "one" to the masculine form to agree with the masculine noun "places," whereas the Ketiv uses the feminine form.
- **17:20** — "Brook of water" translates a rare Hebrew phrase occurring only here in the Old Testament, traditionally understood as a small stream or channel of water.
- **17:25** — The Hebrew text here calls Ithra "the Israelite"; the parallel genealogy in First Chronicles calls him "the Ishmaelite."
- **17:28** — The Hebrew repeats the term for roasted or parched produce once after grain and again after beans and lentils, indicating both parched grain and parched pulses or seeds.
