## Text

**14:1** Now Joab the son of Zeruiah knew that the heart of the king was toward Absalom.

**14:2** And Joab sent to Tekoa and brought from there a wise woman, and he said to her, "Please act as a mourner, and please put on garments of mourning, and do not anoint yourself with oil, but be as a woman who has mourned these many days for the dead.

**14:3** And come to the king and speak to him according to this word." So Joab placed the words in her mouth.

**14:4** And when the Tekoite woman spoke to the king, she fell on her face to the earth and bowed down, and said, "Help, O king!"

**14:5** And the king said to her, "What troubles you?" And she answered, "Truly I am a widow woman, for my husband is dead.

**14:6** And your maidservant had two sons, and the two of them strove together in the field, and there was no deliverer between them; so the one struck the other and slew him.

**14:7** And behold, the whole family has risen up against your maidservant, and they said, 'Deliver up the one who struck his brother, that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he slew; and we will destroy the heir also.' Thus they would quench my coal that remains, so as to leave to my husband neither name nor remnant upon the face of the earth."

**14:8** Then the king said to the woman, "Go to your house, and I will give a command concerning you."

**14:9** And the Tekoite woman said to the king, "My lord the king, let the iniquity be upon me and upon my father's house, and let the king and his throne be guiltless."

**14:10** And the king said, "Whoever speaks to you, bring him to me, and he shall not touch you any longer."

**14:11** Then she said, "Please let the king remember the LORD your God, that the avenger of blood may not multiply destruction, lest they destroy my son." And he said, "As the LORD lives, not one hair of your son shall fall to the ground."

**14:12** Then the woman said, "Please let your maidservant speak a word to my lord the king." And he said, "Speak."

**14:13** And the woman said, "Why then have you devised such a thing against the people of God? For in speaking this word the king is as one who is guilty, in that the king does not bring back his banished one.

**14:14** For we shall surely die, and are like water poured out upon the ground, which cannot be gathered up again. Yet God does not take away a life, but He devises plans so that one who is banished may not remain cast out from Him.

**14:15** Now therefore, I have come to speak this word to my lord the king because the people made me afraid. And your maidservant said, 'Please let me speak to the king; perhaps the king will perform the word of his handmaid.

**14:16** For the king will hear, to deliver his handmaid from the hand of the man who seeks to destroy me and my son together from the inheritance of God.'

**14:17** And your maidservant said, 'Please let the word of my lord the king be for rest, for as the angel of God, so is my lord the king to hear the good and the evil.' And may the LORD your God be with you."

**14:18** Then the king answered and said to the woman, "Please do not conceal from me anything that I am about to ask you." And the woman said, "Please let my lord the king speak."

**14:19** And the king said, "Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?" And the woman answered and said, "As your soul lives, my lord the king, no one can turn to the right hand or to the left from all that my lord the king has spoken. For your servant Joab, he commanded me, and he placed all these words in the mouth of your maidservant.

**14:20** To turn about the face of the matter your servant Joab has done this thing; yet my lord is wise, according to the wisdom of the angel of God, to know all things that are in the earth."

**14:21** And the king said to Joab, "Behold now, I have granted this thing; go therefore, bring back the young man Absalom."

**14:22** Then Joab fell on his face to the ground and bowed down, and blessed the king. And Joab said, "Today your servant knows that I have found favor in your sight, my lord the king, in that the king has fulfilled the word of your servant."

**14:23** So Joab arose and went to Geshur, and brought Absalom to Jerusalem.

**14:24** And the king said, "Let him turn aside to his own house, and let him not see my face." So Absalom turned aside to his own house, and did not see the king's face.

**14:25** Now in all Israel there was no man so handsome as Absalom, to be so greatly praised; from the sole of his foot even to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him.

**14:26** And when he cut the hair of his head—for at the end of every year he would cut it, because it was heavy upon him, therefore he cut it—he weighed the hair of his head at two hundred shekels by the king's weight.

**14:27** And to Absalom were born three sons, and one daughter whose name was Tamar; she was a woman of fair appearance.

**14:28** So Absalom dwelt in Jerusalem two full years, yet he did not see the king's face.

**14:29** Therefore Absalom sent for Joab, to send him to the king, but he was not willing to come to him. And he sent again a second time, but he was not willing to come.

**14:30** So he said to his servants, "See, Joab's field is beside mine, and he has barley there; go and set it on fire." And Absalom's servants set the field on fire.

**14:31** Then Joab arose and came to Absalom at his house, and said to him, "Why have your servants set my field on fire?"

**14:32** And Absalom said to Joab, "Behold, I sent to you, saying, 'Come here, that I may send you to the king,' to say, 'Why have I come from Geshur? It would be better for me to be there still.' Now therefore, let me see the king's face; and if there is iniquity in me, let him put me to death."

**14:33** So Joab came to the king and told him. And when he had called for Absalom, he came to the king and bowed himself on his face to the ground before the king; and the king kissed Absalom.

## Translator Notes

- **14:7** — The Qere reads the infinitive construct sim ("to leave/set"), whereas the Ketiv spells the synonymous root form sum.
- **14:11** — The Qere reads meharbat ("from multiplying"), whereas the Ketiv has the alternate spelling meharbit.
- **14:22** — The Qere reads "your servant," harmonizing with Joab's direct address to the king, whereas the Ketiv reads "his servant."
- **14:30** — The Qere reads the plural imperative "and set it on fire," matching Absalom's command to his servants, whereas the Ketiv has an alternate verbal spelling.
