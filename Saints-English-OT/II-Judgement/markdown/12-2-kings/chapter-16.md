## Text

**16:1** In the seventeenth year of Pekah the son of Remaliah, Ahaz the son of Jotham, king of Judah, began to reign.

**16:2** Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem; yet he did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD his God, as David his father had done.

**16:3** But he walked in the way of the kings of Israel; indeed, he even made his son pass through the fire, according to the abominations of the nations whom the LORD had dispossessed from before the children of Israel.

**16:4** And he sacrificed and burned incense at the high places, and upon the hills, and beneath every green tree.

**16:5** Then Rezin king of Syria and Pekah the son of Remaliah, king of Israel, came up to Jerusalem to wage war; and they besieged Ahaz, yet they could not prevail in battle.

**16:6** At that time Rezin king of Syria recovered Elath for Syria, and expelled the Judeans from Eloth; and the Edomites came to Elath and have dwelt there to this day.

**16:7** So Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria, saying, "I am your servant and your son. Come up and deliver me from the hand of the king of Syria and from the hand of the king of Israel, who are rising up against me."

**16:8** And Ahaz took the silver and the gold that was found in the house of the LORD, and in the treasuries of the king's house, and sent it as a present to the king of Assyria.

**16:9** So the king of Assyria heeded him; for the king of Assyria went up against Damascus and captured it, and carried its people into exile to Kir, and put Rezin to death.

**16:10** Then King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria, and he saw the altar that was in Damascus; and King Ahaz sent to Uriah the priest the likeness of the altar and its pattern, according to all its workmanship.

**16:11** And Uriah the priest built the altar; according to all that King Ahaz had sent from Damascus, so Uriah the priest made it before King Ahaz came from Damascus.

**16:12** And when the king came from Damascus, the king looked upon the altar; and the king drew near to the altar and went up upon it.

**16:13** He burned his burnt offering and his grain offering, and poured out his drink offering, and sprinkled the blood of his peace offerings upon the altar.

**16:14** And the bronze altar that stood before the LORD he moved from the front of the house, from between the altar and the house of the LORD, and set it on the north side of the altar.

**16:15** Then King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, saying, "Upon the great altar burn the morning burnt offering and the evening grain offering, and the king's burnt offering and his grain offering, with the burnt offering of all the people of the land, and their grain offering and their drink offerings; and all the blood of the burnt offering and all the blood of the sacrifice you shall sprinkle upon it. As for the bronze altar, it shall be for me to inquire by."

**16:16** Thus Uriah the priest did according to all that King Ahaz commanded.

**16:17** And King Ahaz cut off the panels of the stands and removed the laver from them; he also took down the Sea from off the bronze oxen that were under it, and placed it upon a pavement of stones.

**16:18** Also the covered portico of the Sabbath that they had built in the house, and the king's outer entryway, he removed from the house of the LORD on account of the king of Assyria.

**16:19** Now the rest of the acts of Ahaz which he did, are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah?

**16:20** So Ahaz slept with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the City of David; and Hezekiah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **16:6** — Translates the Masoretic qere "and the Edomites"; the ketiv reads "and the Syrians" (Arameans), reflecting the visual similarity of the Hebrew letters dalet and resh.
- **16:15** — Translates the qere "commanded" without the redundant object suffix of the ketiv ("commanded him"). The Hebrew verb translated "to inquire by" can denote seeking oracular guidance or priestly inspection.
- **16:18** — Translates the qere musak ("covered portico" or "canopy"); the ketiv misak is an orthographic variant with the same sense.
