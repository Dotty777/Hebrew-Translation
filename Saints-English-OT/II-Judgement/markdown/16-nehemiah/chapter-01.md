## Text

**1:1** The words of Nehemiah the son of Hacaliah. Now it came to pass in the month of Chislev, in the twentieth year, that I was in Shushan the citadel.

**1:2** And Hanani, one of my brethren, came, he and men from Judah; and I asked them concerning the Jews who had escaped, who remained from the captivity, and concerning Jerusalem.

**1:3** And they said to me, "Those who remain, who are left from the captivity there in the province, are in great affliction and reproach; the wall of Jerusalem is also breached, and its gates have been burned with fire."

**1:4** Now it came to pass, upon hearing these words, that I sat down and wept, and I mourned for days; and I continued fasting and praying before the God of heaven.

**1:5** And I said, "I pray, O LORD, God of heaven, the great and awesome God, who keeps the covenant and lovingkindness with those who love Him and with those who keep His commandments:

**1:6** Let Your ear now be attentive, and Your eyes be open, that You may hear the prayer of Your servant which I am praying before You this day, day and night, for the children of Israel Your servants, and confessing the sins of the children of Israel which we have sinned against You; both I and my father's house have sinned.

**1:7** We have acted very corruptly against You, and have not kept the commandments, nor the statutes, nor the ordinances that You commanded Your servant Moses.

**1:8** Remember, I pray, the word that You commanded Your servant Moses, saying, 'If you act unfaithfully, I will scatter you among the peoples;

**1:9** but if you return to Me, and keep My commandments and do them, though your outcasts be at the farthest reaches of the heavens, yet from there I will gather them and bring them to the place that I have chosen to cause My name to dwell there.'

**1:10** Now these are Your servants and Your people, whom You have redeemed by Your great power and by Your mighty hand.

**1:11** O Lord, I pray, let Your ear now be attentive to the prayer of Your servant, and to the prayer of Your servants who delight to fear Your name; and grant success, I pray, to Your servant this day, and grant him mercy in the sight of this man." Now I was cupbearer to the king.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Chislev was the ninth month of the Hebrew calendar, corresponding to November and December; the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes I fell in 445 BC.
- **1:9** — The Hebrew text has a minor ketiv/qere spelling variant for the verb meaning "and I will bring them," with the qere reading followed here.
- **1:11** — A royal cupbearer was not merely an attendant but a high-ranking, trusted court official with direct daily access to the king.
