## Text

**5:1** Now there arose a great outcry of the people and of their wives against their brethren the Jews.

**5:2** For there were those who said, "With our sons and our daughters, we are many; let us obtain grain, that we may eat and live."

**5:3** There were also those who said, "We are pledging our fields, our vineyards, and our houses, that we may obtain grain in the famine."

**5:4** And there were those who said, "We have borrowed silver for the king's tribute upon our fields and our vineyards.

**5:5** Yet now our flesh is as the flesh of our brethren, and our children are as their children; and behold, we are forcing our sons and our daughters to be slaves, and some of our daughters are already brought into bondage. It is not in the power of our hand to help it, for our fields and our vineyards belong to others."

**5:6** And I became exceedingly angry when I heard their outcry and these words.

**5:7** Then I took counsel with myself, and I contended with the nobles and with the officials, and said to them, "You are exacting interest, each man from his brother." And I called a great assembly against them.

**5:8** And I said to them, "According to our ability we have bought back our brethren the Jews who were sold to the nations; and will you now even sell your brethren, that they should be sold to us?" Then they kept silent and could not find a word to say.

**5:9** And I said, "The thing that you are doing is not good. Should you not walk in the fear of our God because of the reproach of the nations, our enemies?

**5:10** I also, with my brethren and my servants, am lending them silver and grain. Please, let us leave off this exacting of interest.

**5:11** Restore to them this very day their fields, their vineyards, their olive groves, and their houses, as well as the hundredth part of the silver, the grain, the new wine, and the oil that you are exacting from them."

**5:12** Then they said, "We will restore it and will require nothing from them; we will do just as you say." So I summoned the priests and put them under oath to do according to this word.

**5:13** I also shook out the fold of my garment and said, "Thus may God shake out from his house and from his labor every man who does not keep this promise; thus may he be shaken out and emptied." And all the assembly said, "Amen," and praised the LORD. And the people did according to this promise.

**5:14** Moreover, from the day that I was appointed to be their governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year even to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes—twelve years—neither I nor my brethren ate the bread of the governor.

**5:15** But the former governors who were before me laid heavy burdens upon the people, and took from them bread and wine, besides forty shekels of silver; indeed, even their servants exercised dominion over the people. But I did not do so, because of the fear of God.

**5:16** Moreover, I also persevered in the work of this wall, and we acquired no land; and all my servants were gathered there for the work.

**5:17** Furthermore, one hundred and fifty men, Jews and officials, were at my table, besides those who came to us from the nations that were around us.

**5:18** Now what was prepared for each day was one ox and six choice sheep; also fowl were prepared for me, and once every ten days an abundance of all kinds of wine. Yet for all this, I did not demand the bread of the governor, because the service lay heavy upon this people.

**5:19** Remember me, O my God, for good, for all that I have done for this people.

## Translator Notes

- **5:7** — Translates the Qere ("exacting interest," from the root nashah); the Ketiv spells the participle with aleph, reflecting a common orthographic variation between nashah and nasa.
- **5:9** — Translates the Qere ("And I said," first-person singular); the Ketiv reads third-person singular ("and he said").
- **5:11** — "The hundredth part" refers to a one-percent rate of interest, likely charged monthly on loans of silver and agricultural produce.
- **5:14** — "The bread of the governor" refers to the customary food allowance and tax revenue allotted to maintain the Persian provincial governor and his household.
