## Text

**31:1** Now the Philistines were doing battle against Israel, and the men of Israel fled from the Philistines and fell slain upon Mount Gilboa.

**31:2** Then the Philistines pressed hard after Saul and his sons; and the Philistines struck down Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchishua, the sons of Saul.

**31:3** And the battle grew fierce against Saul; and the archers, the men with the bow, found him, and he was severely wounded by the archers.

**31:4** Then Saul said to his armor-bearer, "Unsheathe your sword and run me through with it, lest these uncircumcised men come and run me through and treat me shamefully." Yet his armor-bearer was unwilling, for he was exceedingly afraid. So Saul took the sword and fell upon it.

**31:5** Now his armor-bearer saw that Saul was dead, and he too fell upon his sword and died with him.

**31:6** So Saul died, together with his three sons, his armor-bearer, and indeed all his men, on that very day.

**31:7** And when the men of Israel who were on the other side of the valley and those who were on the other side of the Jordan saw that the men of Israel had fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they forsook the cities and fled; and the Philistines came and dwelt in them.

**31:8** And it came to pass on the next day, when the Philistines came to strip the slain, that they found Saul and his three sons fallen on Mount Gilboa.

**31:9** Then they cut off his head and stripped off his armor, and sent word throughout the land of the Philistines round about, to proclaim the tidings in the house of their idols and among the people.

**31:10** And they put his armor in the temple of Ashtaroth, and they fastened his body to the wall of Beth-shan.

**31:11** Now when the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead heard concerning him what the Philistines had done to Saul,

**31:12** all the valiant men arose and marched all night, and took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Beth-shan; and they came to Jabesh and burned them there.

**31:13** Then they took their bones and buried them under the tamarisk tree at Jabesh, and fasted seven days.

## Translator Notes

- **31:3** — The Hebrew verb rendered severely wounded may alternatively be derived from a root meaning to writhe or tremble in terror before the archers.
- **31:10** — Ashtaroth is plural in Hebrew, referring to the temple dedicated to the Canaanite and Philistine fertility goddess Ashtoreth and her cultic images.
- **31:13** — The tamarisk tree translates the Hebrew eshel, a hardy desert evergreen often marking notable landmarks and memorial burial sites in ancient Israel.
