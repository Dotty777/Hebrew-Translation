## Text

**24:1** Then David went up from there and dwelt in the strongholds of En Gedi.

**24:2** Now it came to pass, when Saul returned from pursuing the Philistines, that they told him, saying, "Behold, David is in the wilderness of En Gedi."

**24:3** So Saul took three thousand chosen men from all Israel, and went to seek David and his men upon the Rocks of the Wild Goats.

**24:4** And he came to the sheepfolds along the way, where there was a cave; and Saul entered to cover his feet, while David and his men were sitting in the recesses of the cave.

**24:5** Then the men of David said to him, "Behold, this is the day of which the LORD said to you, 'Behold, I am giving your enemy into your hand, and you shall do to him as it seems good in your eyes.'" So David arose and stealthily cut off a corner of Saul's robe.

**24:6** And it came to pass afterward that David's heart struck him because he had cut off the corner of Saul's robe.

**24:7** And he said to his men, "The LORD forbid that I should do this thing to my lord, the LORD's anointed, to stretch out my hand against him, for he is the anointed of the LORD."

**24:8** So David restrained his men with these words, and did not permit them to rise against Saul. And Saul rose up from the cave and went on his way.

**24:9** David also arose afterward, went out of the cave, and called after Saul, saying, "My lord the king!" And when Saul looked behind him, David bowed with his face to the ground and prostrated himself.

**24:10** And David said to Saul, "Why do you listen to the words of men who say, 'Behold, David seeks your harm'?

**24:11** Behold, this day your eyes have seen how the LORD delivered you today into my hand in the cave, and some urged me to kill you; yet I spared you, and I said, 'I will not stretch out my hand against my lord, for he is the anointed of the LORD.'

**24:12** Moreover, my father, see—yes, see the corner of your robe in my hand! For in that I cut off the corner of your robe and did not kill you, know and see that there is neither evil nor transgression in my hand, and I have not sinned against you, yet you hunt my life to take it.

**24:13** May the LORD judge between me and you, and may the LORD avenge me on you; yet my hand shall not be against you.

**24:14** As the proverb of the ancients says, 'Wickedness proceeds from the wicked.' Yet my hand shall not be against you.

**24:15** After whom has the king of Israel come out? After whom are you pursuing? After a dead dog? After a single flea?

**24:16** May the LORD therefore be judge, and execute judgment between me and you, and look and plead my cause, and vindicate me out of your hand."

**24:17** Now it came to pass, when David had finished speaking these words to Saul, that Saul said, "Is this your voice, my son David?" And Saul lifted up his voice and wept.

**24:18** Then he said to David, "You are more righteous than I; for you have repaid me with good, whereas I have repaid you with evil.

**24:19** And you have shown this day how you have dealt well with me, in that when the LORD delivered me into your hand, you did not kill me.

**24:20** For if a man finds his enemy, will he send him away safely on his way? Therefore may the LORD reward you with good for what you have done to me this day.

**24:21** And now, behold, I know indeed that you shall surely reign, and that the kingdom of Israel shall be established in your hand.

**24:22** Therefore swear now to me by the LORD that you will not cut off my descendants after me, and that you will not destroy my name from my father's house."

**24:23** So David swore to Saul. And Saul went to his own house, while David and his men went up to the stronghold.

## Translator Notes

- **24:1** — In the Masoretic Hebrew versification followed here, 24:1 corresponds to 23:29 in many English Bibles, and the remaining verses of the chapter are numbered one verse higher.
- **24:4** — The Hebrew idiom 'to cover his feet' is a modest euphemism for relieving oneself.
- **24:5** — The Masoretic qere (read form) is singular, 'your enemy,' whereas the ketiv (written form) is plural, 'your enemies.'
- **24:11** — The verb rendered 'I spared you' is literally feminine singular ('it spared you'), an idiomatic ellipsis for 'my eye spared you.'
- **24:19** — Following the Masoretic qere 'and you' (ve'attah); the ketiv reads the shorter form ve'at. Similarly, in 24:9 the qere contracts 'from the cave' into a single word.
