## Text

**19:1** Now Saul spoke to Jonathan his son and to all his servants, that they should put David to death; but Jonathan the son of Saul delighted greatly in David.

**19:2** So Jonathan told David, saying, "Saul my father is seeking to put you to death. Now therefore, please watch carefully in the morning, and abide in a secret place and hide yourself.

**19:3** And I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I will speak with my father concerning you. Then what I see, I will declare to you."

**19:4** Then Jonathan spoke well of David to Saul his father, and said to him, "Do not let the king sin against his servant, against David, for he has not sinned against you, and his deeds have been exceedingly good toward you.

**19:5** For he put his life in his hand and struck down the Philistine, and the LORD worked a great deliverance for all Israel. You saw it and rejoiced. Why then will you sin against innocent blood, by putting David to death without cause?"

**19:6** So Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan, and Saul swore, "As the LORD lives, he shall not be put to death."

**19:7** Then Jonathan called David, and Jonathan told him all these words. And Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence as in times past.

**19:8** And there was war again; and David went out and fought with the Philistines, and struck them with a great slaughter, so that they fled before him.

**19:9** Now an evil spirit from the LORD came upon Saul while he was sitting in his house with his spear in his hand, and David was playing music with his hand.

**19:10** And Saul sought to pin David to the wall with the spear, but he slipped away from before Saul, so that he drove the spear into the wall. And David fled and escaped that night.

**19:11** Saul also sent messengers to the house of David to watch him and to put him to death in the morning. And Michal his wife told David, saying, "If you do not save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be put to death."

**19:12** So Michal let David down through the window; and he went and fled and escaped.

**19:13** And Michal took the household idol and laid it on the bed, put a quilt of goats' hair at its head, and covered it with a garment.

**19:14** And when Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, "He is sick."

**19:15** Then Saul sent the messengers to see David, saying, "Bring him up to me on the bed, that I may put him to death."

**19:16** And when the messengers came in, behold, the household idol was on the bed, with the quilt of goats' hair at its head.

**19:17** Then Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me like this, and sent away my enemy so that he has escaped?" And Michal answered Saul, "He said to me, 'Let me go! Why should I put you to death?'"

**19:18** So David fled and escaped, and came to Samuel at Ramah, and told him all that Saul had done to him. And he and Samuel went and dwelt in Naioth.

**19:19** And it was told to Saul, saying, "Behold, David is in Naioth in Ramah."

**19:20** Then Saul sent messengers to take David. And when they saw the company of the prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing as leader over them, the Spirit of God came upon the messengers of Saul, and they also prophesied.

**19:21** When they told Saul, he sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. And Saul sent messengers again a third time, and they also prophesied.

**19:22** Then he himself also went to Ramah, and came as far as the great cistern that is in Secu. And he asked and said, "Where are Samuel and David?" And one said, "Behold, they are in Naioth in Ramah."

**19:23** So he went there to Naioth in Ramah. And the Spirit of God came upon him also, and he went on prophesying continuously until he came to Naioth in Ramah.

**19:24** He also stripped off his garments, and he too prophesied before Samuel, and fell down naked all that day and all that night. Therefore they say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

## Translator Notes

- **19:13** — The Hebrew teraphim is plural in form yet refers here to a single household image or statue; "quilt of goats' hair" renders a rare Hebrew expression for a woven mat or pillow of goat hair.
- **19:18** — The qere reads "in Naioth" (and likewise in 19:19, 19:22, 19:23, and "from Naioth" in 20:1), denoting the prophetic settlement at Ramah, whereas the ketiv preserves an older orthographic form of the same name.
- **19:20** — "Company of the prophets" translates a rare Hebrew term (lahakah) denoting a prophetic band assembled under Samuel's leadership.
