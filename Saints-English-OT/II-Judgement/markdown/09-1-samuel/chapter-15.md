## Text

**15:1** And Samuel said to Saul, "The LORD sent me to anoint you as king over His people, over Israel. Now therefore, listen to the voice of the words of the LORD.

**15:2** Thus says the LORD of hosts: 'I have taken account of what Amalek did to Israel, how he set himself against him on the way when he came up out of Egypt.

**15:3** Now go and strike Amalek, and utterly devote to destruction all that belongs to him, and do not spare him; rather, put to death from man to woman, from child to nursing infant, from ox to sheep, from camel to donkey.'"

**15:4** So Saul summoned the people and mustered them at Telaim, two hundred thousand foot soldiers and ten thousand men of Judah.

**15:5** And Saul came to the city of Amalek, and set an ambush in the valley.

**15:6** Then Saul said to the Kenites, "Go, depart, go down from the midst of the Amalekites, lest I sweep you away with them; for you showed lovingkindness to all the children of Israel when they came up out of Egypt." So the Kenites departed from the midst of Amalek.

**15:7** And Saul struck Amalek from Havilah as far as Shur, which lies east of Egypt.

**15:8** He also seized Agag king of Amalek alive, and utterly destroyed all the people with the edge of the sword.

**15:9** But Saul and the people spared Agag and the choicest of the sheep and the oxen, the fatlings and the lambs, and all that was good, and they were unwilling to utterly destroy them; yet everything despised and worthless, that they utterly destroyed.

**15:10** Now the word of the LORD came to Samuel, saying,

**15:11** "I regret that I have made Saul king, for he has turned back from following Me, and has not fulfilled My words." And Samuel was deeply distressed, and he cried out to the LORD all night.

**15:12** So Samuel rose early in the morning to meet Saul; and it was reported to Samuel, saying, "Saul came to Carmel, and behold, he set up a monument for himself, and turned about, passed on, and went down to Gilgal."

**15:13** Then Samuel came to Saul, and Saul said to him, "Blessed are you of the LORD! I have fulfilled the word of the LORD."

**15:14** But Samuel said, "What then is this sound of sheep in my ears, and the sound of oxen that I am hearing?"

**15:15** And Saul said, "From the Amalekites they have brought them, for the people spared the choicest of the sheep and the oxen to sacrifice to the LORD your God; and the rest we have utterly destroyed."

**15:16** Then Samuel said to Saul, "Stop, and let me declare to you what the LORD spoke to me this past night." And he said to him, "Speak."

**15:17** So Samuel said, "Though you are little in your own eyes, are you not the head of the tribes of Israel? And the LORD anointed you as king over Israel.

**15:18** Furthermore, the LORD sent you on a mission and said, 'Go and utterly destroy the sinners, the Amalekites, and fight against them until they are consumed.'

**15:19** Why then did you not obey the voice of the LORD, but swooped down upon the spoil and did evil in the sight of the LORD?"

**15:20** And Saul said to Samuel, "Indeed I have obeyed the voice of the LORD, and have gone on the way upon which the LORD sent me; I have brought back Agag king of Amalek, and I have utterly destroyed the Amalekites.

**15:21** Yet the people took from the spoil sheep and oxen, the choicest of the things devoted to destruction, to sacrifice to the LORD your God in Gilgal."

**15:22** Then Samuel said, "Does the LORD delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices as much as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to give heed than the fat of rams.

**15:23** For rebellion is like the sin of divination, and presumption is like iniquity and idolatry. Because you have rejected the word of the LORD, He also has rejected you from being king."

**15:24** Then Saul said to Samuel, "I have sinned, for I have transgressed the command of the LORD and your words, because I feared the people and listened to their voice.

**15:25** And now, please pardon my sin, and turn back with me, that I may worship the LORD."

**15:26** But Samuel said to Saul, "I will not return with you, for you have rejected the word of the LORD, and the LORD has rejected you from being king over Israel."

**15:27** And as Samuel turned to depart, Saul seized the hem of his robe, and it tore.

**15:28** Then Samuel said to him, "The LORD has torn the kingdom of Israel from you this day, and has given it to a neighbor of yours who is better than you.

**15:29** And moreover, the Glory of Israel will not lie nor relent; for He is not a man, that He should relent."

**15:30** Then he said, "I have sinned; yet honor me now, please, before the elders of my people and before Israel, and return with me, that I may worship the LORD your God."

**15:31** So Samuel turned back after Saul, and Saul worshiped the LORD.

**15:32** Then Samuel said, "Bring near to me Agag king of Amalek." So Agag came to him cheerfully, and Agag said, "Surely the bitterness of death has departed."

**15:33** But Samuel said, "As your sword has bereaved women of children, so shall your mother be bereaved among women." And Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before the LORD in Gilgal.

**15:34** Then Samuel went to Ramah, and Saul went up to his house at Gibeah of Saul.

**15:35** And Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death; yet Samuel mourned over Saul, and the LORD regretted that He had made Saul king over Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **15:11** — The Hebrew verb nacham ("regret" in 15:11, 35; "relent" in 15:29) expresses both divine sorrow over human disobedience and the reversal of a decree, contrasting God's grief over Saul with His unchanging purpose.
- **15:16** — The Masoretic ketiv reads the plural "they said," whereas the qere (translated here) reads the singular "he said," referring directly to Saul.
- **15:23** — The term translated "idolatry" is Hebrew teraphim, denoting household idols associated with pagan worship and divination.
- **15:32** — The rare Hebrew adverb ma'adannot ("cheerfully") derives from a root denoting ease or delight, indicating that Agag approached Samuel with confident relief.
