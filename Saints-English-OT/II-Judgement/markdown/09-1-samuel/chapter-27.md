## Text

**27:1** And David said in his heart, "Now I shall perish one day by the hand of Saul. There is nothing better for me than that I should surely escape into the land of the Philistines; then Saul will despair of seeking me any longer within any territory of Israel, and I shall escape from his hand."

**27:2** So David arose and crossed over, he and the six hundred men who were with him, to Achish the son of Maoch, king of Gath.

**27:3** So David dwelt with Achish at Gath, he and his men, each man with his household, and David with his two wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail the Carmelitess, Nabal's widow.

**27:4** And it was told to Saul that David had fled to Gath; so he sought him no more.

**27:5** Then David said to Achish, "If I have now found favor in your sight, let them give me a place in one of the towns of the open country, that I may dwell there; for why should your servant dwell in the royal city with you?"

**27:6** So Achish gave him Ziklag that day. Therefore Ziklag has belonged to the kings of Judah to this very day.

**27:7** Now the length of time that David dwelt in the country of the Philistines was one full year and four months.

**27:8** And David and his men went up and raided the Geshurites, the Gizrites, and the Amalekites; for those peoples were from of old the inhabitants of the land, as one goes toward Shur, even to the land of Egypt.

**27:9** Whenever David struck the land, he would leave neither man nor woman alive, but he took the sheep, the oxen, the donkeys, the camels, and the apparel, and returned and came to Achish.

**27:10** Then Achish would say, "Where did you make a raid today?" And David would say, "Against the Negev of Judah, and against the Negev of the Jerahmeelites, and against the Negev of the Kenites."

**27:11** David left neither man nor woman alive to bring to Gath, saying, "Lest they should report against us, saying, 'Thus David has done.'" And such was his custom all the days that he dwelt in the country of the Philistines.

**27:12** So Achish trusted David, saying, "He has made himself utterly abhorrent to his people Israel; therefore he shall be my servant forever."

## Translator Notes

- **27:4** — Ketiv/Qere: the written text has an imperfect verb form, whereas the traditional reading (Qere) reads the perfect form, both meaning that Saul ceased pursuing David.
- **27:7** — Literally "days and four months"; in biblical Hebrew, "days" often serves idiomatically for a full year.
- **27:8** — Ketiv/Qere: the written text reads "the Girzites," while the traditional reading (Qere) reads "the Gizrites."
- **27:10** — The Masoretic Text has the unusual interrogative particle al, understood idiomatically here as an elliptical question asking where David raided.
