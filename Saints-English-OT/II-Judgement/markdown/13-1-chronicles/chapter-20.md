## Text

**20:1** Now it came about at the turn of the year, at the time when kings go forth, that Joab led out the force of the army and ravaged the land of the sons of Ammon; and he came and laid siege to Rabbah, while David remained in Jerusalem. And Joab struck Rabbah and tore it down.

**20:2** And David took the crown of their king from off his head, and found its weight to be a talent of gold, and precious stones were in it; and it was set upon the head of David. He also brought forth the spoil of the city in exceedingly great abundance.

**20:3** And he brought out the people who were in it, and cut them with the saw, and with iron picks, and with saws; and thus David did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

**20:4** Now it came about after this that war arose at Gezer with the Philistines. At that time Sibbechai the Hushathite struck down Sippai, who was of the descendants of the giants, and they were subdued.

**20:5** And again there was war with the Philistines, and Elhanan the son of Jair struck down Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam.

**20:6** Yet again there was war at Gath, where there was a man of great stature whose fingers and toes were six and six, twenty-four in all; and he also was born to the giant.

**20:7** When he defied Israel, Jonathan the son of Shimea, the brother of David, struck him down.

**20:8** These were born to the giant in Gath, and they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

## Translator Notes

- **20:2** — Hebrew malkam means "their king" according to the Masoretic vocalization, though the same consonants may also be read as the name of the Ammonite deity Milcom.
- **20:3** — The Masoretic verb literally means "and he sawed," though in comparison with the parallel in Second Samuel 12:31 it is often understood as assigning the captives to forced labor with saws and iron implements.
- **20:4** — Hebrew ha-Rephaim refers to the ancient race of giants; the singular ha-Rapha is used in verses six and eight.
- **20:5** — The Qere reads "Jair," whereas the Ketiv has "Jaur."
