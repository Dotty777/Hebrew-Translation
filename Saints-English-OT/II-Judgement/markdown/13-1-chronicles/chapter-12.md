## Text

**12:1** Now these are the ones who came to David at Ziklag while he was still restricted because of Saul the son of Kish; and they were among the mighty men, helpers in warfare,

**12:2** armed with bows, using both the right hand and the left with stones and with arrows from the bow, of the brethren of Saul from Benjamin:

**12:3** the chief was Ahiezer, then Joash, the sons of Shemaah the Gibeathite; Jeziel and Pelet, the sons of Azmaveth; Berachah, and Jehu the Anathothite;

**12:4** Ishmaiah the Gibeonite, a mighty man among the thirty and over the thirty;

**12:5** Jeremiah, Jahaziel, Johanan, and Jozabad the Gederathite;

**12:6** Eluzai, Jerimoth, Bealiah, Shemariah, and Shephatiah the Haruphite;

**12:7** Elkanah, Isshiah, Azarel, Joezer, and Jashobeam, the Korahites;

**12:8** and Joelah and Zebadiah, the sons of Jeroham of Gedor.

**12:9** From the Gadites some separated themselves to David at the stronghold in the wilderness—mighty men of valor, men of the host for warfare, skilled with shield and spear, whose faces were like the faces of lions, and who were swift as gazelles upon the mountains:

**12:10** Ezer the chief, Obadiah the second, Eliab the third,

**12:11** Mishmannah the fourth, Jeremiah the fifth,

**12:12** Attai the sixth, Eliel the seventh,

**12:13** Johanan the eighth, Elzabad the ninth,

**12:14** Jeremiah the tenth, and Machbannai the eleventh.

**12:15** These were of the sons of Gad, commanders of the host; the least was equal to a hundred, and the greatest to a thousand.

**12:16** These are the ones who crossed over the Jordan in the first month, when it was overflowing all its banks; and they put to flight all those of the valleys, both to the east and to the west.

**12:17** Then some of the sons of Benjamin and Judah came to the stronghold to David.

**12:18** And David went out to meet them, and answered and said to them, "If you have come in peace to me to help me, my heart shall be united with you; but if to betray me to my adversaries, seeing there is no violence in my hands, may the God of our fathers look upon it and rebuke you."

**12:19** Then the Spirit clothed Amasai, chief of the captains: "We are yours, O David, and with you, O son of Jesse! Peace, peace to you, and peace to your helpers; for your God has helped you." So David received them and made them captains of the troop.

**12:20** Some from Manasseh also defected to David when he came with the Philistines against Saul to battle; yet they did not help them, for after taking counsel the lords of the Philistines sent him away, saying, "At the cost of our heads he will defect to his master Saul."

**12:21** As he went to Ziklag, there defected to him from Manasseh: Adnah, Jozabad, Jediael, Michael, Jozabad, Elihu, and Zillethai, heads of the thousands who belonged to Manasseh.

**12:22** And they helped David against the raiding band, for they were all mighty men of valor, and they became commanders in the army.

**12:23** For from day to day men came to David to help him, until there was a great host, like the host of God.

**12:24** Now these are the numbers of the divisions armed for war who came to David at Hebron to turn the kingdom of Saul over to him, according to the word of the LORD:

**12:25** of the sons of Judah bearing shield and spear, six thousand eight hundred armed for war;

**12:26** of the sons of Simeon, mighty men of valor for war, seven thousand one hundred;

**12:27** of the sons of Levi, four thousand six hundred;

**12:28** and Jehoiada, the leader of the Aaronites, and with him three thousand seven hundred;

**12:29** and Zadok, a young man, mighty of valor, and from his father's house twenty-two captains;

**12:30** of the sons of Benjamin, the brethren of Saul, three thousand—for until then the majority of them had kept allegiance to the house of Saul;

**12:31** of the sons of Ephraim, twenty thousand eight hundred, mighty men of valor, men of renown in their fathers' houses;

**12:32** of the half-tribe of Manasseh, eighteen thousand, who were designated by name to come and make David king;

**12:33** of the sons of Issachar, men who possessed understanding of the times, to know what Israel should do—their chiefs were two hundred, and all their brethren were at their command;

**12:34** of Zebulun, those going forth to the army, setting battle in array with all manner of weapons of war, fifty thousand, keeping rank with no double heart;

**12:35** of Naphtali, one thousand captains, and with them thirty-seven thousand with shield and spear;

**12:36** of the Danites, who could set battle in array, twenty-eight thousand six hundred;

**12:37** of Asher, those going forth to the army to draw up battle array, forty thousand;

**12:38** and from across the Jordan, of the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh, with all manner of weapons of war for battle, one hundred and twenty thousand.

**12:39** All these men of war, who could keep battle rank, came to Hebron with a whole heart to make David king over all Israel; and all the rest of Israel were also of one heart to make David king.

**12:40** And they were there with David three days, eating and drinking, for their brethren had made provision for them.

**12:41** Moreover those who were near to them, even as far as Issachar and Zebulun and Naphtali, were bringing food on donkeys and on camels, on mules and on oxen—provisions of flour, cakes of figs, clusters of raisins, wine and oil, and oxen and sheep in abundance, for there was joy in Israel.

## Translator Notes

- **12:3** — Following the Masoretic Qere reading, Jeziel; the Ketiv spells the name Jezuel.
- **12:6** — Following the Masoretic Qere reading, the Haruphite; the Ketiv reads the Hariphite.
- **12:16** — Following the Masoretic Qere spelling for its banks; the Ketiv presents a slight orthographic variant of the same word.
- **12:19** — Following the Masoretic Qere reading, the captains (elite officers); the Ketiv reads the thirty. Literally, the Spirit clothed Amasai, an idiom for sudden prophetic empowerment.
- **12:34** — Literally, without a heart and a heart, an idiom signifying undivided loyalty and singleness of purpose.
