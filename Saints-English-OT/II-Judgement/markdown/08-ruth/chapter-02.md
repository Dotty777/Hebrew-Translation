## Text

**2:1** Now Naomi had a kinsman on her husband's side, a man of great standing from the clan of Elimelech, whose name was Boaz.

**2:2** And Ruth the Moabitess said to Naomi, "Let me go now to the field and glean among the ears of grain after him in whose sight I shall find favor." And she said to her, "Go, my daughter."

**2:3** So she departed, and came and gleaned in the field behind the reapers. And as it happened, she came upon the portion of the field belonging to Boaz, who was of the clan of Elimelech.

**2:4** And behold, Boaz came from Bethlehem, and he said to the reapers, "The LORD be with you!" And they answered him, "The LORD bless you!"

**2:5** Then Boaz said to his servant who was set over the reapers, "To whom does this young woman belong?"

**2:6** And the servant who was set over the reapers answered and said, "She is a young Moabite woman who returned with Naomi from the territory of Moab.

**2:7** She said, 'Please let me glean and gather among the sheaves behind the reapers.' So she came and has continued from the morning until now, except that she rested a little in the shelter."

**2:8** Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Listen, my daughter: do not go to glean in another field, nor depart from this one, but stay close here with my young women.

**2:9** Let your eyes be on the field that they reap, and go after them. Have I not commanded the young men not to touch you? And when you are thirsty, go to the vessels and drink from what the young men have drawn."

**2:10** Then she fell on her face and bowed down to the ground, and said to him, "Why have I found favor in your eyes, that you should take notice of me, seeing that I am a foreigner?"

**2:11** And Boaz answered and said to her, "All that you have done for your mother-in-law since the death of your husband has been fully told to me, and how you left your father and your mother and the land of your birth, and came to a people whom you did not know before.

**2:12** May the LORD repay your work, and may a full reward be given you from the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

**2:13** Then she said, "May I continue to find favor in your eyes, my lord, for you have comforted me and spoken tenderly to the heart of your maidservant, though I am not like one of your maidservants."

**2:14** And at mealtime Boaz said to her, "Come here, eat of the bread, and dip your morsel in the vinegar." So she sat beside the reapers, and he passed roasted grain to her; and she ate and was satisfied, and had some left over.

**2:15** When she rose up to glean, Boaz commanded his young men, saying, "Let her glean even among the sheaves, and do not put her to shame.

**2:16** Also pull out some for her from the bundles on purpose, and leave it that she may glean, and do not rebuke her."

**2:17** So she gleaned in the field until evening, and beat out what she had gleaned; and it was about an ephah of barley.

**2:18** Then she took it up and went into the city, and her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned. She also brought out and gave to her what she had kept back after she had eaten her fill.

**2:19** And her mother-in-law said to her, "Where have you gleaned today, and where did you work? Blessed be the one who took notice of you!" So she told her mother-in-law with whom she had worked, and said, "The name of the man with whom I worked today is Boaz."

**2:20** Then Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, "Blessed be he of the LORD, who has not forsaken His lovingkindness toward the living and the dead!" And Naomi said to her, "The man is a near relative of ours, one of our kinsman-redeemers."

**2:21** And Ruth the Moabitess said, "He also said to me, 'You shall stay close by my young servants until they have finished all my harvest.'"

**2:22** And Naomi said to Ruth her daughter-in-law, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women, and that no one harm you in another field."

**2:23** So she stayed close by the young women of Boaz to glean until the end of the barley harvest and the wheat harvest; and she dwelt with her mother-in-law.

## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Qere (read form) means "kinsman" or "relative," whereas the Ketiv (written form) is a participle meaning "acquaintance."
- **2:7** — Hebrew literally reads "in the house," denoting a temporary shelter in the field for the workers.
- **2:20** — The relative clause "who has not forsaken His lovingkindness" may refer either to the LORD, as translated here, or to Boaz.
