## Text

**1:1** Now it came to pass in the days when the judges governed that there was a famine in the land; and a man from Bethlehem of Judah went to sojourn in the territory of Moab, he, his wife, and his two sons.

**1:2** The name of the man was Elimelech, the name of his wife was Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion—Ephrathites from Bethlehem of Judah. And they entered the territory of Moab and remained there.

**1:3** Then Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left, she and her two sons.

**1:4** And they took for themselves Moabite wives: the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the second was Ruth. And they dwelt there about ten years.

**1:5** Then both Mahlon and Chilion also died, and the woman was left bereft of her two children and of her husband.

**1:6** Then she arose, she and her daughters-in-law, and turned back from the territory of Moab, for she had heard in the territory of Moab that the LORD had visited His people, giving them bread.

**1:7** So she departed from the place where she had been, and her two daughters-in-law were with her; and they went along the way to return to the land of Judah.

**1:8** And Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go, return each of you to her mother's house. May the LORD deal in lovingkindness with you, even as you have dealt with the dead and with me.

**1:9** May the LORD grant you that you may find rest, each in the house of her husband." Then she kissed them, and they lifted up their voices and wept.

**1:10** And they said to her, "Surely we will return with you to your people."

**1:11** Then Naomi said, "Turn back, my daughters! Why would you go with me? Are there still sons in my womb, that they may become husbands for you?

**1:12** Turn back, my daughters, go your way, for I am too old to have a husband. Even if I should say, 'I have hope,' even if I should have a husband tonight and also bear sons,

**1:13** would you wait for them until they were grown? Would you restrain yourselves for them from having a husband? No, my daughters, for it is exceedingly bitter for me on your account, because the hand of the LORD has gone out against me."

**1:14** Then they lifted up their voices and wept again; and Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her.

**1:15** And she said, "Behold, your sister-in-law has returned to her people and to her gods; return after your sister-in-law."

**1:16** But Ruth said, "Do not press me to forsake you, to turn away from following you; for wherever you go, I shall go, and wherever you abide, I shall abide. Your people shall be my people, and your God shall be my God.

**1:17** Where you die, I shall die, and there I shall be buried. Thus may the LORD do to me, and more also, if anything other than death separates me from you."

**1:18** When she saw that she was steadfastly resolved to go with her, she ceased speaking to her.

**1:19** So the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. And it came to pass, when they came to Bethlehem, that the whole city was stirred because of them; and the women said, "Is this Naomi?"

**1:20** And she said to them, "Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me.

**1:21** I went out full, yet the LORD has brought me back empty. Why do you call me Naomi, since the LORD has testified against me and the Almighty has brought calamity upon me?"

**1:22** Thus Naomi returned, and with her Ruth the Moabitess, her daughter-in-law, who returned from the territory of Moab. And they arrived in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Hebrew literally reads "fields of Moab" here and in verses 2, 6, and 22 (and singular "field of Moab" in verse 6), an idiom for the territory or country of Moab.
- **1:8** — The Qere (read form) is the jussive "May the LORD deal," whereas the Ketiv (written form) displays the full imperfect spelling.
- **1:13** — The Hebrew preposition can be understood either causally ("on your account") or comparatively ("far more bitter for me than for you").
- **1:20** — Naomi means "Pleasant," whereas Mara means "Bitter."
