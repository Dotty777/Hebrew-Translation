## Text

**3:1** Then Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, should I not seek a place of rest for you, that it may be well with you?

**3:2** And now, is not Boaz our kinsman, with whose young women you have been? Behold, he is winnowing barley at the threshing floor tonight.

**3:3** Bathe therefore, anoint yourself, put your garments upon you, and go down to the threshing floor; do not let yourself be known to the man until he finishes eating and drinking.

**3:4** And it shall come to pass, when he lies down, that you shall mark the place where he lies; then approach, uncover his feet, and lie down, and he himself will declare to you what you are to do."

**3:5** Then she said to her, "All that you tell me, I will do."

**3:6** Then she went down to the threshing floor and carried out everything her mother-in-law had commanded her.

**3:7** And when Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was cheerful, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain; and she came softly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.

**3:8** Now it came to pass at midnight that the man was startled and turned over, and behold, a woman was lying at his feet.

**3:9** And he said, "Who are you?" And she answered, "I am Ruth, your handmaid. Spread the corner of your garment over your handmaid, for you are a kinsman-redeemer."

**3:10** Then he said, "Blessed are you of the LORD, my daughter! You have shown even greater lovingkindness at the end than at the beginning, in that you have not gone after the young men, whether poor or rich.

**3:11** And now, my daughter, do not fear. All that you say I will do for you, for all the gate of my people knows that you are a virtuous woman.

**3:12** Now indeed it is true that I am a kinsman-redeemer; yet there is a kinsman-redeemer nearer than I.

**3:13** Remain this night, and it shall be in the morning that if he will redeem you, good—let him redeem you; but if he is not willing to redeem you, then I myself will redeem you, as the LORD lives. Lie down until morning."

**3:14** So she lay at his feet until morning, and she arose before one person could recognize another. And he said, "Do not let it be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."

**3:15** Also he said, "Bring the shawl that is upon you and hold it out." And she held it, and he measured six measures of barley and laid it upon her; then he went into the city.

**3:16** When she came to her mother-in-law, she said, "Who are you, my daughter?" Then she told her all that the man had done for her.

**3:17** And she said, "These six measures of barley he gave to me, for he said to me, 'Do not go empty-handed to your mother-in-law.'"

**3:18** Then she said, "Wait quietly, my daughter, until you know how the matter will turn out; for the man will not rest until he has settled the matter today."

## Translator Notes

- **3:3** — The Qere (read forms) gives plural "your garments" and the standard feminine ending for "go down," whereas the Ketiv (written forms) has singular "your garment" and an archaic feminine ending (similarly for "lie down" in verse 4).
- **3:9** — Hebrew literally reads "Spread your wing," referring to the corner of a garment as a token of marital protection and echoing Boaz's blessing in 2:12.
- **3:12** — The Ketiv (written text) includes a particle that the Qere (read tradition) omits; conversely, in verses 5 and 17, "to me" is supplied in the Qere though not written in the Ketiv.
- **3:14** — The Ketiv preserves a variant spelling of "before," whereas the Qere gives the standard form.
- **3:15** — The Masoretic Hebrew verb is masculine singular ("he went into the city"), referring to Boaz; the Hebrew phrase "six of barley" implies six measures.
- **3:16** — Literally "Who are you, my daughter?"—an idiom asking both for identification in the pre-dawn dimness and how Ruth had fared.
