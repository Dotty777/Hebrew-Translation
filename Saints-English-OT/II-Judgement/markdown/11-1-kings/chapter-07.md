## Text

**7:1** And King Solomon sent and brought Hiram from Tyre.

**7:2** He was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali, and his father was a man of Tyre, a craftsman in bronze; he was filled with wisdom, understanding, and knowledge to perform every kind of work in bronze. So he came to King Solomon and accomplished all his work.

**7:3** He cast the two pillars of bronze: eighteen cubits was the height of the one pillar, and a cord of twelve cubits encompassed the second pillar.

**7:4** And he made two capitals of molten bronze to set upon the tops of the pillars: five cubits was the height of the one capital, and five cubits was the height of the second capital.

**7:5** There were networks of latticework and wreaths of chainwork for the capitals that were upon the top of the pillars: seven for the one capital, and seven for the second capital.

**7:6** So he made the pillars, with two rows all around upon the one network to cover the capitals that were on top of the pomegranates, and thus he did for the second capital. And the capitals upon the two pillars were also above, opposite the rounded bulge that was beside the network; and the pomegranates were two hundred, in rows all around upon the second capital.

**7:7** Then he set up the pillars for the porch of the temple: he set up the right pillar and called its name Jachin, and he set up the left pillar and called its name Boaz.

**7:8** And the capitals that were upon the top of the pillars in the porch were of lily work, four cubits. And upon the top of the pillars was lily work; thus the work of the pillars was finished.

**7:10** And he made the molten Sea, ten cubits from brim to brim, completely round; its height was five cubits, and a line of thirty cubits encompassed it all around.

**7:11** Beneath its brim all around were gourds encircling it, ten to a cubit, surrounding the Sea all around; the gourds were in two rows, cast together in its casting.

**7:12** Its thickness was a handbreadth, and its brim was fashioned like the brim of a cup, like a lily blossom; it held two thousand baths.

**7:13** It stood upon twelve oxen: three facing north, three facing west, three facing south, and three facing east; the Sea was set upon them above, and all their hinder parts were turned inward.

**7:14** He also made the ten stands of bronze: four cubits was the length of each stand, four cubits its width, and three cubits its height.

**7:15** And this was the workmanship of the stands: they had panels, and panels were between the frames.

**7:16** Upon the panels that were between the frames were lions, oxen, and cherubim, and upon the frames there was a pedestal above; and beneath the lions and the oxen were wreaths of hanging work.

**7:17** Each stand had four bronze wheels and axles of bronze, and its four feet had supports; beneath the laver the supports were cast, with wreaths beside each one.

**7:18** Its opening was within the crown and rose upward by a cubit; its opening was round, of pedestal workmanship, a cubit and a half, and upon its opening also were engravings, while their panels were square, not round. And the four wheels were beneath the panels, and the axletrees of the wheels were attached to the stand; and the height of each wheel was a cubit and a half.

**7:19** The workmanship of the wheels was like the workmanship of a chariot wheel: their axletrees, their rims, their spokes, and their hubs were all cast.

**7:20** There were four supports at the four corners of each stand; its supports were of one piece with the stand itself.

**7:21** And at the top of the stand was a band half a cubit high, round all around; and on the top of the stand, its stays and its panels were of one piece with it.

**7:22** On the plates of its stays and on its panels he engraved cherubim, lions, and palm trees, according to the clear space of each, with wreaths all around.

**7:23** In this manner he made the ten stands: one casting, one measure, and one form for them all.

**7:24** Then he made ten lavers of bronze: each laver held forty baths, each laver was four cubits, and upon each of the ten stands was one laver.

**7:25** And he placed five of the stands on the right side of the house, and five on the left side of the house; and he set the Sea on the right side of the house, toward the east, facing south.

**7:26** Hiram also made the lavers, the shovels, and the bowls. So Hiram finished all the work that he performed for King Solomon for the house of the LORD:

**7:27** the two pillars, and the two bowls of the capitals that were upon the top of the pillars, and the two networks to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were upon the top of the pillars;

**7:28** the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks, two rows of pomegranates for each network, to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were upon the pillars;

**7:29** the ten stands, and the ten lavers upon the stands;

**7:30** the one Sea, and the twelve oxen beneath the Sea;

**7:31** the pots, the shovels, and the bowls. All these vessels that Hiram made for King Solomon for the house of the LORD were of burnished bronze.

**7:32** And Solomon left all the vessels unweighed, because they were exceedingly numerous; the weight of the bronze was not ascertained.

**7:33** In the plain of the Jordan the king cast them, in the clay ground between Succoth and Zarethan.

**7:34** Thus Solomon made all the furnishings that were in the house of the LORD: the altar of gold, and the table of gold upon which was the showbread;

**7:35** the lampstands of pure gold, five on the right and five on the left before the inner sanctuary, with the blossoms, the lamps, and the tongs of gold;

**7:36** the basins, the trimmers, the bowls, the ladles, and the firepans of pure gold; and the hinges of gold, both for the doors of the inner house, the Most Holy Place, and for the doors of the house, that is, of the temple.

**7:37** So all the work that King Solomon performed for the house of the LORD was finished. And Solomon brought in the things that his father David had dedicated: the silver, the gold, and the vessels he placed in the treasuries of the house of the LORD.

**7:38** Now Solomon was thirteen years in building his own house, and he finished all his house.

**7:39** He also built the House of the Forest of Lebanon: its length was one hundred cubits, its width fifty cubits, and its height thirty cubits, upon four rows of cedar pillars, with cedar beams resting upon the pillars.

**7:40** And it was paneled with cedar above, over the chambers that were upon the pillars, forty-five in number, fifteen to a row.

**7:41** There were window frames in three rows, and window faced window in three tiers.

**7:42** All the doorways and doorposts had square frames, and window stood opposite window in three tiers.

**7:43** He also made the Hall of Pillars: its length was fifty cubits and its width thirty cubits; and a porch was in front of them, with pillars and a canopy before them.

**7:44** And he made the Hall of the Throne, where he would administer judgment, the Hall of Judgment; and it was paneled with cedar from floor to floor.

**7:45** And his house where he was to dwell, in the other court inward from the hall, was of like workmanship. He also made a house like this hall for Pharaoh's daughter, whom Solomon had taken in marriage.

**7:46** All these were made of costly stones, hewn to measure, sawed with saws within and without, from the foundation to the coping, and on the outside as far as the great court.

**7:47** The foundation was of costly stones, great stones, stones of ten cubits and stones of eight cubits.

**7:48** And above were costly stones, hewn to measure, and cedar.

**7:49** And the great court all around had three courses of hewn stone and one course of cedar beams, as did the inner court of the house of the LORD and the porch of the house.

## Translator Notes

- **7:6** — The read text (qere) supplies the definite article in "the network," whereas the written text (ketiv) omits it. The Masoretic Hebrew also literally reads "the pillars" and "on top of the pomegranates" in the first sentence.
- **7:10** — The read text (qere) gives the standard spelling for "and a line," while the written text (ketiv) preserves an older orthographic variant with the same meaning.
- **7:22** — The read text (qere) omits a superfluous conjunction on "its panels" that appears in the written text (ketiv) after "and on."
- **7:31** — The read text (qere) has "these" ("all these vessels"), whereas the written text (ketiv) reads "the tent."
- **7:44** — The Hebrew idiom literally says "from floor to floor," denoting complete cedar paneling from the floor up to the ceiling or upper beams.
