## Text

**19:1** And Ahab told Jezebel everything that Elijah had done, and all about how he had slain all the prophets with the sword.

**19:2** Then Jezebel sent a messenger to Elijah, saying, "May the gods do so, and more also; by this time tomorrow I will surely make your life like the life of one of them."

**19:3** And when he saw this, he arose and went for his life, and came to Beersheba, which belongs to Judah, and left his servant there.

**19:4** But he himself went a day's journey into the wilderness, and came and sat down under a broom tree. And he requested for himself that he might die, and said, "It is enough! Now, O LORD, take my life, for I am no better than my fathers."

**19:5** Then he lay down and slept under a broom tree. And behold, an angel touched him and said to him, "Arise and eat."

**19:6** And he looked, and behold, near his head was a cake baked on hot stones and a jar of water. So he ate and drank, and lay down again.

**19:7** And the angel of the LORD returned a second time, and touched him, and said, "Arise and eat, because the journey is too great for you."

**19:8** So he arose, and ate and drank; and he went in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights, to Horeb, the mountain of God.

**19:9** And there he entered into the cave and spent the night there; and behold, the word of the LORD came to him, and He said to him, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

**19:10** And he said, "I have been exceedingly zealous for the LORD God of hosts; for the children of Israel have forsaken Your covenant, torn down Your altars, and slain Your prophets with the sword. And I, even I alone, am left; and they seek my life, to take it away."

**19:11** Then He said, "Go forth, and stand upon the mountain before the LORD." And behold, the LORD was passing by, and a great and mighty wind tore apart the mountains and shattered the rocks before the LORD; yet the LORD was not in the wind. And after the wind came an earthquake, yet the LORD was not in the earthquake.

**19:12** And after the earthquake came a fire, yet the LORD was not in the fire; and after the fire, the sound of a gentle stillness.

**19:13** And it came to pass, when Elijah heard it, that he wrapped his face in his mantle, and went out and stood at the entrance of the cave. And behold, a voice came to him and said, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

**19:14** And he said, "I have been exceedingly zealous for the LORD God of hosts; for the children of Israel have forsaken Your covenant, torn down Your altars, and slain Your prophets with the sword. And I, even I alone, am left; and they seek my life, to take it away."

**19:15** Then the LORD said to him, "Go, return on your way toward the wilderness of Damascus; and when you arrive, you shall anoint Hazael as king over Aram.

**19:16** Also you shall anoint Jehu the son of Nimshi as king over Israel. And Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abel Meholah you shall anoint as prophet in your place.

**19:17** And it shall come to pass, that whoever escapes from the sword of Hazael, Jehu shall put to death; and whoever escapes from the sword of Jehu, Elisha shall put to death.

**19:18** Yet I will leave seven thousand in Israel, all the knees that have not bowed to Baal, and every mouth that has not kissed him."

**19:19** So he departed from there and found Elisha the son of Shaphat, who was plowing with twelve yoke of oxen before him, and he was with the twelfth. Then Elijah passed over to him and cast his mantle upon him.

**19:20** And he left the oxen and ran after Elijah, and said, "Please let me kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow you." And he said to him, "Go, return; for what have I done to you?"

**19:21** So he turned back from following him, and took the yoke of oxen and slaughtered them, and boiled their flesh using the equipment of the oxen, and gave it to the people, and they ate. Then he arose and followed Elijah, and ministered to him.

## Translator Notes

- **19:3** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads "he saw," whereas some ancient versions reflect a graphically similar form meaning "he was afraid."
- **19:4** — Ketiv/Qere: the written form (ketiv) uses the feminine numeral for "one," while the read form (qere) follows the masculine numeral agreeing with the broom tree, as in verse 5.
- **19:12** — The Hebrew phrase literally means "a sound/voice of thin stillness," traditionally rendered "a still small voice."
