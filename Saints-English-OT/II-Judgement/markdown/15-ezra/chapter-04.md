## Text

**4:1** Now the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin heard that the children of the captivity were building a temple to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**4:2** And they approached Zerubbabel and the heads of the fathers' houses and said to them, "Let us build with you, for we seek your God as you do; and to Him we have been sacrificing since the days of Esarhaddon king of Assyria, who brought us up here."

**4:3** But Zerubbabel and Jeshua and the rest of the heads of the fathers' houses of Israel said to them, "It is not for you and us together to build a house for our God, but we alone will build for the LORD, the God of Israel, just as King Cyrus, the king of Persia, has commanded us."

**4:4** Then the people of the land kept weakening the hands of the people of Judah and frightening them from building.

**4:5** They also hired counselors against them to frustrate their purpose all the days of Cyrus king of Persia, even until the reign of Darius king of Persia.

**4:6** And in the reign of Ahasuerus, at the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.

**4:7** And in the days of Artaxerxes, Bishlam, Mithredath, Tabeel, and the rest of his associates wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia; and the writing of the letter was written in Aramaic and translated into Aramaic.

**4:8** Rehum the commander and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes in this manner:

**4:9** Then Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe, and the rest of their associates—the Dinaites, the Apharsathchites, the Tarpelites, the Apharsites, the Archevites, the Babylonians, the Shushanchites, the Dehaites, the Elamites,

**4:10** and the rest of the nations whom the great and noble Osnappar deported and settled in the city of Samaria and the rest of the region Beyond the River—and so forth.

**4:11** This is a copy of the letter that they sent to him: "To King Artaxerxes: Your servants, the men of the region Beyond the River. And now,

**4:12** Let it be known to the king that the Jews who came up from you have come to us at Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that rebellious and wicked city, finishing the walls, and repairing the foundations.

**4:13** Now let it be known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, they will not pay tribute, custom, or toll, and it will bring loss to the treasury of the kings.

**4:14** Now because we eat the salt of the palace, and it is not fitting for us to witness the king's dishonor, therefore we have sent and informed the king,

**4:15** that search may be made in the book of the records of your fathers. You will find in the book of the records and know that this city is a rebellious city, harmful to kings and provinces, and that they have stirred up sedition within it from days of old; for this reason this city was laid waste.

**4:16** We inform the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, by this means you will have no portion in the region Beyond the River."

**4:17** Then the king sent an answer: "To Rehum the commander, to Shimshai the scribe, to the rest of their associates who dwell in Samaria, and to the rest of the region Beyond the River: Peace. And now,

**4:18** The letter which you sent to us has been plainly read before me.

**4:19** And a decree was issued by me, and search was made, and it has been found that this city from days of old has risen up against kings, and that rebellion and sedition have been committed in it.

**4:20** Mighty kings have also ruled over Jerusalem, exercising dominion over all the region Beyond the River; and tribute, custom, and toll were paid to them.

**4:21** Now issue a decree to make those men cease, that this city shall not be rebuilt until a decree is issued by me.

**4:22** And take heed not to be negligent in carrying out this matter. Why should harm increase to the detriment of the kings?"

**4:23** Then, as soon as the copy of the letter of King Artaxerxes was read before Rehum, Shimshai the scribe, and their associates, they went in haste to Jerusalem against the Jews, and by force and power made them cease.

**4:24** Thus the work of the house of God which is at Jerusalem ceased, and it remained halted until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.

## Translator Notes

- **4:2** — The Qere reads 'to Him' (we have been sacrificing), while the Ketiv reads 'not,' which would imply they had not sacrificed since Esarhaddon's time.
- **4:4** — The Qere corrects an orthographic transposition in the Ketiv without altering the meaning 'frightening' or 'troubling.'
- **4:8** — From this verse through Ezra 6:18 the original text shifts from Hebrew into Biblical Aramaic, the official administrative language of the Persian Empire.
- **4:9** — The Qere supplies the emphatic plural gentilic forms rendered 'Archevites' and 'Dehaites,' where the Ketiv writes shorter consonantal forms (the latter sometimes read as 'Dehavites').
- **4:12** — The Qere supplies the emphatic plural 'the walls' and standard Aramaic verb form 'finishing' for the shorter or variant spellings of the Ketiv.
