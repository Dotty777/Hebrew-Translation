## Translator Notes

- **2:1** — The Masoretic ketiv writes the king's name with an extra vav as Nebuchadnezzor, while the traditional qere followed here reads Nebuchadnezzar. The phrase "the province" refers to the Persian administrative district of Judah.
- **2:6** — Pahath-Moab means "governor of Moab" and served as the ancestral name of a prominent Judean clan, here reckoned through its branches of Jeshua and Joab.
- **2:25** — Kiriath Arim is a shortened Masoretic spelling of Kiriath Jearim (compare Nehemiah 7:29), listed together with the neighboring towns of Chephirah and Beeroth.
- **2:31** — "The other Elam" distinguishes this clan or settlement from the Elam recorded earlier in verse 7, though both entries share the exact same total.
- **2:43** — The Hebrew term Nethinim literally means "those given," designating hereditary temple servants appointed to assist the Levites in sanctuary duties.
- **2:46** — Following the Masoretic qere Shalmai (compare Nehemiah 7:48); the ketiv transposes the middle consonants to read Shamlai.
- **2:50** — Following the Masoretic qere Nephusim; the ketiv reads Nephisim.
- **2:63** — The Hebrew title Tirshatha is an Old Persian honorific for the provincial governor. Urim and Thummim were the sacred objects kept in the high priest's breastpiece to discern the LORD's will.
