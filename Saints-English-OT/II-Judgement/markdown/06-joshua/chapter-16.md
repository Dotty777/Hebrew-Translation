## Text

**16:1** Then the allotment went out for the sons of Joseph from the Jordan of Jericho to the waters of Jericho eastward, through the wilderness ascending from Jericho into the hill country of Bethel.

**16:2** From Bethel it extended to Luz, and crossed over toward the territory of the Archites at Ataroth.

**16:3** Then it descended westward to the territory of the Japhletites, as far as the border of Lower Beth Horon and on to Gezer, and its farthest limits reached to the sea.

**16:4** Thus the sons of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim, received their inheritance.

**16:5** Now this was the boundary of the sons of Ephraim according to their clans: the border of their inheritance on the east ran from Ataroth Addar as far as Upper Beth Horon.

**16:6** And the boundary extended toward the sea, with Michmethath on the north; then the boundary curved eastward to Taanath Shiloh, and passed beyond it on the east of Janohah.

**16:7** From Janohah it descended to Ataroth and to Naarah, touched upon Jericho, and came out at the Jordan.

**16:8** From Tappuah the boundary proceeded westward to the Brook of Kanah, and its farthest limits reached to the sea. This is the inheritance of the tribe of the sons of Ephraim according to their clans,

**16:9** together with the cities set apart for the sons of Ephraim within the inheritance of the sons of Manasseh—all those cities along with their surrounding villages.

**16:10** Yet they did not dispossess the Canaanites who dwelt in Gezer; therefore the Canaanites have dwelt in the midst of Ephraim even to this very day, and became subject to forced labor.

## Translator Notes

- **16:1** — The Hebrew phrase 'waters of Jericho' refers to the abundant springs near Jericho that marked the eastern starting point of the southern border of Joseph's territory.
- **16:3** — The Masoretic text spells the word for 'its farthest limits' defectively without a yod, though it conveys the same plural meaning as in verse 8.
- **16:10** — The Hebrew phrase for 'forced labor' denotes an ongoing tribute of conscripted service imposed on the Canaanite inhabitants rather than complete expulsion.
