## Text

**4:1** And he made an altar of bronze: twenty cubits was its length, twenty cubits its width, and ten cubits its height.

**4:2** He also made the molten Sea, ten cubits from one brim to the other, round all around; its height was five cubits, and a line of thirty cubits encompassed it all around.

**4:3** And beneath it was the likeness of oxen encircling it all around, ten to a cubit, surrounding the Sea on every side; the oxen were in two rows, cast when it was cast.

**4:4** It stood upon twelve oxen: three facing north, three facing west, three facing south, and three facing east; the Sea was set upon them above, and all their hindquarters pointed inward.

**4:5** Its thickness was a handbreadth, and its brim was fashioned like the brim of a cup, like a lily blossom; it held three thousand baths.

**4:6** He also made ten lavers, and placed five on the right side and five on the left, to wash in them; what pertained to the burnt offering they would rinse in them, while the Sea was for the priests to wash in.

**4:7** And he made ten lampstands of gold according to their prescribed form, and set them in the temple, five on the right side and five on the left.

**4:8** He also made ten tables and placed them in the temple, five on the right side and five on the left; and he made one hundred bowls of gold.

**4:9** Furthermore he made the court of the priests, and the great court, and doors for the court; and their doors he overlaid with bronze.

**4:10** And he set the Sea on the right side, toward the east, facing the south.

**4:11** Then Huram made the pots, the shovels, and the bowls. Thus Huram finished doing the work that he performed for King Solomon in the house of God:

**4:12** the two pillars, the two bowls and capitals upon the top of the pillars, and the two networks to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on the top of the pillars;

**4:13** and the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks—two rows of pomegranates for each network, to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were upon the pillars.

**4:14** He also made the stands, and he made the lavers upon the stands,

**4:15** the one Sea, and the twelve oxen beneath it.

**4:16** Also the pots, the shovels, the forks, and all their vessels Huram his master craftsman made for King Solomon for the house of the LORD, of burnished bronze.

**4:17** In the plain of the Jordan the king cast them, in the clay ground between Succoth and Zeredathah.

**4:18** And Solomon made all these vessels in exceedingly great abundance, for the weight of the bronze was not ascertained.

**4:19** So Solomon made all the furnishings that were in the house of God: the altar of gold, and the tables upon which the showbread was set;

**4:20** and the lampstands with their lamps of pure gold, to burn according to the ordinance before the inner sanctuary;

**4:21** and the blossoms, the lamps, and the tongs of gold—the purest of gold;

**4:22** and the trimmers, the bowls, the ladles, and the firepans of pure gold; and as for the entrance of the house, its inner doors for the Most Holy Place and the doors of the house for the temple were of gold.

## Translator Notes

- **4:3** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads "oxen" here, whereas the parallel account in 1 Kings 7:24 describes ornamental "gourds" beneath the rim of the Sea.
- **4:5** — The Hebrew pairs two verbs of capacity to state that the molten Sea could hold three thousand baths, likely indicating full brim capacity alongside the two thousand baths of 1 Kings 7:26.
- **4:11** — Following the traditional reading (Qere) "Huram," matching the first occurrence in the verse; the written consonantal text (Ketiv) spells the name "Hiram."
- **4:16** — Literally "Huram his father," an honorific title denoting a master craftsman or chief artisan (compare 2 Chronicles 2:13).
