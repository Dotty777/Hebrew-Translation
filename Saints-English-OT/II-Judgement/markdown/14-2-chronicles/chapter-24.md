## Text

**24:1** Joash was seven years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Zibiah of Beersheba.

**24:2** And Joash did what was right in the sight of the LORD all the days of Jehoiada the priest.

**24:3** And Jehoiada took two wives for him, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**24:4** Now it came to pass after this that Joash purposed in his heart to restore the house of the LORD.

**24:5** Then he gathered the priests and the Levites, and said to them, "Go out to the cities of Judah, and gather silver from all Israel to repair the house of your God from year to year, and hasten to do this matter." Yet the Levites did not hasten.

**24:6** So the king called for Jehoiada the chief, and said to him, "Why have you not required the Levites to bring in from Judah and from Jerusalem the levy of Moses the servant of the LORD, and of the assembly of Israel, for the Tent of the Testimony?"

**24:7** For the sons of Athaliah, that wicked woman, had broken into the house of God, and had also devoted all the holy things of the house of the LORD to the Baals.

**24:8** Then the king commanded, and they made a chest and placed it outside at the gate of the house of the LORD.

**24:9** And they made a proclamation throughout Judah and Jerusalem to bring to the LORD the levy that Moses the servant of God had laid upon Israel in the wilderness.

**24:10** Then all the princes and all the people rejoiced, and brought their contributions and cast them into the chest until it was full.

**24:11** So it came to pass, whenever the chest was brought to the king's office by the hand of the Levites, and when they saw that there was much silver, that the king's scribe and the officer of the chief priest would come and empty the chest, and carry it back and restore it to its place. Thus they did day by day, and gathered silver in abundance.

**24:12** And the king and Jehoiada gave it to those who carried out the work of the service of the house of the LORD; and they hired stonecutters and carpenters to restore the house of the LORD, as well as craftsmen in iron and bronze to repair the house of the LORD.

**24:13** So the workmen labored, and the work of restoration went forward in their hands; and they set the house of God according to its original design and strengthened it.

**24:14** And when they had finished, they brought the remainder of the silver before the king and Jehoiada, and from it were made utensils for the house of the LORD—utensils for ministering and for offering, spoons, and vessels of gold and silver. And they offered burnt offerings in the house of the LORD continually all the days of Jehoiada.

**24:15** Now Jehoiada grew old and was full of days, and he died; he was one hundred and thirty years old at his death.

**24:16** And they buried him in the City of David among the kings, because he had done good in Israel, both toward God and toward His house.

**24:17** Now after the death of Jehoiada, the princes of Judah came and bowed down to the king; and the king listened to them.

**24:18** And they forsook the house of the LORD, the God of their fathers, and served the Asherah poles and the idols; and wrath came upon Judah and Jerusalem because of this guilt of theirs.

**24:19** Yet He sent prophets among them to bring them back to the LORD; and they testified against them, but they would not give ear.

**24:20** Then the Spirit of God clothed Zechariah the son of Jehoiada the priest, who stood above the people and said to them, "Thus says God: 'Why do you transgress the commandments of the LORD, so that you cannot prosper? Because you have forsaken the LORD, He has also forsaken you.'"

**24:21** So they conspired against him, and by the command of the king they stoned him with stones in the court of the house of the LORD.

**24:22** Thus King Joash did not remember the lovingkindness that Jehoiada his father had shown him, but slew his son. And as he was dying, he said, "May the LORD see and require it!"

**24:23** And it came to pass at the turn of the year that the army of Syria marched up against him; and they entered Judah and Jerusalem, destroyed all the princes of the people from among the people, and sent all their spoil to the king of Damascus.

**24:24** For though the army of Syria had come with a small company of men, the LORD delivered a very great host into their hand, because they had forsaken the LORD, the God of their fathers. Thus they executed judgments upon Joash.

**24:25** And when they had departed from him—for they left him in grave afflictions—his own servants conspired against him because of the blood of the sons of Jehoiada the priest, and slew him on his bed, and he died. So they buried him in the City of David, yet they did not bury him in the tombs of the kings.

**24:26** Now these are the ones who conspired against him: Zabad the son of Shimeath the Ammonitess, and Jehozabad the son of Shimrith the Moabitess.

**24:27** Now concerning his sons, and how great was the oracle against him, and the rebuilding of the house of God, behold, they are written in the commentary of the Book of the Kings. Then Amaziah his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **24:13** — The Hebrew idiom rendered 'the work of restoration went forward' literally says 'healing went up to the work,' picturing the repaired temple as restored to wholeness.
- **24:20** — The Hebrew verb 'clothed' vividly portrays the Spirit of God enveloping and empowering Zechariah for his prophetic proclamation.
- **24:25** — The Masoretic Hebrew reads the plural 'sons of Jehoiada,' while some ancient versions harmonize to the singular in light of verse 20; the plural may indicate additional casualties among Jehoiada's family or serve as a generalizing plural.
- **24:27** — Translated from the qere reading yerev, meaning 'was great' or 'multiplied'; the ketiv reads werov, meaning 'and the greatness' or 'multitude of.' The Hebrew term massa may signify either a prophetic oracle against Joash or a heavy burden laid upon him.
