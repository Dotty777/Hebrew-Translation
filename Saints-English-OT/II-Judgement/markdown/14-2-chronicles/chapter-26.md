## Text

**26:1** Then all the people of Judah took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah.

**26:2** He built Eloth and restored it to Judah, after the king rested with his fathers.

**26:3** Uzziah was sixteen years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jecoliah of Jerusalem.

**26:4** And he did what was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that his father Amaziah had done.

**26:5** And he continued to seek God in the days of Zechariah, who had understanding in the visions of God; and as long as he sought the LORD, God caused him to prosper.

**26:6** Now he went out and fought against the Philistines, and broke down the wall of Gath, the wall of Jabneh, and the wall of Ashdod; and he built cities in Ashdod and among the Philistines.

**26:7** And God helped him against the Philistines, against the Arabians who dwelt in Gur Baal, and against the Meunites.

**26:8** Also the Ammonites paid tribute to Uzziah, and his fame spread as far as the entrance of Egypt, for he became exceedingly strong.

**26:9** Moreover, Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem at the Corner Gate, at the Valley Gate, and at the Angle of the wall, and fortified them.

**26:10** He also built towers in the wilderness and hewed out many cisterns, for he had much livestock both in the lowlands and on the plain; and he had farmers and vinedressers in the hills and in the fertile fields, for he loved the soil.

**26:11** Moreover, Uzziah had an army of fighting men who went out to war by divisions, according to the number of their muster made by Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the officer, under the hand of Hananiah, one of the king's commanders.

**26:12** The whole number of the heads of the fathers' houses among the mighty men of valor was two thousand six hundred.

**26:13** And under their command was an army of three hundred seven thousand five hundred men, making war with mighty power to help the king against the enemy.

**26:14** And Uzziah provided for them—for the entire army—shields, spears, helmets, coats of mail, bows, and stones for slinging.

**26:15** And in Jerusalem he made engines of war, devised by skillful men, to be placed on the towers and on the corners, to shoot arrows and great stones. So his fame spread far abroad, for he was marvelously helped until he became strong.

**26:16** But when he became strong, his heart grew proud to his own destruction; and he trespassed against the LORD his God, for he entered the temple of the LORD to burn incense upon the altar of incense.

**26:17** And Azariah the priest went in after him, and with him eighty priests of the LORD, men of valor.

**26:18** And they withstood King Uzziah and said to him, "It is not for you, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD, but for the priests, the sons of Aaron, who are consecrated to burn incense. Go out of the sanctuary, for you have trespassed, and this will bring you no honor from the LORD God."

**26:19** Then Uzziah became enraged, while holding a censer in his hand to burn incense; and while he was raging against the priests, leprosy broke out on his forehead in the presence of the priests in the house of the LORD, beside the altar of incense.

**26:20** And Azariah the chief priest and all the priests looked upon him, and behold, he was leprous on his forehead; so they hurried him out from there, and indeed he himself hastened to go out, because the LORD had struck him.

**26:21** Thus King Uzziah was a leper until the day of his death; he dwelt in a separate house as a leper, for he was cut off from the house of the LORD. And Jotham his son was over the king's house, judging the people of the land.

**26:22** Now the rest of the acts of Uzziah, from first to last, Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz, wrote.

**26:23** So Uzziah rested with his fathers, and they buried him with his fathers in the burial field that belonged to the kings, for they said, "He is a leper." Then Jotham his son reigned in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **26:3** — Translates the qere reading "Jecoliah" (Yekolyah); the ketiv spells the name with an extra yod (Yekilyah).
- **26:5** — The Masoretic text literally reads "in the visions of God" (from the verb "to see"), describing Zechariah's prophetic discernment.
- **26:7** — Translates the qere form for "the Arabians" (ha-Arvim); the ketiv preserves a longer gentilic spelling (ha-Arviyyim) with the same meaning.
- **26:11** — Translates the qere reading "Jeiel" (Yeiel); the ketiv reads "Jeuel" (Yeuel).
- **26:21** — Translates the qere reading "a separate house" (bet ha-chofshit), denoting a secluded residence for a leper; the ketiv reads an abstract plural form (ha-chofshut) with the same sense.
