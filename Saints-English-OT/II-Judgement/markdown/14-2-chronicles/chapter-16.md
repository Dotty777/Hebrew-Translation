## Text

**16:1** In the thirty-sixth year of the reign of Asa, Baasha king of Israel came up against Judah and built Ramah, so that he might allow no one to go out or come in to Asa king of Judah.

**16:2** Then Asa brought out silver and gold from the treasuries of the house of the LORD and of the king's house, and sent them to Ben-Hadad king of Aram, who dwelt in Damascus, saying,

**16:3** "Let there be a covenant between me and you, as there was between my father and your father. Behold, I have sent you silver and gold; come, break your covenant with Baasha king of Israel, so that he may withdraw from me."

**16:4** So Ben-Hadad heeded King Asa and sent the commanders of his armies against the cities of Israel; and they struck Ijon, Dan, Abel Maim, and all the storehouses of the cities of Naphtali.

**16:5** And it came to pass, when Baasha heard of it, that he stopped building Ramah and ceased his work.

**16:6** Then King Asa took all Judah, and they carried away the stones and timber of Ramah with which Baasha had been building, and with them he built Geba and Mizpah.

**16:7** At that time Hanani the seer came to Asa king of Judah and said to him, "Because you relied on the king of Aram, and did not rely on the LORD your God, therefore the army of the king of Aram has escaped from your hand.

**16:8** Were not the Cushites and the Lubim a massive host with exceedingly many chariots and horsemen? Yet because you relied on the LORD, He delivered them into your hand.

**16:9** For the eyes of the LORD roam across all the earth, to prove Himself strong for those whose heart is undivided toward Him. You have acted foolishly in this; indeed, from this time forward you shall have wars."

**16:10** Then Asa grew angry with the seer and put him in the stocks, for he was in a rage against him over this; and Asa oppressed some of the people at that same time.

**16:11** Now behold, the acts of Asa, first and last, are indeed written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.

**16:12** In the thirty-ninth year of his reign, Asa became diseased in his feet, and his illness grew exceedingly severe; yet even in his illness he did not seek the LORD, but only the physicians.

**16:13** So Asa slept with his fathers, and died in the forty-first year of his reign.

**16:14** They buried him in his own tomb, which he had hewn out for himself in the City of David; and they laid him on a bier filled with spices and various perfumes blended by the perfumer's craft. And they made an exceedingly great fire in his honor.

## Translator Notes

- **16:1** — Many interpreters reckon the thirty-fifth and thirty-sixth years (15:19; 16:1) from the division of the kingdom, since Baasha's reign ended earlier in Asa's rule (compare 1 Kings 16:8).
- **16:2** — Aram is the Hebrew name for Syria, and Chronicles uses the form Darmeseq for Damascus.
- **16:8** — The Lubim were a North African people associated with ancient Libya who fought alongside the Cushites.
- **16:10** — Hebrew beth hammahpekheth literally means "house of the stocks," a prison where captives were confined in a twisting wooden restraint.
- **16:14** — This great burning was a royal ceremonial fire of fragrant spices made in the king's honor, distinct from his burial in the hewn tomb.
