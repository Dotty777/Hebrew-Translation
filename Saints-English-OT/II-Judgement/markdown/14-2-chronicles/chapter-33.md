## Text

**33:1** Manasseh was twelve years old when he began to reign, and he reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem.

**33:2** And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, according to the abominations of the nations whom the LORD had dispossessed before the children of Israel.

**33:3** For he rebuilt the high places which Hezekiah his father had torn down; he raised up altars for the Baals, and made Asherah poles, and bowed down to all the host of heaven and served them.

**33:4** He also built altars in the house of the LORD, of which the LORD had said, "In Jerusalem shall My name be forever."

**33:5** And he built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the house of the LORD.

**33:6** He also caused his sons to pass through the fire in the Valley of the Son of Hinnom; he practiced soothsaying, used divination, practiced sorcery, and appointed mediums and spiritists. He did much evil in the sight of the LORD, to provoke Him to anger.

**33:7** And he set the carved image of the idol which he had made in the house of God, of which God had said to David and to Solomon his son, "In this house and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel, I will put My name forever;

**33:8** and I will not again remove the foot of Israel from the land which I have appointed for your fathers, if only they take care to do all that I have commanded them, according to all the law and the statutes and the ordinances given by the hand of Moses."

**33:9** Thus Manasseh led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem astray, so that they did more evil than the nations whom the LORD had destroyed before the children of Israel.

**33:10** And the LORD spoke to Manasseh and to his people, yet they did not pay heed.

**33:11** Therefore the LORD brought upon them the commanders of the army of the king of Assyria, who captured Manasseh with hooks, bound him with bronze fetters, and led him away to Babylon.

**33:12** And when he was in distress, he implored the favor of the LORD his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers.

**33:13** And he prayed to Him; and He was moved by his plea, and heard his supplication, and brought him back to Jerusalem into his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the LORD, He is God.

**33:14** Now after this he built an outer wall for the City of David on the west side of Gihon, in the valley, extending to the entrance of the Fish Gate; and he encircled Ophel and raised it to a very great height. He also stationed commanders of the army in all the fortified cities of Judah.

**33:15** And he removed the foreign gods and the idol from the house of the LORD, and all the altars that he had built on the mountain of the house of the LORD and in Jerusalem, and cast them outside the city.

**33:16** He also rebuilt the altar of the LORD, and sacrificed upon it peace offerings and thank offerings, and commanded Judah to serve the LORD, the God of Israel.

**33:17** Nevertheless the people still sacrificed on the high places, yet only to the LORD their God.

**33:18** Now the rest of the acts of Manasseh, and his prayer to his God, and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the LORD, the God of Israel, behold, they are in the Acts of the Kings of Israel.

**33:19** Also his prayer, and how God responded to his plea, and all his sin and his unfaithfulness, and the places where he built high places and set up the Asherah poles and the carved images before he humbled himself, behold, they are written in the records of Hozai.

**33:20** So Manasseh slept with his fathers, and they buried him in his own house. Then Amon his son reigned in his place.

**33:21** Amon was twenty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem.

**33:22** And he did that which was evil in the sight of the LORD, as Manasseh his father had done; for Amon sacrificed to all the carved images which Manasseh his father had made, and served them.

**33:23** And he did not humble himself before the LORD, as Manasseh his father had humbled himself; rather, this same Amon multiplied his guilt.

**33:24** Then his servants conspired against him, and put him to death in his own house.

**33:25** But the people of the land struck down all those who had conspired against King Amon; and the people of the land made Josiah his son king in his place.

## Translator Notes

- **33:11** — The Hebrew 'with hooks' reflects the Assyrian practice of leading captives with hooks or rings, though the same noun can also mean 'thorns.'
- **33:16** — The Masoretic Qere reads 'rebuilt' (or 'built'), which is followed here; the Ketiv reads 'prepared' (or 'restored').
- **33:19** — The Masoretic Text reads 'Hozai' as a proper name, though the form closely resembles the plural noun 'seers' used in verse eighteen.
