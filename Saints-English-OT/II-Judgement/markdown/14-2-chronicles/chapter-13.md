## Text

**13:1** In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam, Abijah became king over Judah.

**13:2** He reigned three years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Micaiah the daughter of Uriel of Gibeah. And there was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.

**13:3** Abijah entered into battle with an army of valiant warriors, four hundred thousand chosen men; and Jeroboam drew up in battle array against him with eight hundred thousand chosen men, mighty men of valor.

**13:4** Then Abijah stood up on Mount Zemaraim, which is in the hill country of Ephraim, and said, "Hear me, Jeroboam and all Israel:

**13:5** Should you not know that the LORD God of Israel gave the kingdom over Israel to David forever, to him and his sons, by a covenant of salt?

**13:6** Yet Jeroboam the son of Nebat, the servant of Solomon the son of David, rose up and rebelled against his lord.

**13:7** Then worthless men, sons of Belial, gathered to him and strengthened themselves against Rehoboam the son of Solomon, when Rehoboam was young and fainthearted and could not withstand them.

**13:8** And now you think to withstand the kingdom of the LORD, which is in the hand of the sons of David; and you are a great multitude, and with you are the golden calves that Jeroboam made for you as gods.

**13:9** Have you not cast out the priests of the LORD, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites, and made for yourselves priests like the peoples of other lands, so that whoever comes to consecrate himself with a young bull and seven rams may become a priest of what are not gods?

**13:10** But as for us, the LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him; and the priests who minister to the LORD are the sons of Aaron, and the Levites attend to their work.

**13:11** And they burn to the LORD every morning and every evening burnt sacrifices and fragrant incense; the showbread is set upon the pure table, and the golden lampstand with its lamps is lit every evening; for we keep the charge of the LORD our God, but you have forsaken Him.

**13:12** And behold, God Himself is with us at our head, and His priests with the trumpets of alarm to sound the blast against you. O children of Israel, do not fight against the LORD God of your fathers, for you shall not succeed."

**13:13** But Jeroboam had sent an ambush around to come up from behind them; so his troops were in front of Judah, and the ambush was behind them.

**13:14** When Judah looked back, behold, the battle was before and behind them; and they cried out to the LORD, while the priests sounded the trumpets.

**13:15** Then the men of Judah raised a battle shout; and as the men of Judah shouted, God struck down Jeroboam and all Israel before Abijah and Judah.

**13:16** And the children of Israel fled before Judah, and God delivered them into their hand.

**13:17** Then Abijah and his people struck them with a great slaughter; so five hundred thousand chosen men of Israel fell slain.

**13:18** Thus the children of Israel were subdued at that time; and the children of Judah prevailed, because they relied on the LORD God of their fathers.

**13:19** And Abijah pursued Jeroboam and captured cities from him: Bethel with its villages, Jeshanah with its villages, and Ephrain with its villages.

**13:20** And Jeroboam did not recover strength again in the days of Abijah; and the LORD struck him, and he died.

**13:21** But Abijah grew mighty, and took for himself fourteen wives, and fathered twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters.

**13:22** Now the rest of the acts of Abijah, his ways, and his sayings are written in the commentary of the prophet Iddo.

**13:23** So Abijah slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the City of David. Then Asa his son reigned in his place. In his days the land was quiet for ten years.

## Translator Notes

- **13:5** — A covenant of salt signifies a permanent, incorruptible, and binding pledge, reflecting salt's ancient role as a preservative in covenant meals.
- **13:14** — The Ketiv and Qere preserve two slightly different participial spellings of the Hebrew verb for blowing trumpets, with identical meaning.
- **13:19** — The Ketiv reads Ephron, whereas the Qere followed here reads Ephrain; the Hebrew word translated villages literally means its daughters, denoting dependent surrounding towns.
- **13:22** — The Hebrew term midrash denotes an investigative historical account, exposition, or commentary.
- **13:23** — Following the Masoretic versification, this verse concludes chapter thirteen; many English translations number it as chapter fourteen, verse one.
