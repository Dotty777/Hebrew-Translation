## Text

**19:1** And it came to pass in those days, when there was no king in Israel, that there was a certain Levite sojourning in the remote parts of the hill country of Ephraim. And he took for himself a concubine from Bethlehem in Judah.

**19:2** And his concubine played the harlot against him, and went away from him to her father's house, to Bethlehem in Judah, and was there for four whole months.

**19:3** Then her husband arose and went after her, to speak tenderly to her and to bring her back, having his servant and a pair of donkeys with him. And she brought him into her father's house; and when the father of the young woman saw him, he rejoiced to meet him.

**19:4** So his father-in-law, the father of the young woman, detained him, and he remained with him three days. And they ate and drank, and lodged there.

**19:5** And it came to pass on the fourth day that they arose early in the morning, and he rose up to depart; but the father of the young woman said to his son-in-law, "Refresh your heart with a morsel of bread, and afterward you may go."

**19:6** So they sat down, and the two of them ate and drank together. Then the father of the young woman said to the man, "Please consent to stay the night, and let your heart be glad."

**19:7** And when the man rose up to depart, his father-in-law urged him strongly, so he turned back and lodged there again.

**19:8** And he arose early in the morning on the fifth day to depart, and the father of the young woman said, "Please refresh your heart, and tarry until the day declines." So the two of them ate.

**19:9** Then the man rose up to depart, he and his concubine and his servant. And his father-in-law, the father of the young woman, said to him, "Behold now, the day has drawn toward evening; please lodge for the night. Behold, the day is coming to an end; lodge here, and let your heart be glad, and tomorrow you shall rise early for your journey and go to your tent."

**19:10** Yet the man was not willing to lodge for the night; so he rose up and departed, and came opposite Jebus, that is, Jerusalem. With him were a pair of saddled donkeys, and his concubine was with him.

**19:11** When they were near Jebus, and the day was far spent, the servant said to his master, "Come, please, and let us turn aside into this city of the Jebusites and lodge in it."

**19:12** And his master said to him, "We will not turn aside into a city of foreigners, who are not of the children of Israel; rather, we will pass on to Gibeah."

**19:13** And he said to his servant, "Come, and let us draw near to one of these places, and we will lodge in Gibeah or in Ramah."

**19:14** So they passed on and went their way; and the sun went down on them beside Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin.

**19:15** And they turned aside there to go in and lodge in Gibeah. And he entered and sat down in the open square of the city, for there was no man who took them into his house to lodge.

**19:16** And behold, an old man was coming from his work out of the field at evening. Now the man was from the hill country of Ephraim, and he was sojourning in Gibeah; whereas the men of the place were Benjaminites.

**19:17** And when he lifted up his eyes and saw the traveler in the open square of the city, the old man said, "Where are you going, and from where do you come?"

**19:18** And he said to him, "We are passing from Bethlehem in Judah to the remote parts of the hill country of Ephraim; I am from there. I went to Bethlehem in Judah, and now I am going to the house of the LORD; yet there is no man who takes me into his house.

**19:19** Moreover, there is both straw and fodder for our donkeys, and bread and wine as well for me, for your maidservant, and for the young man who is with your servants; there is no lack of anything."

**19:20** And the old man said, "Peace be to you! Only let all your needs be upon me; only do not lodge in the open square."

**19:21** So he brought him into his house and gave fodder to the donkeys; and they washed their feet, and ate and drank.

**19:22** While they were making their hearts glad, behold, the men of the city, worthless men, surrounded the house, beating on the door; and they spoke to the old man, the master of the house, saying, "Bring out the man who came into your house, that we may know him."

**19:23** And the man, the master of the house, went out to them and said to them, "No, my brethren! Please do not act so wickedly! Seeing that this man has come into my house, do not commit this outrageous folly.

**19:24** Behold, here are my virgin daughter and his concubine; let me bring them out now, and humble them and do to them whatever seems good in your eyes; but against this man do not commit such an outrageous folly."

**19:25** But the men would not listen to him. So the man seized his concubine and brought her out to them; and they knew her and abused her all night until the morning, and they let her go as the dawn broke.

**19:26** Then the woman came as morning appeared, and fell down at the entrance of the man's house where her master was, until it was light.

**19:27** When her master arose in the morning and opened the doors of the house, and went out to go on his way, behold, the woman, his concubine, was fallen at the entrance of the house with her hands upon the threshold.

**19:28** And he said to her, "Arise, and let us go." But there was no answer. So he lifted her onto the donkey, and the man rose up and went to his place.

**19:29** And when he entered his house, he took a knife, laid hold of his concubine, and divided her, limb by limb, into twelve pieces, and sent her throughout all the territory of Israel.

**19:30** And it came to pass that all who saw it said, "Nothing like this has ever happened or been seen from the day the children of Israel came up from the land of Egypt until this day! Consider it, take counsel, and speak out!"

## Translator Notes

- **19:3** — Following the Masoretic Qere 'to bring her back'; the written Ketiv reads 'to bring it back' or 'to bring him back.'
- **19:18** — The Masoretic Text reads 'the house of the LORD,' referring to the sanctuary in Shiloh; some ancient versions read 'my house.'
- **19:21** — Following the Masoretic Qere verb form meaning 'gave fodder'; the written Ketiv reflects an orthographic variant of the same verb.
- **19:25** — Following the Masoretic Qere 'as the dawn broke' (literally 'as the rising of the dawn'); the written Ketiv uses the preposition 'at' rather than 'as.'
