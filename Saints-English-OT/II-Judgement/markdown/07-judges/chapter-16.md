## Text

**16:1** Now Samson went to Gaza and saw a harlot there, and he went in to her.

**16:2** Word came to the Gazites, saying, "Samson has come here." So they surrounded the place and lay in wait for him all night at the gate of the city. They kept silent all night, saying, "Wait until the light of morning; then we will slay him."

**16:3** And Samson lay until midnight; then he arose at midnight, took hold of the doors of the city gate and the two gateposts, pulled them up together with the bar, laid them upon his shoulders, and carried them up to the top of the mountain that faces Hebron.

**16:4** And it came to pass afterward that he loved a woman in the Valley of Sorek, whose name was Delilah.

**16:5** Then the lords of the Philistines came up to her and said to her, "Entice him, and see where his great strength lies, and by what means we may prevail against him, that we may bind him to afflict him; and each one of us will give you one thousand one hundred pieces of silver."

**16:6** So Delilah said to Samson, "Please tell me where your great strength lies, and with what you may be bound to afflict you."

**16:7** And Samson said to her, "If they bind me with seven fresh bowstrings that have not been dried, then I shall become weak and be like any other man."

**16:8** So the lords of the Philistines brought up to her seven fresh bowstrings that had not been dried, and she bound him with them.

**16:9** Now men lying in ambush were waiting with her in the inner chamber. And she said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" Yet he snapped the bowstrings as a strand of tow snaps when it scents fire. So his strength was not made known.

**16:10** Then Delilah said to Samson, "Behold, you have mocked me and spoken lies to me. Now please tell me with what you may be bound."

**16:11** And he said to her, "If they securely bind me with new ropes with which no work has been done, then I shall become weak and be like any other man."

**16:12** Therefore Delilah took new ropes and bound him with them, and said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" And the men in ambush were waiting in the inner chamber. Yet he snapped them off his arms like a thread.

**16:13** Then Delilah said to Samson, "Until now you have mocked me and spoken lies to me. Tell me with what you may be bound." And he said to her, "If you weave the seven locks of my head into the web of the loom."

**16:14** And she fastened it tightly with the pin, and said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" And he awoke from his sleep and pulled out the pin of the loom along with the web.

**16:15** Then she said to him, "How can you say, 'I love you,' when your heart is not with me? These three times you have mocked me, and you have not told me where your great strength lies."

**16:16** And it came to pass, because she pressed him with her words day after day and urged him, that his soul was vexed to the point of death.

**16:17** So he told her all his heart, and said to her, "No razor has ever come upon my head, for I have been a Nazirite to God from my mother's womb. If I am shaved, then my strength will depart from me, and I shall become weak and be like all other men."

**16:18** When Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart, she sent and summoned the lords of the Philistines, saying, "Come up this once, for he has told me all his heart." So the lords of the Philistines came up to her and brought the silver in their hands.

**16:19** Then she lulled him to sleep upon her knees, and called for a man and had him shave off the seven locks of his head. Then she began to afflict him, and his strength departed from him.

**16:20** And she said, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" So he awoke from his sleep and said, "I will go out as at other times and shake myself free." Yet he did not know that the LORD had departed from him.

**16:21** Then the Philistines seized him and gouged out his eyes; and they brought him down to Gaza, bound him with bronze fetters, and he became a grinder in the prison house.

**16:22** Yet the hair of his head began to grow again after he had been shaved.

**16:23** Now the lords of the Philistines gathered together to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god, and to rejoice; and they said, "Our god has delivered into our hand Samson our enemy!"

**16:24** And when the people saw him, they praised their god, for they said, "Our god has delivered into our hand our enemy, the destroyer of our land, and the one who has multiplied our slain!"

**16:25** And it came to pass, when their hearts were merry, that they said, "Summon Samson, that he may make sport for us." So they summoned Samson from the prison house, and he made sport before them; and they stationed him between the pillars.

**16:26** Then Samson said to the lad who held him by the hand, "Let me alone, and let me feel the pillars upon which the house rests, that I may lean against them."

**16:27** Now the house was full of men and women, and all the lords of the Philistines were there; and upon the roof were about three thousand men and women watching while Samson made sport.

**16:28** Then Samson called to the LORD and said, "O Lord GOD, remember me, I pray, and strengthen me, I pray, just this once, O God, that I may take vengeance at one stroke upon the Philistines for my two eyes!"

**16:29** And Samson grasped the two middle pillars upon which the house rested, and he braced himself against them, one with his right hand and the other with his left.

**16:30** Then Samson said, "Let my soul die with the Philistines!" And he bowed himself with might, and the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people who were in it. So the dead whom he slew in his death were more numerous than those whom he had slain in his life.

**16:31** Then his brethren and all the house of his father went down and carried him up, and they buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of Manoah his father. Now he had judged Israel twenty years.

## Translator Notes

- **16:13** — The Masoretic Hebrew leaves Samson's conditional statement abruptly unfinished before Delilah acts in verse fourteen; this translation follows the Masoretic Text without harmonizing to the Septuagint.
- **16:18** — Following the Masoretic Qere (to me) rather than the Ketiv (to her), which preserves direct speech in Delilah's message.
- **16:21** — Prison house translates the Masoretic Qere (house of the bound ones); the Ketiv reads house of the prisoners.
- **16:25** — Follows the Masoretic Qere in both variants: when their hearts were merry (ketiv reads because it was merry) and prison house (house of the bound ones, ketiv prisoners).
- **16:26** — Let me feel follows the Masoretic Qere (from the root for touching or feeling), clarifying the unusual spelling of the Ketiv.
