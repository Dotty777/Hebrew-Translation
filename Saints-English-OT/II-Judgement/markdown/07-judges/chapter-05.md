## Text

**5:1** Then Deborah and Barak the son of Abinoam sang on that day, saying:

**5:2** "When leaders took the lead in Israel, when the people offered themselves willingly, bless the LORD!

**5:3** Hear, O kings! Give ear, O princes! I, even I, will sing to the LORD; I will make melody to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**5:4** O LORD, when You went forth from Seir, when You marched from the field of Edom, the earth trembled, and the heavens also dripped; yes, the clouds dripped with water.

**5:5** The mountains flowed before the LORD, even Sinai before the LORD, the God of Israel.

**5:6** In the days of Shamgar the son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the highways were deserted, and travelers kept to winding byways.

**5:7** Village life ceased in Israel, it ceased, until I, Deborah, arose, until I arose as a mother in Israel.

**5:8** They chose new gods; then war was at the gates. Not a shield or a spear was seen among forty thousand in Israel.

**5:9** My heart goes out to the commanders of Israel, who offered themselves willingly among the people. Bless the LORD!

**5:10** Speak forth, you who ride on white donkeys, who sit upon rich carpets, and who walk along the road.

**5:11** Far from the clamor of archers among the watering places, there they shall rehearse the righteous deeds of the LORD, the righteous deeds for His villagers in Israel; then the people of the LORD went down to the gates.

**5:12** Awake, awake, Deborah! Awake, awake, utter a song! Arise, Barak, and lead away your captives, O son of Abinoam!

**5:13** Then the remnant marched down against the nobles of the people; the LORD marched down for me against the mighty.

**5:14** Out of Ephraim came those whose root is in Amalek, after you, Benjamin, among your peoples; from Machir commanders marched down, and from Zebulun those who bear the marshal's staff.

**5:15** The princes of Issachar were with Deborah; and as was Issachar, so was Barak, sent forth into the valley at his heels. Among the divisions of Reuben there were great resolves of heart.

**5:16** Why did you linger among the sheepfolds, to hear the piping for the flocks? For the divisions of Reuben there were great searchings of heart.

**5:17** Gilead abode beyond the Jordan; and why did Dan tarry with the ships? Asher sat still along the seashore and dwelt by his inlets.

**5:18** Zebulun is a people who scorned their lives even to death, and Naphtali also, upon the heights of the field.

**5:19** The kings came and fought; then the kings of Canaan fought at Taanach, by the waters of Megiddo; they took no spoils of silver.

**5:20** From the heavens they fought; the stars from their courses fought against Sisera.

**5:21** The torrent of Kishon swept them away, that ancient torrent, the torrent of Kishon. March on, O my soul, in strength!

**5:22** Then the hooves of the horses hammered, with the galloping, the galloping of his mighty steeds.

**5:23** 'Curse Meroz,' said the Angel of the LORD, 'bitterly curse its inhabitants, because they did not come to the help of the LORD, to the help of the LORD against the mighty.'

**5:24** Most blessed among women is Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite; blessed is she above women in the tent.

**5:25** He asked for water, and she gave him milk; in a lordly bowl she brought forth curds.

**5:26** She put her hand to the tent peg, and her right hand to the hammer of the workmen; she struck Sisera, she crushed his head, and she shattered and pierced through his temple.

**5:27** Between her feet he sank, he fell, he lay still; between her feet he sank, he fell; where he sank, there he fell slain.

**5:28** Through the window she looked out and cried aloud, the mother of Sisera through the lattice: 'Why does his chariot delay in coming? Why do the hoofbeats of his chariots tarry?'

**5:29** Her wisest ladies answer her; indeed, she repeats her own words to herself:

**5:30** 'Are they not finding and dividing the spoil: a maiden or two for every warrior, spoil of dyed garments for Sisera, spoil of dyed embroidered garments, a dyed cloth doubly embroidered for the necks of the spoil?'

**5:31** Thus let all Your enemies perish, O LORD! Yet let those who love Him be as the sun going forth in its might." And the land had rest forty years.

## Translator Notes

- **5:2** — The Hebrew phrase rendered "When leaders took the lead" can also be understood as "When locks were unbound," referring to a warrior's vow in battle.
- **5:5** — The verb rendered "flowed" literally denotes flowing or melting, though some take it from a closely related root meaning "quaked."
- **5:13** — The rare poetic verb form here may be read either as "marched down" or as "had dominion."
- **5:30** — The Hebrew literally says "a womb, two wombs," a harsh military idiom referring to captive maidens.
