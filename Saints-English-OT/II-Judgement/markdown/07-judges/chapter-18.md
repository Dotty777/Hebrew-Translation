## Text

**18:1** In those days there was no king in Israel; and in those days the tribe of the Danites was seeking an inheritance for itself to dwell in, for until that day nothing had fallen to it among the tribes of Israel as an inheritance.

**18:2** And the children of Dan sent from their clan five men from their whole number, men of valor from Zorah and from Eshtaol, to spy out the land and to explore it. And they said to them, "Go, explore the land." So they came to the hill country of Ephraim, to the house of Micah, and lodged there.

**18:3** While they were by the house of Micah, they recognized the voice of the young Levite; so they turned aside there and said to him, "Who brought you here? And what are you doing in this place? And what do you have here?"

**18:4** And he said to them, "Thus and thus has Micah done for me; he has hired me, and I have become a priest to him."

**18:5** And they said to him, "Please inquire of God, that we may know whether our way on which we are going will prosper."

**18:6** And the priest said to them, "Go in peace. Your way in which you are going is before the LORD."

**18:7** Then the five men went and came to Laish, and they saw the people who were in its midst, dwelling securely after the manner of the Sidonians, quiet and unsuspecting; and there was no one in the land possessing dominion to put them to shame in anything. Moreover, they were far from the Sidonians, and they had no dealings with anyone.

**18:8** And they came back to their brethren at Zorah and Eshtaol, and their brethren said to them, "What do you say?"

**18:9** And they said, "Arise, and let us go up against them, for we have seen the land, and behold, it is exceedingly good. And are you keeping still? Do not be slothful in going to enter and possess the land.

**18:10** When you go, you will come to an unsuspecting people, and the land is spacious; for God has given it into your hand, a place where there is no lack of anything that is on the earth."

**18:11** So six hundred men of the clan of the Danites set out from there, from Zorah and from Eshtaol, girded with weapons of war.

**18:12** And they went up and encamped at Kiriath-jearim in Judah. Therefore they have called that place Mahaneh-dan to this day; behold, it is behind Kiriath-jearim.

**18:13** And they passed from there to the hill country of Ephraim, and came to the house of Micah.

**18:14** Then the five men who had gone to spy out the land of Laish answered and said to their brethren, "Do you know that in these houses there are an ephod, household idols, a carved image, and a molten image? Now therefore, consider what you should do."

**18:15** So they turned aside there and came to the house of the young Levite, the house of Micah, and inquired of his welfare.

**18:16** Now the six hundred men girded with their weapons of war, who were of the children of Dan, stood at the entrance of the gate.

**18:17** And the five men who had gone to spy out the land went up, entered there, and took the carved image, the ephod, the household idols, and the molten image, while the priest stood at the entrance of the gate with the six hundred men who were girded with weapons of war.

**18:18** When these went into Micah's house and took the carved image, the ephod, the household idols, and the molten image, the priest said to them, "What are you doing?"

**18:19** And they said to him, "Be silent! Put your hand over your mouth, and come with us, and be to us a father and a priest. Is it better for you to be a priest to the household of one man, or to be a priest to a tribe and a clan in Israel?"

**18:20** So the priest's heart was glad; and he took the ephod, the household idols, and the carved image, and went into the midst of the people.

**18:21** Then they turned and departed, and placed the little ones, the livestock, and the goods before them.

**18:22** When they had gone a distance from the house of Micah, the men who were in the houses near Micah's house were gathered together and overtook the children of Dan.

**18:23** And they called out to the children of Dan, who turned their faces around and said to Micah, "What ails you, that you have gathered a company?"

**18:24** And he said, "You have taken away my gods which I made, and the priest, and have departed! Now what more do I have? How then can you say to me, 'What ails you?'"

**18:25** And the children of Dan said to him, "Do not let your voice be heard among us, lest men bitter of soul fall upon you, and you lose your life and the lives of your household."

**18:26** So the children of Dan went their way. And when Micah saw that they were stronger than he, he turned around and went back to his house.

**18:27** And they took the things which Micah had made, and the priest who had belonged to him, and they came against Laish, against a quiet and unsuspecting people; and they struck them with the edge of the sword and burned the city with fire.

**18:28** And there was no deliverer, because it was far from Sidon, and they had no dealings with anyone; and it was in the valley that belongs to Beth-rehob. Then they rebuilt the city and dwelt in it.

**18:29** And they called the name of the city Dan, after the name of Dan their father, who was born to Israel; however, the name of the city at the first was Laish.

**18:30** And the children of Dan set up the carved image for themselves; and Jonathan the son of Gershom, the son of Manasseh, he and his sons were priests to the tribe of the Danites until the day of the captivity of the land.

**18:31** So they set up for themselves Micah's carved image which he had made, all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh.

## Translator Notes

- **18:7** — The Hebrew phrase rendered 'no one in the land possessing dominion to put them to shame in anything' literally describes the absence of any restraining authority or regional magistrate over the isolated city of Laish.
- **18:14** — 'Household idols' translates Hebrew teraphim, small cultic images used in household shrines and divination.
- **18:30** — The Masoretic Text reads 'Manasseh,' traditionally written with a suspended letter nun, reflecting a scribal convention that shielded the name of Moses from association with Danite idolatry.
