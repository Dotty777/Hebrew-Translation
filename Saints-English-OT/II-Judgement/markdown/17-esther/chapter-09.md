## Text

**9:1** Now in the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar, on the thirteenth day of the month, when the king's command and his decree came due to be executed, on the day that the enemies of the Jews had hoped to have mastery over them, the very opposite came to pass, that the Jews themselves gained mastery over those who hated them.

**9:2** The Jews gathered together in their cities throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus to lay hands on those who sought their harm; and no one could stand before them, for the dread of them had fallen upon all the peoples.

**9:3** And all the princes of the provinces, the satraps, the governors, and those who did the king's work supported the Jews, because the dread of Mordecai had fallen upon them.

**9:4** For Mordecai was great in the king's house, and his fame went out throughout all the provinces; for this man Mordecai grew greater and greater.

**9:6** And in Shushan the citadel the Jews slew and destroyed five hundred men.

**9:7** And Parshandatha, and Dalphon, and Aspatha,

**9:8** and Poratha, and Adalia, and Aridatha,

**9:9** and Parmashta, and Arisai, and Aridai, and Vaizatha—

**9:10** the ten sons of Haman the son of Hammedatha, the adversary of the Jews, they slew; yet on the plunder they did not lay their hand.

**9:11** On that day the number of those who were slain in Shushan the citadel came before the king.

**9:12** And the king said to Queen Esther, "In Shushan the citadel the Jews have slain and destroyed five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman. What have they done in the rest of the king's provinces? Now what is your petition? It shall be granted to you. And what is your further request? It shall be done."

**9:13** Then Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let it be granted to the Jews who are in Shushan to act tomorrow also according to today's decree, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged upon the gallows."

**9:14** So the king commanded this to be done; a decree was issued in Shushan, and they hanged the ten sons of Haman.

**9:15** And the Jews who were in Shushan assembled also on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and slew three hundred men in Shushan; yet on the plunder they did not lay their hand.

**9:16** Now the rest of the Jews who were in the king's provinces assembled and defended their lives, had rest from their enemies, and slew seventy-five thousand of those who hated them; yet on the plunder they did not lay their hand.

**9:17** This took place on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar; and on the fourteenth day of the month they rested and made it a day of feasting and gladness.

**9:18** But the Jews who were in Shushan assembled on the thirteenth day and on the fourteenth day of the month; and on the fifteenth day of the month they rested and made it a day of feasting and gladness.

**9:19** Therefore the Jews of the villages, who dwell in the unwalled towns, observe the fourteenth day of the month of Adar as a day of gladness and feasting, a festive day, and for sending portions to one another.

**9:20** And Mordecai wrote down these things and sent letters to all the Jews who were in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, both near and far,

**9:21** to establish among them that they should observe the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and the fifteenth day of the same, year by year,

**9:22** as the days on which the Jews gained rest from their enemies, and as the month that was turned for them from sorrow into gladness and from mourning into a festive day, that they should make them days of feasting and gladness, of sending portions to one another and gifts to the poor.

**9:23** So the Jews accepted what they had begun to do and what Mordecai had written to them.

**9:24** For Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the adversary of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them, and had cast Pur, that is, the lot, to crush them and to annihilate them;

**9:25** but when it came before the king, he commanded by letter that his wicked scheme, which he had devised against the Jews, should return upon his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged upon the gallows.

**9:26** Therefore they called these days Purim, after the name of Pur. Therefore, because of all the words of this letter, and what they had seen concerning this matter, and what had come upon them,

**9:27** the Jews established and took upon themselves, and upon their descendants, and upon all who joined themselves to them, without fail, that they should observe these two days according to what was written concerning them and at their appointed time, year by year;

**9:28** and that these days should be remembered and observed throughout every generation, every family, every province, and every city, and that these days of Purim should never cease from among the Jews, nor should the remembrance of them come to an end among their descendants.

**9:29** Then Queen Esther, the daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter concerning Purim.

**9:31** To confirm these days of Purim at their appointed times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had established for them, and as they had established for themselves and for their descendants regarding the matters of the fasts and their lamentation.

**9:32** And the decree of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim, and it was written in the book.

## Translator Notes

- **9:19** — The ketiv reads 'happeruzim' and the qere reads 'happerazim,' referring to rural Jews living in unwalled villages as distinct from those inside the fortified city of Shushan.
- **9:25** — The Hebrew feminine suffix translated 'when it came' can refer either to Haman's plot coming to the king's attention or to Queen Esther coming before the king.
- **9:27** — The ketiv uses the singular verb 'veqibbel' ('and took,' treating 'the Jews' as a collective as in 9:23), whereas the qere reads the plural 'veqibbelu' to match the preceding plural verb 'established.'
