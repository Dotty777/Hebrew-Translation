## Text

**1:1** Now it came to pass in the days of Ahasuerus, the same Ahasuerus who reigned from India to Cush over one hundred and twenty-seven provinces,

**1:2** in those days when King Ahasuerus sat on the throne of his kingdom, which was in Shushan the citadel,

**1:3** in the third year of his reign he made a feast for all his princes and his servants; the army of Persia and Media, the nobles, and the princes of the provinces were before him,

**1:4** as he displayed the riches of his glorious kingdom and the honor of his splendid majesty for many days, one hundred and eighty days.

**1:5** And when these days were fulfilled, the king made a feast of seven days for all the people who were present in Shushan the citadel, from the greatest to the least, in the court of the garden of the king's palace.

**1:6** There were white and blue cotton hangings, fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings and pillars of marble; couches of gold and silver were upon a mosaic pavement of porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl, and dark stone.

**1:7** And drinks were served in vessels of gold, the vessels differing one from another, and royal wine was in abundance, according to the bounty of the king.

**1:8** And the drinking was according to the law: there was no compulsion, for so the king had directed all the officers of his house, that they should do according to each man's pleasure.

**1:9** Queen Vashti also made a feast for the women in the royal house that belonged to King Ahasuerus.

**1:10** On the seventh day, when the heart of the king was merry with wine, he commanded Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha, Abagtha, Zethar, and Carcas, the seven eunuchs who ministered in the presence of King Ahasuerus,

**1:11** to bring Queen Vashti before the king wearing the royal crown, in order to show the peoples and the princes her beauty, for she was lovely to behold.

**1:12** But Queen Vashti refused to come at the king's command delivered by the hand of the eunuchs; therefore the king became exceedingly angry, and his fury burned within him.

**1:13** Then the king spoke to the wise men who understood the times, for such was the king's custom before all who knew law and justice;

**1:14** and those closest to him were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena, and Memucan, the seven princes of Persia and Media who saw the king's face and sat foremost in the kingdom:

**1:15** "According to the law, what shall be done to Queen Vashti, because she has not carried out the command of King Ahasuerus delivered by the hand of the eunuchs?"

**1:16** And Memucan answered before the king and the princes, "Queen Vashti has not wronged the king alone, but also all the princes and all the peoples who are in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus.

**1:17** For the queen's deed will go forth to all the women, causing them to despise their husbands in their eyes when they say, 'King Ahasuerus commanded Queen Vashti to be brought before him, yet she did not come.'

**1:18** And this very day the noble ladies of Persia and Media who have heard of the queen's deed will speak of it to all the king's princes, and there will be plenty of contempt and wrath.

**1:19** If it pleases the king, let a royal decree go forth from before him, and let it be written among the laws of Persia and Media so that it may not be altered, that Vashti shall come no more before King Ahasuerus; and let the king give her royal position to another who is better than she.

**1:20** And when the king's edict which he shall make is heard throughout all his kingdom—great though it is—all the women will give honor to their husbands, from the greatest to the least."

**1:21** And the saying pleased the king and the princes, and the king did according to the word of Memucan.

**1:22** So he sent letters to all the king's provinces, to every province according to its script and to every people according to its language, that every man should be master in his own house and speak in the language of his own people.

## Translator Notes

- **1:1** — Cush refers to ancient Nubia and Upper Ethiopia south of Egypt; the pairing of India and Cush marks the eastern and southwestern limits of the Persian Empire under Ahasuerus (Xerxes I).
- **1:6** — This description uses rare loanwords for fine white cotton (karpas) and four luxury paving materials—porphyry, white marble, mother-of-pearl, and dark stone—whose exact mineral identifications remain partly uncertain.
- **1:16** — Ketiv/Qere: the written consonantal text (ketiv) spells the prince's name Mwmkn, while the traditional reading (qere) reads Memucan, matching verses 14 and 21.
