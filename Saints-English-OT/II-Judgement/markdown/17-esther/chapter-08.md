## Text

**8:1** On that day King Ahasuerus gave to Queen Esther the house of Haman, the adversary of the Jews. And Mordecai came before the king, for Esther had declared what he was to her.

**8:2** And the king took off his signet ring, which he had taken from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai; and Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman.

**8:3** Now Esther spoke again before the king, fell at his feet, and wept and implored him to put away the evil of Haman the Agagite and his scheme which he had devised against the Jews.

**8:4** And the king held out the golden scepter toward Esther. So Esther arose and stood before the king.

**8:5** And she said, "If it pleases the king, and if I have found favor before him, and the matter is right before the king, and I am pleasing in his sight, let it be written to revoke the letters, the scheme of Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, which he wrote to annihilate the Jews who are in all the provinces of the king.

**8:6** For how can I bear to see the calamity that will overtake my people? And how can I bear to see the destruction of my kindred?"

**8:7** Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther and to Mordecai the Jew, "Behold, I have given the house of Haman to Esther, and they have hanged him upon the gallows because he stretched out his hand against the Jews.

**8:8** Now you yourselves write concerning the Jews as is good in your eyes, in the king's name, and seal it with the king's signet ring; for a writing that is written in the king's name and sealed with the king's signet ring cannot be revoked."

**8:9** So the king's scribes were summoned at that time, in the third month, which is the month of Sivan, on the twenty-third day of the month; and it was written according to all that Mordecai commanded to the Jews, and to the satraps, the governors, and the princes of the provinces from India to Cush, one hundred and twenty-seven provinces, to every province according to its own script, to every people according to its own language, and to the Jews according to their own script and according to their own language.

**8:10** And he wrote in the name of King Ahasuerus, sealed it with the king's signet ring, and sent letters by couriers on horseback, riding swift royal steeds bred from the royal stud.

**8:11** By these letters the king granted to the Jews who were in every city to assemble and defend their lives, to destroy, to slay, and to annihilate every armed force of any people or province that might assault them, both little ones and women, and to plunder their spoil,

**8:12** on one day in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar.

**8:13** A copy of the document was to be issued as a decree in every province and openly published to all the peoples, that the Jews might be ready on that day to avenge themselves on their enemies.

**8:14** The couriers, riding the swift royal steeds, went out, hastened and pressed on by the king's command; and the decree was issued in Shushan the citadel.

**8:15** And Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal apparel of blue and white, with a great crown of gold and a mantle of fine linen and purple; and the city of Shushan shouted aloud and rejoiced.

**8:16** For the Jews there was light and gladness, joy and honor.

**8:17** And in every province and in every city, wherever the king's word and his decree arrived, there was gladness and joy for the Jews, a feast and a festive day. And many among the peoples of the land declared themselves Jews, for the dread of the Jews had fallen upon them.

## Translator Notes

- **8:1** — Here and in 8:7, 8:13, 9:15, and 9:18, the ketiv preserves an extended double-yod spelling while the qere reads the standard form for 'the Jews.'
- **8:10** — The Persian loanword 'achashteranim' denotes horses reserved for royal service, paired with 'benei harammakhim,' referring to thoroughbred stock from the royal stud.
- **8:13** — The ketiv reads 'atudim' and the qere reads the standard Hebrew form 'atidim,' both meaning 'ready' or 'prepared.'
- **8:17** — The reflexive verb 'mityahadim' denotes 'declaring themselves Jews' or 'identifying as Jews,' encompassing religious conversion or protective alignment with the Jewish community.
