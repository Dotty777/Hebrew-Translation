## Text

**3:1** After these things King Ahasuerus promoted Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, and exalted him, and set his seat above all the princes who were with him.

**3:2** And all the king's servants who were at the king's gate bowed down and paid homage to Haman, for so the king had commanded concerning him. Yet Mordecai would neither bow down nor pay homage.

**3:3** Then the king's servants who were at the king's gate said to Mordecai, "Why do you transgress the king's commandment?"

**3:4** Now it came to pass, when they spoke to him day after day and he would not listen to them, that they told Haman, to see whether Mordecai's words would stand; for he had told them that he was a Jew.

**3:5** And when Haman saw that Mordecai neither bowed down nor paid homage to him, Haman was filled with wrath.

**3:6** Yet he disdained to lay hands on Mordecai alone, for they had told him of the people of Mordecai; therefore Haman sought to destroy all the Jews throughout the entire kingdom of Ahasuerus, the people of Mordecai.

**3:7** In the first month, which is the month of Nisan, in the twelfth year of King Ahasuerus, they cast Pur—that is, the lot—before Haman from day to day and from month to month, to the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar.

**3:8** Then Haman said to King Ahasuerus, "There is a certain people scattered abroad and dispersed among the peoples in all the provinces of your kingdom; and their laws differ from those of every other people, and they do not keep the king's laws. Therefore it is not to the king's profit to let them remain.

**3:9** If it pleases the king, let a decree be written that they be destroyed, and I will weigh out ten thousand talents of silver into the hands of those who carry out the business, to bring it into the king's treasuries."

**3:10** So the king took his signet ring off his hand and gave it to Haman the son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews.

**3:11** And the king said to Haman, "The silver is given to you, and the people also, to do with them as seems good in your eyes."

**3:12** Then the king's scribes were summoned on the thirteenth day of the first month, and everything was written according to all that Haman commanded to the king's satraps, and to the governors who were over every province, and to the princes of every people, to every province according to its script and to every people according to its language. In the name of King Ahasuerus it was written, and it was sealed with the king's signet ring.

**3:13** And letters were sent by the hand of couriers to all the king's provinces, to destroy, to slay, and to annihilate all the Jews, both young and old, little children and women, in one day, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar, and to plunder their spoil.

**3:14** A copy of the writing was to be issued as a decree in every province and published openly to all the peoples, that they should be ready for that day.

**3:15** The couriers went forth in haste at the king's command, and the decree was issued in Shushan the citadel. And the king and Haman sat down to drink, while the city of Shushan was in perplexity.

## Translator Notes

- **3:4** — Ketiv/Qere: the written text (ketiv) reads "in their speaking" (with the preposition be-), while the traditional reading (qere) reads "when they spoke" (with the preposition ke-).
- **3:7** — Pur is a loanword from Akkadian puru meaning "lot," which the narrator glosses with the Hebrew term haggoral ("the lot") and which later gives the Festival of Purim its name.
- **3:12** — Satraps translates an Old Persian loanword (achashdarpenim) designating the chief royal viceroys who oversaw major regions of the empire.
