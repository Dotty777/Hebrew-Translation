## Text

**7:1** So the king and Haman came to drink with Esther the queen.

**7:2** And on the second day also, at the banquet of wine, the king said to Esther, "What is your petition, Queen Esther? It shall be granted to you. And what is your request? Up to half of the kingdom, it shall be done."

**7:3** Then Queen Esther answered and said, "If I have found favor in your sight, O king, and if it pleases the king, let my life be granted to me as my petition, and my people as my request.

**7:4** For we have been sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be slain, and to be annihilated. If we had been sold as male servants and female servants, I would have kept silent, although the adversary could not compensate for the loss to the king."

**7:5** Then King Ahasuerus spoke and said to Queen Esther, "Who is he, and where is he, whose heart has emboldened him to do such a thing?"

**7:6** And Esther said, "An adversary and an enemy—this wicked Haman!" And Haman was struck with terror before the king and the queen.

**7:7** Then the king arose in his wrath from the banquet of wine and went into the palace garden, while Haman stayed behind to plead for his life from Queen Esther, for he saw that evil was determined against him by the king.

**7:8** When the king returned from the palace garden to the hall of the banquet of wine, Haman had fallen upon the couch where Esther was. Then the king said, "Will he even assault the queen with me in the house?" As the word went out from the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

**7:9** Now Harbona, one of the eunuchs before the king, said, "Behold, also, the gallows fifty cubits high, which Haman made for Mordecai, who spoke good on behalf of the king, is standing at the house of Haman." And the king said, "Hang him upon it!"

**7:10** So they hanged Haman upon the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the wrath of the king subsided.

## Translator Notes

- **7:4** — The closing Hebrew clause literally reads 'for the adversary [or: affliction] is not equal to the loss of the king,' indicating either that the adversary could not offset the royal loss or that the hardship of servitude would not warrant troubling the king.
- **7:9** — The Hebrew word translated 'gallows' literally means 'tree' or 'wooden pole,' referring to a tall stake for public hanging or impalement, fifty cubits (about seventy-five feet) high.
