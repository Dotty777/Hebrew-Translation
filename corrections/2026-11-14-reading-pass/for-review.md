## Exodus 1–18

# hebrew-001 (Exodus 1–18): notes

Verses left for a specialist or an editorial decision (not changed):

- **16:15 “What is it?”**: Rashi (with Onkelos’ *manna hu*) reads *man hu* as “It is a portion of food” (they gave it that name because they did not know what it was), not as Aramaic “what?”. Adopting this would also affect 16:31.
- **3:14 Ehyeh**: the column has “I am who I am” and “‘I am has sent me to you.’” Rashi reads *ehyeh* as future (“I will be what I will be”: with them in this trouble and in later ones). Also, the lowercase “I am” used as a name reads oddly. Editorial decision on wording and capitals.
- **8:17, 8:18, 8:20, 8:25, 8:27 *he‘arov***: rendered “swarms”. Rashi reads it as a mixture of wild beasts (snakes, scorpions). I left the neutral rendering. This needs one decision covering all five rows.
- **8:8 “the frogs He had brought upon Pharaoh”**: *asher sam le-Pharaoh* may mean the time or terms Moses had set with Pharaoh (commentators such as Rashbam read it that way). The capital “He” commits the verse to God. Specialist point.
- **7:17 “with the staff in My hand”**: the capital “My” makes God the one holding the staff, but Moses and Aaron hold it in 7:19–20. Whether the speaker here is God or Moses is a specialist point.
- **4:16, 7:1 “as God”**: Onkelos (*lerav*) and Rashi read “as a master/judge”. Left literal.
- **17:5 “the Nile”**: the column renders *ha-ye’or* as “the river” everywhere else in this chunk (7:15–25, 8:5). This is a consistency question only.

Data:

- No data problems found: every row has both SRC and EN, there are no straight quotes, archaic pronouns or stray whitespace, and none of the rows is LOCKED.
- Rows 7:26–8:28 follow Hebrew/Greek numbering and carry `[english_reference]` tags (= English 8:1–8:32). Their content is aligned.
- In 4:2, 16:2 and 16:7 the SRC shows the ketiv with the qere in brackets. This is expected.
- Convention: every verse inside a continuing speech reopens with “ (e.g. 1:10, 3:8–10). Not changed.

## Exodus 19–33

# hebrew-002 (Exodus 19–33): notes

## Editorial or specialist decisions (not changed)
- **21:6, 22:7, 22:8 (and 22:27)**: (הָ)אֱלֹהִים is rendered “God”. Onkelos and Rashi read it as “the judges”, and 22:8 even has a plural verb (אֲשֶׁר יַרְשִׁיעֻן אֱלֹהִים). These were left as “God” under the column rule Elohim = God. Following the Rabbinic reading would change all of these verses together.
- **25:12 changed to “corners”** (Onkelos/Rashi). The parallel at 37:3 is in hebrew-003 and should match.
- **30:35 changed to “well blended”** (Onkelos/Rashi). Ibn Ezra reads “salted”, and the Talmud’s list of incense ingredients includes salt. Revert if the editor prefers “salted”.
- **25:29** אֲשֶׁר יֻסַּךְ בָּהֵן: Rashi reads it as “with which it is covered” (the supports over the bread), not “for pouring libations”. Needs a specialist.
- **22:25** עַד־בֹּא הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ: the column has “by sunset”. Rashi reads “until sunset”, meaning a day garment returned for the whole day. Left as is.
- **22:8** “claimed as one’s own” loses כִּי־הוּא זֶה (“this is it”), the basis of the partial-admission oath. Hard to put in English that is easy to hear aloud. Left as is.
- **28:19** “ligure” (לֶשֶׁם) is obscure when read aloud. Every gem name here is uncertain, so it is an editorial call.
- **24:7** “We will do and obey” keeps the order of na‘aseh ve-nishma. “We will do and we will hear” is the traditional form, if the editor wants it.

## Data and punctuation issues seen
- **20:13–20:15**: each verse ends with a closing ” even though the Decalogue speech runs on to 20:17. 20:16 has no closing mark. This probably comes from splitting Masoretic 20:13 into four rows.
- **25:2–30:10**: the speech opened at 25:2 (“Speak to the sons of Israel…) never gets a closing ” before the narration at 30:11. Chapters 19–20 reopen the quote mark at the start of each verse, but chapters 25–30 do not.
- **The column wording varies** between “the sons of Israel” and “the Israelites” (29:28). “The land of Egypt” is shortened to “Egypt” throughout chapters 32–33. Neither was changed.

## Exodus 34–40; Leviticus 1–9

# hebrew-003 notes (Exodus 34–40; Leviticus 1–9)

489 rows, 10 locked, 479 reviewed; 7 changed (all minor). The tabernacle and offering texts are accurate on the whole; most readings were left alone.

## For a specialist or an editorial decision (not changed)

- **Exodus 34:28** “He wrote on the tablets”: the subject is open. Ibn Ezra and Ramban say God wrote (34:1 “I will write”; Deut 10:4). Aloud, the sentence-initial “He” sounds like Moses. Should YHWH be named?
- **Exodus 38:3 (v-37-3)** “four gold rings for its four feet”: Onkelos and Rashi (on 25:12) read פַּעֲמֹתָיו as corners (the upper corners). The column says “feet” at 25:12 as well, so any change should cover both chunks.

- **Exodus 37:21 (v-37-21)**: the threefold “a knob under two branches” is merged into “a knob beneath each pair”, as the column also does at 25:35. Left as it is for consistency.
- **Exodus 40:4, 40:25 “set up the lamps”** (העלה נרות): Rashi on Num 8:2 reads this as kindling. The column says “set up” throughout Exodus (25:37, 30:8), so this is a column-wide decision.

## Leviticus 10–24

# hebrew-004 notes (Leviticus 10–24)

## Not changed — needs a specialist or an editorial decision
- **Lev 19:20** “there shall be an inquiry” for בִּקֹּרֶת תִּהְיֶה: Rashi (following Sifra and Keritot 11a) reads this as a flogging of the woman, not an inquiry. Editors should decide whether to follow Rashi here.
- **Lev 21:20** “a hunchback, one who is wasted”: Rashi reads גִבֵּן as long, drooping eyebrows and דַק as a film over the eye, so both are eye defects in his reading. The current wording is the usual modern one; this needs a specialist’s call.
- **Lev 10:1** “unauthorized fire” for אֵשׁ זָרָה (literally “strange” or “foreign” fire). Kept because the column uses the same words in Num 3:4 and 26:61. Any change should cover all three verses.
- **Lev 22:4–5** “Nor shall one who touches …” runs across two rows and stops at the end of 22:5 without its own verb (the reader has to carry over “eat the holy things”). It reads acceptably, but editors could smooth it.
- **Lev 21:23** “shall not enter toward the curtain” sounds a little awkward aloud (לֹא יָבֹא means “shall not come”). Left as it is.

## Data and conventions
- In Leviticus (here and in hebrew-003) direct speech has a colon and no quotation marks. Exodus, Numbers and Deuteronomy use curly quotes. I kept the Leviticus style; the column may want one convention.
- The SRC for 18:17 and 19:1 has no sof pasuq (׃). The SRC for 21:10 has a stray zero-width joiner in אֲ‍שֶׁר. The English is not affected.

## Leviticus 25–27; Numbers 1–7

# hebrew-005 notes (Leviticus 25–27; Numbers 1–7)

## For a specialist or editorial decision (not changed)

- Leviticus 25:5, 25:11: נְזִירֶךָ / נְזִרֶיהָ is rendered “untended vine(s)”. That is the plain sense (unpruned, like a Nazirite’s hair). Rashi, following Sifra, reads it as grapes you guarded and kept from others (did not make ownerless). This is an editorial choice.
- Leviticus 25:33: this is a hard verse. The English says “the city of their possession”, but the Hebrew has אֲחֻזָּתוֹ (singular). The meaning of יִגְאַל here (“redeem” or “buy”, as some commentators read it) is worth a specialist look.
- Numbers 3:9: נְתוּנִם נְתוּנִם is rendered “wholly given”. If the column wants doubled words kept as they are (“given, given”), this verse and Numbers 8:16 should be changed together.
- Numbers 6:26: “set peace for you” is kept as the literal rendering of וְיָשֵׂם; “give you peace” would be smoother if the editors want that.

## Data problems

- Numbers 6:27 (card v-6-27, placed after 6:23 in the Greek order) has the reference “Numbers 6:23”, the same as card v-6-23. validate.py looks rows up by reference, so the second row overwrites the first. A change to the real 6:23 (v-6-23) would fail validation. Neither row was changed.
- The formula וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל־מֹשֶׁה לֵּאמֹר is rendered several ways across the column: “YHWH spoke to Moses, saying,” (hebrew-002), “YHWH said to Moses, saying,” (hebrew-006, line 769), and “YHWH said to Moses:” (hebrew-007). This chunk’s Numbers 7:4 now uses “YHWH said to Moses:”. hebrew-006 may want the same change.

## Numbers 8–22

# hebrew-006 notes (Numbers 8–22)

## For a specialist or editorial decision (not changed)

- Numbers 8:16: נְתֻנִים נְתֻנִים is rendered “wholly given”. This is the same question as Numbers 3:9 (see the hebrew-005 notes). Decide both verses together.
- Numbers 9:22: יָמִים is rendered “days on end”. Rashi reads it as “a year” (as in Leviticus 25:29).
- Numbers 14:34: תְּנוּאָתִי is rendered “My opposition”. Rashi reads it as Israel turning its heart away from God, not God opposing Israel.
- Numbers 18:7: “a service of gift” (עֲבֹדַת מַתָּנָה) is hard to follow aloud. Editors may prefer “a service given as a gift”, which matches Rashi’s reading that it is given to you as a gift.
- Numbers 19:2: “sound and without defect” for תְּמִימָה. Rashi (from Sifrei) reads it as “perfect in its redness”, because “without defect” comes straight after it.
- Numbers 21:1: “the road of Atharim” treats הָאֲתָרִים as a place name. Rashi and Onkelos both read it as “the way of the spies”. The editors should decide whether to keep it as a name.
- Numbers 21:14: “Waheb in Suphah”. Rashi and Onkelos read אֶת־וָהֵב בְּסוּפָה as God’s deeds at the Sea of Reeds. This obscure verse needs a specialist.
- Numbers 21:30: “We shot them down”. Rashi and Onkelos read וַנִּירָם as “their dominion” (נִיר), which has perished from Heshbon to Dibon. This needs a specialist.

- The column's style changes inside this chunk. Chapters 8–10 and 18–22 use “sons of Israel” and put speech after a colon without quotation marks. Chapters 11–17 use “Israelites” or “Israelite congregation” and curly-quoted speech, and their formulas end in “saying,” where the other chapters have “saying:”. Not changed.
- “Ark” is capitalised in 10:33 (“the Ark of YHWH’s Covenant”) but not in 10:35 (v-10-35) or 14:44. Not changed.

## Numbers 23–36

# hebrew-007 notes (Numbers 23–36)

## For a specialist or editorial decision (not changed)

- Num 23:23: כָּעֵת is rendered “At the appointed time”. The plain sense is “now” or “at this time”. Rashi reads it as “there will yet be a time like this time”. “Appointed” is not in the Hebrew. Editors may prefer “Now” or “At such a time”.
- Num 25:4: in “Take all the leaders of the people and expose them”, Rashi takes the leaders as the judges and “them” as the worshippers of Peor who are hanged. The English follows the plain wording. A specialist should decide.
- Num 24:22: לְבָעֵר is rendered “burned away”. The verb can also mean “removed” or “driven out”, and Rashi applies it to exile. Left as it is.
- Num 28:19, 28:27, 29:8, 29:13: aloud, “seven year-old lambs” and “fourteen year-old lambs” can sound like “seven-year-old lambs”. The same wording occurs about 13 times in Numbers 7 (hebrew-005), so any fix (e.g. “seven lambs a year old”) should be made across the whole column.

## Data and conventions

- Num 26:1 (masoretic_reference 25:19–26:1): the SRC has וַיְהִי אַחֲרֵי הַמַּגֵּפָה twice. Merging 25:19 into 26:1 seems to have duplicated it. The English is correct.
- Chapters 23–29 introduce speech with a colon and no quotation marks, use straight apostrophes (Balaam's) and say “sons of Israel”. Chapters 30–36 use curly quotation marks and apostrophes and say “Israelites”. Each changed row keeps its own chapter’s style. The straight apostrophes in 23–29 go against house style and could be converted in one sweep.

- **Deut 30:3** “restore your fortunes”: Rashi reads וְשָׁב … אֶת־שְׁבוּתְךָ as God returning with Israel’s captives.
- **Deut 28:51** “your livestock’s offspring” renders פְּרִי (“fruit”), as in 28:11 and 28:18 (changed). Editors may also want “fruit” here for consistency. Left as it is.

## Joshua 19–24; Judges 1–8

# hebrew-011 notes (Joshua 19–24; Judges 1–8)

## For a specialist / editorial decision (not changed)
- Judges 8:13 “by the ascent of Heres”: Targum and Rashi read מִלְמַעֲלֵה הֶחָרֶס as a time phrase (“before the sun went down”, חֶרֶס = sun), not a place name. Following Rashi would remove the name Heres, so this is left for a decision.
- Judges 5:2 “When leaders took the lead”: Rashi takes פְּרֹעַ פְּרָעוֹת as punishments being exacted (“when retribution struck Israel”). The current rendering follows the modern reading.
- Judges 5:11 “those dividing at the watering places”: Rashi derives מְחַצְצִים from חֵץ (archers or raiders among the wells). The current rendering is defensible.
- Judges 5:13 “the mighty of the nations”: the Hebrew has עָם (singular, “people”). Targum supports “nations”, so it is left as is.
- Judges 5:14 “those whose root was in Amalek”: Rashi applies this to Joshua of Ephraim, who fought Amalek. The literal wording is kept.
- Joshua 22:34 “named the altar, ‘For it is a witness…’”: the MT gives no name (many translations supply “Witness”/“Ed”). The current wording is acceptable but slightly elliptical aloud.
- Joshua 22:17 “though the plague came”: וַיְהִי is just “and”. “Though” is an interpretation, but a reasonable one.

## Data

- Joshua 19:47/48, 21:42a–d and 24:29–33 are reordered or have placeholder rows to follow the Greek numbering. The masoretic_reference values look consistent.

## Judges 9–21; Ruth 1–4; 1 Samuel 1–1

# hebrew-012 notes (Judges 9–21; Ruth 1–4; 1 Samuel 1)

## For a specialist or editorial decision (not changed)

- Judg 10:1: בֶּן־דּוֹדוֹ is rendered as a name (“son of Dodo”). Rashi reads it as “his uncle’s son”, i.e. Tola was a cousin of Abimelech. Both readings are possible.
- Judg 18:30: the English has “son of Manasseh”, following the written text. The nun in מְנַשֶּׁה is suspended, and Rashi (with Bava Batra 109b) explains that Jonathan was Moses’ grandson and the nun was added out of respect for Moses. An editor may want a footnote.
- Judg 20:16 (and 3:15 in hebrew-011): אִטֵּר יַד־יְמִינוֹ is rendered “restricted in their right hands”. It is literal and consistent, but listeners may not realise it means left-handed. Any change should be made in both places.
- Judg 20:48: מֵעִיר מְתֹם is rendered “the city in its entirety”. Many read it as מְתִם, “men” (“from the city, men and beasts”). This needs a specialist.
- Judg 15:19: in הַמַּכְתֵּשׁ אֲשֶׁר־בַּלֶּחִי, Rashi takes the “hollow” to be in the jawbone (a tooth socket), not at the place Lehi. The English follows the place reading.
- Judg 19:8: וְהִתְמַהְמְהוּ is rendered as narrative (“They delayed”). It can also be the father’s imperative (“and linger until the day declines”). Left as it is.
- Judg 11:10: “YHWH shall hear between us” is literal and a little opaque aloud (the sense is “YHWH will be witness between us”).
- Ruth 1:13: מַר־לִי מְאֹד מִכֶּם is rendered as a comparison (“more bitter for me than for you”). Rashi reads it as “bitter for me because of you”.
- Ruth 4:4: “But if he will not redeem it” follows the written third person (יִגְאַל). Targum and many versions read “you”. Aloud the switch is confusing, but the MT was kept.
- 1 Sam 1:5: מָנָה אַחַת אַפָּיִם is rendered “a double portion”. Targum (“one choice portion”) and Rashi read it as a portion of honour. A specialist should choose.

## Data and conventions

- Judg 21:8–9 use “behold”, but the rest of the chunk uses “Look” for הִנֵּה. Left alone.
- Judg 17:1–4 spell the name “Micaiah” and 17:5 onward use “Micah”. This follows the Hebrew (מִיכָיְהוּ / מִיכָה). It is not an error.
